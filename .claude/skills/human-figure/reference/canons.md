# Canons: how artists have proportioned the figure

A canon is a chosen ideal, not a measurement. Compare each with the measured figure (proportions.md: 7.4 heads,
hip joint at half height, shoulders about 2.2 heads wide for men and 2.0 for women).

Figures below are the commonly cited ones. Exact values differ between sources, and between a canon's text and
the statues said to follow it.

| canon | heads tall | character | use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Polykleitos, *Canon* and the Doryphoros (c. 450 BC) | about 7 | sturdy, square, every part a ratio of another; contrapposto (weight on one leg, hips and shoulders tilted against each other) | grounded, strong, "real but perfected" |
| Lysippos (4th c. BC) | about 8 | smaller head, slimmer, longer legs: deliberately elegant | heroes, the upper class |
| Vitruvius, *De architectura* III (1st c. BC) | 8 (head = 1/8 of height, face = 1/10) | span = height; navel at the centre of the circle (the "Vitruvian Man", Leonardo c. 1490) | geometry; schematics, symbols |
| Dürer, *Four Books on Human Proportion* (1528) | about 7 to 10 | not one ideal but many measured **types**, from stocky to very slender | variety in a crowd |
| Richer, *Canon* (1893) | 7.5 | an anatomist's average adult man | realism |
| Loomis, *Figure Drawing for All It's Worth* (1943) | normal 7.5, "idealistic" 8, "heroic" 8.5 to 9, fashion 9+ | his heroic figure: wider shoulders, longer legs, smaller head | heroes (8 to 8.5), fashion (9) |
| Comics and game heroes | 8 to 9 | Loomis's heroic figure, shoulders often 2.5 to 3 heads | protagonists, power fantasies |
| Stylised (Disney, anime, chibi) | 2 to 6 | big head = young, friendly, readable face | children's games, mascots |

## Reading them against the data

- **Polykleitos is close to real** at about 7 heads; Richer and Loomis's "normal" figure are the measured adult.
- **8 heads is the measured 95th percentile.** That is why it reads as ideal: tall, small-headed,
  long-legged, and those three go together in real tall people too (proportions.md, "Allometry"). An 8-head
  figure built from a scaled-up average looks wrong. Build it from the tall end: longer legs, same-size head.
- **Heroic shoulders** of 2.5 to 3 heads are beyond the measured p95 (2.43 for men). They read as
  superhuman. In a realist world they should belong to almost nobody.
- **Contrapposto** is not a proportion but it is the canon's real gift. The weight leg straight, its hip
  high, the shoulders tilted the other way. It makes a standing figure look alive. Use it for idle poses.

## Children (head count by age)

Artist charts, after Stratz (*Der Körper des Kindes*, 1902); approximate. The head grows little after
early childhood, so the head count rises with age.

| age | heads tall | stature, approx. (WHO/CDC median, both sexes) |
|---|---|---|
| newborn | 4 | 0.50 m |
| 1 | 4 to 4.5 | 0.75 m |
| 3 | 5 | 0.95 m |
| 6 | 5.5 to 6 | 1.15 m |
| 10 | 6 to 6.5 | 1.38 m |
| 14 | 7 | 1.60 m (girls near adult height, boys still growing) |
| adult | 7.4 (measured) | |

In children the midpoint of the body is higher: at the navel around age 2, moving down to the hip joint by
adulthood. The legs grow the most.

## Ageing (past the ANSUR range)

- Stature falls slowly from middle age, a few centimetres by the late 70s. The cause is mostly the spine
  (discs, posture), so the trunk shortens and the legs don't.
- The shoulders round forward and the head comes forward.
- Muscle is lost and fat moves to the trunk.
- Treat these as directions, not data; this skill has no measured numbers past 58.
