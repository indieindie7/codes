# Beauty: what the research supports

Attractiveness research is noisy. Most studies rate photos or line drawings, use students from a few
countries, and get smaller effects on replication. Below, each claim is marked with how much weight it
carries. None of it is a rule: a believable cast needs plain, odd and tired faces far more than beautiful ones.

## Faces

- **Averageness. Strong.** A composite of many faces is rated more attractive than most of the faces in it
  (Langlois & Roggman 1990, widely replicated). For a game: a face near the population mean reads as handsome.
  Distinctive faces come from pushing one or two features away from it.
- **Symmetry. Real but small.** Symmetric faces are rated a little more attractive (Rhodes and colleagues). Part
  of the effect is averageness again. Real faces are slightly asymmetric, and perfect mirror symmetry looks
  artificial, so keep a small asymmetry.
- **Sexual dimorphism. Mixed.**
  - In women's faces, more feminine features are preferred fairly consistently.
  - In men's faces, preferences for masculinity vary between studies, cultures and raters. Perrett et al. 1998
    found slightly feminised male faces preferred; later work is mixed.
- **Skin. Moderate.** Even colour and texture read as young and healthy (Fink and colleagues).
- **Youth cues. Moderate.** Large eyes relative to the face, full lips, a small lower face. These read as
  young, which in women is rated attractive. Pushed far, they read as childlike.

## Bodies

- **Women: waist-to-hip ratio. Contested.**
  - Singh (1993) found a ratio of about 0.7 preferred, and it is the most quoted figure.
  - Later studies (Tovée and colleagues) found body mass explains more of the ratings, and preferences shift
    with culture and food supply.
  - The ANSUR women's median is 0.84 (p5 0.75), so 0.7 is an ideal at the edge of the real range.
- **Men: upper-body strength. Fairly strong.** Cues of upper-body strength (shoulders, arms, chest) account
  for most of the variance in rated male body attractiveness (Sell, Lukaszewski & Townsley 2017). The V shape
  (shoulder to waist) is the visible cue. ANSUR men's shoulder-to-hip breadth: median 1.48, p95 1.60.
- **Leg length. Moderate.** Legs slightly longer than average, about 5% above the mean leg-to-body ratio, are
  preferred. Much longer is not (Sorokowski and colleagues, including cross-cultural samples). This matches the
  8-head canon: a little leggier than real.
- **Body mass. Strong, but culture-bound.** Preferred body mass follows food security and era. Thin is not
  universal, and nor is heavy.
- **Height. Moderate.** Taller-than-average men are rated higher in many studies; the effect is weaker for
  women.

## What this means for a game cast

1. **Most of the cast should be average, with distinct features.** Averageness is what reads as "normal, maybe
   good-looking". Character comes from 1 or 2 pushed features (concept-art.md).
2. **Beauty is a story signal.** A clean, symmetric, well-fed, well-proportioned body says "comfortable,
   cared for". In a poor or worn setting that tells you who has money.
3. **Don't build every woman at a 0.7 waist-to-hip ratio or every man at heroic shoulders.** It flattens the
   cast and reads as fantasy. Keep the measured spread and put the ideal on a few people, on purpose.
4. **Beauty in classical art** comes from the canon's order: clear proportions, contrapposto, the eye led
   along long curves. It does not come from extreme features. Classical statues are near-average faces on
   slightly leggy, athletic bodies (canons.md).
