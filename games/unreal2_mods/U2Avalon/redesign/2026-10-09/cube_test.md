# The creative team takes the cube-in-the-desert test (2026-10-09)

The user asked for the "desert personality test", taken by each of the five roles separately. The test is the Kokology
"cube in the desert": imagine a desert, a cube, a ladder, a horse, flowers and a storm (plus an optional wall or door).
Each role ran as its own parallel agent. Each one read its redesign file and its block in `tools/codirect.py`, then
answered in character.

The common reading: desert = how you see the world, cube = yourself, ladder = allies, horse = drive / ideal partner,
flowers = the people you care for or your output, storm = trouble.

## What it says about the team

- **The same five shapes, five jobs.** Every role built a heavy, grounded, weathered cube with a ladder on its far or
  shadow side and a storm coming in. The team shares one taste (the user's: whole shape first, a dark ground with a
  little ornament, the lone figure at dusk). It differs only in what each role reads into it.
- **The horse divides them.** Three of them (Writer, Director, Artist) keep it still, a silhouette that waits. The
  Engineer ties it to the water supply. Only the Level Designer makes it move. That is the team's Emotion x Action
  balance in miniature: four voices for emotion and composition, one for action. So the Level Designer needs real
  weight in reviews, or the town becomes a beautiful still life (the user's design philosophy: emotion x action).
- **The storm divides them too:**

  | Role | What the storm becomes |
  |---|---|
  | Writer | meaning |
  | Director | something kept on the edge of frame |
  | Engineer | a spec |
  | Level Designer | a timer |
  | Artist | backlight |

  Nobody makes it dangerous in the moment. That's a note for the drain's reveal and the E3 brawl.
- **Shared blind spot: people at 2 m.** The Engineer forgets them, the Director stays at 80 m, the Writer stops them
  acting, and the Artist poses them. This matches the user's real marks: too dark, floating props, Q74 (who lives
  where). Keep the in-game marks checklist as the tie-breaker.
- **Clashes they predicted themselves:**
  - Director vs Artist: "who owns the frame".
  - Engineer vs Level Designer: stair risers and knolls vs grading.
  - Writer vs Director: where the storm goes.
  - Artist vs Engineer: palette stripes and plinths cost.

  All four already show up in plan.md's open decisions.

## Full answers
The five full texts follow, as each role wrote them.

### Writer
Salt flat, drained sea, tide lines and a survey stake. The cube is two metres of old poured concrete, 30 m off, stained
on the downwind face, with a brass plate whose letters have worn off. The ladder is a steel one bolted to the back face;
I only know it's there from a coffee tin of cigarette ends at its foot, from someone who climbs it every evening. The
horse is a thin grey working horse in the cube's shadow, waiting for the person who always comes at the same hour,
facing where the climber faces. The flowers are eleven small red ones planted in a line along a crack toward the
horizon, ending where the water underneath runs out. The storm is a brown wall lit from inside like smoke over a works,
coming the way a quarterly report comes; nobody reacts. Far right, on the only rise, a battered wall with one shut
door and a light behind it.
*Reading:* the world as evidence. The self is the oldest, poorest building, the one who remembers. The allies are
hidden, known by their traces. The drive is ritual. The output is remembrance (a memorial). Stress is slow, corporate,
unnamed. *Blind spot:* beautiful melancholy, little action. Ask the others for the Action half earlier.

### Director
Dusk, sun 7 degrees up behind the left shoulder, raking light on the dunes, the horizon on the top third, haze in
layers. The cube is about 4 m, dark weathered concrete with a battered base, half buried, on the right third 60-80 m
off, back-lit to a silhouette with a rim along its top. I didn't walk closer; from here it reads. The ladder is
faded safety orange on the shadow side, the frame's one warm accent and its leading line, never climbed. The horse is
dark, still, 150-200 m out on a ridge, facing where the sun went: the peak frame's lone figure, further out. The
flowers are three pale ones in the lee of the base at the ladder's foot, a reward for whoever walks up. The storm is
far left, a brown-grey wall flash-lit by lightning, coming slowly; it stays at the edge of frame, never the subject.
Far behind, a battered wall with one doorway lit from the other side; I never show what's through it.
*Reading:* the world is raw material for one frame at the right hour. The self is solid, guarded, and lets its shape
speak. The friends are a small chosen circle. The drive is aimed at the far view. The cares are few and unadvertised.
Stress becomes atmosphere. *Blind spot:* never walked up to the cube; judges at 80 m and dusk while the player lives at
2 m and in the dark.

### Engineer
Hard gravel falling 2 % east to a dry wash, which is where the water goes. Flat white midday light, old survey stakes,
work left unfinished. The cube is 6 x 6 x 6 m, one bay of board-formed concrete with the tie holes showing, on a slab
cut into the slope (not filled, so it won't settle), 80 m away (16 s of walking), with a 5 x 5 roller door opening and
nothing inside yet. The ladder is a caged galvanised one on the leeward face, to a roof guardrail (1.1 m top rail,
mid-rail, safety orange); call it decoration until someone checks the climb. The horse is a dun working horse at a
trough on the north side, fed by a pipe I'm already tracing uphill to a tank. The flowers are about a dozen small
yellow ones along the drip edge where the runoff lands: a seepage map. The storm is a dust storm from the west, coming
in; the door and ladder are already on the lee side. Upslope, a gabion retaining wall holding the cut, with no door.
*Reading:* the world as a site and a load case. The self is grounded, built to spec, open but empty until it has a
function. The allies are trusted once tested. The drive is a working system. Care is shown through infrastructure.
Stress turns into a spec. *Blind spot:* no people in the desert, only what keeps them alive.

### Level designer
Cracked hardpan with 6000 UU of open ground to the haze, no cover, no sightline breakers; looking for any bank 64 UU
high. The cube is 384 UU (two Skaarj tall), concrete stained by rebar rust, sunk 30 UU so it reads heavy, about 1500 UU
off (six seconds of walking, the far edge of the fair fight band). The ladder is a steel one on the shady face up to a
+384 high spot; the climb exposes your back, and that's the price of the view. The horse is a dark bay in the only
shade, walking slow laps around the cube: the one thing that moves, and it pulls me in. The flowers are seven yellow
ones in a loose dotted line from me to the cube, two pressed against the wall in the damp shade. The storm is a brown
wall behind the cube, the light gone amber; the cube is the only refuge, about 60 s of walking away. To the left, a
chest-high wall broken twice: a lane with two entries.
*Reading:* the world is raw ground to shape. The self is built to be used, not admired. The friendships are working
ties toward a shared view. The drive is drawn to what moves through my spaces (players). The output is a route with
rhythm. Stress becomes a pacing problem. *Blind spot:* never asked what the cube is, only what it's for; let spaces be
strange (the Writer's ground).

### Artist
Dusk, low sun behind the left shoulder, ochre to brown sand combed one way by the wind; squint and it's three values.
It's empty but not blank: a ground waiting for one shape. The cube is about 4 m, one panel module, 60 m off, its top
just crossing the skyline. It's charcoal steel with grey coping and a brown dirt band at the foot, with one rust-red
panel off-centre, a short battered plinth, and sand drifted up its windward side. The ladder is a caged steel one on
the leeward face, the top three rungs hazard yellow, with rust bleeding from the bolts: someone had to get up there.
The horse is dark, in profile halfway out, against the sky: a still, posed silhouette. The flowers are seven
rust-orange ones in a tight cluster at the foot, on the shadow side, where the eye ends up after the ladder. The storm
is slate blue with red lightning low inside it, drifting toward the cube; really I placed it there because it's the
best backlight. One orange door offset right, with one window strip above it, never two windows over a door (that
would make a face).
*Reading:* the world is something to compose. The self is grounded, weathered windward, its colour off-centre on
purpose (on the skyline, not in its middle). The friends are a working structure. The partner completes the
composition. The cares are few, dense, placed in the shadow. The threat becomes lighting. *Blind spot:* reads threat
and motion as composition and underweights what the player does. The Level Designer should put the horse back in
motion and make the storm dangerous.
