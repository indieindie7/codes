# Sanctuary redesign: reference board

Links for the look in SANCTUARY_REDESIGN.md, grouped by the part of the
brief each one serves, with what to look at. Found by web search from a
container that could not load the pages or images, so none of these were
checked by eye. Cull anything that turns out wrong.

## The colony on pylons

Real structures raised on legs, rusting. The closest real thing to layer 2.

- Maunsell sea forts, Thames estuary. Steel boxes on concrete legs, joined
  by catwalks, seventy years of rust. Red Sands and Shivering Sands are the
  two still standing.
  - https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/maunsell-army-sea-forts
  - https://www.higgypop.com/urbex/maunsell-sea-forts/
  - https://www.warhistoryonline.com/instant-articles/british-maunsell-sea-forts.html
  - https://www.gettyimages.com/photos/maunsell-forts (photo search)
  Look at: the proportions of box to leg, how the catwalks sag, the
  streaking below every joint, how they read against a flat horizon. The
  cluster-around-a-central-tower layout is a good colony plan.

- Ian McQue. Rusted, cable-strung industrial hulls with hand-painted
  detail. The texture language for every module and pylon.
  - https://www.artstation.com/artwork/DLEWzy (a fan model, shows the style)
  - https://www.pinterest.com/artofdavidarroyo/art-of-ian-mcque/
  Look at: how much of a surface is plain rust versus detail (mostly plain),
  hazard stripes worn to nothing, the little ladders and railings that give
  scale.

## Gothic ornament in iron

The rule was steel that is Gothic, never steel pretending to be stone.
Victorian engineers did exactly this.

- Oxford University Museum of Natural History (1860). Cast-iron columns
  and pointed lancet arches holding a glass roof, wrought-iron leaves on the
  capitals. The architects called it trying "how Gothic art could deal with
  those railway materials, iron and glass".
  - https://victorianweb.org/art/architecture/oxford/9.html
  - https://oumnh.ox.ac.uk/the-interior-of-the-museum-1854-1914
  - https://historicengland.org.uk/images-books/photos/item/IMP01/01/0386
  Look at: the arch profiles, the rib spacing, where ornament sits (capitals
  and spandrels, not the shafts). Then imagine it rusted and unglazed. That
  is the Nave.

## The halls

- Halo: Combat Evolved, The Library. Halopedia hosts the Anniversary concept
  art (files named pavel-belov-library-concept) and level screenshots.
  - https://www.halopedia.org/CE:The_Library
  - https://www.halopedia.org/Category:Halo:_Combat_Evolved_Anniversary_concept_art
  Look at: the rhythm of repeating ribs, the height-to-width ratio, side
  galleries one floor up, the doorway visible from the far end. Ignore the
  colour and the Forerunner angles; we keep the rhythm and swap in rust and
  lancet arches.

- Blame!, Tsutomu Nihei. Concrete megastructure corridors, stairs to
  nowhere, people tiny against them. The mood of the halls once the
  colonists' things are gone.
  - https://www.yokogaomag.com/editorial/infinite-world-of-blame-manga
  - https://www.tumblr.com/2001hz/683104397855768576/blame-megastructure-architectural
  Look at: how little detail the walls need when the space is big enough,
  and how one figure gives the scale.

## The blocks in the ground

- Baalbek, Lebanon. The Trilithon and the quarry stones: single blocks of
  800 to 1,650 tonnes, two of them still half buried where they were cut.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baalbek_Stones
  - https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/baalbek-trilithon
  - https://www.ancient-origins.net/news-history-archaeology/largest-known-megalithic-block-antiquity-revealed-baalbek-002385
  Look at: the quarry blocks specifically. Flat top, one crisp edge showing,
  soil banked against the rest, a person standing on top for scale. That
  is exactly a block top under the colony. The grey-green colour is ours,
  Baalbek is limestone; keep the form, change the stone.

## The sky

- Wildfire skies: smoke turns daylight dark red-orange with no visible sun,
  which is the brief's sky. Mars dust-storm photos give the same thing with
  a duller, browner band.
  - https://www.theweathernetwork.com/en/news/science/explainers/ask-a-met-how-wildfire-smoke-turns-the-sky-an-eerie-red
  - https://www.gettyimages.com.au/photos/red-dust-storm (photo search)
  - https://www.istockphoto.com/photos/wildfire-smoke-sky (photo search)
  Look at: how shadows disappear and everything goes flat, then how one
  artificial light reads against it. We want the horizon band brighter
  than the zenith.

## The Keepers

- Fallout: New Vegas, Honest Hearts. The White Legs (chalk paint, borrowed
  Legion hair, Great Khan gear), the Dead Horses and the Sorrows. The design
  doc note about body paint multiplying texture count is worth reading; it
  is the same asset problem you'll have.
  - https://fallout.fandom.com/wiki/White_Legs
  - https://fallout.fandom.com/wiki/Tribals
  - https://tcrf.net/Fallout:_New_Vegas/Honest_Hearts
  Look at: which pieces of old-world gear are recognisable at a glance and
  which are not. Keep only the recognisable ones for the Keepers.

- Shadow of the Tomb Raider, the Yaaxil. Guardians of a sealed thing, bone
  and paint, skull-like masks, ambush fighters in the dark.
  - https://www.wikiraider.com/index.php/Yaaxil
  - https://raidingtheglobe.com/gallery/crystal-dynamics/shadow-of-the-tomb-raider/concept-art
  - https://tombraider.fandom.com/wiki/Shadow_of_the_Tomb_Raider/Artwork
  Look at: the paint as the readable element on a dark body, the mask
  silhouette, and the fact that they never carry anything that glows. The
  red is theirs; ours is grey-green bands.

## What we are replacing

- Unreal II, Sanctuary as shipped. Elara V, jungle world, a Liandri mining
  complex across M08A1, M08A2 and M08B.
  - https://unrealarchive.org/wikis/the-liandri-archives/Sanctuary.html
  - https://www.mobygames.com/game/8377/unreal-ii-the-awakening/screenshots/
  Look at: the layout you're keeping (pad, lift, bridges, main hall) and
  the jungle-and-clean-metal look you're throwing away. Useful as the
  before picture.
