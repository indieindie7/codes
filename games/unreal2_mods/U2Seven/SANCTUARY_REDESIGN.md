# Sanctuary redesign brief

Episode 1 of The Seven, re-imagined as a new look for the colony. The intro
sequence stays: the crew lands, the colony is silent, the colonists are dead
where they stood. Everything else about the place changes.

This is an art and level-design brief. U2Seven runs on the untouched maps;
building this would be a new map through U2EdBridge, like the U2Hover test
map, or a reskin of M08A. Neither is scoped here.

## One line

A rusted human colony lifted off the ground on pylons, dressed in Gothic
ornament, standing on a plain where the tops of ancient alien blocks barely
break the surface, under a dark red sky. Inside, the halls are long, tall and
repetitive in the manner of Halo's Library.

## The three layers

The design is three things stacked, and the player should be able to read
each one at a glance:

1. **The ground.** Dark, dry, cracked soil. Under it, the ancient blocks:
   huge flat-topped cubes of grey-green stone, buried almost entirely, with
   only edges and corners showing through the dirt. Same material as the
   artifacts. They are laid out on a grid that has nothing to do with where
   the colony was built. The colony ignores them. The player should slowly
   notice they are everywhere.
2. **The colony.** Human, industrial, rusted. Raised three to eight metres
   off the ground on thick pylons and trusses. Nothing human touches the
   soil except the landing pad and the pylon feet. Catwalks and enclosed
   bridges join the modules. Below is shadow, dripping pipes, and the tops
   of the blocks.
3. **The ornament.** Gothic details bolted onto the industrial shells: pointed
   arches over doorways, ribbed vault trusses inside the big halls, buttress
   shapes on the pylons, tracery cut into the vent grilles, finials on the
   antenna masts. All of it is the same rusted steel as the structure. It is
   not stone. It was cast and welded by the colonists, who were making the
   place feel like home.

Rule: layer 1 is alien and old, layers 2 and 3 are human and recent. Keep
the materials separate. Stone below, steel above, and never steel that
pretends to be stone.

## Sky and light

- Sky: dark red-brown, heavy, low cloud, no visible sun. A brighter dull
  orange band near the horizon. Slow-moving cloud layer.
- Key light: warm, low, from the horizon band. Long shadows. Everything above
  gets a rust-orange rim, everything below the colony is near black.
- Fill: none outdoors. Interiors have their own light: cold white strips in
  the halls that flicker, and amber emergency lamps at floor level.
- Fog: thin, red-grey, only at ground level. The block tops poke out of it.

Palette, in order of area covered: rust orange-brown, dark red sky, near
black shadow, grey-green stone (small, important), cold white strip light
(interiors), amber emergency light (interiors, floor level).

## Exteriors

- **Landing pad.** The one ground-level structure. A big rusted disc with a
  Gothic-arched gantry over it. Cargo containers. The crew's dropship sits
  here for the whole episode with engines warm.
- **Pylons.** Massive, tapering, with buttress fins. Warning stripes long
  faded. Ladders and a lift cage on the main one.
- **Modules.** Boxy habitat and work blocks with pointed-arch window frames
  and ribbed roofs. Antenna masts with finials. Stains running down from
  every joint.
- **Bridges.** Enclosed, with tracery grilles for windows so the red sky
  shows through as patterned light on the floor. The first bridge is where
  the first dead colonists are found.
- **Under the colony.** Reachable. Pipes, a maintenance path, and the block
  tops, here fully exposed because the colonists dug around them to set the
  pylons. One block has a pylon foot set directly on it, cracked.

## Interiors: the Library rule

The big halls copy what makes Halo's Library work and change what does not:

- **Long, straight, tall.** Corridors wider than they need to be, ceilings
  three storeys up, ending in a doorway you can see from the start. Repetition
  every twelve metres or so: same rib, same strip light, same floor grate.
- **Symmetry.** Everything is mirrored down the centre line. It is meant to
  feel built by a rule, not by people.
- **Layered depth.** Side galleries a floor up, visible through arches,
  where things can move.
- **Colour switch.** The Library was cold blue. Here the halls are rust with
  cold white strips, so the interior reads as the same world as outside.
- **Changed:** the Library's sameness was the point. Here each hall gets one
  human thing that breaks the rule: a mess table, laundry on a line, a
  child's drawing taped to a rib. Otherwise the dead colonists are just
  props.

Hall types:

1. **The Nave.** Main hall, the colony's spine. Ribbed vault, central strip
   of floor grate showing pipes below, doors to every module off it.
2. **Habitat rows.** Narrower, lower, doors every few metres, arched. The
   most personal things are here.
3. **The Works.** Machinery hall, biggest space, cranes on rails under the
   vault. Open floor. The one place for a real fight.
4. **The Well.** Vertical shaft down the main pylon to the ground and the
   dig around the block. Lift cage, ladders, and at the bottom a block top
   with the colony's marks on it: measurements, chalk, a tarp.

## The intro sequence

Same beats as the mod's Episode 1, placed in this space.

1. Dropship lands on the pad. Red sky, engines glowing. Nothing moves.
   Ne'Ban: "Landing zone is clear, Marshal. Nothing moves."
2. Lift cage up the main pylon. The colony above, the block tops below, seen
   at the same time for the first time.
3. First enclosed bridge. Colonists on the floor. No wounds visible. Tracery
   light across them. Aida: "Three hundred people. Nothing moves."
4. The Nave. Strip lights flicker on ahead of the player one by one. Every
   door is open. Every light is on.
5. The Works. The first Izarians, and the mid-episode line about the leash.
6. The Well down to the block. Where the episode's artifact is found, in a
   marine's hands, as the log later says.

## Enemy read

Izarians in this palette are dark against rust and nearly invisible in the
under-colony shadow. Give them one bright thing (eyes, a weapon glow) so the
player can find them, or the halls stop being fair.
