# Ration the attacks, voice the minds, pace the fights

Across DOOM, F.E.A.R., Horizon, Halo, Left 4 Dead, Alien: Isolation, The Last of Us and Half-Life, enemies feel smart for three cheap reasons rather than because of a clever planner. First, attacks are **rationed**: only a few enemies may attack at once, through tokens, slots or roles. Second, decisions are **made legible** through barks, telegraphs and exaggerated breaking points. Third, a separate **pacing layer** decides when pressure rises and falls. The planners themselves turn out to be thin. F.E.A.R.'s celebrated GOAP produced plans of only 1–2 actions, and its action costs had no visible effect on behaviour. Its "flanking" was never coded; it emerged from cover choice and was sold by dialogue. For Advent Rising this means the ModMinds layer is already the right kind of brain, and the next gains lie around it rather than inside it. The recommended order is: port U2FairFights' attack tokens and let feelings decide which token each creature asks for; make breaking points audible with `pawnSoundEvent` barks; tie ModReact's stagger to a DOOM-style damage bucket; add a TLOU-style sighting timer, last known position and search; then build an L4D-style intensity director that changes tempo but never difficulty. Keep utility scoring plus tasks as the architecture. Do not build GOAP or HTN. Every source studied here is reference-only for code: the F.E.A.R. SDK is Monolith all-rights-reserved, and the Half-Life SDK licence covers only free mods of Valve games.

## Restraint beats aim: eight studios cap who may attack

The most consistent technique in the corpus is a hard cap on simultaneous attackers, combined with the illusion that nobody is idle. In DOOM 2016, every attack type (melee, ranged, charged) has its own token pool. A demon must hold a token to attack and releases it afterwards, and pool sizes scale with difficulty ([Game Developer / AI and Games](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/cyber-demons-the-ai-of-doom-2016-)). The crucial refinement is **stealing**: a demon in front of the player can take a token from one off-screen, and the victim cancels its attack. The player therefore rarely sees enemies standing around in view. Bigger pools at higher difficulty make enemies "seem more aggressive" ([serfofcinder devlog](https://serfofcinder.itch.io/archetype-portal-to-hell/devlog/738495/enemy-ai-tech-talk)). Half-Life 1 did the same thing in 1998 with squad slot bit flags: **2 ENGAGE and 2 GRENADE slots per grunt squad**, three ATTACK slots for houndeyes, and cover for everyone else ([squadmonster.h](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/halflife/blob/master/dlls/squadmonster.h)). The Last of Us formalised the idea as a global Combat Coordinator. It hands out roles (Flanker, Approacher, Investigator, StayUpAndAimer, OpportunisticShooter) because "only one NPC needs to shoot at a time". Its single Flanker role goes to the best-rated flank path ([McIntosh, Game AI Pro 2 ch.34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)). Horizon's machines, as reported by AI and Games, circle and wait while one attacks ([Thompson Pt.1](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/behind-the-ai-of-horizon-zero-dawn-part-1-)). Guerrilla's own developers, however, warn that Watcher "buddies can swarm and overwhelm you" ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2017/02/24/the-making-of-horizon-zero-dawns-machines/)). The cap is soft, not one-at-a-time.

Rationing works because it is paired with fairness rules that reward the player's movement and give them a window to act. DOOM's ranged demons **miss more when the player moves fast**, and they will not shoot explosive barrels near the player ([Game Developer / AI and Games](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/cyber-demons-the-ai-of-doom-2016-)). Half-Life grunts that re-find a player who is not facing them play a schedule whose code comment says it exists to "give the player a chance to turn" ([hgrunt.cpp](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/halflife/blob/master/dlls/hgrunt.cpp)). Halo's Isla calls these windows "good mistakes": a Hunter's missed melee has a long recovery that lets the player flank ([Gamasutra 2008](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/in-depth-bungie-on-eight-years-of-i-halo-i-ai)). Restraint must not read as weakness, though. Bungie's playtests found that only **8% rated weak enemies "very intelligent" against 43% for tough ones**, while 92% still called the difficulty "about right" ([Butcher & Griesemer, GDC 2002](https://www.jmeiners.com/shamans/papers/ai/the_illusion_of_intelligence.pdf)). The token cap therefore has to coexist with enemies that hurt when they do attack. TLOU's lesson points the same way: lethality came from full-body hit reactions on the player, because "it was the loss of control that most affected players" ([McIntosh ch.34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)).

DOOM adds two ideas that matter for Advent. Its pain system escalates with sustained damage: a twitch spoils the demon's aim, a falter interrupts its behaviour, and a stagger opens a glory kill, signalled by a flash ([Game Developer / AI and Games](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/cyber-demons-the-ai-of-doom-2016-)). In Eternal, destroying a weak point removes an attack and forces a new behaviour; an Arachnotron that loses its turret charges in to melee ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/116635/hell-razer-the-making-of-doom-eternal?page=4)). id also inverted Rage's cover query into "exposed cover", positions near cover that keep the demon *visible* to the player ([Game Developer / AI and Games](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/cyber-demons-the-ai-of-doom-2016-)). That inversion is the right query for Advent's hounds and Seeker brutes, which should never hide.

## Planners are thin: F.E.A.R.'s plans ran one or two steps

F.E.A.R.'s reputation rests on Orkin's GOAP. Its soldiers run a three-state FSM (Goto, Animate, UseSmartObject) under a regressive A* planner. Actions carry symbolic preconditions and effects over an agent-centric world-state array of about 20 keys. Expensive checks are pushed into time-sliced sensors: SensorNodeCombat polls for cover 3 times a second ([Orkin GDC06](https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/three-states-plan-ai-of-fear.pdf); [Orkin AIIDE05](http://web.archive.org/web/20230912173042/https://alumni.media.mit.edu/~jorkin/aiide05OrkinJ.pdf)). The genuine wins were authoring wins. Designers mixed goal sets and action sets per character type, and Orkin's "seven layer dip" (attack, dodge, melee, cover, blind fire, ambush, dialogue) was added "without manually specifying transitions". Measured play data deflates the planning itself. Jacopin's analysis of logged F.E.A.R. sessions found **plans "rather short (1 or 2 actions)"**, half of all actions occurring 22 times or fewer, action costs with "no clear influence" on usage, and most planner time spent on patrol and animation rather than combat ([Jacopin, AIIDE 2014](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/12728/12728-52-16245-1-2-20201228.pdf)). Orkin himself listed the costs: no scheduling, no compound actions even though "designers always want a specific sequence", and planning for individuals only ([Orkin AIIDE05](http://web.archive.org/web/20230912173042/https://alumni.media.mit.edu/~jorkin/aiide05OrkinJ.pdf)).

The squad layer is the more instructive part. F.E.A.R. had **no complex squad behaviours at all**. A coordinator re-clusters AIs by proximity every second and runs at most one of four simple activities per squad (GetToCover, AdvanceCover, OrderlyAdvance, Search). It sends orders as facts that each AI's own goals may ignore, so fleeing a grenade beats an advance order. A flank is "a side effect of moving to the only available valid cover he is aware of" ([Orkin GDC06](https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/three-states-plan-ai-of-fear.pdf)). Dialogue then sells the result. The last survivor calls for reinforcements that do not exist, and "I've got nowhere to go!" explains a failed cover search. These lines are chosen "after the fact", in a system "completely separate from the action planning system" ([Orkin GDC06](https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/three-states-plan-ai-of-fear.pdf)).

Guerrilla went the other way. Every Horizon machine runs a JSHOP2-derived HTN planner, every herd is a bodiless "group agent" with its own blackboard, and a world-level "Collective" manages spawning and group transfers ([Verweij 2026 slides](https://d3d3g8mu99pzk9.cloudfront.net/TimVerweij/Tim%20Verweij%20-%20AI%20and%20Games%20Summer%20School%202026%20-%20From%20Byrd%20Box%20to%20Debug%20Boxes%20-%20HTN%20Introduction%20and%20Application%20in%20DECIMA.pdf); [Thompson Pt.1](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/behind-the-ai-of-horizon-zero-dawn-part-1-)). The transferable idea is not the planner. It is that **group membership and roles are just facts the individual reads**. The group agent does not tick every frame, so alarm spreads with a delay and you can pick off stragglers. On alert, a herd splits into a flee group and a fight group ([Thompson Pt.1](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/behind-the-ai-of-horizon-zero-dawn-part-1-)). The HFW base melee loop is deliberately plain: intercept, attack when the target is inside the attack's trigger volume, evade if too close, repeat. Richer behaviour is layered "additive on this loop" ([Speck deck](https://d3d3g8mu99pzk9.cloudfront.net/DavidSpeck/David_Speck+-+AiAndGames+-+FlyingAndSwimmingAI.pptx)). High Moon, which shipped both, found its HTN "considerably faster than our GOAP system" because the hierarchy prunes the search. It also warned that replanning on every world-state change caused bugs ([Humphreys, Game AI Pro ch.12](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter12_Exploring_HTN_Planners_through_Example.pdf)).

Halo supplies the counterweight on how decisions should be scored. Halo 2 ran about 115 behaviours as a priority-list DAG with **binary relevancy**, because Isla found float desire tuning stopped scaling beyond 2–3 options. Its event handlers insert short-lived "stimulus behaviours" (1–2 s windows) such as `flee_because_leader_died`, which still lose to higher priorities ([Isla 2005](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2005-proceeding-handling-complexity-in-the-i-halo-2-i-ai)). Halo 3 then replaced designer FSMs with a declarative tree of prioritised tasks with capacities. Squads "plinko" down the tree via a greedy min-cost assignment, and a dead leader puts a task into a "broken" state ([Isla, Halo 3 slides](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~rich/courses/imgd4000-d09/lectures/halo3.pdf)).

**Recommendation: keep ModMinds' utility-plus-tasks design, and do not build GOAP-lite or HTN.** Advent's action vocabulary is already a set of compound Bot calls (`DoCharge`, `DoFallback`, `DoFlee`, `TryNearbyCoverPoint`). A planner would chain these into the same 1–2 step plans F.E.A.R. actually produced, at the cost of UnrealScript instruction budget and the debugging pain that dominated Crystal Dynamics' GOAP retrospective ([GDC Vault 1022019](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022019/Goal-0riented-Action-Planning-Ten)). Three additions would capture what the planners really bought. The first is Halo's discrete thresholds with hysteresis on top of the float feelings. ModMinds already does this for pinned (in at 0.7, free below 0.35), and every task should follow that pattern. The second is Halo 2-style stimulus windows for events. The third is HZD's group-agent facts, so the squad writes roles and each mind reads them. If squad tactics later outgrow this, Orkin's own advice was HTN for squads only, not for individuals ([Orkin GDC06](https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/three-states-plan-ai-of-fear.pdf)).

## Directors change frequency, not amplitude

Left 4 Dead's AI Director is cruder than its reputation suggests, and that crudeness is the point. Each survivor has one intensity value. It rises with damage taken from Infected, incapacitation and being pulled off ledges, and with **nearby Infected deaths, inversely proportional to distance**. It decays toward zero except while Infected are actively engaging that survivor. A four-state machine reads the maximum across survivors: **Build Up** (full population until intensity crosses the peak threshold), **Sustain Peak** (3–5 s more), **Peak Fade** (minimal population until a "natural break in the action"), and **Relax** (30–45 s, or until the team travels far enough), then Build Up again ([Booth, AIIDE 2009](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)). Booth's two sentences are the design brief: "amplitude (difficulty) is not changed, frequency (pacing) is", and "Survivor Intensity estimation is crude, yet the resulting pacing works". Boss encounters are deliberately left outside the pacing control. Spawns use "structured unpredictability": mobs every 90–180 s on Normal, 75% of them from behind the team's flow distance, specials in areas no survivor can see. Valve could not publish gain or decay constants; none exist in the slides, so Advent will have to tune its own.

Alien: Isolation inverts the metric for a stalker. Its "menace" gauge rises from proximity, line of sight and motion-tracker closeness rather than from damage. When menace peaks or stays high too long, the director sends the alien "backstage" into the vents. The director always knows where the player is but feeds the alien only vague area hints, which the creature's own senses must confirm. Behaviours unlock as the player repeats an escape method, and **never because of player deaths** ([Thompson 2017](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-perfect-organism-the-ai-of-alien-isolation); [Thompson 2020](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/revisiting-the-ai-of-alien-isolation)). Halo 3's Objectives add the spatial half of pacing. Enemies hold a territory, fall back to a second point, then to a last stand, and are finally broken, with transitions driven by alive fraction ([Isla, Halo 3 slides](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~rich/courses/imgd4000-d09/lectures/halo3.pdf)). DOOM, by contrast, documents no intensity director at all: its arenas are hand-built and wave-scripted ([Game Developer / AI and Games](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/cyber-demons-the-ai-of-doom-2016-)). For a linear, authored campaign like Advent's, that is a reminder that a director should modulate existing encounters, not replace them.

The Advent director therefore needs a **player-side** intensity, which is a different quantity from the ModMinds feelings. The creatures' fear, anger and pressure measure what happened to *them*. L4D's intensity measures what happened *to the player*. Combining the two is the design opportunity: the director sets the tempo, and the minds decide how each creature reacts to it.

## Legibility is the feature: perception timers, breaking points, barks

The Last of Us shows how perception becomes readable. A plain view frustum failed because players right next to NPCs went unseen, so the view angle was made **inversely proportional to distance**. A sighting timer counts up while the player is visible and down while hidden, and the player counts as perceived at about **1–2 s** for a typical NPC: much lower in combat, much higher if the NPC has never seen the player. Perception raycasts go to a single chest point in stealth, because weighted multi-joint rays were unpredictable. Knowledge is never cheated: a sighting broadcasts a position and timestamp, and if nobody sees the player, that position goes stale ([McIntosh ch.34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)). A pacing fix matters most for Advent. The reveal, surround and search cycle took about 2 minutes in focus tests. Treating the player as having snuck away once they were **more than 5 m from their believed position**, and sending one NPC immediately, cut it to about 30 s. The search itself is a grid of probability that diffuses outward over time, with any cell an NPC can see cleared every frame. The Infected hear through designer "logical sounds" with per-type multipliers (about 6× human hearing), and the player's movement noise radius scales with speed ([Botta, Game AI Pro 2 ch.33](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter33_Infected_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)).

Halo supplies the rule that governs Advent's feelings. Bungie explicitly discarded a "fuzzy" emotion system and hidden states. Its enemies have breaking points, "Flee in Terror, Berserk, Retreat, Defensive State", and they communicate intent through "language, posture, gesture, and focus of attention" ([Butcher & Griesemer](https://www.jmeiners.com/shamans/papers/ai/the_illusion_of_intelligence.pdf)). Players at first did not notice grunts fleeing when an Elite died. By ship, every grunt fled every time, with exaggerated panic ([Sturtevant lecture](https://www.cs.du.edu/~sturtevant/s13-ai/lecture2.pdf); [Bungie Weekly Update](https://www.halopedia.org/Bungie_Weekly_Update/03-16-01)). Halo 1 generated dialogue from 57 events into 166 dialogue types, filtered by priority, context, uniqueness and relevance, with nearby characters replying. Half-Life's grunts bark at each decision point ("HG_GREN", "HG_THROW") ([hgrunt.cpp](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/halflife/blob/master/dlls/hgrunt.cpp)). F.E.A.R.'s SDK shows the call-and-response pattern. One AI orders an advance, and the ally who cannot find cover replies NegativeStrong 0.5 s later ([SDK AIActivityAdvanceCover.cpp](https://github.com/xfw5/Fear-SDK-1.08/blob/master/Game/ObjectDLL/AIActivityAdvanceCover.cpp)). This is the strongest single argument in the corpus for Advent's next step. ModMinds already makes creatures pinned, panicked, enraged and falling back, but a player cannot hear a feeling. Without barks and exaggerated transitions, the current system risks being the "smooth fear value players never notice" that Bungie hit first.

## Roadmap: nine small slices for Advent Rising

The order below follows two rules. Each slice can be verified in a hidden pilot run on its own. And legibility (barks) comes before sophistication (the director), because the director's effects are invisible if creatures cannot announce them. The hook names come from the AdventMod recon in `AI-MINDS-DESIGN.md`. Where a name is not yet verified in the build 2226 script export, the table says so.

### Slices 1 and 2: port the token deal, then let feelings choose the token

U2FairFights' `Deal()` ports almost line for line. It collects minds whose enemy is the player and scores each one as **100 × seconds since its last token − 0.2 × distance + 800 if it has line of sight**. The top `RangedTokens` (2, plus one per `TokensPer` = 4 engaged beyond 4) may fire for `TokenTime` (2 s). Everyone else is blocked for `TokenTime + 0.6`, and movement is untouched. The single API change is that Unreal II's `BCBlockFiring` becomes AdventMod's existing `ModMinds.HoldFire(M, true)`, which drives `bDisableTimedFire`. Advent then needs a second pool that Unreal II did not: a **melee/charge pool**, sized 1 for a single human squad and 2 when hounds are present. It gates the `DoCharge`/`DoEnrage` tasks and the hound pack's "charge together" moment. This makes the existing 550-unit hound circle the HZD pattern: circling hounds without a token keep circling, and the holder commits.

Feelings feed the token layer in three places. First, **feelings choose which pool a creature asks for**. A mind with anger above `ChargeAnger` requests melee first and ranged second. A mind that is pinned (pressure above `PinPressure`) or above `FleeFear` requests nothing and stays in its cover task. A calm, cunning mind requests the flanker role (slice 8) instead of a ranged token. This turns emotion into a visible difference in *who attacks*, which the related-games notes identify as the way to make fear and anger readable. Second, **feelings bias the score**: add something like 300 × anger and subtract 400 × fear, so an enraged Seeker wins a token over a calm one at equal distance. Third, **stealing**, the DOOM refinement FairFights lacks: a candidate with line of sight may take a token from a holder without line of sight that has not yet fired in this deal. The victim's `HoldFire` resumes at once. The numbers are starting points to tune in pilot runs, not sourced values.

Slice 2 copies FairFights' fairness levers where Advent exposes them. The acquire grace can be emulated without engine access: hold fire for 0.6 s whenever a mind's enemy changes, and bark during that window (slice 3) so the delay reads as a reaction. DOOM's movement-scaled miss belongs in the Bot's aim adjustment. That function was not identified in the recon, so verify it in the 2226 export before promising it.

### Slice 3: barks make breaking points legible

Build `ModBarks` before the director. It should hold a global cap (one bark per 1.5 s across all enemies), a per-category cooldown, and a "responder" option for two-voice exchanges. The rule from F.E.A.R. is that barks fire **on task entry, after the decision**, never as the cause. Mapping onto existing ModMinds tasks: entering *charge* plays the enrage line (EnragedGrunt is a known `pawnSoundEvent`); entering *panic* plays a scream on the way into `DoPanic`/`DoFlee`; entering *pinned* plays an under-fire line; a failed `FindCover` while fear is high plays a "nowhere to go" line, if the game has one, or a grunt; the flanker leaving plays an order from the squad's highest-discipline member, and the flanker answers. Half-squad loss (the existing morale rule) should become a **Halo-style discrete break**. When a Seeker veteran dies or the squad crosses half strength, every human gets a 1–2 s stimulus window that forces a fall-back or panic check, with a bark and a visible run. That makes the existing smooth fear creep audible and unmissable. Which `pawnSoundEvent` names exist per species, and how to call them, must be enumerated from the script export first. Slice 3 begins with that inventory. If a species has no fitting line, it simply skips the bark.

### Slice 4: stagger tied to feelings

ModReact already has flinch and stagger (`StaggerDamage`, `StaggerPush`, `StaggerTime`). The missing pieces are DOOM's sustained-damage bucket and its link to behaviour. Each mind accumulates recent damage that decays over about 1 s. Below the first threshold it flinches, which adds aim error and delays the next shot. At the second it falters: it releases its token, `HoldFire` runs for 0.5 s, and the current task is cancelled. At the third, ModReact staggers it, which opens a short window. **Feelings move the thresholds**. Anger raises the falter threshold, so berserkers and hounds shrug off hits mid-charge. High fear lowers it, and each stagger adds pressure, so a staggered human is likely to end up pinned. A failed hound leap should get Halo's long recovery as a deliberate opening. This is the cheapest way to make "fear" and "anger" feel physical.

### Slices 5 and 6: perception that can be fooled

Since we adopt bots externally and cannot override `Bot.SeePlayer`, the sighting timer works as a gate rather than a replacement. Each mind accumulates sight while `LineOfSightTo(Player)` succeeds and the player falls inside an FOV that widens as distance shrinks, and decays it otherwise. Until sight passes the threshold, the mind holds fire and turns toward the stimulus. Thresholds should be about 1.5 s unaware, 0.3 s in combat, and longer for species with weak eyes; hounds also get a proximity "smell" radius. The squad keeps a last known position and timestamp, refreshed only by real sightings. When the player gets more than 5 m (about 260 Unreal units, by the usual approximation; check Advent's scale) from it, one investigator is sent with `DoMoveToDestination` and a "where is he" bark. Search then diffuses over PathNodes rather than a bitmap. Every 0.5 s each node passes probability to its neighbours, nodes any squad member can `FastTrace` to are zeroed, and pairs walk to the highest-probability nodes. The TLOU target to measure against is the 2 min → 30 s cycle.

### Slice 7: the encounter director

`ModDirector` keeps one intensity value for the player, from 0 to 1. **Inputs**: player damage from `ModMindRules.NetDamage` (when `Injured` is the player), proportional to the share of health lost; near misses on the player, by reusing `ModMinds.SenseShots`' segment-distance test against the player instead of against creatures; kills within about 1500 units via `ScoreKill`, weighted by 1/distance (L4D's rule); and, for hounds and stalkers, an Alien-style menace term from enemies within short range with line of sight. **Decay** holds while any mind has the player as enemy with line of sight. **States** follow Booth. In **Build-Up**, the token budget is normal, the flanker is allowed, and the charge bias is on. **Sustain Peak** lasts 3–5 s. In **Peak Fade**, the ranged budget drops to 1, the melee budget to 0, flanking and new charges are suppressed, and squads at full fear are nudged to `DoFallback`, all through `SquadAI.AssignState` so that `bLockState` is respected. **Relax** lasts 30–45 s, or until the player has moved a set distance along a PathNode Dijkstra "flow distance" computed at map load.

Spawning is where Advent differs from L4D. Mutator replacement does not run and squads come from the level. The director therefore borrows U2Seven SevenSanctuary's proven trick instead of spawning: **squads not yet engaged are held** (`DoWait`, hold fire) during Relax and Peak Fade, and released with a tell (a bark, a scare sound) during Build-Up. Like Valve's bosses, scripted set pieces and bosses are excluded. The director also never changes damage or accuracy; amplitude stays the game's. Booth's "crude yet works" claim is what this slice tests. Log intensity and state every second, and plot the timeline with the U2Sanctuary arena-pacing tooling (adapting its input format if needed). Pass condition: each arena shows at least one full Build-Up → Peak → Relax cycle, rather than a flat line or a sawtooth.

### Slices 8 and 9: territories, roles, components

Halo 3 territories fit SquadAI directly. Give each squad forward, fallback and last-stand PathNode sets, switched when alive fraction falls below about 0.75 and 0.5. The existing cover finder then searches only inside the active set, which fixes "wanders anywhere" without new pathing. Roles become facts on each mind, following HZD's pattern: one Shooter per token, one Flanker (already built; adopt TLOU's fixed "combat vector" cost so the flank swings wide consistently), and one Investigator. Slice 9 is optional polish. Gore hit-bone flags (a crippled leg means no leap; a lost arm means melee only) give Eternal's weak-point behaviour switch. A clamped velocity nudge toward the target during the airborne part of a hound leap approximates HFW's attack warping. Inverting the cover query for hounds and brutes produces DOOM's exposed positioning.

## Conclusion

The research reframes what the next step for ModMinds is. The creatures already *have* rich inner states. What they lack is a way to express them through the three channels every studio here relied on: who is allowed to attack, what they say when they change their mind, and when the fight breathes. Tokens, barks and a tempo director all sit around the existing minds rather than inside them. That keeps the work in small, testable UnrealScript slices, and it reuses the token system already proven in Unreal II.

One tension is worth watching in pilot runs. Bungie's data says players read toughness as intelligence, while L4D and DOOM throttle pressure. The resolution in the sources is that throttling changes *frequency*, not amplitude: fewer enemies attack at once, but each attack lands as hard as before. If hits on the player drop and fights also start to feel easy, raise the token budget during Build-Up rather than restoring accuracy. The director's whole value lies in making the quiet moments quiet so that the peaks hit.
