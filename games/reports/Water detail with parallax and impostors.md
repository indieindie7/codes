# Stack refracted planes, not cubes, under coarse water

Yes: ripples and floating detritus on a 20-unit-cell shallow-water surface can be rendered entirely as a layered material in one Direct3D 9 / ps_3_0 pass, but the right primitive is a stack of horizontal planes intersected by the refracted view ray, not an interior-mapped cube. Every "cube" technique in the family (van Dongen's interior mapping, box-projected and parallax-corrected cubemaps, Chris Oat's layered depth parallax) is the same per-pixel operation, a ray against axis-aligned planes with one reciprocal per plane, and for a water cell seen from above the vertical faces never contribute anything but seams, so the box degenerates into Oat's 2006 "depth parallax" layers plus van Dongen's alpha-tested "furniture plane" ([Oat 2006](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2006/Chapter4-Rendering_gooey_materials_with_multiple_layers.pdf); [van Dongen 2008](https://www.proun-game.com/Oogst3D/CODING/InteriorMapping/InteriorMapping.pdf)). Ripples themselves should not be marched at all: the shipped answer is Valve's flow-advected normal map, **+2 texture fetches and +21 ALU instructions on ps2.0b**, driven here by the simulation's own velocity field instead of an artist flow map ([Vlachos 2010](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2010/Vlachos-Waterflow(SIGGRAPH%202010%20Advanced%20RealTime%20Rendering%20Course).pdf)). Burnout Paradise's "distorted cube" look has no paper trail: Criterion never published a rendering talk on the game, the box-projection technique the memory evokes was first posted two years after the game shipped, and the most probable explanation is uncorrected cube-map reflections (a per-frame low-resolution dynamic cube on the player car, as Burnout 2 is documented to do, or static cubes on flat glass and wet road), which produce exactly the warped-box artefact the memory describes ([xboxrecomp PR #3](https://github.com/phobos665/xboxrecomp/pull/3); [GameDev.net 2010](https://www.gamedev.net/forums/topic/568829-box-projected-cubemap-environment-mapping/)). Every building block is proven on SM2/SM3 hardware: interior mapping fits ps_2_0's 64 instructions and cost about 5× a diffuse-plus-reflection material on a GeForce 6600 GT; Lagarde's box projection cost 0.25 ms per cubemap at 25 % screen coverage on PS3; Tatarchuk's parallax occlusion mapping ran at 255 fps with 8–50 samples per pixel on a Radeon X1600 XL ([van Dongen 2008](https://www.proun-game.com/Oogst3D/CODING/InteriorMapping/InteriorMapping.pdf); [Lagarde 2012](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2012/09/29/image-based-lighting-approaches-and-parallax-corrected-cubemap/); [Tatarchuk 2006](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2006/Chapter5-Parallax_Occlusion_Mapping_for_detailed_surface_rendering.pdf)). The concrete recipe is three stackable stages, flow ripples, refracted slab layers (floor plus two or three detritus planes with Beer–Lambert attenuation), and optional close-up POM, costing roughly seven texture reads and 40–60 ALU per pixel before the existing reflection and refraction samples, well inside ps_3_0's 512-slot minimum. No shipped game or paper has used interior mapping for water; the water application is a synthesis of verified primitives, and the report marks it as such throughout.

## Burnout Paradise's cubes have no paper trail, and the timeline points to uncorrected cubemaps

The first thing to establish is what the "distorted cube" memory can and cannot be anchored to. Criterion's public technical output on Burnout Paradise (2008, RenderWare, 60 fps on PS3 and 360, 720p-class with a PS3 hardware-scaler upscale to 960×1080) is about production and parallelisation, not rendering ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnout_Paradise); [PlayStation Blog comment thread](https://blog.playstation.com/2008/06/23/new-burnout-paradise-updates-coming-to-ps3/comment-page-2/)). CTO Richard Parr's GAME 07 talk is remembered for the line "We're getting into diminishing returns when it comes to rendering and shaders" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/the-euro-vision-criterion-goes-back-to-college)), and the one developer-level interview, Eurogamer/Digital Foundry with Parr and rendering lead Alex Fry in June 2009, is summarised as "it's all about parallelization"; Slashdot commenters who asked about the rendering got no answer ([Slashdot](https://games.slashdot.org/story/09/06/18/0518237/a-look-at-the-tech-behind-burnout-paradise); [EA, Alex Fry](https://www.ea.com/news/in-their-own-words-alex-fry)). No GDC Vault session, slide deck or paper with a title like "Rendering in Burnout Paradise" or "Deferred shading in Burnout Paradise" exists; the only Criterion Vault entry surfaced is about rapid prototyping ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1022968/Rapid-Idea-Visualisation-at-Criterion)). Any claim about the game's reflection or wet-road technique is therefore unverified, and the report treats the "cube effect" as something to be inferred from precedent and timing.

Timing rules out the techniques the memory most resembles. Box-projected cubemaps were first posted by "behc" on GameDev.net on 20 April 2010, two years after the game, and parallax-corrected cubemaps with console measurements date to SIGGRAPH 2012 ([GameDev.net](https://www.gamedev.net/forums/topic/568829-box-projected-cubemap-environment-mapping/); [SIGGRAPH 2012](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=140207)). Interior mapping was published at CGI 2008, the year Paradise shipped, cites GTA, Crackdown and Superman Returns as motivation and names no shipped user; van Dongen's first named adopter is SimCity in 2013 ([van Dongen 2008](https://www.proun-game.com/Oogst3D/CODING/InteriorMapping/InteriorMapping.pdf); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/interior-mapping-rendering-real-rooms-without-geometry)). What Criterion is documented to have done is the opposite of parallax correction: Burnout 2 on Xbox "renders its environment map into a 128x128 cube every frame and samples it on the car", and Burnout 3 exposes "track reflections" as a separate debug drawing flag ([xboxrecomp PR #3](https://github.com/phobos665/xboxrecomp/pull/3); [mattkc forum](https://forum.mattkc.com/viewtopic.php?p=1183)). That is also the genre norm: "it's quite common to render a dynamic cubemap every frame from roughly the position of the player car" and mix it "with other static or dynamic cubemaps placed throughout the track" ([GameDev.net](https://gamedev.net/forums/topic/708272-cheap-way-to-do-paint-job-reflections/5432258)).

Put those facts together and the most defensible reading of the memory is this. A low-resolution cube refreshed every frame on highly reflective paint reads as a warped box sliding over the car, most visibly where the six faces meet; and a plain, uncorrected cubemap on a large flat surface (building glass, wet asphalt) is wrong in exactly the way behc set out to fix, "Pre-rendered cubemaps are good for complex objects but wrong for big flat elements, like a polished floor" ([GameDev.net](https://www.gamedev.net/forums/topic/568829-box-projected-cubemap-environment-mapping/)). Both artefacts are "distorted cubes". Interior-mapped windows were technically possible in 2008, since the paper says the effect fits "within the 64 instructions allowed in pixel shader model 2.0" and is "efficient enough ... for the Xbox 360, Playstation 3", but nothing links them to Criterion and the plausibility is low ([van Dongen 2008](https://www.proun-game.com/Oogst3D/CODING/InteriorMapping/InteriorMapping.pdf)). Crash deformation is mesh deformation, not a cube technique. A RenderDoc or PIX capture of the 2009 PC version would settle it; none is public. The practical consequence for this project is that the Burnout look is not a technique to copy. It is the artefact that the technique family below was invented to remove, and the water recipe borrows the family's maths, not the game.

## Every box trick is one ray against axis-aligned planes, and water only needs the horizontal ones

Interior mapping, box-projected cubemaps and Oat's layered materials share one primitive. Interior mapping transforms the camera and the pixel into object space, picks the ceiling plane above the pixel with `ceil(y/d)·d` (or the floor, `(ceil(y/d) − 1)·d`, when the camera looks down), intersects the view ray with it, and uses the hit's x and z as texture coordinates; walls use the same calculation on the XY and YZ planes and "Of the resulting three intersections, the one closest to the camera will be used" ([van Dongen 2008](https://www.proun-game.com/Oogst3D/CODING/InteriorMapping/InteriorMapping.pdf)). Because the planes are regularly spaced "the ray can be collided in constant time, regardless of the number of rooms", and van Dongen later packed the three plane tests into float4 operations and noted only three of six faces need testing given the view direction ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/interior-mapping-rendering-real-rooms-without-geometry)). Lagarde's parallax-corrected cubemap is the same slab test on a reflection ray: compute the exit distance against each of the three slab pairs, take the minimum, and look the cubemap up with the hit point minus the capture position ([Lagarde 2012](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2012/09/29/image-based-lighting-approaches-and-parallax-corrected-cubemap/)). The older GPU Gems 2004 ancestor intersects the reflection vector with a unit sphere instead, in SM2 Cg, and remarks that the cube shape "is really a convenience for the texturing hardware" ([GPU Gems ch. 19](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-iii-materials/chapter-19-image-based-lighting)). Oat's 2006 ATI chapter drops the walls entirely: by "making the simplifying assumption that the layers are parallel in tangent space", the inner layer's offset is closed-form, transmission distance `s = d / |T.z|` for layer thickness d, offset `uv + s·T.xy·texelSize`, and "there's no limit to the number of material layers", each composited with a lerp on the outer layer's opacity ([Oat 2006](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2006/Chapter4-Rendering_gooey_materials_with_multiple_layers.pdf)).

In instruction terms, the unified step in box space with half-extents h, ray origin P and direction D is `t_i = (sign(D_i)·h_i − P_i)/D_i` per axis, `t = min(t_x, t_y, t_z)`, hit `Q = P + t·D`, then either `texCUBE(cube, Q − C)` or a 2D read on the face that produced the minimum; that is three reciprocals, a few mad/min/cmp and one to four texture reads, which is why it fit ps_2_0 (the counts are derived from the published code; no source gives D3D9 instruction counts for Oat or Lagarde). The failure modes are equally well documented and all live on the vertical faces or at the proxy boundary: "edge artifacts can't be eliminated because the cube-map contains limited information about the scene, and using different AABB sizes produces some discontinuities" ([GameDev.net](https://www.gamedev.net/forums/topic/568829-box-projected-cubemap-environment-mapping/)); the correction "only work[s] perfectly with mirror surface", for glossy surfaces "artists must ensure that the reflected camera will always remain within the box volume", and blending several probes is "even worse" ([Lagarde 2012](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2012/09/29/image-based-lighting-approaches-and-parallax-corrected-cubemap/)); pre-filtering before parallax correction "will result in errors, even if the proxy geo is a perfect fit" ([GameDev.net, localizing IBR](https://www.gamedev.net/forums/topic/667710-localizing-image-based-reflections-issues-questions/)); interior mapping shows "a seam at the corner of the building" and its furniture plane "will show distortions and even seams" on curved or cornered geometry ([van Dongen 2008](https://www.proun-game.com/Oogst3D/CODING/InteriorMapping/InteriorMapping.pdf)); and a naive UV-offset interior "end[s] up with an infinite interior and nasty perspective distortion. Using a box fixes this" ([Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2651410)). The one division hazard, an exactly horizontal ray, is "so rare in practice that this problem is negligible" for buildings, but it is not rare for water at grazing angles, where `1/|T.z|` blows up and must be clamped ([van Dongen 2008](https://www.proun-game.com/Oogst3D/CODING/InteriorMapping/InteriorMapping.pdf)).

This is why the cube is the wrong unit for tiled water. A 20-unit cell viewed from above has no visible vertical sides; a refracted ray that reaches a side face should continue into the neighbouring cell, and giving each cell walls would manufacture van Dongen's corner seam at every cell boundary. Drop the walls and the "box" becomes an infinite slab stack: a bed at depth h (the simulation's own per-cell depth) and N detritus planes at fixed insets d_k, each reached in closed form with one shared reciprocal, exactly Oat's layers with van Dongen's alpha-tested furniture plane as the detritus carrier ("the alpha channel of the texture that is used for the furniture plane determines whether the colour of the furniture plane is discarded or not"; "By using an animated texture, the objects on the furniture plane can actually move"). The shipped liquid and ice precedents confirm this is the form that survives contact with production: Star Wars Battlefront's Hoth lake "used parallaxing distorted texture layers, plus a negated cubemap for fake translucency" ([Troive, ArtStation](https://johntroive.artstation.com/projects/Vg5Von)); Tiny Tina's Wonderlands' ice "uses a cheap parallax effect over a landscape material beneath it, with dynamic branching to run on Xbox One" ([Mills, ArtStation](https://sashamills.artstation.com/projects/eJ5rYP)); the Skyrim multilayer-parallax mod adds a subsurface texture that "seems to sit inside the geometry and shifts as the view changes" and credits Dragon Age: Inquisition's frozen lakes with "a more advanced version" ([Nexus Mods](https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrim/mods/65531)); Leadwerks' ice keeps "the surface normals and the outer texture coordinates unshifted, so the surface stays put while the ice appears to sit beneath it" ([Leadwerks](https://www.leadwerks.com/community/blogs/entry/2815-cool-ice-effect/)); and the minimal Godot "Parallax Ice" shader is literally `rd = normalize(p − ro − normal·k)·depth; under = texture(under, UV + rd.xz + normal.xy)`, with a commenter's caveat that it "only seems to work well on flat surfaces" ([godotshaders](https://godotshaders.com/shader/parallax-ice-for-fake-depth-to-plane-meshes/)). All of these are artist-portfolio or hobby sources, so depth counts and costs are not public, but the pattern is unanimous: a surface normal map that stays put, one or more inner layers offset by the view (sometimes bent by the normal), and a cubemap for reflection. Nothing found uses a six-faced interior for water, puddles or aquaria; keep the walls only for a rock pool with a visible rim, and then use Lagarde's min-of-three-slabs with a single cube texture per pool type.

The one substitution worth making to Oat's formula is physical. His transmission vector is the reflection vector with z negated, a convenience rather than Snell's law; for water the right ray is `T = refract(−V_ts, N_ts, 1/1.33)`, available in ps_3_0 at roughly 8–10 ALU, with N_ts the animated ripple normal. Because every layer's lookup then moves by `d_k·T.xy/|T.z|`, deeper layers swing more under a ripple than shallow ones, which is the depth-plus-ripple cue the question is after, and the Godot shader's `V − N·k` is the cheap non-Snell approximation of the same thing. One subtlety matters for consistency: build the tangent frame from the cell's geometric normal so all layers share one slab stack, and let only the ripple normal perturb the per-layer offset; otherwise the furniture-plane distortion van Dongen warns about on tilted surfaces appears wherever the height field slopes.

## Shipped games keep ripples and detritus in texture space; impostors are for props

The shipped practice for sub-grid ripples is not ray marching but a flow-advected normal map. Valve's Portal 2 water had to fit "Min hardware ps2.0b (6-year-old hardware) & Xbox 360" with a shader that "had limited instructions left"; artists author a flow map at "~4 texels/meter", the normal map's UVs are displaced by flow × time, and because distortion "looks reasonable for the first 1/3 of uv space" the animation is split into two layers offset half a phase to hide the restart, with a noise texture to de-synchronise the pulse; cost over two scrolling normal maps is "Additional texture fetches: 2 – flow & noise; Additional arithmetic pixel shader instructions: 21" ([Vlachos 2010](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2010/Vlachos-Waterflow(SIGGRAPH%202010%20Advanced%20RealTime%20Rendering%20Course).pdf)). Chentanez and Müller did the same thing on top of a shallow-water solver: an FFT texture containing "only high frequency waves" is looked up through three sets of texture coordinates per simulation cell, semi-Lagrangian-advected by the velocity field, reset every τ ≈ 3 s with a τ/3 phase shift, weighted by `(1/3)(1 − cos(2π(t − t0)/τ))` so the sum is constant, and faded by `e^(−βμ)` where μ is the largest Green-strain eigenvalue of the coordinate map (β 0.5–2) so over-stretched ripples disappear instead of smearing; the coordinates are "computed on the simulation grid points" and bilinearly interpolated for per-pixel bump mapping, with displacement reserved for a render grid at Δx/4 in one example ([Chentanez & Müller 2010](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)). That is the hook the earlier simulation report left open ("appearance above physics"), and it needs no GPU compute: three float2 per cell on a 1200-cell room is a few kilobytes of vertex data per frame. Catlike Coding's write-up supplies the exact two-phase formulas, triangle-wave weights `w = 1 − |1 − 2p|`, per-phase jumps of 0.2–0.25 so the same texel does not cycle at one spot, and the derivative-height map trick where summing the two phases' slopes before normalising is the correct way to combine height layers ([Catlike Coding](https://catlikecoding.com/unity/tutorials/flow/texture-distortion/)).

Detritus follows the same path, with one documented wrinkle. Portal 2 "Wanted to also flow debris in dirty water" and found that colour, unlike normals, must favour the at-rest position: the distortion interval is offset "from –fraction to +fraction so the peak of the interval is at zero" ([Vlachos 2010](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2010/Vlachos-Waterflow(SIGGRAPH%202010%20Advanced%20RealTime%20Rendering%20Course).pdf)). Across the rest of the shipped record, foam and scum are a per-cell scalar multiplied against a tiling texture: Sea of Thieves seeds foam at wave peaks and at depth-buffer intersections, then "progressively blur[s] the result of the foam buffer with feedback to simulate the foam dispersing" before blending it with artist textures ([Ang et al. 2018](https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2018-Talks-Ang_The-Technical-Art-of-Sea-of-Thieves.pdf)); Assassin's Creed III packs "Three grayscale foam maps (coarse, sparse, medium)" into RGB at 512² over a 4 km map and treats large debris as geometry with "a single probe" ([fxguide](https://www.fxguide.com/?p=38193)); Uncharted 3 outputs foam modulation "as a vertex color" from its flow grid ([GDC 2012](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/water-technologyofunchartedgdc2012/12977478)); Cords' dissertation models foam as a cellular automaton, `f_new = d·f + (1−d)/4·Σ neighbours`, a five-tap stencil per cell ([Cords 2009](https://rosdok.uni-rostock.de/file/rosdok_disshab_0000000334/rosdok_derivate_0000004116/Dissertation_Cords_2009.pdf)). For the research systems that do use particles, Chentanez's foam is "rendered as diffuse disks with normals perpendicular to the height field water surface", surface-aligned rather than camera-facing, with 83K–250K foam, spray and splash particles active per frame in whole-frame times of 10–18 ms on a GTX 480 ([Chentanez & Müller 2010](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)).

Octahedral and multi-view impostors are the wrong tool at detritus scale. Epic's Impostor Baker uses a 2048² atlas with 12×12 or 16×16 frames and an 8-triangle card, "the pixel shader is more expensive for imposters due to blending the three nearest frames", and the documentation says nothing about tilting an impostor to a surface normal ([Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/impostor-baker-plugin-in-unreal-engine)). A 144-frame 2048² atlas yields about 170 px per frame; a leaf covering ten screen pixels cannot use it, and three frame blends per pixel cost more than a single-frame sprite with nothing to show for it. No shipped game was found rendering floating debris as octahedral impostors. Impostors earn their cost only for a handful of near-camera floating props (crates, bodies) where a cubemap or atlas per object type is affordable, and for those the D3D9 path is ordinary: 2D DXT atlases, hardware instancing on SM3 parts, pure ALU plus three to six fetches. For everything smaller, the hierarchy is clear: a texture-space layer inside the water material is free of sorting problems and automatically reflected and refracted; a surface-aligned sprite drawn after the water with depth test but no depth write handles the few larger items, faded against the opaque scene with the GPU Gems 3 soft-particle term `saturate(scale·(myDepth − sceneDepth))` and, cheaper still, against the water using the CPU height field as the fade term since the water surface writes no depth ([GPU Gems 3 ch. 23](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iv-image-effects/chapter-23-high-speed-screen-particles)). The fill numbers are reassuring: a 2007 GeForce 8800 GTX ran a 73-instruction particle shader over 46.9 M particle-pixels at 25 fps, so a few thousand 10–30 px detritus sprites, about a million pixels, are an order of magnitude below the regime where off-screen particle tricks start paying ([GPU Gems 3 ch. 23](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iv-image-effects/chapter-23-high-speed-screen-particles)).

Where POM does fit is a narrow close-up band. Tatarchuk's parallax occlusion mapping, written in "DirectX 9.0c shader programs" with SM3 dynamic flow control, samples the height profile with `n = lerp(nMax, nMin, N·V)`, intersects it piecewise-linearly to avoid relief mapping's "visible stair-stepping artifacts at steep viewing angles", switches to plain bump mapping above an explicit mip threshold of 4 because "Simply using mip-mapping for LOD management is ineffective since it does not reduce shader complexity", and explicitly supports "a dynamically rendered height field" with Sobel-derived normals; a 1,100-polygon soldier with a 2048² map ran at 255 fps on a Radeon X1600 XL with bounds [8, 50], and ToyShop's cobblestones used 8–40 samples ([Tatarchuk 2006](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2006/Chapter5-Parallax_Occlusion_Mapping_for_detailed_surface_rendering.pdf)). Cone-stepping variants are out: relaxed cone stepping needs an offline O(n²) cone map, which a per-frame height field cannot have ([GPU Gems 3 ch. 18](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iii-rendering/chapter-18-relaxed-cone-stepping-relief-mapping)). POM's worst limitation, "lack of detailed silhouettes", is irrelevant on a sheet viewed from above, and its branching is cheap here because the view angle varies smoothly across a water sheet, the "spatially coherent" case GPU Gems 2 identifies as the one where SIMD groups do not pay for both branch sides ([GPU Gems 2 ch. 34](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems2/part-iv-general-purpose-computation-gpus-primer/chapter-34-gpu-flow-control-idioms)). But because POM only moves texture lookups, not the depth buffer, legs and debris still cut the surface at the geometric height, which is one more reason to keep ripple amplitude small and let Valve-style bump-only ripples carry the normal.

## The volume reads through refraction, depth fog and attenuated layers, all of it SM3

A layered material only sells "stuff suspended in water" if the water itself reads as a volume, and every shipped reference does that with the same three ingredients. Refraction is a normal-perturbed sample of the opaque scene copy, with a mask against above-water leakage: Sousa's Far Cry chapter clears the scene texture's alpha to white, renders refractive meshes black into alpha, and falls back to the unperturbed sample where the perturbed coordinate lands on a masked texel, "inaccurate but practical", with scales of 0.075 for refraction and 0.02 for reflection and a Fresnel of bias 0.2, power 5 ([GPU Gems 2 ch. 19](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems2/part-ii-shading-lighting-and-shadows/chapter-19-generic-refraction-simulation)). Crysis reused the back buffer directly and fixed leaks by disabling the offset where "water depth is greater than world depth", rendered reflections at half resolution and uploaded a 64×64 CPU wave grid as an FP32 texture ([Crysis GDC 2008](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/crysis-nextgen-effects-gdc-2008/25051981)). Depth fog is Beer–Lambert on the water-column thickness: `fogFactor = exp2(−density·depthDifference); lerp(fogColor, background, fogFactor)` with density 0.1–0.15 in Catlike's example, the refraction offset scaled by `saturate(depthDifference)` so it fades to nothing at the shoreline and never pulls foreground pixels under ([Catlike Coding](https://catlikecoding.com/unity/tutorials/flow/looking-through-water/)). Unreal's Single Layer Water formalises the same thing as per-channel absorption and scattering coefficients in reciprocal metres, demo values of **R 0.0033, G 0.0016, B 0.0011** for clear sea water, and falls back on low-end hardware to a plain translucent material with no volume integration ([Unreal docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/single-layer-water-shading-model-in-unreal-engine)). Those coefficients are useless for a flooded ship; a turbid-tank measurement puts dirty-water attenuation at roughly **3–47 m⁻¹** per channel, two to four orders of magnitude above pure water, so per-channel k should be set from the designed visibility (1/k of a few metres) with red attenuated fastest for brown-green interior water ([arXiv underwater imaging review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.01869)). 0 A.D.'s split into a deep "water colour" and a shallow "water tint" is a useful two-constant parameterisation of the same term ([Wildfire Games](https://trac.wildfiregames.com/ticket/1706)).

The shallow-water simulation changes the economics of the thickness term. Every reference computes thickness as scene depth minus surface depth, which on D3D9 means an INTZ, RAWZ, DF16 or DF24 depth texture or an R32F copy, with vendor caveats: INTZ needs DX10-class GPUs, RAWZ is GeForce 6/7 only, DF24 cannot be sampled and depth-tested at once, and AMD HD3xxx–5xxx on certain Catalyst versions were slow sampling INTZ while testing against it ([Aras Pranckevičius, D3D9 GPU Hacks](https://www.aras-p.info/texts/D3D9GPUHacks.html)). A height-field sim already knows the water depth h per cell, so the material can compute the in-water path length as `h / |T.z|` from the refracted ray with no depth read at all, and use the depth buffer only for props standing in the water and for shoreline softening. Both paths should exist: the sim-depth path is the fallback on hardware without usable depth textures and in a d3d9 proxy that cannot reach the game's depth buffer, and it is what makes each detritus layer's attenuation `exp(−k·d_k/|T.z|)` exact without any extra sampling. The remaining surface terms are cheap. Sea of Thieves blends "between a deep water colour and a sub-surface water colour based on a combination of view angle, sun direction and a wave peak mask" ([Ang et al. 2018](https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2018-Talks-Ang_The-Technical-Art-of-Sea-of-Thieves.pdf)); indoors the sun becomes the room's key light and the peak mask becomes the sim's height-above-mean or |velocity|. Caustics on the bed come either from Crysis's projected procedural layers or from the GPU Gems 1 construction that refracts a light ray at the surface and intersects the floor with `distance = (planeD − lineP.z)/lineN.z`, rendered once per light into a texture-space light map at the sim's resolution and attenuated by the same turbidity so murky water does not show crisp caustics ([GPU Gems ch. 2](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-2-rendering-water-caustics); [Crysis GDC 2008](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/crysis-nextgen-effects-gdc-2008/25051981)). Wet bands above a rising water line are Lagarde's Remember Me process, `Diffuse *= lerp(1, lerp(1, 0.2, porosity), WetLevel)`, gloss pushed toward 1 at half that rate, normals flattened toward the vertex normal where water pools, judged "suitable for XBOX360/PS3 games" ([Lagarde, Water drop 3b](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2013/04/14/water-drop-3b-physically-based-wet-surfaces/)); the wet level comes from a max-height-reached field tracked beside the sim's height field and decayed at the drying rate.

What the clear-coat family does not do is also worth stating. Unreal's clear coat with a second normal models two BRDF lobes at one surface for carbon fibre and the like; nothing in it models thickness, absorption, refraction offset or parallax between layers ([Unreal dual normals](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/using-dual-normals-with-clear-coat-in-unreal-engine)). The water surface is structurally a clear coat whose base is the refracted, fogged, layered volume rather than a diffuse lobe, so the coat mental model is right for the Fresnel-weighted planar reflection (F0 ≈ 0.02, roughness from ripple magnitude) and wrong for the base. All of the above runs on ps_3_0 with room to spare: Microsoft's profile guarantees at least 512 instruction slots, 16 samplers, 32 temporaries, dynamic flow control nested to 24, gradient instructions and "no dependent read limits" ([Microsoft ps_3_0 reference](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/direct3dhlsl/dx9-graphics-reference-asm-ps-3-0)). What needs SM4 or compute is only true many-step volumetric marching (a fill-rate problem, not a capability one), texture arrays (substitute a volume texture or an atlas) and GPU-side reductions.

## A concrete SM3 recipe: three stackable stages and a per-pixel budget

The table ranks the candidate techniques against the question's constraints. "Proven" means a primary source documents it in a shipped game or a measured paper; the water-specific use of the slab stack is a synthesis and is marked so.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flow-advected ripple normal map | Portal 2 / L4D2 ([Vlachos 2010](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2010/Vlachos-Waterflow(SIGGRAPH%202010%20Advanced%20RealTime%20Rendering%20Course).pdf)); SWE-advected variant ([Chentanez & Müller 2010](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)) | ps2.0b | +2 fetches, +21 ALU | Adopt; drive with sim velocity |
| Layered depth parallax (slab stack) | Oat 2006 heart demo ([Oat 2006](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2006/Chapter4-Rendering_gooey_materials_with_multiple_layers.pdf)); Battlefront / Wonderlands / DA:I ice (portfolio sources) | SM3 (ps2.0 feasible) | 1 rcp + 1 mad + 1 tex2D + 1 lerp per layer (derived) | Adopt for bed + 2–3 detritus planes; water use is synthesis |

Shared inputs come from the simulation per cell and are bilinearly interpolated: surface height η, 2D velocity u, depth above the bed h = η − B, and optionally foam or detritus density and the three advected texture-coordinate pairs. Pack them as vertex attributes on the 20-unit grid (or a Δx/4 render grid, as the earlier report recommends), since a 1200-cell sheet is a trivial upload and vs_3_0 vertex texture fetch is less portable. All parameter values below that are not attributed are engineering choices to be tuned in the target renderer.

**Stage A, flow ripples (ps2.0b-class).** Two phases of a tiled derivative-height map displaced by the sim velocity, triangle-wave weights, per-phase jumps and a noise de-sync, with normal strength scaled by flow speed as Valve did:

```hlsl
float2 f   = flow;                                  // sim velocity in uv units / s (vertex attribute)
float  t   = time * Speed + noise;                  // low-frequency noise de-syncs the pulse
float  pA  = frac(t),  pB = frac(t + 0.5);
float  wA  = 1 - abs(1 - 2*pA),  wB = 1 - abs(1 - 2*pB);
float2 uvA = (uv - f*(pA + FlowOffset))*Tiling + (t - pA)*jumpA;
float2 uvB = (uv - f*(pB + FlowOffset))*Tiling + 0.5 + (t - pB)*jumpB;
float4 dhA = tex2D(RippleDH, uvA), dhB = tex2D(RippleDH, uvB);   // A,G = dh/dx, dh/dy ; B = h
float  hs  = length(f) * HeightScaleModulated + HeightScaleConstant;
float2 d   = (dhA.ag*2-1)*wA*hs + (dhB.ag*2-1)*wB*hs;         // sum derivatives, then normalise
float  hRip= dhA.b*wA + dhB.b*wB;                            // blended ripple height for Stage C
float3 nTS = normalize(float3(-d, 1));
```

Tiling so that one ripple tile spans one to two cells (20–40 units); DH texture 512²–1024² DXT5 with the two derivatives in the A and G channels; jumps of 0.2–0.25 per phase; FlowOffset 0 for normals and −0.5 for any colour layer, per Valve's debris rule ([Vlachos 2010](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2010/Vlachos-Waterflow(SIGGRAPH%202010%20Advanced%20RealTime%20Rendering%20Course).pdf); [Catlike Coding](https://catlikecoding.com/unity/tutorials/flow/texture-distortion/)). For the full Chentanez treatment, replace the pixel-shader distortion with three CPU-advected coordinate sets per cell, τ ≈ 3 s, strain fade β ≈ 1, so stretched ripples and stretched leaves vanish instead of streaking ([Chentanez & Müller 2010](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)).

**Stage B, the refracted slab stack (ps2.0-class, the answer to the question).** Tangent frame from the cell's geometric normal; refracted ray from the ripple normal; bed at the sim depth; two or three alpha-tested detritus planes at fixed fractions of h; front-to-back composite with per-layer transmittance:

```hlsl
float3 Vr    = refract(-V_ts, nTS, 1.0/1.33);         // Vr.z < 0, heading down into the water
float  invZ  = 1.0 / max(-Vr.z, 0.1);                 // the one reciprocal; clamp kills the grazing blow-up
float2 slope = Vr.xy * invZ;
// Bed (or replace with the Sousa/Catlike screen-space refraction sample where the real floor is visible)
float2 uvBed = uvWorld + slope * h * BedTexScale;
float3 col   = tex2D(BedTex, uvBed).rgb;
float3 T     = exp2(-K_rgb * h * invZ);               // Beer-Lambert along the in-water path
col = lerp(MurkColor, col, T);
// Detritus planes, deepest first: d_k = {0.6h, 0.25h}; each has its own flow scroll / advected UVs
[unroll] for (int k = 0; k < 2; k++) {
    float2 uvK = uvWorld + slope * d[k] * LayerScale[k] + scroll[k];
    float4 s   = tex2D(DetritusTex, uvK);             // van Dongen furniture plane: alpha = presence
    float3 Tk  = exp2(-K_rgb * d[k] * invZ);
    col = lerp(col, s.rgb * Tk + MurkColor * (1 - Tk), s.a);   // lerp, not clip: keep it branch-free
}
// Surface: Fresnel-weighted planar reflection + specular from nTS, then foam / subsurface tint
```

Because h is one smooth value per cell, there is no occlusion to resolve and a single step is exact for a flat bed, which is why no steep or occlusion variant for water exists in the literature. The `invZ` clamp is the only guard against van Dongen's division hazard, which is rare for buildings but routine for water at grazing angles. Use `lerp` by alpha rather than `clip`: the detritus alpha is the one spatially incoherent branch in the shader, and GPU Gems 2's advice is predication for short incoherent paths ([GPU Gems 2 ch. 34](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems2/part-iv-general-purpose-computation-gpus-primer/chapter-34-gpu-flow-control-idioms)). Where the real floor is already in the refraction target, keep only the detritus planes from this stage and take the bed from the screen-space sample offset by `nTS.xy × 0.075` and masked by Sousa's alpha or Catlike's `saturate(depthDifference)`; the two compete only where real geometry sits between two fake layers, and the GameDev.net advice to keep dynamic things out of the proxy and composite them "over the top" is the same principle ([GameDev.net, localizing IBR](https://www.gamedev.net/forums/topic/667710-localizing-image-based-reflections-issues-questions/)). Scalar detritus density, if the sim carries one (Cords' five-tap diffusion or a Sea-of-Thieves feedback blur), multiplies each layer's alpha, and the Chentanez strain weight fades layers where the flow has stretched them. A flow-advected layer costs one extra fetch and about six ALU (two phases instead of one); nothing in the literature does flow advection on interior-mapped planes specifically, but the lookup is just a 2D UV, so the composition is straightforward.

**Stage C, close-up POM (ps_3_0 only).** Only below Tatarchuk's explicit mip threshold (3–4), march the blended ripple height with `n = (int) lerp(nMax, nMin, dot(N_geo, V))`, nMin 6–8 and nMax 16–24 for a low-amplitude field, `tex2Dgrad` with derivatives computed outside the loop, piecewise-linear intersection, then use the resulting uv as `uvWorld` for Stage B and as the sample point for the Stage A normal ([Tatarchuk 2006](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2006/Chapter5-Parallax_Occlusion_Mapping_for_detailed_surface_rendering.pdf)). Each step costs two fetches because the marched height is the two-phase blend, so expect 12–48 fetches per pixel inside the close-up band and none outside it. Skip this stage entirely until A and B are tuned; ripple amplitude on 20-unit cells is a few units at most, so the parallax vector is short and most of the visible gain is already in the slab stack.

The per-pixel budget on ps_3_0, before the renderer's existing reflection and refraction samples, is then: Stage A 2 fetches (plus 1 if flow and noise are not vertex attributes) and about 25 ALU; Stage B 1 bed fetch, 2–3 detritus fetches, 1 `refract` at 8–10 ALU, 1 reciprocal, 1 mad and 1 lerp and 1 exp per layer, about 20–30 ALU; Fresnel and reflection about 6 ALU plus 1 `texCUBE` or planar fetch; total about **7 fetches and 40–60 ALU**, comparable to van Dongen's SM2 budget plus a cube read, and an order of magnitude below the 512-slot minimum. The structural advice from the same sources applies: draw the water after opaque geometry so early-z works (z-cull gave interior mapping +44 %), keep the DH and detritus atlases at 512²–1024² DXT5 (around 1.3 MB each, Valve's class of budget), and do not reach for ps_2_0 if flow-advected layers and absorption are wanted, because the dependent reads add up. What cannot be verified from any source is a measured millisecond cost of this exact combination on 2011 PC hardware; the nearest data points are van Dongen's 6600 GT frame ratios, Lagarde's PS3 timings and Tatarchuk's X1600 XL frame rates, all of which place the whole stack comfortably within a frame for a screen-filling sheet on GTX 400 or HD 5000 class parts.

## Conclusion

The research inverts the question's premise in a useful way. The "distorted cube" of Burnout Paradise is not a technique that produced detail; it is the artefact that uncorrected environment maps produce on flat surfaces, and the box-projection family that followed exists to suppress it. What the cube lineage contributes to water is its maths, not its shape: once the vertical faces are dropped, interior mapping, parallax-corrected cubemaps and Oat's gooey layers collapse into one closed-form operation per plane, and the water version of that operation, a refracted ray against a bed and a few alpha-tested planes, is cheaper than the building version because the simulation already supplies the one parameter (depth) that a building shader has to invent. The genuinely new capability this enables is suspended detritus with depth parallax; Portal 2's debris was a flat colour map on the surface, and no shipped title has published the layered version for liquids, so a game that ships it would be documenting something the ice shaders of Battlefront and Wonderlands only hint at.

The second shift is where the simulation's value shows up in the renderer. A height-field solver is usually treated as a source of displacement and normals; here it is also the source of the per-pixel thickness term, the flow field that advects every texture layer, the strain measure that fades stretched layers, the wetness history for the water line and the normal field for caustics. Each of those replaces a depth read, a hand-authored flow map or a particle system that the shipped references needed because their water was a plane. The implication for the ordering of work is that the rendering upgrades should be wired to the simulation outputs first (velocity and depth as vertex attributes, the three advected coordinate sets, a max-height field) and the shader stages layered on afterwards; done in that order, Stage B is a day's work on top of Stage A, and Stage C may never be needed.

## In-house starting point

The research notes folder also holds `in_house_water_work.md`, collected from the Advent Rising session: the Advent blood mod already runs a 64x64 CPU shallow-water sheet per pool inside the d3d8to9-gi layer (`source/blood.hpp`), paints its depth into a texture every frame and swaps that texture in by hash, at well under 0.1 ms for eight pools, and its decal shader already does a parallax "deep middle sinks" offset. That loop is the prototype path for a Hydrophobia d3d9 proxy: lift the paint-and-swap, put Stage A and Stage B on the water draw, and reuse the fork's `gloss=` / `glossdry=` rules for the wet line. That session proposed the same flow-advected ripples and refracted bed-plus-detritus layers independently, which is a useful second opinion on the recipe but not evidence that it ships.
