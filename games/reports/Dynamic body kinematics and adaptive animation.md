# Bend the clips, spring the bones, defer physics

Games make characters and creatures move and react convincingly by keeping authored clips as the base and correcting them every frame. Three cheap families of correction do most of the work. Closed-form IK and aim chains put feet, hands and heads where the world needs them. Warping bends root motion, playrate and stride to fit gameplay. Critically damped springs supply lean, recoil, flinch, secondary motion and inertialized transitions. Physics is layered on top only for reactions, as Euphoria does in Red Dead Redemption 2 ([wccftech](https://wccftech.com/rockstar-euphoria-evolved-rdr2/amp/)). The data-hungry tier (motion matching, neural controllers, balance-walking ragdolls) needs mocap, kilohertz physics or engine access that Advent Rising does not have. For the AdventMod the realistic order is as follows. First, a gate slice that pins down what `GetBoneCoords` actually returns, since the failed foot-slide meter showed it cannot be trusted as-is. Then come the springs and aim chains, which only write bones and never read them. Next is root-motion skew warping for ModAction's vault, slam and barge and for enemy lunges, then playrate matching and creature body layers (hound spine, ground pitch and threat poses; Seeker per-arm aim). Standing foot IK and powered-ragdoll tuning follow. The native DLL items come last: full-skeleton inertialization, real leg IK and foot locking, and per-substep ragdoll drives. Those wait on the running engine decompile to confirm bone-controller semantics and a post-pose hook point. Motion matching, learned controllers and SIMBICON-style balance stay off the list.

## The shipped stack is clip first, correction second, physics last

Across the sources, modern character animation settles into four layers. A base pose comes from a state machine, blend space or motion matching. Root motion is reconciled with the gameplay capsule. Procedural layers then adjust the pose: warping, foot and hand IK, aim and look-at, additive leans. Physics, or "active ragdoll", handles reactions last. Rockstar is explicit that Euphoria enhances "physics-based reactions of both humans and animals" in RDR2: riders rolling off shot horses, being dragged in stirrups, being trapped under a dead horse. Euphoria is not the walk ([wccftech](https://wccftech.com/rockstar-euphoria-evolved-rdr2/amp/)). Naughty Dog's climbing in Uncharted 4 layers "full-body inverse kinematics and full-body physics systems" over animation ([Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2015/01/23/how-uncharted-4-is-taking-game-technology-to-the-next-level.aspx)). Far Cry 4's mountain wildlife combines additive animation, environment detection, IK and character physics on top of clips ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1022027/Grounding-Wildlife-in-the-Mountains)). The indie end of the spectrum confirms the pattern from below. David Rosen's Overgrowth talk shows fluid, interactive motion produced from very few keyframes plus simple procedural layers ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/video-an-indie-approach-to-procedural-animation)). That is the closest model for a script-level mod, because the mod also has a fixed set of old clips and a scripting layer that can rotate bones after the animation runs.

Motion matching is the headline technique of the last decade, and the one least suited to this mod. Simon Clavet's For Honor system replaced hand-built transition graphs with a continuous search over long mocap takes. The navigation data was "5 or 10 minutes of a person running around", and a frame is chosen by matching mostly foot positions and velocities against the desired future trajectory ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022985/Motion-Matching-and-The-Road); [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/programming/most-inspiring-game-animation-tech-talks-of-2016)). The Last of Us Part II used it for NPCs, horses and dogs "with as little blending as possible at every foot plant and turn" ([PSU](https://www.psu.com/news/the-last-of-us-part-2-motion-matching/)). Epic's Pose Search wants root-motion clips and warns that every added channel and sample costs memory and CPU ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/motion-matching-in-unreal-engine)). The search itself would be trivially cheap in a DLL. The blocker is data. Advent's clips are short authored loops and transitions, so a matcher over them could only re-pick loop frames; it cannot invent plants, pivots or starts that were never captured. The usable open datasets cut the other way on licensing. LAFAN1, which ships with Holden's MIT-licensed reference code, is CC BY-NC-ND and cannot be redistributed in derived form ([GitHub](https://github.com/orangeduck/Motion-Matching)). CMU mocap is free and may be included in products if not resold ([4TU](https://data.4tu.nl/datasets/0448aab2-3332-449f-a8e2-d208cb58c7df)), but retargeting it onto Advent skeletons and importing it as UE2 animation is a pipeline project in its own right. Learned motion matching cuts memory, which is not the mod's problem, at the cost of compute ([Orange Duck](https://www.theorangeduck.com/page/learned-motion-matching)).

What *is* worth taking from motion matching is its scaffolding, none of which needs data. The first piece is the critically damped spring that predicts the trajectory. The second is inertialization, which stores the pose offset at a switch and decays it, evaluating only one animation. The third is the damped reconciliation between a simulation object and the visible character ([Orange Duck](https://theorangeduck.com/page/code-vs-data-driven-displacement)). The Coalition built Gears of War 4's transitions on inertialization precisely because blended transitions evaluate both poses, roughly doubling animation cost during the blend ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025331/Inertialization)). Holden's spring form is a few lines. At the switch, store `off = (src + off) − dst` for position and velocity. Then each frame decay the offset with `y = 2·ln2/halflife`, `j1 = v + y·x`, `x' = e^(−y·dt)(x + j1·dt)`, `v' = e^(−y·dt)(v − y·j1·dt)`, and output `in + off`. There is no fixed duration and no stored transition time ([Orange Duck](https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call)). This is the shared primitive under nearly every slice below.

## Closed-form IK and warping fit a slow script, and iterative solvers do not

Solver cost decides what can live in UnrealScript. Aristidou and Lasenby's FABRIK paper benchmarked a 10-joint chain in unoptimised MATLAB. FABRIK reached a reachable target in **15.5 iterations and 13.3 ms**, against CCD's 26.3 iterations and 123.6 ms and the Jacobian transpose's **1,311 iterations and 12.99 s**. The authors summarise FABRIK as about 10x faster than CCD and a thousand times faster than Jacobian methods ([FABRIK paper](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/alexandre.meyer/teaching/master_charanim/M2_3_video_IK_Procedurale/FABRIK.pdf)). They also note that CCD "can roll and unroll itself" and over-bends the joints near the tip. That is acceptable for tails and cables, wrong for human limbs. Final IK's documentation disagrees on per-iteration cost: FABRIK needs fewer iterations, but each one is slower, especially with rotation limits ([RootMotion](http://www.root-motion.com/finalikdox/html/page6.html)). The discrepancy is implementation-dependent and only a measurement in the mod's own code settles it. For a script that runs per pawn per tick, the answer is to avoid iteration altogether. Holden's analytic two-joint IK clamps reach to `[eps, l1+l2−eps]`, computes hip and knee interior angles with the law of cosines, rotates both joints about the bend axis, then swings the hip to put the heel on target. It keeps the animator's bend plane by default ([Orange Duck](https://theorangeduck.com/page/simple-two-joint)). ozz-animation's two-bone job adds a pole vector, a twist angle and a "soften" ratio that stops the leg snapping straight ([ozz-animation](https://guillaumeblanc.github.io/ozz-animation/documentation/ik/)). Aim and look-at are the second closed-form primitive. ozz spreads a look-at across a spine-to-head chain ([ozz-animation](https://guillaumeblanc.github.io/ozz-animation/documentation/ik/)). Advent already aims `Spine2` from its camera system and drives the player's right arm chain with `SetBoneDirection` in slots 2–7 (local source, `PlayerController.uc` around 1800–1825, `EonCameraSystem.uc:351`). Full-body solvers (Unreal PBIK, Unity Animation Rigging, Final IK FBBIK) are native, iterative and multi-effector, and belong only behind the DLL ([Epic PBIK](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Plugins/PBIK)).

Warping is how studios stretch a small clip set. Epic's tools are the best-documented public reference, and they sort neatly by what they need. **Playrate matching** scales clip speed to ground speed. **Distance matching** drives a clip by remaining distance instead of time, so a stop lands on its mark and a landing tracks height above the ground ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/distance-matching-in-unreal-engine)). **Motion warping** marks a window in a root-motion clip and skews translation and rotation so the clip ends exactly on a gameplay-supplied target; the warp point can be the root or a bone such as a hand ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/motion-warping-in-unreal-engine)). None of the three touches individual bones, so all three map onto UE2's clip time, playrate and root-motion controls. **Stride warping** (scale = locomotion speed / root-motion speed, with a pelvis solver that drops the hips as legs extend) and **orientation warping** (legs twist toward travel, spine counter-twists, clamped near 90°) both need two-bone leg IK ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/pose-warping-in-unreal-engine)). Epic still marks slope warping as not production-ready. The interaction pattern behind ledge grabs, cover entries and melee alignment combines the two families. An authored contact clip carries markup for the contact frame and warp window. Gameplay supplies a target, root motion is skewed to arrive, and hand or foot IK snaps the end effector over the last frames. Gears of War 4 reportedly confined warping to ranges between solid contacts so the adjustment is least visible, though that comes from a low-confidence forum recollection ([AWS re:Post](https://repost.aws/questions/QURJZ_VL2NT2GjizmNOge4hw/emotionfx-animation-skewing)).

The mod's own data adds a constraint the literature does not mention. ModMoves' foot-slide meter treated the lower foot from `GetBoneCoords` as planted and proved unreliable. The bone coordinates did not reflect real foot contact, and pawns that are off-screen are not posed at all. That shifts the whole IK plan. Every layer that *reads* the posed skeleton (foot IK, foot locking, contact detection, grip IK) is suspect until a gate test establishes when `GetBoneCoords` is refreshed, whether it includes last frame's bone controllers, and what each `Space` value means. The IK notes predicted a one-frame lag and controller feedback, and recommended solving against cached pre-IK data or clearing controllers before reading ([ik_procedural notes, citing Holden](https://theorangeduck.com/page/simple-two-joint)). The meter's failure is consistent with that and worse: un-rendered pawns return stale or reference poses. Two consequences follow. First, every bone-reading layer must skip pawns that were not rendered last frame (`LastRenderTime` against `Level.TimeSeconds`). This costs nothing visually, because nobody sees an off-screen pawn's feet. Second, contact timing should come from the clip rather than the live pose. `GetBoneLocationAtFrame` returned world positions in earlier tests (local recon, `advent_hooks.md`), so foot height and velocity can be sampled per clip offline or at load. This is Holden's automatic contact annotation, which thresholds foot height and velocity ([Orange Duck](https://theorangeduck.com/page/code-vs-data-driven-displacement)), applied to the clip instead of the screen. Layers that only *write* bones (springs, leans, aim offsets, flinches) are unaffected, which is why ModMoves' lean works with its verified direction while the meter does not.

## Creatures move by gait tables and passive secondary motion, not more clips

Non-humanoid animation in shipped games is the clearest case of "parameters over clips". Spore's system, the best-documented shipped example, groups legs by length into clusters whose relative gait frequencies are small rational ratios. Each foot gets a **duty factor** (fraction of the cycle on the ground) and a **step trigger** (phase offset). The hips are "translated and rotated as the feet are moved for believable torso motion" ([Hecker et al. 2008](https://www.cse.chalmers.se/edu/year/2011/course/TDA361/Advanced%20Computer%20Graphics/027-hecker%20copy.pdf)). Limping and lumbering are gait *styles* layered on top, and different leg groups can run different gaits at once. The biology supplies the table directly. Walks have duty factors above 0.5, trots move diagonal pairs together at 50% phase, and a horse's duty factor falls from about 0.6 to 0.5 at the walk-to-trot transition ([eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/29495)). Procedural walkers converge on one stepping rule. Predict each foot's ideal spot from its rest offset plus velocity, trace to the ground, step when the planted foot drifts past a threshold, and let only one leg group move at a time ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/ik-driven-procedural-spider-locomotion-in-godot-4-5); [Spider Walker](https://digitalproduction.com/2026/08/28/spider-walker-automates-multi-leg-animation/)). The Flame in the Flood shows the cheapest spine trick: a procedural spine on the wolf against a rigid spine on the boar, avoiding authored gait transitions ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1023209/Animating-Quadruped-Characters-in-The)). Rain World pushes the idea all the way, building every creature from "a bunch of points in space" with a paper doll drawn on top ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/crafting-the-complex-chaotic-ecosystem-of-i-rain-world-i-)). That is too far from Advent's skinned meshes to borrow, but it shows how much readability comes from soft bodies driven by drives.

Spore's most transferable lesson concerns secondary motion. Its "Jiggles" layer runs a highly damped pseudo-physical simulation on sub-trees the current animation does not drive. It is "completely passive with respect to the keyed bodies and does not feed back". The earlier "Wiggles" system set IK goals instead and "negatively impacted the quality of the animators' work" ([Hecker et al. 2008](https://www.cse.chalmers.se/edu/year/2011/course/TDA361/Advanced%20Computer%20Graphics/027-hecker%20copy.pdf)). For the mod this argues for one-way spring layers: tails, idle Seeker arms, armour, the hound's head lag. They read the parent's motion and never write back into what the clip is trying to do. The same logic covers emotion. The Last Guardian's Trico conveys mood through small channels (ears that swivel at sounds, eye colour turning pink for anger) rather than whole-body clips ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Guardian)). A fear or threat display therefore needs no new clips: a few additive rotations on 4–8 bones blended by an emotion scalar. Threat raises head and shoulders and arches the spine side-on, which is exactly the "angry-bird plume" read in the user's design references. Fear lowers pelvis and head and slows the gait. Hit reactions follow the same additive model. The UE "Easy Procedural Hit Reactions" plugin takes impact location, direction, hit bone and strength, and outputs rotational springs with radius falloff and per-bone limits, all after the normal pose ([UE forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/kettunen-easy-procedural-hit-reactions/2741781)). ModReact's flinch is already a one-bone version of that.

The mod's three enemy types map onto this differently. **Human soldiers** take the full biped stack: aim chain, flinch chain, lean, stride and playrate matching, and later foot IK. **Seekers** are where per-limb aim pays most. No public game source documents a multi-arm rig. Spore's answer is to animate limbs by role ("graspers" and "feet") with shared variant groups, so one animation drives any number of arms ([Hecker et al. 2008](https://www.cse.chalmers.se/edu/year/2011/course/TDA361/Advanced%20Computer%20Graphics/027-hecker%20copy.pdf)). The practical translation is to treat each arm pair as its own layer, aim the upper pair at the target with clamped shoulder and elbow rotations, and stagger the pairs by a phase offset so four arms never move in lockstep. That is the step-trigger idea applied to arms. The Seeker's native spine flinch (`Seeker.uc:420-446`) must be left alone, and ModReact already respects it. **Hounds** are the hardest. Their spine bend toward the target is native (`SeekerDogNative` uses `SetBoneRotationOnly` on spine through neck02), their ragdolls crash, and their locomotion is the same four-way human blend. A full procedural gait would mean disabling native movement animation and posing about 14 bones per tick in script (local recon, `advent_hooks.md`). The realistic hound layer leaves the native spine alone and works around it: ground pitch from front-pair versus rear-pair traces applied at the hips or as a mesh-level tilt, a passive tail or neck spring, a head lag, threat and fear pose offsets, and later per-leg IK only after the DLL gives real leg solving. A limp from part damage is a gait-parameter change in Spore's model ([Hecker et al. 2008](https://www.cse.chalmers.se/edu/year/2011/course/TDA361/Advanced%20Computer%20Graphics/027-hecker%20copy.pdf)). Over native clips it becomes a hip drop on the hurt side plus a slower playrate, which is cheap and readable.

## Karma can flavour reactions, but it cannot hold a body up

Every shipped active ragdoll drives each simulated bone toward the animated pose with per-bone strength and a maximum force, and fades a master multiplier between animation and ragdoll. UE's Physical Animation component stores position, orientation, velocity and angular-velocity strengths plus max linear and angular force per bone. `ApplyPhysicalAnimationProfileBelow` gives partial ragdoll ("only the upper body") ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/python-api/class/PhysicalAnimationData); [Epic API](https://docs.unrealengine.com/4.26/API/Runtime/Engine/PhysicsEngine/UPhysicalAnimationComponent/index.html)). Jolt offers two drives. `DriveToPoseUsingKinematics` sets each body's velocity to reach the pose within one step, which is the closest open reference to the mod's impulse-only situation. `DriveToPoseUsingMotors` uses constraint motors instead ([Jolt](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/class_ragdoll.html)). A CEDEC 2010 deck lists "impulses / joint motors" and the standard get-up recipe: compare the ragdoll's orientation with several get-up start frames, drive toward the closest, then start that clip and blend ([CEDEC 2010](https://2018.cedec.cesa.or.jp:443/2010/archive/files/C10_I0052/C10_I0052.pdf)). The control theory explains why this is hard in Karma. Explicit PD control goes unstable as gains rise against small inertias and large timesteps. Stable PD fixes that by evaluating the law at the next step's state ([Tan et al.](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/5719567/figures)). Research controllers such as AMP simply simulate at **1.2 kHz under a 30 Hz policy** ([AMP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.02180)). SIMBICON's balance comes from foot-placement feedback on the centre of mass, with mostly feedforward torques at low gain, surviving a 350 N push ([SIMBICON](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)). Script impulses into Karma are the opposite of all three: explicit, a frame late, at frame rate.

Karma also has a specific gap. MathEngine supports motors and springs on `KHinge` actors (`KDesiredAngVel`, `HT_Springy`), but none of that is exposed for `KarmaParamsSkel` ragdoll joints in script ([UDN](https://docs.unrealengine.com/udk/Two/UsingKarmaActors.html)). The UT2004-era community workaround is the realistic ceiling for script. It applies `KAddImpulse` at the hit bone from the incoming momentum, then recovers by reading ragdoll bones with `GetBoneCoords` and blending `SetBoneDirection` from alpha 1 to 0. It avoids bone lifters for recovery, because they need collision off, and retries `PlayAnim` a tick later if the un-ragdoll tick drops it ([Unreal Wiki](https://unrealarchive.org/wikis/unreal-wiki/Legacy:Karma_Ragdoll_Injury_System.html)). ModReact's untuned powered-ragdoll prototype is a step beyond that. It drives per-bone impulse springs toward `GetBoneLocationAtFrame`, but a gain of 1e7 crashed and 2e5 launched the body. The stability rules from the notes explain both failures and say how to tune. Treat gains as accelerations, scaling impulse by bone mass. Damp near critical and relative to the parent. Cap the per-tick velocity change and the total speed. Prefer setting a target velocity to accumulating impulses. Keep neighbouring mass ratios within about 3:1. Fade a master strength. These are standard physics, not sourced tuning values. The mod's own Karma traps narrow the scope further. `KWake` is a no-op, `KFreezeRagdoll` drops bodies through the floor and hound ragdolls crash. The powered ragdoll should therefore stay human-only, and death-only at first. Recovery should be designed so it never needs to freeze or wake a body; it should follow the impulse-then-blend recipe instead. Euphoria-style standing balance is out of reach, and the learned controllers (DeepMimic, AMP, PHC, ProtoMotions) assume Isaac/MuJoCo-class simulators and 100M+ training samples ([AMP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.02180); [ProtoMotions](https://github.com/NVlabs/ProtoMotions)). The one shipped learned case, ARC Raiders' legged robots, ran physics plus deep RL in a modern engine ([4Gamer](https://www.4gamer.net/games/609/G060942/20260312064/)).

The DLL changes that ranking only partly. A native hook could apply the PD drive inside Karma's substep loop, which removes the frame lag. It could enable MathEngine's own joint motors on ragdoll constraints, if the decompile shows ball-and-socket joints have them; the notes found no source either way. Or it could host Jolt (MIT, with both drive modes built in) for one or two hero bodies and copy their bone transforms back. Each step is larger than the last, and all of them wait on the native findings.

## Ten slices, ordered by what they read from the skeleton

The ordering rule follows from the foot-meter lesson. **Slices that only write bones come first; slices that read the posed skeleton wait for a gate test; slices that need native bone control wait for the decompile.** Every test runs hidden through `ModPilot` steps (`[AdventMod.ModPilot] Steps=`), logs to `System\AdventNative.log`, and uses `SHOTP` frames for visual checks. These are the pilot vocabulary from the earlier locomotion report; `BONETEST`, `FOOTLOG`, `SPAWN`, `HURT`, `DOGS`, `TURN`, `MOVE`, `WHERE` and `CORPSELIST` are assumed or proposed there. One caveat applies to every bone-reading test. Off-screen pawns are not posed, so the pilot must frame the test pawn with the camera (or log `LastRenderTime`) and reject samples from un-rendered frames. A "hidden" run still needs the pawn rendered into the hidden window.

The order spends effort where the payoff shows on every enemy and the risk is lowest. K0 is a one-session experiment that decides which later slices are possible in script at all. K1 and K2 make every soldier and Seeker feel weighted and attentive using only writes. K3 is the best single return for ModAction. Its moves already use the game's own clips, and skew warping is how studios align vaults and lunges without new data ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/motion-warping-in-unreal-engine)). K4 replaces the failed meter with a metric that does not depend on live foot contact. K5 gives hounds most of their visual gain without touching the native spine or the crashing ragdoll. K6 and K8 are the fragile items and wait on their gates. K9 is where the DLL earns its keep. Real leg IK, foot locking and full-pose inertialization all need per-bone overrides after the pose is evaluated, which is exactly the post-pose hook point the decompile is looking for. If the decompile shows bone controllers already apply in a post-pose pass with clear local-space semantics, K6 may move into the DLL directly and skip the script version.

## Conclusion

The research reframes "dynamic bodies" for this mod around one axis the literature rarely names: **whether a layer reads the skeleton or only writes it**. Springs, aim offsets, flinches, leans, warping and threat poses only write, so they work today, on every pawn, regardless of render state. Foot IK, foot locking, contact detection and powered ragdolls must read the live pose, and the slide meter has already shown that the live pose in build 2226 is not what it appears to be. That makes the K0 gate and the native decompile the true critical path. They decide whether the reading tier lives in script, in the DLL, or not at all, while the writing tier ships in parallel.

The second shift is about creatures. The convincing hound or Seeker is not a better clip set but a parameter set. A duty factor and phase per limb, a role per arm pair, a passive spring per dangling part, and an emotion scalar that bends posture are all short, licence-free algorithms that can be rewritten clean-room in UnrealScript for the GPL mod. They are also the same knobs the AI layer already wants to drive. A ModMinds state that sets "threat", "fear" or "hurt leg" can therefore change how a creature stands and moves without a single new animation.
