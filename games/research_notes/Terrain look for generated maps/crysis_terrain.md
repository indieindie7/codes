# Crysis (2007) / CryEngine 2-3 outdoor terrain: how it was built and rendered, and what an Unreal Engine 2 modder can borrow

Scope note: Crytek never published a single "terrain paper". The facts below are stitched from (a) Crytek's own SIGGRAPH 2006/2007 course chapters (Wenzel, Mittring) and GPU Gems 3 ch. 16 (Sousa), (b) the CryEngine 3 Sandbox manual and Sandbox 2/3 tutorials written from it, (c) the game's own cvar names as they appear in community tweak guides and `sys_spec_*.cfg` dumps. Where a number comes only from a forum post or a search snippet it is flagged **[community / medium confidence]**. The official docs.cryengine.com pages now redirect to a JS portal and could not be fetched directly; their content is cited via the tutorials and search extracts that quote them.

---

## 1. Terrain representation: heightmap, unit scale, sectors/LOD, draw distance, horizon

### Takeaway
CryEngine terrain is a regular heightmap (power-of-two, 128² up to 8192² samples; default 2 m per sample; default max height 256 m) rendered as a quadtree of fixed-size sectors with distance/screen-error geomipmap LOD, drawn all the way to the horizon with no "skybox terrain" trick: the far ground is simply the lowest LOD with the baked colour texture, faded into physically-matched sky/fog colour. Crysis levels topped out at about 4 km × 4 km.

### Cited Findings
- Design asked for a 21 km × 21 km play area; Crytek "ended up having multiple levels with up to 4km×4km" because production, streaming and world persistence "would not be worth the effort" — [Mittring, Finding Next Gen: CryEngine 2, SIGGRAPH 2007 course notes §8.2](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2007/Mittring-Finding_NextGen_CryEngine2(Siggraph07%20Course%20Notes).pdf)
- The jungle environment is characterised as "Many objects, height map, ocean, big view distance, ambient lighting with one main directional light source" — [Mittring 2007 course notes §8.2](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2007/Mittring-Finding_NextGen_CryEngine2(Siggraph07%20Course%20Notes).pdf)
- Heightmap resolution range in Sandbox: 128×128 up to 8192×8192; "Meters Per Unit" is "the distance between two vertices (lying on a perpendicular grid) in meters"; default is 2 m, "meaning that every 2 meters there is a new point"; terrain size = resolution × meters-per-unit; raising meters-per-unit enlarges the level but "will decrease the fidelity of the heightmap" — [CryENGINE 3 Manual, Modifying Terrain (via search extract)](https://docs.cryengine.com/display/SDKDOC2/Modifying+Terrain); [CryENGINE 3 Cookbook sample chapter (Tuts+)](https://design.tutsplus.com/articles/cryengine-3-cookbook-sample-chapter-terrain-sculpting--cg-10607)
- Worked examples from the Cookbook: 128×128 at 4 m = 512×512 m; 1024×1024 at 2 m = 2048×2048 m; 512×512 at 16 m = 8192×8192 m. Default "Max Terrain Height" recommended = 256 m; heightmap white (255) = max height, black = 0 — [CryENGINE 3 Cookbook sample chapter (Tuts+)](https://design.tutsplus.com/articles/cryengine-3-cookbook-sample-chapter-terrain-sculpting--cg-10607)
- Rendering scheme (as described by a gamedev.net poster analysing Crysis): "chunked geomipmap-style terrain", LOD chosen by "a screen error metric combined with distance"; each chunk is 32×32 vertices (32 m × 32 m at the highest LOD at 1 m/vertex), lowest LOD is "8m×8m (4 vertices squared)"; the structure is quadtree-based and visible in-game with `e_terrain_bboxes 1`; the largest shipped heightmap was 4096×4096 (the island level); no asynchronous terrain-geometry streaming was observed — [gamedev.net thread "CryEngine Terrain" (search extract; page itself returned 403)](https://gamedev.net/forums/topic/608202-cryengine-terrain/4886487/) **[community / medium confidence; the 4096 figure is consistent with the 4 km × 4 km at ~1 m spacing above]**
- `e_terrain_lod_ratio` shipped as 1.5 (Low), 1 (Medium), 0.5 (High/Very High) in `sys_spec_ObjectDetail.cfg`; players who set it to 0.2 report large FPS loss — [overclockers.co.uk sys_spec_ObjectDetail thread (search extract)](https://forums.overclockers.co.uk/threads/crysis-sys_spec_objectdetail-file.18278839/); [hardforum pop-up thread (search extract)](https://hardforum.com/threads/crysis-and-pop-ups-oh-my.1235467/) **[community]**
- `e_view_dist_ratio` "Controls the maximum view distance for general objects"; `e_view_dist_ratio_vegetation` and `e_view_dist_ratio_detail` do the same for vegetation and small detail objects; `e_lod_ratio` scales the LOD-vs-distance curve; the quality presets live in `Crysis\Game\Config\CVarGroups\sys_spec_ObjectDetail.cfg` with sections [1]..[4] = Low..Very High — [TweakGuides Crysis guide mirror](https://vandalfsen.me/tweakguides/Crysis_11.html); [Steam Community draw-distance thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/17300/discussions/0/3159832911121469016/)
- `e_terrain_texture_lod_ratio` controls terrain texture mip bias: "higher values make textures appear blurrier"; `e_terrain_occlusion_culling` culls terrain-occluded geometry; a tweak guide uses `e_terrain_occlusion_culling_max_dist=200` — [TweakGuides mirror](https://vandalfsen.me/tweakguides/Crysis_11.html); [Gamecritics "noob's guide to optimizing Crysis"](https://gamecritics.com/mike-doolittle/the-noobs-guide-to-optimizing-crysis/)
- Horizon handling is via atmosphere, not geometry: Wenzel's sky model keeps an average of the horizon samples so that per-pixel fog colour matches "the sky's color at the horizon for a given direction", so "a mountain in the distance which is partially in fog should automatically correlate with the colors of the sky" — [Wenzel, Real-time Atmospheric Effects in Games, SIGGRAPH 2006 course ch. 6, §6.5](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2006/Chapter6-Real-time%20Atmospheric%20Effects%20in%20Games.pdf)
- Mittring on why LOD matters for this design: "Most games have either quite limited view range often realized with fog or strong occlusion set up by level designers... in Far Cry we wanted to show big landscapes with many details without restricting the players view or position. In Crysis we kept the view range but added more objects" — [Mittring 2007 course notes §8.6.1](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2007/Mittring-Finding_NextGen_CryEngine2(Siggraph07%20Course%20Notes).pdf)

### Inferences
- A Crysis level is on the order of 2048–4096 heightmap samples at 1–2 m spacing. UE2's 256×256 heightmap at the same 1–2 m spacing is only a 256–512 m square; to get "Crysis distance" an Unreal II map must use coarser spacing (e.g. 8–16 m/sample for a 2–4 km square) and spend the detail budget on the macro texture and static-mesh rocks instead of vertex density. CryEngine itself documents exactly this trade (512×512 at 16 m = 8 km, lower fidelity).
- CryEngine's lowest terrain LOD (4×4 vertices per 32 m chunk) is roughly what UE2 terrain looks like everywhere beyond its own near LOD; the far-field look therefore does not depend on geometry density but on the baked colour map and the fog/sky match.

### Gaps
- No primary Crytek source for the chunk size / LOD metric; only forum analysis. The CRYENGINE GitHub `terrain.h` could not be fetched (404 on both `release` and `main` paths tried).
- Exact per-preset values of `e_view_dist_ratio*` in Crysis 1 were not retrievable (the forum pages carrying the full `sys_spec_ObjectDetail.cfg` returned 403).

## 2. Materials: layer system, macro colour vs detail, slope/altitude rules, hiding tiling

### Takeaway
Each terrain layer = a low-frequency **Surface (colour) texture** painted into one big baked "surface texture" (recommended final bake 16384², tiles 64²–2048²) plus a tiling **Detail material** (Terrain.Layer shader) that is deliberately a grey high-pass diffuse + normal map, tiled at ~0.25–0.5 and faded out beyond a distance cvar (`e_detail_materials_view_dist_xy`, 64 on Low to 2048–4096 on Very High). Colour lives only in the baked map; detail adds only frequency. Layers carry per-layer Altitude (m) and Slope (deg) masks (cliff example: 55°–90°) that constrain the painter, and vegetation groups carry the same two filters.

### Cited Findings
- "Each terrain layer consists of a Surface texture and a Detail material. The Surface texture is what the player sees from a long range and is blended together with the Detail material when viewed close up to provide higher quality shading and realism." — [CryENGINE 3: Breaking Ground with Sandbox (ModDB, search extract)](https://www.moddb.com/games/crysis/tutorials/cryengine-3-breaking-ground-with-sandbox)
- Layer Texture = "far away and color information texture" (colour only); Layer Material = "close up detail texture" (detail only, not colour); "Color is defined by layer texture"; the switch distance is `RollupBar → Terrain → Environment → DetailLayersViewDistRatio` — [World of Level Design, CryEngine 3 SDK: Terrain Layer Texture Painting](https://www.worldofleveldesign.com/categories/cryengine-3-sdk/cryengine-3-sdk-06-terrain-layer-texture-painting.php)
- Sandbox 2 (Crysis) described the same three-part system: Detail Texture (close range), Distance Texture ("neutral tiling texture for distant terrain, typically greyscale to avoid solid color appearance on far hills") and Layer Color applied with the Layer Painter; "theoretically you can build an entire level using only ONE distance texture"; example detail material `plains_grass_green_and_dry` with X/Y tiling 0.5 — [narby.net CryEngine 2 Sandbox tutorial](https://www.narby.net/?page_id=39)
- Detail material rule: "The detail material has to use Terrain.Layer shader and should output high frequency variations of gray color. Usually this can be achieved by applying a high-pass filter on the diffuse texture in Photoshop and by setting material diffuse color to full white." — [CRYENGINE docs via search extract (Terrain.Layer Shader / Creating Terrain Textures)](https://docs.cryengine.com/display/SDKDOC2/Terrain.Layer+Shader)
- "The Terrain.Layer shader is used for all the terrain materials"; "you always need a Diffuse map"; "For terrain layers you want to get somewhere between 0.25 - 0.5 tiling" — [CRYENGINE V Manual, Terrain.Layer Shader (search extract)](https://docs.cryengine.com/display/CEMANUAL/Terrain.Layer+Shader); [polycount "Confused about terrain Textures" (search extract)](https://polycount.com/discussion/127279/confused-about-terrain-textures)
- Detail map channel packing (CryEngine 3): detail bump in Green/Alpha, greyscale detail diffuse in Red, greyscale gloss in Blue, each with its own scale slider — [CRYENGINE 3 Manual, Detail Maps (search extract)](https://docs.cryengine.com/display/SDKDOC2/Detail+Maps)
- Per-layer masks: "Altitude and Slope are parameters that can be set per layer allowing you to only paint that layer within the Altitude (meters) or Slope (degrees) threshold. This is especially useful for cliff-like layers (for example, 55 to 90)." — [CRYENGINE 3 Manual via search extract](https://www.cryengine.com/docs/static/engines/cryengine-3/categories/1114113/pages/15008665); same numbers in [Cookbook sample chapter](https://design.tutsplus.com/articles/cryengine-3-cookbook-sample-chapter-terrain-sculpting--cg-10607)
- Sandbox 2 Layer Painter has min/max Altitude masks ("the brush will only apply within these boundaries") plus slope and layer-based masking — [search extract from Sandbox 2 manual / narby.net](https://www.narby.net/?page_id=39)
- Surface-texture bake: terrain is split into tiles paintable at 64×64 to 2048×2048 ("Tile Resolution... higher resolution providing better layer blending as well as softer transitions in the Layer Texture"); tile resolution must be a multiple of 16; for final quality "16384x16384 resolution is recommended when generating surface texture with high quality and terrain sky accessibility calculation, though 4096x4096 is sufficient for testing" — [search extract quoting Sandbox 2 manual (docs.crymod.org) and CE3 manual](https://docs.crymod.org/roadieCE2-manual.xhtml); [Cookbook sample chapter](https://design.tutsplus.com/articles/cryengine-3-cookbook-sample-chapter-terrain-sculpting--cg-10607)
- Generate Surface Texture compresses the painted layers into `terraintexture.pak`, "reducing size to 1/6 of its original size and lowering drawcalls" — [Cookbook sample chapter](https://design.tutsplus.com/articles/cryengine-3-cookbook-sample-chapter-terrain-sculpting--cg-10607)
- "Terrain Color Multiplier is used when the generated surface texture is compressed using DXT... entering a multiplier value of 4 would make colors in the 0-63 range fill the entire 0-255 range" — [CryENGINE 3 Manual, Creating a New Level (search extract)](https://docs.cryengine.com/display/SDKDOC2/Creating+a+New+Level)
- Example painter colour for a grass layer: RGB 191,215,138 — [Cookbook sample chapter](https://design.tutsplus.com/articles/cryengine-3-cookbook-sample-chapter-terrain-sculpting--cg-10607)
- Detail-fade distances in `sys_spec_ObjectDetail.cfg`: `e_detail_materials_view_dist_xy=64` on the Low preset; Very High configs use 2048 (some tweakers 4096) with `e_detail_materials_view_dist_z=128`–512; the guide describes `_z` as "primarily controls the global amount of detail on the ground, with the lower the value the more blurry certain ground features will appear" — [overclockers.co.uk sys_spec thread (search extract)](https://forums.overclockers.co.uk/threads/crysis-sys_spec_objectdetail-file.18278839/); [TweakGuides mirror](https://vandalfsen.me/tweakguides/Crysis_11.html); [Gamecritics guide](https://gamecritics.com/mike-doolittle/the-noobs-guide-to-optimizing-crysis/); [overclock.net config thread (search extract)](https://www.overclock.net/threads/crysis-optimization-the-crysis-config-thread.341798/) **[community]**
- Later CryEngine (3.5+) added height-based layer blending: "The Blend Factor shader parameter changes the visibility of the blended layer and requires a heightmap" instead of "basic fading between each other" — [CRYENGINE docs via search extract](https://www.cryengine.com/docs/static/engines/cryengine-3/categories/1114113/pages/1048772) (Crysis 1 itself used plain alpha fading.)
- Crytek 3Dc/BC5 compression for normal maps, "reconstruct z in pixel shader", DXT5 fallback — [Mittring 2007 slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2007/Mittring-Finding_NextGen_CryEngine2(Siggraph07).pdf)

### Inferences
- The visual trick that matters most is the **macro/detail split**: because colour comes from one continuous, hand-painted, non-tiling 16k map and the tiled detail texture is colourless high-pass, tiling repetition carries no colour pattern and is far less visible; and beyond ~64–2048 m the detail texture is faded out entirely so the far hills show only the smooth painted colour. This is cheap: one big (or several 2048² tiled) colour textures plus one modulate2x grey detail pass.
- Grey high-pass detail at tiling 0.25–0.5 means a detail texel period of 2–4 m in world space, i.e. the detail texture tiles about every 2–4 m; combined with the colour map this is what reads as "sharp near, calm far".
- The 55°–90° cliff rule and altitude rules are editor-time painting masks, not runtime shaders; the same thing can be done offline by any heightmap tool producing alpha maps for UE2 layers.

### Gaps
- Maximum layer count per level in Sandbox 2/3 was not found in any fetchable source (claims of "up to 15 surface types/layers" exist in community lore but no citation was retrievable; left out).
- No fetchable primary source for the exact blend function between surface colour and detail (modulate vs 2x modulate) — inferred from "detail only, not color" and "set material diffuse to full white".

## 3. Lighting: terrain shadows, terrain AO ("sky accessibility"), time of day, how vegetation shading fits

### Takeaway
Sun shadows on Crysis terrain are **variance shadow maps cast by the terrain only**, deferred into a screen-space shadow mask alongside 2–4 cascades of PCF/jittered shadow maps for objects; ambient darkening comes from a **baked "terrain sky accessibility" (AO) term computed during the surface-texture bake** plus runtime SSAO; the sky/fog colour is computed from a Nishita single-scattering model updated only when time of day changes. Vegetation picks up terrain colour at its base and uses a per-vertex hemisphere ambient.

### Cited Findings
- Terrain shadows: "For terrain we initially wanted to pre-compute a texture with start and end angle... the problem has always been objects on the terrain... We tried to use our normal shadow map approach and it gave us a consistent look that wasn't soft enough... Here we tried variance shadow maps [DL06] and this approach has worked out nicely. The usual drawback of variance shadow maps arises with multiple shadow casters behind each other but that's a rare case with terrain shadows." Slides: "Only terrain is casting VSM", "Deferred applied to the shadow mask", "Inexpensive very soft shadows" — [Mittring 2007 course notes §8.5.3.8](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2007/Mittring-Finding_NextGen_CryEngine2(Siggraph07%20Course%20Notes).pdf); [slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2007/Mittring-Finding_NextGen_CryEngine2(Siggraph07).pdf)
- Sun uses "2-4 parallel shadowmap slices"; shadow mask is one ARGB screen texture holding up to four lights; shadow-map lookups are randomised (8 samples, screen-space or light-space jitter, "Tiled 64x64" / "Tiled 256x256" noise) — [Mittring 2007 slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2007/Mittring-Finding_NextGen_CryEngine2(Siggraph07).pdf)
- Far Cry (CryEngine 1) baseline: "shadow maps and projected shadows per object for the sun"; "For performance reasons we pre-computed vegetation shadows but memory restrictions limited us to very blurry textures" (Figure 7: soft precomputed shadows combined with real-time shadows) — [Mittring 2007 course notes §8.5.1](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2007/Mittring-Finding_NextGen_CryEngine2(Siggraph07%20Course%20Notes).pdf)
- Shadow cvars: `e_shadows_max_texture_size` 256–4096; `e_gsm_lods_num` "Controls the number of shadow maps used"; `e_gsm_range` controls cascade range/edge look — [TweakGuides mirror](https://vandalfsen.me/tweakguides/Crysis_11.html)
- Baked terrain AO: generate surface texture with "high-quality settings and 'Calculate Terrain Sky Accessibility' enabled to 'darken the ground around the trees and add some nice additional depth to the scene' (processing takes several minutes)" — [narby.net Sandbox 2 vegetation tutorial](https://www.narby.net/?page_id=40); CE3 manual: "make sure that the 'Calculate Terrain Sky Accessibility' check box has been selected during the terrain texture generation" — [CryENGINE 3 Manual, Creating a New Level (search extract)](https://docs.cryengine.com/display/SDKDOC2/Creating+a+New+Level)
- Cvar `e_terrain_ao`: "Terrain Ambient-Occlusion with 0=OFF 1=ON. It's a global lighting system to provide realistic lighting"; `e_terrain_normal_map` "0=Blurry/NO detail..." (community config comments) — [overclock.net / overclockers.co.uk config threads via search extract](https://forums.overclockers.co.uk/threads/crysis-custom-cfg.17825617/) **[community; cvar names are real, descriptions are player-written]**
- SSAO: full-screen pass on the depth buffer, applied to ambient only ("works best on ambient only... because it changed the look away from being realistic" otherwise); samples in a sphere reflected by a per-pixel random plane from a 4×4 texture; "Results depend on asset tessellation and tweaking" — [Mittring 2007 course notes §8.5.4.3 and slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2007/Mittring-Finding_NextGen_CryEngine2(Siggraph07%20Course%20Notes).pdf)
- Real-time Ambient Maps (one scalar AO per texel, reconstructed with light colour/position/normal) were used for indoor; "For huge outdoor areas computing the RAM data for every surface wouldn't be feasible so we approached that differently" — [Mittring 2007 course notes §8.5.4.2](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2007/Mittring-Finding_NextGen_CryEngine2(Siggraph07%20Course%20Notes).pdf)
- Sky / time of day: Nishita (1993) single scattering; assumes viewer at ground height so "the sky only ever needs to update when time changes"; CPU solves the scattering integral for 128×64 hemisphere samples with n=32 exponentially distributed atmosphere layers into a float texture, update spread over frames ("One distributed update usually takes 15 to 20 seconds"); phase function evaluated per pixel to de-block the low-res texture; ~200 shader instructions × 32 loops if ported to GPU — [Wenzel 2006 ch. 6 §6.3](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2006/Chapter6-Real-time%20Atmospheric%20Effects%20in%20Games.pdf)
- Global fog: analytic height/distance fog with exponential falloff, `f(x,y,z)=b·e^(−cz)` integrated along the view ray (HLSL `ComputeVolumetricFog` listing); fog colour = sky colour at horizon via averaged horizon samples — [Wenzel 2006 ch. 6 §6.4–6.5](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2006/Chapter6-Real-time%20Atmospheric%20Effects%20in%20Games.pdf)
- Cloud shadows: "cast in single full screen pass using the scene depth"; the figure caption notes they break "up the regularity of shading giving a more natural look" on an island scene — [Wenzel 2006 ch. 6 §6.9.1](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2006/Chapter6-Real-time%20Atmospheric%20Effects%20in%20Games.pdf)
- Time-of-day presets are imported in Sandbox; one tutorial recommends sun at 10am or 2pm "to avoid straight-down shadow casting that appears unnatural" — [narby.net Sandbox 2 tutorial](https://www.narby.net/?page_id=39)
- Vegetation shading (Crysis): per-vertex hemisphere ambient `lerp(ambient*0.5, ambient, saturate(normal.z*0.5+0.5))` "to eliminate flat shadowed surfaces"; subsurface approximated with an artist "subsurface texture map" × `−N·L` × `E·L`; grass shaded per vertex (fill rate), foliage per pixel; vertex alpha stores precomputed AO; all in world space — [GPU Gems 3 ch. 16, Sousa](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iii-rendering/chapter-16-vegetation-procedural-animation-and-shading-crysis)
- Vegetation "Use Terrain Color" option "is used to blend your grass with the underlying terrain coloration... it works best with grass. This effect is especially valuable for disguising grass fading in the distance." — [CRYENGINE docs via search extract](https://docs.cryengine.com/display/SDKDOC2/Vegetation+Creation)

### Inferences
- The "nice" terrain lighting in Crysis is mostly two low-frequency terms a UE2 engine can bake: (1) very soft terrain self-shadow (VSM-soft — i.e. blurry, not crisp) and (2) sky-accessibility AO darkening valleys and the ground under tree groups. Both are static for a fixed time of day and map directly onto UE2 lightmaps/vertex lighting if the terrain is lit by a sunlight actor with a large soft radius and an ambient sky term.
- Cloud shadows are called out by Crytek specifically for breaking up shading regularity — a scrolling projected dark texture is a cheap UE2-compatible approximation (projectors / panning modulated texture on terrain).
- Grass inheriting the terrain colour map at its base is the single trick that keeps grass from "popping" against the ground; in UE2 decoration layers this maps to colouring grass vertices from the terrain's colour at placement (or simply authoring grass cards with a neutral bottom that is vertex-tinted).

### Gaps
- No primary-source numbers for Crysis's shadow cascade distances or VSM resolution.
- The exact algorithm behind "Calculate Terrain Sky Accessibility" (ray count, hemisphere sampling, whether vegetation is included as occluder) was not found; tutorials only show its effect (darkening under trees).

## 4. Vegetation and clutter: density, slope/elevation rules, grass, rocks, sprites

### Takeaway
Vegetation is painted per **group** with Density (0–100, "distance between each vegetation"), Elevation Min/Max and Slope Min/Max filters, random rotation, Size/SizeVar, Align-to-Terrain and Use-Terrain-Color; `Use_OnTerrain_Layers` distributes a group procedurally over chosen terrain surface types using those density/slope/altitude rules. Far trees become auto-generated sprites (impostors) at a distance ratio cvar; LOD transitions are hidden by screen-space dissolve.

### Cited Findings
- Vegetation group parameters: "Density dictates the proximity of vegetation objects, where higher values cause objects to be placed further apart, with settings ranging from 0 to 100 representing the distance between each vegetation"; "Elevation Min/Max defines the minimum and maximum values of terrain elevation at which vegetation may be painted"; "Slope Min/Max defines the minimum and maximum angle of terrain at which vegetation may be placed" — [CRYENGINE 3 Manual, DBView Vegetation Tab (search extract)](https://www.cryengine.com/docs/static/engines/cryengine-3/categories/1114113/pages/1048864)
- "Bending... ranges from 0 to 100"; "Bending is ignored by AutoMerged settings. AirResistance is similar to 'Bending' setting but specifically for AutoMerged vegetation"; "Use_OnTerrain_Layers option sets up the vegetation group to be procedurally distributed across the level on selected surface types with the specified density, slope and altitude parameters" — [CRYENGINE 3 Manual, Vegetation Creation (search extract)](https://docs.cryengine.com/display/SDKDOC2/Vegetation+Creation)
- Practical values from a CE3 tutorial: Density "anywhere between 10-50m"; grass material Shader Generation Params: "Leaves, Grass, Fit to Terrain, Detail Bending, Merged Textures"; "Use On Terrain Layers option can automatically distribute grass across terrain but may cause performance issues" — [World of Level Design, CryEngine 3 SDK: Add Vegetation](https://www.worldofleveldesign.com/categories/cryengine-3-sdk/cryengine-3-sdk-08-add-vegetation-foliage-trees.php)
- Sandbox 2 (Crysis) tutorial settings: Size var 0.1–0.25 for variation; Random rotation, Align to terrain, Use terrain color, Cast shadow all checked; "Use sprites" unchecked for grass only; Snap to Terrain for placement — [narby.net Sandbox 2 vegetation tutorial](https://www.narby.net/?page_id=40)
- `e_vegetation_sprites_distance_ratio` = 1 at Low and 1.5 in Very High configs ("Determines the distance threshold where vegetation converts to simplified sprites"); `e_vegetation_min_size` thins out small vegetation; `e_proc_vegetation` toggles procedurally generated vegetation ("marginally improving FPS with minimal visual impact" when off); `e_view_dist_ratio_vegetation` limits vegetation draw distance — [TweakGuides mirror](https://vandalfsen.me/tweakguides/Crysis_11.html); [overclockers.co.uk sys_spec thread (search extract)](https://forums.overclockers.co.uk/threads/crysis-sys_spec_objectdetail-file.18278839/) **[community values]**; a Low-end tweak set uses `e_view_dist_ratio_vegetation=60`, `e_view_dist_ratio_detail=60`, `e_vegetation_min_size=1` — [Gamecritics guide](https://gamecritics.com/mike-doolittle/the-noobs-guide-to-optimizing-crysis/)
- "We also used impostors for vegetation and improved them for Crysis"; "Specialized Solutions: ocean, static LOD, vegetation sprites"; dissolve "to fade out far away objects and to hide the transition to impostors"; "Lower LODs are usually small on the screen so per pixel cost is low. Having many LOD levels can be even counterproductive, as those cannot be instanced together" — [Mittring 2007 course notes §8.6.1–8.6.2 and slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2007/Mittring-Finding_NextGen_CryEngine2(Siggraph07%20Course%20Notes).pdf)
- Vegetation animation: main bending displaces vertices in XY by wind scaled by normalized height with a spherical length constraint; detail bending uses four triangle waves smoothed by cubic interpolation; vertex colours drive it (R = edge stiffness, G = per-leaf phase, B = overall stiffness, A = baked AO); grass alpha-blended, leaves alpha-tested, both double-sided — [GPU Gems 3 ch. 16](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iii-rendering/chapter-16-vegetation-procedural-animation-and-shading-crysis)
- "thousands of different vegetation objects in Crysis, often covering the entire screen" motivated the quality/efficiency trade-offs — [GPU Gems 3 ch. 16 (search extract)](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iii-rendering/chapter-16-vegetation-procedural-animation-and-shading-crysis)
- Sandbox's "Vegetation Editor allows defining group properties such as density, size, alignment, slope, minimum and maximum height. These properties are applied to all meshes in a group when painted" — [CRYENGINE V Manual, Vegetation Editor (search extract)](https://docs.cryengine.com/display/CEMANUAL/Vegetation+Editor)

### Inferences
- Rocks/boulders in Crysis are placed through the very same vegetation-group painter (they are "vegetation" objects with Slope Min/Max set high and Align-to-Terrain on), so the "steep slope = rocks" rule is a group filter plus hand painting, not a runtime scatter. An offline scatter script reading the UE2 heightmap can reproduce it exactly: for each group, sample candidate points at the group's Density spacing, reject by slope/elevation range, random-rotate, scale by Size ± SizeVar, align to the terrain normal, write as static-mesh actors or decoration-layer entries.
- Grass density in Crysis is high but its cost is controlled by view distance, min-size culling and per-vertex shading — all three exist in UE2 decoration layers (fade distance, density, unlit/vertex-lit grass).

### Gaps
- Exact Crysis grass density/fade distance numbers from shipped levels were not found.
- No fetchable source on how the automatic sprite (impostor) atlas was generated (resolution, angle count) beyond Mittring's statement that impostors existed.

## 5. Slope decoration: cliff-by-angle, beaches/waterline

### Takeaway
Cliffs are a layer with a Slope filter (documented example 55°–90°), beaches are an Altitude-filtered layer around sea level, and the waterline is sold by the water shader (procedural shoreline/foam, soft depth blending) rather than by terrain texturing.

### Cited Findings
- Slope-filtered cliff layer "for example, 55 to 90" degrees; Altitude filter in metres — [CRYENGINE 3 Manual (search extract)](https://www.cryengine.com/docs/static/engines/cryengine-3/categories/1114113/pages/15008665); [Cookbook sample chapter](https://design.tutsplus.com/articles/cryengine-3-cookbook-sample-chapter-terrain-sculpting--cg-10607)
- Water: "Shading: procedural shore, fog, shadow receiving, RGB independent refraction, Caustics"; earlier square-sector approach had "Shoreline based on vertex animated static mesh"; per-pixel depth is used for "Soft Z Buffered Particles... Beach/Ocean" — [Mittring 2007 slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2007/Mittring-Finding_NextGen_CryEngine2(Siggraph07).pdf)
- Ocean rendered as camera-aligned top-down-projected mesh snapped to the camera, "Extruded horizon border", perturbation faded before the horizon — [Mittring 2007 slides / notes §8.6.6](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2007/Mittring-Finding_NextGen_CryEngine2(Siggraph07).pdf)
- Terrain "holes" and sea level are editor settings on the Modifying Terrain page (listed in index; details not retrievable) — [CryENGINE 3 Manual, Modifying Terrain](https://docs.cryengine.com/display/SDKDOC2/Modifying+Terrain)

### Inferences
- The beach look comes from three stacked cheap things: a sand layer limited to an altitude band around sea level, the terrain colour map painted lighter/wetter near the water, and a water surface that fades softly where it meets the ground (depth-based). UE2 has no depth buffer access, so the soft edge must be faked by a translucent "wet sand"/foam decal strip or a vertex-alpha fade on the water sheet along the shore.

- No source found for Crysis's exact beach altitude band or wet-sand treatment.

## 7. What is cheap enough to borrow in Unreal Engine 2 (actionable checklist)

### Takeaway
Roughly 80 % of what makes Crysis terrain "read nice" is offline data (painted colour map, slope/altitude-driven layers, baked AO/shadow, scatter rules, fog/sky colour match) rather than shader tech; those parts fit UE2's heightmap + alpha-layer + static-mesh + decoration-layer model. The runtime-only parts (VSM, SSAO, per-pixel depth fog, dissolve) have passable fixed-function stand-ins.

### Inferences (each derived from the cited findings above)
1. **Macro/detail split (highest value, cheapest).** Mirror CryEngine's rule: colour comes from one large, non-tiling painted/baked colour texture; detail layers are grey high-pass + modulate. In UE2 terms: layer 0 = a 2048² (or a few 1024² tiles across TerrainInfo sectors) baked colour map stretched once over the whole terrain with no tiling; layers 1..n = tiling grey detail textures at a 2–4 m world period (Crysis uses tiling 0.25–0.5), blended by alpha maps and modulated over the colour. Fade the detail layers out with distance (UE2 has no per-layer distance fade, but a TerrainLayer can use a texture whose high mip levels are flat grey — the mip chain then "fades out" detail with distance for free, which is the same effect `e_detail_materials_view_dist_xy` achieves). Sources: [§2](#2-materials-layer-system-macro-colour-vs-detail-slopealtitude-rules-hiding-tiling).
2. **Slope/altitude layer masks offline.** Generate the alpha maps from the heightmap: cliff where slope > ~55°, sand within an altitude band around water, grass elsewhere, plus noise to break the lines. This is exactly what Sandbox's per-layer Altitude/Slope filter does at paint time.
3. **Bake AO and soft sun shadow into the colour map.** Crysis's "terrain sky accessibility" and terrain-only VSM are both low-frequency; in UE2 render them into the terrain lightmap (large sunlight radius, high-ish ambient) or pre-multiply them into the baked colour texture so even the lowest LOD and fixed-function path show them.
4. **Fog colour = sky horizon colour.** Choose the DistanceFog colour from the skybox's horizon band (Wenzel's horizon-average rule) so the far hills blend into the sky; UE2 distance fog is per-vertex linear, so pick a start distance well inside the draw range and a dark-to-light gradient skybox that matches.
5. **Cloud shadows.** A panning dark-cloud texture projected onto the terrain (Projector actor or a second terrain layer with a panning UV) reproduces the "breaks up the regularity of shading" effect Crytek singles out.
6. **Scatter rules for rocks and grass.** Implement CryEngine's vegetation-group filters offline: Density (spacing in metres), Slope min/max, Elevation min/max, Random rotation, Size ± SizeVar, Align to terrain normal; emit static-mesh rocks for steep slopes and decoration-layer grass for flat ones.
7. **Grass inherits terrain colour.** Tint grass vertices (or the decoration-layer material) from the baked colour map under each clump and fade grass out with distance; Crytek documents this as the way to hide the grass fade line.
8. **Draw distance by coarser spacing, not more vertices.** Use 8–16 m heightmap spacing on a 256² UE2 terrain for a 2–4 km square, matching the documented CryEngine trade (resolution × meters-per-unit), and let the colour map plus static-mesh rocks carry the near detail.
9. **Dissolve LOD.** Not available in UE2 fixed-function; use alpha-fade distance (`CullDistance`/fade on decoration layers) instead; Mittring notes small far LODs are cheap per pixel, so fewer LODs with fade is acceptable.
10. **Skip.** SSAO, VSM, per-pixel depth fog, procedural shoreline from depth: not feasible on DX8/UE2; their visual contribution is covered by items 3 and 4 plus a shoreline decal strip.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies how much of the perceived quality each element contributes; the ranking above is the researcher's inference from Crytek's own emphasis (VSM for terrain, sky-accessibility bake, horizon-matched fog, cloud shadows, terrain-colour grass).
