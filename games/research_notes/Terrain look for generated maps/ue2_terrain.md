## KQ1. TerrainInfo: data model, heightmap limits, scale, layers, blending, DecoLayers, lighting, visibility, LOD, collision

### Takeaway
UE2 terrain is one TerrainInfo actor per heightmap: a square power-of-two P8/G16 heightmap texture (256x256 is the documented sweet spot, 2048x2048 theoretical ceiling), one quad per heightmap pixel scaled by TerrainScale (default 64 UU per quad, Z also 64), up to 32 layers each with a tiling Material plus an RGBA8 alpha map that must match the heightmap's dimensions, blended top-down in a contiguous stack, plus an array of DecoLayers (non-colliding static meshes scattered from a density texture with distance fade). Lighting is baked per terrain vertex (sharpness tied to heightmap density); there is no separate LOD/tessellation system beyond sector-based rendering, quad visibility bits, and edge-turn bits.

### Cited Findings

**The core structure (what a TerrainInfo is)**
- "The basic concept of Terrain in the Unreal Engine (UT2003 and beyond) is based around creating a large grid of polygons based on a heightmap and centered around a TerrainInfo Actor"; each square of the grid is a *quad*, groups of quads rendered together are *sectors* — [UnrealWiki: Terrain](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/terrain.html); [UnrealWiki: TerrainInfo](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/terraininfo.html)
- "The TerrainInfo IS the terrain, all the hills and valleys are drawn around it with the TerrainInfo in the center." — [UDN EditingTerrainLayers](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/EditingTerrainLayers.html)
- Terrain only works inside a zone whose ZoneInfo has `bTerrainZone=True`; "setting the LevelInfo to bTerrainZone doesn't work, you have to use a ZoneInfo." You can have as many TerrainInfos as you want in a zone, but terrains that spill into a non-TerrainZone are not visible from there — [UDN CreatingTerrain](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/CreatingTerrain.html)
- The enclosing subtracted cube: "Subtract a very large cube, for example 65536*65536*65536. UnrealEd now supports sizes up to 524288*524288*524288, but I advise against making such a big space." — [UDN CreatingTerrain](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/CreatingTerrain.html)
- Terrain is one-sided: "normally a terrain is invisible from the down side"; `Inverted` renders it upside-down "Used for cave ceilings etc" — [UDN CreatingTerrain](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/CreatingTerrain.html); [UnrealWiki: TerrainInfo](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/terraininfo.html)

**Heightmap (TerrainMap) format and size**
- "The TerrainMap should be a P8 or G16 texture that is in the MyLevel package, and the AlphaMap should be a RGBA8 texture that is in MyLevel." — [UDN EditingTerrainLayers](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/EditingTerrainLayers.html)
- "The TerrainMap is a 16-bit texture, but you can also import 8-bit textures in the editor. When you edit the terrain, you have to convert it to 16-bit then ... black ... represents the lowest parts of the terrain, and the white color or the last color in the palette the highest parts ... it is possible to use any colored 8-bit palletized texture. Theoretically you can use textures up to 2048*2048, but enabling a texture of this size as TerrainMap takes much longer than rebuilding the lighting of a very large map. Most of the times textures with dimensions 256*256 will do fine." — [UDN CreatingTerrain](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/CreatingTerrain.html)
- New Terrain dialog: "XSize & YSize ... determine the size of the HeightMap only, not the actual Terrain itself. Make sure that these are set to powers of 2. For every pixel of the HeightMap a quad is created"; initial fill "Height ... It's best to leave this number at 32768 since that is directly in the middle of the spectrum of useable values (exactly 1/2 of 2^16)." — [UDN CreatingTerrain](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/CreatingTerrain.html)
- Heightmaps must be square and powers of two or the editor crashes; "A 256×256 texture produces terrain measuring 64 × 255 units" per side (fencepost: N pixels -> N-1 quads); sizing guide: 256x256 large area, 512x512 huge, 16x16 small features; 128x128 for an 8192-cube — [UnrealWiki: Creating A Terrain](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/creating-a-terrain.html)
- "An 8-bit heightmap can create 'stair' effects on the terrain, but if it's 16-bit (and after you used the Smooth tool), it's much smoother" — [UDN EditingTerrainMaps](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/EditingTerrainMaps.html)
- Community-reported size ceilings: a user "crashed at 768×768 scale" (interpreted as 768x768 heightmap at 64 UU = 49152 UU), and "Smooth over the edge of the map" crashes on 512x512 maps; advice is to scale quads larger instead of raising XSize/YSize — [UnrealWiki: Using The Terrain Tool](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/using-the-terrain-tool.html)
- Typical UT2004 practice: "UT2004 maps tend to keep the terrain size smaller, typically 128 through 512" — [UT99.org thread on converting UT2004 terrain](https://ut99.org/viewtopic.php?t=15108) (secondary; forum opinion)
- G16 is a non-standard container: "The G16 'BMP' format is not actually a valid BMP file, and will not work correctly with most programs"; 65536 grey levels; RGBA8/P8 textures can be converted to G16 inside UnrealEd — [Gildor forum: umodel and G16 BMP](https://www.gildor.org/smf/index.php?topic=667.0)

**TerrainScale and TerrainSectorSize**
- `TerrainScale` (vector): "X and Y values give the size of a sector...default size is 64 UU square. Z value scales terrain vertically"; `TerrainSectorSize` "default is 16, ie each sector is a square 16 quads across" and "Terrain is rendered sector by sector, and changing this value may help level optimization" — [UnrealWiki: TerrainInfo](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/terraininfo.html) (note: the wiki says "size of a sector" for X/Y but every other source, incl. the defaults below, treats it as quad size)
- Defaults from the UT2004 class: `TerrainSectorSize=16`, `TerrainScale=(X=64,Y=64,Z=64)`, `bStaticLighting=true` — [ericdives UnCodex: Engine.TerrainInfo source](http://ericdives.com/UT2004-UnCodex/Source_engine/terraininfo.html); [kos.informatik UT2004 TerrainInfo](http://kos.informatik.uni-osnabrueck.de/download/utdocu/engine/terraininfo.html)
- Z scaling semantics: for the "fake displacement map floor" trick, "In TerrainScale, the Z should be a very low value, for example 0.05, if you leave the value at 1 the tiles will be 256 units high" (i.e. with Z=1 the full 16-bit range spans 256 UU; Z=64 default spans 16384 UU) — [UDN EditingTerrainLayers](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/EditingTerrainLayers.html)
- World size = TerrainScale x heightmap pixel count; rescaling later "affects all existing features"; placement: align TerrainInfo to grid, set `bEdShouldSnap` — [UnrealWiki: Creating A Terrain](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/creating-a-terrain.html); [UnrealWiki: TerrainInfo](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/terraininfo.html)

**Layers (32) and how they blend**
- "You can give the terrain up to 32 different layers. Every layer has a Texture and an AlphaMap. The Texture is a tileable pattern ... and the AlphaMap is a large alpha channel that determines on what places of the terrain the texture will be present ... The Texture can be any texture or material, the AlphaMap has to be a RGBA texture. You probably want Layer[0] to have a completely white AlphaMap, otherwise the terrain becomes transparent." — [UDN EditingTerrainLayers](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/EditingTerrainLayers.html)
- UT2004 `struct TerrainLayer { Material Texture; Texture AlphaMap; float UScale, VScale, UPan, VPan; ETexMapAxis TextureMapAxis; float TextureRotation; rotator LayerRotation; matrix TerrainMatrix; float KFriction, KRestitution; transient Texture LayerWeightMap; }`, declared as `Layers[32]` — [kos.informatik UT2004 TerrainInfo](http://kos.informatik.uni-osnabrueck.de/download/utdocu/engine/terraininfo.html); [ericdives UnCodex source](http://ericdives.com/UT2004-UnCodex/Source_engine/terraininfo.html)
- Layer hierarchy: "the closer the layer is to the bottom of the contiguous stack, the more towards the top it is drawn on the Terrain. The Layer that is at the top of the stack (Layer [0]) will automatically be given a completely white AlphaMap ... Only the contiguous layers from the top will show up on the Terrain" (an empty slot terminates the stack) — [UDN EditingTerrainLayers](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/EditingTerrainLayers.html)
- Alpha map size rule: "AlphaHeight & AlphaWidth - It is very important to make sure that these values match the dimensions of the texture you are using. Otherwise, the texture may only appear as spots on a regular grid." — [UDN CreatingTerrain](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/CreatingTerrain.html); the wiki states alpha "must match the dimensions of the heightmap for your terrain" and mismatches make "the fade out ... not what you're expecting" — [UnrealWiki: Terrain Texture Layer](https://unrealarchive.org/wikis/unreal-wiki/Legacy:Terrain_Texture_Layer.html); "AlphaHeight and AlphaWidth values must be the same as the Size values in the Terrains tab ... must always be a power of two" — [UnrealWiki: Terrain Mode](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/terrain-mode.html)
- UScale/VScale: "a texture with scale 0 is invisible, so set them to 1 or less or more if you want the texture to be smaller or bigger"; `TextureRotation` is "expressed in degrees"; UPan/VPan pan the texture — [UDN EditingTerrainLayers](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/EditingTerrainLayers.html)
- `TextureMapAxis`: TEXMAPAXIS_XY projects from above; TEXMAPAXIS_XZ / TEXMAPAXIS_YZ project from the side so a layer "looks straight" on east/west or north/south-facing walls; `LayerRotation` (a rotator) projects a layer "in directions other than just straight down ... useful for painting Textures onto Terrain that is more vertical (like a cliff ...) and it won't stretch like it normally would if projected from directly above" — [UDN EditingTerrainLayers](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/EditingTerrainLayers.html)
- Painting the base layer's alpha darker makes the terrain transparent there but still solid ("not the same as the Visibility tool") — [UDN EditingTerrainLayers](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/EditingTerrainLayers.html)
- `AlphaFill` default 0 and `ColorFill` "is ignored" in build 2110+ (older builds could tint the terrain) — [UDN CreatingTerrain](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/CreatingTerrain.html); older wiki advice for pre-2110 builds: base layer Alpha=0, RGB=255, other layers the opposite — [UnrealWiki: Using The Terrain Tool](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/using-the-terrain-tool.html)
- KFriction/KRestitution per layer exist but are documented as "Information to be added later" — [UDN EditingTerrainLayers](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/EditingTerrainLayers.html)

**DecoLayers**
- "DecoLayers generate Static Meshes everywhere on the terrain. You can only see the ones that are inside a radius you can set, to save resources. Also, the StaticMeshes from a DecoLayer ... do not impede players, so using them for trees isn't realistic, but DecoLayers are better used for grass, flowers and ferns." — [UDN CreatingDecoLayers](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/CreatingDecoLayers.html)
- UT2004 `struct DecorationLayer { int ShowOnTerrain; Texture ScaleMap, DensityMap, ColorMap; StaticMesh StaticMesh; rangevector ScaleMultiplier; range FadeoutRadius; range DensityMultiplier; int MaxPerQuad; int Seed; int AlignToTerrain; ESortOrder DrawOrder; int ShowOnInvisibleTerrain; int LitDirectional; int DisregardTerrainLighting; int RandomYaw; EDetailMode DetailMode; }` plus a global `float DecoLayerOffset` on TerrainInfo — [kos.informatik UT2004 TerrainInfo](http://kos.informatik.uni-osnabrueck.de/download/utdocu/engine/terraininfo.html)
- DensityMap: white (or highest palette index for P8) = most meshes; must be RGBA8 with alpha or P8; the workflow is to create a throwaway terrain layer at the bottom of the stack, paint it, and "Set Density Texture from Current". Example FadeoutRadius Min 500 / Max 1200 UU. "Do NOT paint while you have the DecoLayer selected under the Decorations tab" (creates holes in the density map) — [UDN CreatingDecoLayers](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/CreatingDecoLayers.html)
- ScaleMap is RGBA8: "the red and green channel determine the width and length, and the blue channel the height"; ColorMap must be RGBA8 "otherwise all decorations will become black"; ScaleMultiplier has min/max for X,Y,Z and each decoration gets a random size between them — [UDN CreatingDecoLayers](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/CreatingDecoLayers.html)
- AlignToTerrain=1 aligns meshes to the quad normal ("useful for such things as rocks and debris, but when used on trees and grass it can look out of place"); RandomYaw "helps break up 'tiling' appearance" but "may cause AlignToTerrain to work incorrectly"; LitDirectional=1 "forces the StaticMeshes to use sunlight. It can look very bad in some cases, as it will cause shadows on things like grass"; DisregardTerrainLighting=1 makes them fullbright; DrawOrder SORT_BackToFront fixes alpha-test sorting of distant meshes behind near ones — [UDN CreatingDecoLayers](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/CreatingDecoLayers.html)
- DetailMode: DM_Low always shown "unless decolayers are completely turned off (DecoLayers in [WinDrv.WindowsClient])", DM_High needs HighDetailActors, DM_SuperHigh needs SuperHighDetailActors — [UDN CreatingDecoLayers](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/CreatingDecoLayers.html)
- Wiki performance guidance: "If you want more dense meshes, then try fading them out sooner"; for continuous cover at distance "consider using relatively sparse density maps, but let things fade away further"; deco layers are translucent-sorted and "other translucent actors in your game ... may get overdrawn totally by deco layers"; the wiki's own recommendation for grass contradicts UDN: "LitDirectional ... Use this!" — [UnrealWiki: Terrain Decoration Layer](https://unrealarchive.org/wikis/unreal-wiki/Legacy:Terrain_Decoration_Layer.html)
- `stat render` reports terrain sectors/triangles in view and render time, and DecoLayer triangle/instance counts and time — [UDN AdditionalTerrainTips](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/AdditionalTerrainTips.html)

**Lighting**
- `bStaticLighting=true` is the TerrainInfo default; the class also has `VertexLightMap` (Texture) and native `VertexColors`/`PaintedColor` arrays — [ericdives UnCodex source](http://ericdives.com/UT2004-UnCodex/Source_engine/terraininfo.html); [kos.informatik](http://kos.informatik.uni-osnabrueck.de/download/utdocu/engine/terraininfo.html)
- "Shadows become more unsharp when the heightmap gets smaller. Shadows are also affected by how big the scale of the terrain is"; "Objects smaller than terrain quads won't cast visible shadows; taller objects create blurred shadows" (i.e. shadowing is resolved at terrain-vertex resolution) — [UnrealWiki: Using The Terrain Tool](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/using-the-terrain-tool.html)
- Sunlight: "a light that comes from infinitely far away ... all the rays are parallel ... the rays of a light are stopped by every wall, so also by the sides of the large box, except if they are set to Fake Backdrop. That's why you made this SkyBox." Position is irrelevant; rotation sets direction; in build 927+ the arrow points where the light shines to. Sunlights only light the zone they are placed in; normal lights also light terrain — [UDN AdditionalTerrainTips](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/AdditionalTerrainTips.html)
- Set ZoneInfo AmbientBrightness to 128 to see unlit terrain while building; later decrease it and raise SunLight Brightness — [UDN CreatingTerrain](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/CreatingTerrain.html)
- Static meshes in UE2 are vertex lit: "The only spots where a mesh can determine if there is light or not are its vertices"; low-poly meshes get light gaps and bad shadows; fixes are tessellating meshes, modelling connected pieces, flipping edges for even triangles; `bStaticLighting` off gives blurry dynamic-style lighting — [Hourences: Vertex Lighting basics](http://www.hourences.com/tutorials/vtx-lighting.htm)
- Unreal II: "the terrain always display in Dynamic Light mode" and appears completely black without a light source — [OldUnreal: Unreal 2 editor thread](https://www.oldunreal.com/phpBB3/viewtopic.php?t=4351)

**Visibility, edge turning, LOD, collision**
- "The visibility layer is an extra 1-bit layer: visible or invisible ... The invisible parts make ALL the layers invisible on that part, and are also non-solid ... The sides of the invisible part get blocky, so you'll have to hide them with normal or hardware brushes" (used for caves/basements under terrain) — [UDN EditingTerrainMaps](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/EditingTerrainMaps.html); stored natively as `QuadVisibilityBitmap` (int array) alongside `EdgeTurnBitmap` — [kos.informatik](http://kos.informatik.uni-osnabrueck.de/download/utdocu/engine/terraininfo.html)
- Edge Turn "turn[s] the individual triangles within the terrain ... to smooth out some sharper slopes"; its radius follows the Outer Radius slider, "slide it down to an Outer Radius = 1" for control — [UDN EditingTerrainMaps](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/EditingTerrainMaps.html)
- Collision: `bKCollisionHalfRes` "use half the graphics res for Karma collision" and Karma then ignores per-quad invisibility/edge-turn — [UnrealWiki: TerrainInfo](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/terraininfo.html); Unreal II adds `bNonColliding` — [greatemerald UnCodex: Unreal II Engine.TerrainInfo](https://greatemerald.eu/uncodex/U2/engine/terraininfo.html); terrain "will only collide in its own Zone" — [UDN AdditionalTerrainTips](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/AdditionalTerrainTips.html)
- DistanceFog as a draw-distance LOD: "all polygons of the terrain that are behind this distance will not be displayed"; `DistanceFogEndMin` ties into the user's DrawDistanceLOD slider; match the skybox fog color so culled geometry is not visible — [UDN AdditionalTerrainTips](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/AdditionalTerrainTips.html)
- Render hardware: "UT2003 uses PS 1.4 for terrain rendering which allows a shorter pixel shader program than using PS 1.1 ... (FWIW, it's 4 vs 7 instructions)" — [Beyond3D forum](https://forum.beyond3d.com/threads/ut4-t-l-or-cpu.9376/) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Because alpha maps must match the heightmap resolution (one alpha texel per quad vertex), texture blending resolution equals geometric resolution. To get finer splat boundaries you must raise heightmap resolution (and therefore triangle count), not alpha resolution. Procedurally this means the layer alpha maps are literally the same grid as the heightmap and can be generated by the same per-pixel slope/height rules.
- There is no continuous LOD: cost scales directly with the number of visible sectors x (TerrainSectorSize^2 x 2) triangles x number of layers covering them. Culling is per sector (16x16 quads by default), so sector size is the only "LOD knob", plus DistanceFog draw distance and antiportals.
- The 2048x2048 ceiling is theoretical; practical generated maps should stay at 256x256 or 512x512 and use TerrainScale (128–256 UU quads) to cover large areas, matching the documented UT2004 norm (128–512).
- `Height=32768` as the initial fill plus Z=64 implies a generated G16 heightmap should centre its mean around 32768 and use the TerrainInfo's Z location to set sea level; vertical resolution is 65536 steps over Z*256 UU (at Z=64: 16384 UU range, 0.25 UU per step).

### Gaps
- Exact triangle count/draw-call cost per layer and how many layers the renderer composites per pass on DX8-class hardware: not found in any reachable source (a hint exists that an UT2004 community renderer later "increased the maximum number of layers rendered in a single pass", but the release notes I fetched were UT99's, not UT2004's).
- Whether lightmap-style (texel) lighting exists for terrain in any UE2 build: no source says so; everything observed (VertexLightMap, "shadows get blurrier with smaller heightmaps") points to per-vertex only. Treat "terrain lightmaps" as not available.
- What `DecoLayerOffset` default is and the default `MaxPerQuad`: the class dumps I fetched did not list the struct defaults.
- The UnrealWiki TerrainInfo page's statement that TerrainScale X/Y "give the size of a sector" conflicts with all other sources (quad size); UDN/UnCodex defaults make "quad size" the correct reading.

## KQ2. How good UT2004 outdoor maps were textured and built (hiding tiling, cliffs, alpha vs heightmap resolution, reference maps)

### Takeaway
Documented good practice is: a few tileable layers at different UScale/VScale and rotations alpha-blended over each other (which "generally don't tile badly"), side-projected or LayerRotation layers for steep faces, painting with the editor's Noise and Smooth brushes to roughen and soften alpha edges, hiding steep terrain and holes behind static-mesh rocks/cliffs from the stock cliff packages, grass as DecoLayers, antiportals under hills, and DistanceFog for draw distance. Hard numbers about alpha-vs-heightmap resolution are fixed by the engine (they must be equal); per-map breakdowns of ONS-Torlan/DM-Antalus were not found in any reachable source.

### Cited Findings
- Hiding tiling by layering: "Two textures with alpha blending on top of each other, with different scaling and rotation generally don't tile badly" — [UnrealWiki: Terrain](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/terrain.html)
- Steep faces: use `TextureMapAxis` XZ/YZ or `LayerRotation` so cliff layers "won't stretch like it normally would if projected from directly above" — [UDN EditingTerrainLayers](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/EditingTerrainLayers.html); the community restates this as duplicating a layer "for a total of three with each one applied on a different axis to apply textures on every surface possible without stretching" — [UT99.org thread](https://ut99.org/viewtopic.php?t=15108) (secondary)
- Cliff control via geometry: "make the slope less steep by having sets of vertices at certain points along the wall, which lets you have better control over cliff face texturing" — [UT99.org thread](https://ut99.org/viewtopic.php?t=15108) (secondary)
- Alpha-painting brushes: Painting (CTRL+LMB brighten / RMB darken the alpha), Smoothing ("everything in the radius of the brush will become smoother", removes paint artifacts), Noise ("random patches of the texture inside your brush"); Inner Radius / Outer Radius / Strength / Adjust shape the brush falloff — [UDN EditingTerrainLayers](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/EditingTerrainLayers.html)
- Tex Pan / Tex Rotate / Tex Scale tools adjust a layer's UV alignment interactively; Tex Scale can mirror a texture if dragged past zero — [UDN EditingTerrainLayers](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/EditingTerrainLayers.html)
- Stock UT2004 cliff/rock static-mesh packages: ArboreaLandscape (natural cliffs and rocks), AntalusStatic (rocks and cliffs), CB-Desert1 (canyon cliffs, rocks, pillars), IceCliffs (rock/snow walls) — [UT99.org thread](https://ut99.org/viewtopic.php?t=15108) (secondary, but package names are verifiable in the game); terrain texture packages: AbaddonTerrain, AlleriaTerrain (snow/rock), ArboreaTerrain (grass/rock/moss), BarrensTerrain (rock walls, sand, dry earth), cp_Evillandscape (dark rock), Terrain/Terrain2/Terrain_w — [UnrealWiki: Texture Package Reference (UT2004)](https://unrealarchive.org/wikis/unreal-wiki/UE2:Texture_Package_Reference_(UT2004).html)
- Remove unseen terrain: "Excess terrain should be removed using appropriate tools" (visibility tool) so hidden parts are not rendered — [UT99.org thread](https://ut99.org/viewtopic.php?t=15108) (secondary)
- Antiportals: "invisible volumes that blocked rendering behind terrain. Drop one under a hill, and boom: sudden smoothness" — [Ian Chapin, Fragging My Past](https://www.ianchapin.com/blog/ut2004-maps); practical tip: build the antiportal brush as simple and big as possible without the player entering it, and temporarily add it as a solid-coloured brush to check it does not poke through the terrain — [CEONSS: UT2004 Editor Tutorial Links & Special Tips](https://ceonss.net/viewtopic.php?t=887)
- Static mesh performance on outdoor maps: "All static meshes should have CastDynamicShadows set to false" and small meshes should not occlude — [Optimizing UE3 Levels (evilmrfrank), applied by the community to UE2 too](https://www.evilmrfrank.com/Optimizing.html) (secondary; UE3-focused page)
- Naming hazard: do not name a map terrain.ut2 or terrain2.ut2 because terrain.utx/terrain2.utx exist — [UDN AdditionalTerrainTips](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/AdditionalTerrainTips.html)
- Multiple terrains: "You can have as many TerrainInfo Actors as you want in a zone", they simply intersect — so multi-scale terrain (coarse far hills + fine play area) is possible — [UDN AdditionalTerrainTips](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/AdditionalTerrainTips.html); the wiki also notes "Supports multiple terrains of varying scales within single level" — [UnrealWiki: Terrain](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/terrain.html)
- The official UT2004 Special Edition / 3D Buzz VTM series covers terrain in order: Introduction to Terrain, Terrain Creation, Terrain Editing 1–2, Building into Terrain 1–2, DecoLayers 1–2, Terrain Properties 1–2, Antiportals How-to/Practical, Terrain Optimization (video list) — [CEONSS tips thread](https://ceonss.net/viewtopic.php?t=887); 3DBuzz's "Terrain Creation" (2 of 7) is mirrored on YouTube — [YouTube mirror](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=69xkqyP1IC0)
- Hourences' UE2-relevant tutorials are the vertex-lighting basics, texturing, Photoshop texture tricks, source photos for tiling textures, and a "Vertex Colors Texture Blending" tutorial for blending textures on a static mesh via vertex colours in UE2 (a cliff-mesh technique) — [Hourences tutorials index](http://www.hourences.com/tutorials/)
- ONS-Torlan is a stock Onslaught map "mostly symmetrical, however ... the terrain is slightly different on each side" — [Unreal Wiki (fandom) ONS-Torlan](https://unreal.fandom.com/wiki/ONS-Torlan) (no technical breakdown available)

### Inferences
- The engine-enforced "alpha map = heightmap resolution" rule means UT2004 mappers could not use high-res splat masks; crispness came instead from (a) layer textures at different tiling scales (a macro/low-tiling layer blending with detail layers), (b) Noise-brush speckling of alpha edges, and (c) static-mesh rocks on anything steep. A generator should reproduce exactly these three moves.
- Since steep terrain stretches top-projected layers, the proven procedural rule is: slope above ~45° -> switch to a side-projected (XZ/YZ or LayerRotation) rock layer, and/or scatter cliff static meshes aligned to the slope normal; the stock cliff packages are the reference assets.
- The 3D Buzz VTM module list ("Building into Terrain", "Terrain Optimization", antiportals) is a good proxy for the pro workflow: terrain first, buildings cut in with the visibility tool, then antiportals under major hills.

### Gaps
- No reachable source gave a per-map technical breakdown of ONS-Torlan, ONS-RedPlanet, or DM-Antalus (layer counts, heightmap sizes, scales). Suggested fallback: open the stock .ut2 files in UnrealEd and read TerrainInfo properties directly (HeightmapX/Y, Layers, TerrainScale).
- Angel Mapper's Terrain and Onslaught Mapping tutorials on ModDB are behind Cloudflare and not archived in Wayback; only search-snippet facts were available (uses AlleriaTerrain snow01AL, covers layers and DecoLayers).
- Hourences' "The Hows and Whys of Level Design" terrain chapter could not be read (PDF mirrors unreachable).

## KQ3. Procedural tools and script-side terrain access (Terragen/World Machine -> G16, G16 editors, UnrealScript functions, Unreal II differences)

### Takeaway
The import path is: external 8-bit grayscale BMP (or any P8/RGBA8) -> Texture Browser (mipmaps off) -> set as TerrainMap -> "Convert to G16"; keeping full 16-bit precision externally needs G16-aware tools (G16Ed, HMCS 2.0, which converts RAW-16 and others to Epic G16). At runtime UnrealScript exposes only `PokeTerrain(WorldLocation, Radius, MaxDepth)`; everything else (heightmap, alpha maps, deco density) must be authored as textures before the map is saved. Unreal II's editor is the same terrain system with the same TerrainInfo layout minus KFriction/KRestitution/LitDirectional/DisregardTerrainLighting/RandomYaw/DetailMode, plus `bNonColliding` and a global `DecoLayerDensity`, and it cannot create heightmaps in-editor.

### Cited Findings
- Import workflow: heightmap as "8 bit grayscale image where white is interpreted as the highest point", saved as BMP, power-of-two and square; import with mipmaps off; set TerrainInfo -> TerrainMap; right-click in the Terrains tab -> "convert to G16" to make it editable — [UnrealWiki: Creating A Terrain](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/creating-a-terrain.html); [UnrealWiki: Terrain](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/terrain.html)
- Terragen is explicitly named as a heightmap source for UnrealEd 3 terrain — [UnrealWiki: Terrain](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/terrain.html)
- "The Unreal Editor's native heightmap format is called G16, which will require an intermediary conversion from Raw-16 if the heightmap software does not natively support G16. A few tools exist for conversion to G16 including G16Ed and HMCS" — [Unreal Texture / UT3 tools page](http://www.unrealtexture.com/General/Website/Downloads/3DEditing/UT3Editor/Tools/ToolsMain.htm) (search snippet)
- HMCS 2.0 "converts between Unreal G16 Heightmaps and numerous other heightmap, bitmap and 3D formats", opens Epic G16 and RAW 16-bit files and saves Epic G16 — [LCS HMCS 2.0](http://www.lilchips.com/hmcs2.htm) (site did not respond to a direct fetch; description from search snippet)
- G16Ed: "the only graphics application that allows you to work with this format and keeps the 16-bit accuracy of the heightmap"; fractal noise creation, PSP-like UI; workflow is export the heightmap as BMP from UnrealEd, edit, re-import; originally hosted at http://homepage.ntlworld.com/martingbell/ut2003/ (dead) — [UnrealWiki: G16ed](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/g16ed.html)
- World Machine's Unreal export uses RAW16 from a Height Output device ("High Precision" = RAW16) — [World Machine: Export to Unreal Engine](https://help.world-machine.com/topic/export-to-unreal-engine/) (modern doc; the RAW16 -> G16 step is the UE2-specific part)
- In-editor Terrain Generator (Misc tab): "Steps" sets average height differences (examples 50 vs 5000), "Strength" sets the width of the hills (100 vs 1000 = "100 times wider"); it is buggy (asks for a selection even for whole heightmap, undo only inside selection, may need a Paint stroke at Strength 1 to refresh) — [UDN CreatingTerrain](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/CreatingTerrain.html)
- UnrealScript access: the "sole terrain manipulation function" is `native final function PokeTerrain( vector WorldLocation, int Radius, int MaxDepth );` — [ericdives UnCodex source](http://ericdives.com/UT2004-UnCodex/Source_engine/terraininfo.html); native const data includes `HeightmapX/Y`, `SectorsX/Y`, `Vertices`, `FaceNormals`, `ToWorld`/`ToHeightmap` matrices, `DecoLayerData`, `CollapsedLayers`, `VertexColors` — [kos.informatik](http://kos.informatik.uni-osnabrueck.de/download/utdocu/engine/terraininfo.html)
- Unreal II's Engine.TerrainInfo: `Layers[32]`, `DecoLayers`, `TerrainScale` default (64,64,64), `bNonColliding`, `Inverted`, global config `DecoLayerDensity` default 1.0, `bStaticLighting=true`; TerrainLayer has 10 members (Texture, AlphaMap, UScale, VScale, UPan, VPan, TextureMapAxis, TextureRotation, LayerRotation, TerrainMatrix — no KFriction/KRestitution); DecorationLayer has 13 members (ShowOnTerrain, ScaleMap, DensityMap, ColorMap, StaticMesh, ScaleMultiplier, FadeoutRadius, DensityMultiplier, MaxPerQuad, Seed, AlignToTerrain, DrawOrder, ShowOnInvisibleTerrain — no LitDirectional, DisregardTerrainLighting, RandomYaw, DetailMode) — [greatemerald UnCodex: Unreal II Engine.TerrainInfo](https://greatemerald.eu/uncodex/U2/engine/terraininfo.html)
- Unreal II editor specifics: "Unreal II's version of UnrealEd is not binary-compatible with UT2003's editor"; "The terrain editor in Unreal II works exactly like in UnrealEd 3.0 from UT2003/2004, with one exception: the heightmap cannot be created 'on the fly' in the editor—you have to make one outside"; Convert-to-16-bit "does not seem to always work ... BMP seems not to work" (PCX recommended), workaround: create the heightmap in UT2003/2004, export it, import into Unreal II; sculpt tools are disabled until conversion succeeds; "It might crash randomly ... Save often"; imported heightmaps often sit far too low (fix with Vertex Edit then Flatten); first layer alpha must be white, later layers A=255 — [OldUnreal: Unreal 2 editor thread](https://www.oldunreal.com/phpBB3/viewtopic.php?t=4351)
- Unreal II level designers: Glen Dahlgren (Marsh, Hell, Acheron, Severnaya, Obolus, Dorian Gray), Scott Dalton (Tutorial, Sanctuary, Sulferon, Janus, Avalon) — [Unreal Wiki (fandom): Unreal II](https://unreal.fandom.com/wiki/Unreal_II:_The_Awakening)

### Inferences
- For a script-driven generator the engine offers no API to write heightmaps or alpha maps; the practical route is to write the textures (G16 heightmap, RGBA8 alpha maps, RGBA8 density/scale/color maps) to disk and import them, or write the texture objects directly into the .ut2/.un2 package (which is what the user's pipeline already does). `PokeTerrain` is only useful for runtime craters.
- Because the Unreal II struct lacks RandomYaw and LitDirectional, grass DecoLayers in Unreal II will all face the same way and take terrain vertex lighting; variety must come from several DecoLayers with different meshes/seeds, or from pre-rotated mesh variants.
- Because Unreal II lacks DetailMode on DecoLayers but has a global `DecoLayerDensity` config, density scaling is user-global; the generator should assume DensityMultiplier is tuned for density 1.0.
- G16 "BMP" is a non-standard container; if writing it from a script, verify against a heightmap exported from UnrealEd rather than trusting generic BMP writers (the user's existing G16 writer should be validated this way).

### Gaps
- The exact byte layout of Epic's G16 .bmp (header fields) was not found in any reachable source; only the fact that it is "not a valid BMP" is documented.
- Whether Unreal II's TerrainInfo has any extra native lighting/shadow variables beyond the UT2004 set could not be confirmed from the UnCodex summary (it lists only the script-visible members above).
- No UE2-era slope/height auto-painting tool was found (see KQ5).

## KQ4. Rendering limits that matter: fixed-function/DX8 era, materials on layers, detail textures, texture memory

### Takeaway
A layer's `Texture` is typed `Material`, so Shaders, Combiners and TexModifiers are legal layer inputs, and the UE2 Shader class offers Diffuse/Opacity/Specular/SpecularityMask/SelfIllumination/Detail channels — but there are no normal maps in UE2, lighting is per-vertex, and terrain already runs a multi-layer PS1.4 path, so every documented good-looking UE2 terrain uses plain tiling textures (optionally with a Detail texture) rather than shaders. No source quantified layer or texture-memory budgets.

### Cited Findings
- Layer input type: `var() Material Texture;` in TerrainLayer, and UDN: "The Texture can be any texture or material" — [ericdives UnCodex source](http://ericdives.com/UT2004-UnCodex/Source_engine/terraininfo.html); [UDN EditingTerrainLayers](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/EditingTerrainLayers.html)
- UE2 Shader capabilities: "Shaders can change a material's opacity mask, apply a specularity and/or light map and a detail material"; the specularity channel "is a material that is applied over the diffuse channel to create shining effects"; "unlike Combiners or TexModifiers, shader changes to a material will be lost if the shader is used in another shader or combiner" — [UnrealWiki: UE2:Shader (UT2004)](https://wiki.beyondunreal.com/UE2:Shader_(UT2004)) (search snippet; page itself 403 to fetch)
- Texture class has `Material Detail` ("Detail material to apply") and `float DetailScale`; `LODSet` includes a dedicated `Terrain` LOD set — [UnrealWiki: Texture](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/texture.html)
- UE2 material toolbox available for layers: TexScaler (UScale/VScale/UOffset/VOffset), TexOscillator, Combiner (e.g. CO_Multiply, CO_Alphablend_With_Mask), Shader with Diffuse/Opacity/TwoSided/SurfaceType; "No real-time reflection ... we'll use predefined environment maps" — [UnrealWiki: Creating A Material](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/creating-a-material.html)
- Terrain uses pixel shader 1.4 (4 instructions vs 7 for PS1.1) — [Beyond3D forum](https://forum.beyond3d.com/threads/ut4-t-l-or-cpu.9376/) (secondary)
- The engine's only displacement/bump substitute in this era is the "Fake DisplacementMaps" trick: a terrain with tiny Z scale (0.05) used as a 3D-tiled floor; UDN notes "In one of the later builds, there might be added support for real DisplacementMaps or BumpMaps" (i.e. none existed) — [UDN EditingTerrainLayers](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/EditingTerrainLayers.html)
- UT2004 is mostly CPU-limited; biggest stock texture mentioned is 2048x2048 (CavernTextures), terrain packs are not described by size — [Anandtech/Neowin snippets via search](https://www.neowin.net/forum/topic/141152-conclusion-graphics-cards-for-ut2004/); [UnrealWiki: Texture Package Reference](https://unrealarchive.org/wikis/unreal-wiki/UE2:Texture_Package_Reference_(UT2004).html)
- UT2004 custom textures need a SurfaceType set in the Material properties for footstep sounds — [Hourences tutorials (texturing)](http://www.hourences.com/tutorials/) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- Since a Shader as a layer input is legal but undocumented for terrain, treat specular/detail-shader layers as "possible, test per build" — the safe proven path is a plain Texture with the Texture's own `Detail`/`DetailScale` set (detail textures are a Texture property, so they ride along into terrain layers automatically if the renderer honours them there; unverified).
- The absence of normal maps and per-vertex lighting means terrain "AO" must be faked either by the baked vertex lighting from SunLight (sharper with denser heightmaps), by a darker painted "shadow/dirt" alpha layer under cliffs and in creases (a slope/curvature-driven layer the generator can produce), or by DecoLayer ColorMaps.

### Gaps
- No source confirms whether a Shader's Specular or Detail channel actually renders when used as a terrain layer in UT2004/Unreal II; needs an in-editor test.
- No documentation of terrain texture memory budgets or recommended layer texture resolution for UE2 was found; stock packs are the only reference (open AlleriaTerrain/ArboreaTerrain in the Texture Browser to read sizes).
- The UDN "ExampleMapsAdvLighting" page (docs.unrealengine.com/udk/Two) was unreachable.

## KQ5. Automatic painting by slope/height, detail textures, and baked "ambient occlusion"-like lighting

### Takeaway
No UE2-era tool or editor feature for automatic slope/height-based layer painting was found; UDN only describes the manual recipe (e.g. a snow layer whose alpha is white "at the places where there are high peaks in your TerrainMap"), which a generator can implement directly because alpha maps share the heightmap grid. Baked terrain lighting is SunLight + normal lights resolved per vertex and stored as vertex colours; its softness depends on heightmap density, so "AO-like" darkening in creases comes from the sun's own vertex shadowing plus hand-painted dark layers.

### Cited Findings
- Manual height-based painting recipe: "to create snow tops, use a new layer a snowy Texture, and use an AlphaMap that is black everywhere, except at the places where there are high peaks in your TerrainMap" — [UDN EditingTerrainLayers](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/EditingTerrainLayers.html)
- Alpha maps are plain RGBA8 textures in MyLevel that can be made "with a painting program and import[ed] into the Texture Browser" — [UDN EditingTerrainLayers](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/EditingTerrainLayers.html)
- The editor's Noise brush on alpha maps "create[s] random patches of the texture inside your brush" and Smooth softens alpha edges — [UDN EditingTerrainLayers](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/EditingTerrainLayers.html)
- Baked lighting: terrain is lit by SunLight(s) and normal lights after "rebuild the lighting"; sun rays are parallel and blocked by any non-Fake-Backdrop wall; multiple coloured suns are allowed — [UDN AdditionalTerrainTips](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/AdditionalTerrainTips.html)
- Shadow resolution is tied to terrain vertex density and scale ("Shadows become more unsharp when the heightmap gets smaller ... also affected by how big the scale of the terrain is"); objects smaller than a quad cast no visible shadow — [UnrealWiki: Using The Terrain Tool](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/using-the-terrain-tool.html)
- DecoLayer meshes inherit the lighting of the quad they sit on unless DisregardTerrainLighting=1 (fullbright) or LitDirectional=1 (sun) — [UDN CreatingDecoLayers](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/CreatingDecoLayers.html)
- The `Color` terrain tool paints vertex colours onto DecoLayers only ("This tool can't be used on the TerrainMap, it's for DecoLayers only") — [UDN EditingTerrainMaps](http://web.archive.org/web/2004id_/http://udn.epicgames.com/Two/EditingTerrainMaps.html)
- Texture `Detail` + `DetailScale` exist as Texture properties — [UnrealWiki: Texture](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/texture.html)

### Inferences
- A generator should compute slope (from the heightmap gradient), height bands and curvature (Laplacian) on the heightmap grid and write them straight into same-size RGBA8 alpha maps: steep -> side-projected rock layer; high -> snow; concave/low -> dirt or dark "grime" layer; plus a noise-dithered edge to mimic the Noise brush. This is exactly the manual UDN recipe, automated.
- Baked AO substitute: rebuild lighting with a SunLight at a low-ish elevation plus a bluish, dim second sun from the opposite side (multiple coloured suns are supported) to get crease darkening at vertex resolution; expect blur at 256x256/64-UU density and sharper results if quads are denser.
- Since lighting is per vertex and DecoLayers read the quad's light, grass placed in sun-shadowed creases will darken automatically, which helps sell the AO effect without LitDirectional.

### Gaps
- No UE2 slope/height auto-painting tool, script, or editor command was found in any reachable source; the capability simply did not exist in the stock editor (the only automatic tool is the random Terrain Generator for height).
- Whether Detail textures render on terrain layers in UT2004/Unreal II is undocumented.
- The precise storage of baked terrain light (VertexLightMap texture vs. VertexColors array) and whether it can be pre-written by a tool to fake AO was not documented beyond the variable names.
