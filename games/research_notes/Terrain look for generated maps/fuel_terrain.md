# FUEL (Asobo Studio, 2009) — terrain technology of the 14,400 km² procedural world

Scope note for the report writer: Asobo never published a technical paper, GDC/Develop talk or postmortem on FUEL's terrain (none found after ~15 searches covering Gamasutra/Game Developer, GDC Vault, Digital Foundry, Edge, Develop). The only first-party statements are (a) a Codemasters press release, (b) two early-2009 interviews with Codemasters executive producer David Brickley (VideoGamer, Rock Paper Shotgun), (c) a Codemasters "Behind-the-Scenes with Asobo Tech" video with Asobo co-founders David Dedeine and Sebastian Wloch (YouTube; no transcript obtainable), and (d) a Dec-2008 GamesRadar preview. Everything about internals (heightmap resolution, chunking, material rules, LOD) comes from reviewer inference (mainly programmer Shamus Young's 2009 series) or from fan reverse-engineering (widberg's FMTK/bff/decompilation). Each item below is tagged STATED (Asobo/Codemasters), INFERRED (reviewer), or RE (reverse-engineering community).

## Key question 1 — Source data and generation: real-world DEM? compression/amplification? procedural layers? roads?

### Takeaway
Codemasters/Asobo stated the world is built from satellite data of the western USA and then generated in real time by deterministic ("same result every time") procedural rules rather than stored, with artists adjusting the data for gameplay; a stored version would have needed "about four Blu-rays" (Brickley) or ~40–45 DVDs (Shamus Young's arithmetic). No source gives the base heightmap resolution, the DEM dataset, the noise/erosion stack or the road algorithm; the best account of roads is Shamus Young's observational analysis (hierarchical highway → secondary → dirt networks, emergent bends, no overpasses or cloverleafs, texture seams at junctions).

### Cited Findings
- STATED (Codemasters EP David Brickley, Feb 2009): the engine "generates data on the fly instead of just loading and decompressing it"; multicore hardware lets it "procedurally create high quality environments, in real-time" with a 40 km draw distance; "Generated doesn't mean random though, it's the same result every time"; locations were based on satellite data with artistic adjustments for gameplay — [PCG Wiki: FUEL (summarising the RPS interview)](http://pcg.wikidot.com/pcg-games:fuel); original: [Rock Paper Shotgun, "Interview: Codies on FUEL", 24 Feb 2009](http://www.rockpapershotgun.com/2009/02/24/interview-codies-on-fuel/) (could not be fetched directly; quotes come via the PCG wiki's citation).
- STATED (Brickley, VideoGamer): the landscape tech is "using satellite data and fully recreating it in real time"; "if you were to build it in a traditional manner it would fill about four Blu-rays. A gargantuan amount of data, just enormous"; Asobo spent "four years trying to figure out" the landscape generation; the goal was "a race experience generated according to an algorithmic rule set, which is of a standard that is indistinguishable from one that's been done by hand"; the world fits on disc "in an extraordinarily small amount of space, without sacrificing" — [VideoGamer: FUEL Interview](https://www.videogamer.com/previews/fuel-interview/).
- STATED (Codemasters press release, 2008): playfield "over 5,000 square miles"; proprietary Asobo engine "over four years in development"; world includes a tsunami-wrecked Pacific coast, Nevada wastelands, Grand Canyon, snow-capped mountains, thick forests, arid deserts, abandoned lakeside resorts; Barry Jafrato (Codemasters): "The technology Asobo Studios has developed to build FUEL is ground-breaking" — [Spong press release](https://spong.com/press_release/16061/Codemasters-Signs-Asobo-Studios-Fuel-Reveals-Biggest-Racing-Environment-Ever).
- STATED (Asobo site): Guinness record "Largest playable area in a console game" (14,000 km² / 5,500 sq mi); Asobo "invested 4 years developing a proprietary engine" — [Asobo Studio: FUEL](https://www.asobostudio.com/games/fuel).
- PREVIEW (GamesRadar, Steve Hogarty, 20 Dec 2008): the world is "an unbound chunk of west America, procedurally generated using satellite imagery and laced with 11,000 miles of road"; 5,000 sq mi of tarmac and dirt — [GamesRadar: FUEL](https://www.gamesradar.com/fuel-2/) (direct fetch returned truncated content; sentence confirmed via search snippet and via Wikipedia citation [6]).
- Wikipedia synthesis: "Fuel uses procedural generation with a fixed seed, as the map likely wouldn't have fitted on the game disk if stored traditionally"; map "over 14,400 km²" (size of Connecticut); evolved from Asobo's 2005-announced "Grand Raid Offroad" — [Wikipedia: Fuel (video game)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_(video_game)).
- Guinness: 5,560 sq mi (14,400 km²) of accessible terrain with rivers, deserts, forests and mountains, demonstrated in London on 20 May 2009 — [Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/110300-largest-playable-area-in-a-racing-videogame).
- INFERRED (Shamus Young, The Escapist, 2009): at 1 m spacing (resolution "estimated from observable glitches") 14,000 km² = 16.384 billion height points ≈ "196 gigabytes", "a stack of around forty DVD disks just to hold the terrain", ~45 DVDs with roads/buildings; "the gameworld is built on the fly as you drive around", deterministically; "There are a smattering of hand-made courses and buildings added to the world to serve as set pieces and landmarks, and the rest comes from the Asobo gameworld-o-matic" — [The Escapist: The Future is Procedural](https://www.escapistmagazine.com/the-future-is-procedural/).
- INFERRED (Shamus Young, "Fuel: Terrain"): the terrain is probably a "large-scale, super-low resolution heightmap" (his guess: points ~100 m apart, ~1280×1280 ≈ 1.6 M points) that defines mountain ranges and water bodies, plus small repeating detail patches layered on top (analogy: two 32×32 brick textures blended to hide tiling); he spotted recurring landforms ("a horseshoe-shaped ridge" reappearing kilometres away at a different scale/texture), i.e. fine-detail relief is recycled, and vegetation/roads mask the repetition; he frames the whole as "~200GB of world generated from a gigabyte or so of data" — [Twenty Sided: Fuel: Terrain](https://www.shamusyoung.com/twentysidedtale/?p=5267).
- INFERRED (Shamus Young, "Fuel: Roads"): hierarchical network — "backbone highways that cut across the zone. Between those, it will form a loose grid of evenly spaced secondary roads. And between those it makes a network of dirt roads"; in the perfectly flat White Flats zone roads still meet at varied angles, so "the bends in the road are emergent" from the algorithm; no overpasses or cloverleaf junctions anywhere (placing those on arbitrary topography is something "artists make by hand in games"; FUEL is the first to attempt roads by code); road textures carry lane stripes that cannot be stretched at a splice, so FUEL handles junctions by "blurring and fading the textures where they meet", leaving visible seams, "staircase-shaped seams" and a misplaced guardrail at one highway merge — [Twenty Sided: Fuel: Roads](https://www.shamusyoung.com/twentysidedtale/?p=5237).
- INFERRED (Shamus Young, Introduction): three procedural systems — terrain generation, road generation, world-object population; world "~114 km on a side" — [Twenty Sided: FUEL: Introduction](https://www.shamusyoung.com/twentysidedtale/?p=5094).
- Observation (2016 blog): procedural flaws include "improbable road placements over mountains" and buildings that spawned inconsistently between playthroughs; terrain keeps consistent detail everywhere, "no blank areas", "you can often see many kilometres far"; explains the approach as Minecraft-style fixed-seed generation ("packing the parts of a chair and instructions... takes less space than packing an entire assembled chair") — [Sword and Torch Inn](http://swordandtorchinn.blogspot.com/2016/08/taking-look-at-fuel-asobos-only-game.html).
- STATED (Codemasters, via VideoGamer): a former GRID/DIRT track designer refined procedurally generated roads by hand for drama — e.g. where "the drop is 20 feet from your car, so you'd miss that 'whoa' experience. All of a sudden the road extends to the edge of the mountain" — iterating on builds that arrived "two days later" — [VideoGamer](https://www.videogamer.com/previews/fuel-interview/).

### Inferences
- "Satellite data" in 2008–09 press language most plausibly means low-resolution elevation data (DEM) of the western US used as the macro heightmap, with sub-100 m relief synthesised procedurally; this matches Shamus's recycled micro-landforms and the "four Blu-rays if stored" framing (what is stored is the coarse base plus rules). The dataset (SRTM/USGS NED) and resolution are unconfirmed.
- "Generates instead of decompressing" + "same result every time" = deterministic amplification of coarse data at runtime (seeded, position-keyed detail synthesis), not pre-baked chunks; Brickley's emphasis on multicore CPUs suggests this ran on 360/PS3 CPU cores.
- Zones are hand-partitioned regions (19 named; see Q3) with per-zone road density and biome parameters — "backbone highways that cut across the zone" implies the road generator works per zone.
- The road generator appears to route a graph over the heightfield between seed points, flatten terrain under the ribbon and splice textures at junctions; grade-separated junctions were avoided entirely (a scope decision).

### Gaps
- No Asobo statement on which DEM/satellite dataset was used, its ground resolution, or how it was compressed; "four Blu-rays"/"45 DVDs" are what-if figures, not shipped-data measurements.
- No source on the detail stack (noise type, erosion, terracing) or on how the base DEM was amplified; Shamus's 1280×1280/100 m figure is explicit speculation.
- No source on how roads were carved into the heightfield or how routes were chosen; "11,000 miles of road" is an unsourced preview figure.
- The Dedeine/Wloch video ([YouTube VNx-H32RoOc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VNx-H32RoOc)) could not be transcribed with the available tools; it is the most likely place Asobo explained the pipeline on camera.

## Key question 2 — Texturing by rules: slope/height/biome material assignment, detail textures, avoiding repetition

### Takeaway
No Asobo or reviewer source documents FUEL's material-assignment rules. Sourced: reviewers saw decent blur-filtered ground detail but low-resolution textures and heavy prop repetition; Shamus inferred a two-level scheme (coarse heightmap + repeated small detail patches, with vegetation and roads breaking the repetition). Slope/height texturing rules have to be borrowed from documented contemporaries (Q6).

### Cited Findings
- INFERRED (Shamus Young): detail is layered — base heightmap plus repeated small patches; repetition is disguised by different vegetation, foliage and road patterns laid over the same topography; "you probably won't notice this unless you were looking for it. Which, now that I've pointed it out, you will be." — [Twenty Sided: Fuel: Terrain](https://www.shamusyoung.com/twentysidedtale/?p=5267).
- REVIEW (bit-tech, PS3): some ground textures "appear detailed (particularly with blur filtering applied)" while "some in-game objects are incredibly disappointing even to a casual inspection"; "distinct lack of AA" on PS3 — [bit-tech FUEL review p.3](https://bit-tech.net/reviews/gaming/playstation/fuel-review/3/).
- REVIEW (2009 reviews via search snippets): "textures were extremely low resolution and there was a lot of texture and object pop-in"; "the same damn broken tree again and again" — [IndianVideoGamer review](https://www.indianvideogamer.com/reviews/review-fuel/2769/2), [Game Informer review](https://gameinformer.com/games/fuel/b/xbox360/archive/2009/09/22/review) (snippets only; pages not fetched).
- REVIEW (GameWatcher, PC): "The draw distance is superb"; terrain ranges "from forest greenery and muddy mountain roads to barren sand dunes and treacherous snow and ice plains" but reads as "sterile", "vast stretches of admittedly rather pretty grass, trees and dirt tracks" — [GameWatcher FUEL review](https://www.gamewatcher.com/fuel-2009/review).
- INFERRED (Shamus Young, "Final Thoughts"): prop repetition is the main failure — "the same pre-fab building, placed in many different locations" with "green tractor, elevated propane tank, windmill, ramp, carport, etc. Everything in exactly the same arrangement"; nonetheless the biomes (mountains, deserts, forests, canyons, coastlines), palette and day/night cycle were visually stunning and the whole an "amazing technological achievement" — [Twenty Sided: Fuel: Final Thoughts](https://www.shamusyoung.com/twentysidedtale/?p=5356).

### Inferences
- FUEL's variety came from the macro level (real-world-shaped mountains and coasts, 19 zones, 7 biomes, weather, lighting), not from material detail; close-up texturing was ordinary 2009 console quality. Lesson for a UE2 modder: spend effort on large-shape variety and biome colour; near-field repetition is what reviewers noticed.
- Because the world is generated per position deterministically, materials were almost certainly assigned by rules evaluated from terrain attributes (slope, height, zone/biome) rather than hand-painted alpha maps — painting 14,400 km² is infeasible — but no source states this.

### Gaps
- No documentation of FUEL's texturing rules, number of ground layers, detail-texture resolution or blend method.
- No Digital Foundry / Eurogamer tech analysis of FUEL exists (Digital Foundry searches return only MSFS material).

## Key question 3 — Vegetation and prop placement rules; biomes and weather

### Takeaway
Sourced: 19 named zones and (per reviewers) 7 main biomes; dynamic weather with tornadoes, sandstorms, thunderstorms, lightning, blizzards, torrential rain and an accelerated day/night cycle; vegetation and buildings are placed procedurally with heavy prefab reuse and visible tree pop-in. The placement rules are not documented by Asobo; Shamus Young's essay describes the class of rules such a system uses (trees avoid steep slopes, prefer soil over rock, canopy suppresses undergrowth, humans paint "forest here" regions) as general method, not confirmed FUEL internals.

### Cited Findings
- STATED (Codemasters): "19 environmental regions"; "some of the most dramatic weather effects ever seen in a racing game"; crossing the map takes "over 3 hours straight" — [Gaming Nexus](https://www.gamingnexus.com/News/12643/Fill-er-up--FUEL-is-Biggest-Console-Game-of-All-Time).
- STATED (Codemasters press release): "a dynamic weather system with full day and night transitions, brilliant sunshine, torrential rain and everything in between, plus destructive tornados, sandstorms, thunderstorms, lightning strikes, and blizzards requiring mid-race strategy changes"; a tornado was shown ripping through a building with debris forcing drivers to reroute — [MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/after-dirt-and-grid-codemasters-turns-to-asobo-for-fuel/), [Spong](https://spong.com/press_release/16061/Codemasters-Signs-Asobo-Studios-Fuel-Reveals-Biggest-Racing-Environment-Ever).
- Wikipedia: "occasional tornadoes and sandstorms, as well as an accelerated day-night cycle"; snowy distant mountains — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_(video_game)).
- Zone list (fan wiki, via search snippet; page returned HTTP 402 on fetch): Big Cauldron's Edge, Big Cleave, Blacktrail Pass, Dead Lady's Lake, Drownington Cove, Dry Rigs, Dustbowl City, Firemoor Fields, Offshore Shack, Pinwheels Ridge, Rainier Peak, Redrock Bluffs, Roughwood Hill, Smokester's Crater, Stargazers' Heights, The Ashtray, The Hangar, The White Flats, Tsunami Reef — [FUEL Game Wiki: Zones](https://fuel.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Zones).
- Biomes (reviews): "7 main biomes" — "dense green forests, vast plains, mountains, scorched wilderness, abandoned and partially ruined cities, deserts" — [nicksnv Substack review](https://nicksnv.substack.com/p/fuel-game-review-fuel); another observer lists forests, tundra, sandy deserts, rocky deserts, salt flats, dunes, mountain ranges, burnt-out forests — [Sword and Torch Inn](http://swordandtorchinn.blogspot.com/2016/08/taking-look-at-fuel-asobos-only-game.html).
- REVIEW (bit-tech): "your horizon is spoiled by trees that ungraciously materialise in front of you" (compared to Oblivion) — [bit-tech](https://bit-tech.net/reviews/gaming/playstation/fuel-review/3/).
- INFERRED/GENERAL (Shamus Young, "Defining Procedural"): artists designate regions ("forest over this area") and the algorithm distributes individuals under natural rules — trees avoid steep hills, dense forest suppresses undergrowth, vegetation prefers soil over rock, grass grows where trees/shrubs don't, species have elevation/moisture preferences; "The more specific you want the content, the more it needs to be built by a human being"; procedural ≠ random — [Twenty Sided: FUEL: Defining Procedural](https://www.shamusyoung.com/twentysidedtale/?p=5134). (Description of the technique class, not confirmed FUEL code.)
- INFERRED (Shamus, Final Thoughts): buildings are compound-level prefabs stamped at many locations with identical sub-arrangements rather than per-prop scatter — [Twenty Sided: Final Thoughts](https://www.shamusyoung.com/twentysidedtale/?p=5356).
- Observation: building spawns were inconsistent between playthroughs for at least one player — [Sword and Torch Inn](http://swordandtorchinn.blogspot.com/2016/08/taking-look-at-fuel-asobos-only-game.html). This conflicts with Brickley's "same result every time" and may reflect streaming/LOD timing rather than non-determinism.

### Inferences
- Biome identity is zone-driven (hand-defined regions with a palette, vegetation set and weather set); the per-instance scatter is algorithmic. The variety players praised came from zone-level art direction (colour, sky, weather, macro landform), not from clever scatter.
- Prefab compounds (a whole farmstead as one unit) are cheap to author and place but were the single most noticed repetition artefact; a modder should at least randomise prefab sub-element presence and rotation.

### Gaps
- No Asobo source on vegetation density rules, slope limits, species-per-biome tables, or how biome boundaries were blended.
- No source confirming how weather was tied to biome (e.g. blizzards only in mountain zones).

## Key question 4 — Streaming and LOD: chunk sizes, runtime vs offline, memory budget, view distance

### Takeaway
Stated: generation happens at runtime ("on the fly"), seamlessly with no loading while driving, with a 40 km draw distance where the visible horizon is real drivable land; loading screens appear only on vehicle reset/crash recovery and at session start (PS3 install ~15 min). Nothing is published on chunk sizes, LOD scheme or 360/PS3 memory budget; the shipped data format (Zouna "BigFile"/DPC archives with Asobo's LZ variant) is documented only by the modding community.

### Cited Findings
- STATED (Brickley): 40 km draw distance; the horizon is not faked — what you see where land meets sky "is a piece of earth you'll be able to drive to"; environments created "in real-time" using parallel processing — [PCG Wiki: FUEL](http://pcg.wikidot.com/pcg-games:fuel), [VideoGamer](https://www.videogamer.com/previews/fuel-interview/).
- Wikipedia: free-roam driving anywhere "without incurring loading times", but crashing or using the reset function "does invoke a loading screen" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_(video_game)).
- REVIEW (bit-tech, PS3): no in-game loading while traversing the 5,560 sq mi world, but long pre-game loading and a ~15-minute PS3 install; heavy tree pop-in — [bit-tech](https://bit-tech.net/reviews/gaming/playstation/fuel-review/3/).
- REVIEW (GameWatcher, PC): the whole map "can be traversed seamlessly without a single loading time" — [GameWatcher](https://www.gamewatcher.com/fuel-2009/review).
- INFERRED (Shamus Young): the engine recreates areas on demand rather than storing the map, which is what makes a seamless 5,000+ sq mi world feasible — [Twenty Sided: Final Thoughts](https://www.shamusyoung.com/twentysidedtale/?p=5356).
- RE: FUEL runs on Asobo's Zouna engine (a.k.a. ACE, "Asobo Conception Engine", first used in FUEL per MobyGames), descended from Kalisto Entertainment's late-1990s engine; "With Fuel (2009), Zouna evolved into an engine capable of large open worlds", later adapted for MSFS 2020/2024 world streaming — [PCGamingWiki: Engine:Zouna](https://www.pcgamingwiki.com/wiki/Engine:Zouna) (search snippet; direct fetch 403), [MobyGames: ACE engine](https://www.mobygames.com/group/15595/3d-engine-ace-asobo-conception-engine/).
- RE: shipped assets are in Zouna BigFile/DPC archives (FUEL versions v1.381.64–67.09); compression is a byte-aligned sliding-window LZ variant ("LZRS") with 32-bit control flags, reimplemented in Rust; tools: bff, dpc, ImZouna (ImHex patterns), fmt_fuel (Noesis), blender_fuel, 010 Editor templates — [widberg/fmtk wiki: Asobo LZ Compression](https://github.com/widberg/fmtk/wiki/Asobo-LZ-Compression), [widberg/dpc](https://github.com/widberg/dpc), [widberg/bff](https://github.com/widberg/bff), [Zouna Modding Resources](https://github-wiki-see.page/m/widberg/fmtk/wiki/Zouna-Modding-Resources).
- RE: a FUEL decompilation project exists (CMake, DirectX SDK Aug 2008, shared Ghidra server), analysing `libfuel.obj` — [widberg/FUELDecompilation](https://github.com/widberg/FUELDecompilation).
- Lineage: Asobo "adapted Asobo's procedural game engine to work with planet-scale projects" for MSFS 2020, tech "developed for the 2009 game Fuel" — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/microsoft-flight-simulator-2020-redefining-game-production); Lionel Fuentes (Asobo technical director) presented MSFS's hybrid procedural/real-world-data terrain at GDC 2022, "Designing the Terrain System of Flight Simulator: Representing the Earth" (DEM for ground height, aerial/satellite imagery, vector data) — [Asobo at GDC 2022](https://www.asobostudio.com/news/asobo-gdc-2022); slides PDF (too large to fetch here): https://www.asobostudio.com/files/inline-images/Designing_Terrain_System_Fuentes_Lionel.pdf.

### Inferences
- A 40 km horizon over real terrain on 2009 consoles implies continuous LOD (quadtree/clipmap-style) of the coarse heightfield, with procedural detail (fine relief, textures, vegetation) synthesised only in rings near the camera — consistent with the tree pop-in and with "generate instead of decompress". Not confirmed by Asobo.
- The reset/crash loading screen suggests that teleport-like jumps force full regeneration of the near-field cache, while continuous driving lets generation keep pace.
- The MSFS GDC 2022 talk is the closest Asobo has come to publishing the FUEL-lineage method; cite it as the descendant, not as FUEL fact.

### Gaps
- No chunk/tile size, LOD ring distances, vertex density or RAM budget for 360/PS3 found anywhere.
- No Digital Foundry analysis of FUEL exists; the 40 km figure is a publisher statement, not measured.
- The decompilation/FMTK community has not (in fetched pages) documented the terrain/world resource classes; the Ghidra project and the "Chum World wiki" referenced by FMTK are the places to look next.

## Key question 6 — Comparable documented systems worth borrowing (brief)

### Takeaway
Far Cry 2 (Ubisoft Montreal, 2008, ~50 km²) and Just Cause 2 (Avalanche, 2010, 32×32 km) are the documented contemporaries: Far Cry 2 built tools where artists place rules (type of tree, road splines) and terrain/vegetation re-flow automatically; Just Cause 2 used 33×33-vertex terrain cells, 5×5 cells at full detail around the player, LOD chunks merging 4×4…32×32 cells, procedural shader splatting by painted masks, spline/quad decals for roads and mask-driven mesh decoration.

### Cited Findings
- Far Cry 2 (Dominic Guay, MIGS 2008): world ~50 km²; artists "build not a random tree, but a type of tree" via parameters; level designers "move roads around" and the terrain "smooth[s] in according to how the rules were designed, and the vegetation will move and regrow"; a purely algorithmic sky was "a total failure", replaced by a procedural skybox with artistic oversight; ~3 years for the vegetation pipeline after an 8-month prototype; 15× more effort on environment tools than on the engine — [Game Developer: MIGS: Far Cry 2's Guay on procedural content](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/migs-i-far-cry-2-i-s-guay-on-the-importance-of-procedural-content); GDC 2008 session — [GDC Vault: Procedural Data Generation in FAR CRY 2](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/355/Procedural-Data-Generation-in-FAR).
- Far Cry 2 production goal: "We literally needed to be able to build a square kilometer of the game in one day, and then be prepared to throw it away the next day"; the proof-of-concept "included all the terrain, dynamic vegetation, roads, structures, AI, and gameplay" — [Game Developer: Behind the Scenes of Far Cry 2](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/in-depth-behind-the-scenes-of-i-far-cry-2-i-).
- Just Cause 2 (Avalanche, Emil Persson et al.): 32×32 km = 1,024 km²; terrain cells of 33×33 vertices, 5×5 cells around the player at full detail, chunks of 4×4/8×8/16×16/32×32 cells for LOD; "procedural shader splatting with arbitrary shaders splatted according to painted masks", spline and quad decals for roads, terrain decoration (trees, rocks) placed by masks; floating-point precision became a problem at that size; 50,000 m view distance handled via depth-buffer tricks — [GameDev Pensieve: 3D landscape example](https://www.gamedevpensieve.com/graphics/3d/3d_landscape/3d_landscape_example), [SlideServe: Creating Vast Game Worlds (Persson)](https://www.slideserve.com/tanek/creating-vast-game-worlds), [ACM SIGGRAPH 2012 course PDF](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/2343045.2343089).
- MSFS (FUEL's descendant at Asobo): terrain from DEM (ground height), satellite/aerial imagery and vector data in a hybrid procedural + real-data design (Lionel Fuentes, GDC 2022) — [Asobo at GDC 2022](https://www.asobostudio.com/news/asobo-gdc-2022).

### Inferences
- For a UE2 modder (256×256-cell heightmaps, alpha-blended layers, static meshes, no streaming), the FUEL ideas that transfer offline: (1) start the macro shape from real DEM tiles (free SRTM/USGS data) and amplify with seeded noise so landforms read as "real"; (2) generate the alpha layer maps from slope/height/biome rules instead of painting them (JC2's "splat by mask", with the masks produced procedurally); (3) generate roads as a hierarchical network (highway → secondary → dirt) routed over the heightfield, flatten under the ribbon and splat a road layer; skip grade-separated junctions as FUEL did; (4) scatter vegetation and props by rules (slope limit, biome, canopy-suppresses-undergrowth) and randomise prefab compounds; (5) put the variety budget into zone-level art direction (palette, sky, fog, weather), which is what reviewers credited in FUEL, and accept near-field repetition.
- Several UE2 levels each covering one 256×256 terrain can stand in for FUEL's zones; FUEL's seamlessness cannot be reproduced without streaming, but its look (DEM-shaped macro relief + rule-based materials + rule-based scatter) can be baked offline.
