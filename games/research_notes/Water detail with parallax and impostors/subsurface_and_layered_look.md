# Appearance of shallow, dirty indoor water: subsurface / volumetric look, layered materials, wetness, on D3D9 SM3

Scope: what makes a coarse height-field water surface (20-unit cells, 2D shallow-water sim, per-pixel ripple normals) read as "a volume with a floor under it" in a flooded-ship interior, and which of the published techniques fit a 2011 D3D9 / ps_3_0 renderer (or a d3d9 proxy injected into such a game) that already has planar reflection and refraction passes.

Legend used throughout: **[V]** = verified from a primary source I fetched; **[S]** = from a search snippet / secondary source, not the original document; **[I]** = my inference / synthesis, not stated by any source.

---

## Key question 1: Depth-based water colour (refraction + depth fog), coefficients, D3D9 depth access, shoreline edge artefacts

### Takeaway
Every shipped reference (Far Cry/Crysis, Unreal, Unity examples) does the same thing: sample the opaque scene colour at a normal-perturbed screen position, compute the water-column thickness from (scene depth - water-surface depth), and lerp the refracted colour toward a water colour with exp(-k * thickness) (Beer-Lambert). On D3D9 the scene depth is read from an INTZ/RAWZ/DF24 depth texture or an R32F depth copy; the shoreline artefacts (above-water objects leaking into the refraction, hard edges) are handled by a refraction mask in the refraction target's alpha and by scaling the refraction offset with saturate(depth difference).

### Cited Findings

**The canonical "scene-texture refraction" and its mask (Far Cry / Crytek, 2005) [V]**
- Tiago Sousa, GPU Gems 2 ch. 19 "Generic Refraction Simulation" describes the Far Cry water/heat-haze technique: render the scene into texture S without refractive meshes, fetch a normal map, unpack to [-1,1], scale XY by a small factor (0.05 in the basic example) and add it to the projected screen coordinates before sampling S. — [GPU Gems 2 ch.19](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems2/part-ii-shading-lighting-and-shadows/chapter-19-generic-refraction-simulation)
- Refraction mask: the alpha of S is cleared to white, refractive meshes are rendered black into alpha only; the shader then blends `vRefrB * vRefrA.w + vRefrA * (1 - vRefrA.w)` (vRefrA = perturbed sample, vRefrB = unperturbed sample) so a perturbed coordinate landing on an above-water object falls back to the unperturbed pixel. The chapter calls this "inaccurate but practical" and notes residual artefacts because occluder colours are replaced by background. — [GPU Gems 2 ch.19](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems2/part-ii-shading-lighting-and-shadows/chapter-19-generic-refraction-simulation)
- Water shader (Example 19-4): four scrolled bump layers summed and remapped `normalize(2.0 * (sum of four) - 4.0)`; separate perturbation scales for refraction (0.075) and reflection (0.02); Fresnel `max(bias + (1-bias) * pow(1-NdotL, power), 0)` with bias 0.2, power 5; a distance term `fDistScale = saturate(10.0 / Wave0.w)` lerps the refraction colour toward a deep-water colour (0, 0.15, 0.115); final = `reflection * fresnel + waterColor`. The single-pass water needs no separate refraction pass; the reflection map is the scene mirrored through the water plane with below-plane geometry clipped. — [GPU Gems 2 ch.19](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems2/part-ii-shading-lighting-and-shadows/chapter-19-generic-refraction-simulation)
- Note: the GPU Gems 2 tint is distance-based (view distance), not thickness-based; the depth-buffer version arrived with Crysis (next bullet). [V]

**Crysis (GDC 2008 "Crysis Next Gen Effects") [V, from the slide deck text]**
- Refraction reuses the current back buffer (no re-render), masks out everything above the water surface, and applies chromatic dispersion by scaling R, G, B offsets differently. — [Crysis Next Gen Effects slides](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/crysis-nextgen-effects-gdc-2008/25051981)
- Leak fix: "leaking occurs when water depth is greater than world depth; the fix is to disable the refraction texture offset in that case" (a depth comparison between the water surface and the scene). — [Crysis Next Gen Effects slides](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/crysis-nextgen-effects-gdc-2008/25051981)
- Shore blending uses the distance between surface depth and world depth ("soft water intersections"); water particles are treated as soft particles; the reflection render target is half screen resolution; the wave sim is a 64x64 CPU Tessendorf grid uploaded to an FP32 texture. The deck gives no explicit fog formula; "underwater light-scattering" is listed as a goal citing Wenzel's GDC 2007 talk. — [Crysis Next Gen Effects slides](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/crysis-nextgen-effects-gdc-2008/25051981)

**A fully written-out depth-fog shader (Catlike Coding, Unity) [V]**
- Depth difference: `backgroundDepth = LinearEyeDepth(SAMPLE_DEPTH_TEXTURE(_CameraDepthTexture, uv)); surfaceDepth = UNITY_Z_0_FAR_FROM_CLIPSPACE(screenPos.z); depthDifference = backgroundDepth - surfaceDepth;`. — [Catlike Coding "Looking Through Water"](https://catlikecoding.com/unity/tutorials/flow/looking-through-water/)
- Fog: `fogFactor = exp2(-_WaterFogDensity * depthDifference); return lerp(_WaterFogColor, backgroundColor, fogFactor);` with density 0.15 in the example (0.1 default) and fog colour = water albedo (hex 4E83A9). — [Catlike Coding](https://catlikecoding.com/unity/tutorials/flow/looking-through-water/)
- Refraction offset: `uvOffset = tangentSpaceNormal.xy * _RefractionStrength` (0.25) with `uvOffset.y *= texelSize.z * abs(texelSize.y)` to correct aspect ratio. — [Catlike Coding](https://catlikecoding.com/unity/tutorials/flow/looking-through-water/)
- Above-water leak: if `depthDifference < 0` after the offset, reset the UV to the unrefracted position and resample depth; later replaced by the smoother `uvOffset *= saturate(depthDifference)`, which also fades the refraction out where the water is thinner than 1 unit, removing the "refraction pops off at the shore" artefact. — [Catlike Coding](https://catlikecoding.com/unity/tutorials/flow/looking-through-water/)

**The depth-difference edge/foam trick is the soft-particles trick [V/S]**
- NVIDIA's soft-particles paper: sprites get hard seams where they meet geometry; fix by reading the depth of the first opaque pixel behind the sprite (possible because particles don't write depth) and fading opacity to zero as the two depths converge; depth can come from a depth texture or from writing depth to a second render target; cost = one extra depth sample and a blend. — [NVIDIA SoftParticles (DX10 SDK whitepaper)](https://developer.download.nvidia.com/whitepapers/2007/SDK10/SoftParticles_hi.pdf)
- Shoreline foam in engine examples is the same comparison: subtract scene depth from the surface pixel's depth, smoothstep the result, blend in scrolling foam where it is small. [S] — [godotshaders water with foam and depth](https://godotshaders.com/shader/water-with-foam-and-depth/); [Unity HDRP shoreline thread](https://discussions.unity.com/t/unity-6-hdrp-water-shorelines-contactfoam/1599006/2)

**Physically-parameterised version (Unreal Single Layer Water) [V]**
- Unreal's SingleLayerWater shading model is "a cost-effective translucency method using a single depth layer"; it runs in its own pass after the base pass and deferred lighting but before regular translucency. Inputs: Scattering Coefficients, Absorption Coefficients, PhaseG, Color Scale Behind Water; the water is a homogeneous participating medium with a Schlick phase function; refraction comes from reading the depth and colour below the surface and distorting those samples; reflections (SSR, captures, sky) are composited later. — [Unreal Single Layer Water docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/single-layer-water-shading-model-in-unreal-engine)
- Absorption is per RGB channel in reciprocal metres (value = 1 / absorption distance); the doc's demo uses R 0.0033, G 0.0016, B 0.0011 (very clear water, red absorbed fastest). Scattering values in the demo step from 0.5 to 32 ("higher values make the water look thicker and hide objects at depth"). PhaseG: positive = forward (toward sun), negative = backward, 0 = isotropic; demo values -0.75 and 0.5. Color Scale Behind Water only scales brightness of things under the surface (fakes caustic/shadow contrast, casts nothing). On mobile/low-end it falls back to a plain translucent material with no volume integration. — [Unreal Single Layer Water docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/single-layer-water-shading-model-in-unreal-engine)

**Real absorption coefficients of pure water [V/S]**
- Pope & Fry 1997 ("Absorption spectrum (380-700 nm) of pure water. II. Integrating cavity measurements", Appl. Opt. 36) is the standard dataset; absolute minimum 0.0044 +/- 0.0006 m^-1 at 418 nm. — [OMLC Pope & Fry summary](https://omlc.org/spectra/water/abs/pope97.html); [Wikipedia, Electromagnetic absorption by water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_absorption_by_water)
- At 680 nm a model table citing Pope & Fry gives 0.465 m^-1. [S, from a search snippet of the COMETS model table]; the full corrected Pope & Fry table (400-715 nm) is in NASA's ocean-optics erratum. — [NASA ocean optics erratum PDF](https://oceancolor.gsfc.nasa.gov/files/resources/docs/technical/volivch1err1.pdf)
- A turbid ("milky water") tank measurement gave attenuation of order 3-47 m^-1 for RGB, i.e. 2-4 orders of magnitude above pure water, with blue deviating from Beer's law in the more turbid case. [S] — [arXiv underwater imaging review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.01869)
- Mental ray's "Submerge" shader documents the look of murk: light "scattered by a myriad of tiny particles" makes the water glow, deeper water glows less; a Density control sets how quickly distant objects fade "the same way fog thickness works"; it warns of banding without dithering. [S] — [Autodesk mental ray Submerge](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2016/ENU/mental-ray-docs/mr_docs/tutorials/lume/Submerge.html)
- 0 A.D. (Wildfire Games) splits murky water into two colours: "water colour" = colour at high depth (and everywhere when murkiness is 100%), "water tint" = colour in semi-transparent shallow areas. [S] — [Wildfire Games ticket 1706](https://trac.wildfiregames.com/ticket/1706)

**Reading scene depth in D3D9 [V]**
- Aras Pranckevicius, "D3D9 GPU Hacks": depth-as-texture is exposed through FOURCC formats checked with CheckDeviceFormat: INTZ (NVIDIA GeForce 8+, AMD HD4xxx+, Intel G45+; "DX10+ class"), RAWZ (GeForce 6/7 only, depth encoded across four channels), DF16 (AMD Radeon 9500+, Intel G45), DF24 (AMD X1300+, Intel SandyBridge; enables Fetch4). INTZ can be sampled while still bound for depth *testing* but not depth *writing*; DF16/DF24 cannot be sampled and depth-tested simultaneously. — [D3D9 GPU Hacks](https://www.aras-p.info/texts/D3D9GPUHacks.html)
- AMD Radeon HD3xxx-5xxx with Catalyst 9.10-10.5: INTZ test+sample at once was slow; workaround = render into INTZ, RESZ-copy it to a second surface, sample one and depth-test the other. RESZ resolves an MSAA depth-stencil to a non-MSAA depth texture (sample zero per pixel); NULL render target is a zero-memory dummy colour surface for depth-only passes. — [D3D9 GPU Hacks](https://www.aras-p.info/texts/D3D9GPUHacks.html)
- Unity on D3D9: the depth texture "is either a native depth buffer, or a single channel 32 bit floating point texture (R32F)"; the card must support INTZ or FP render targets; the shader gets a 0..1 value and linear eye depth has to be computed manually. — [Unity 3.5 depth textures doc](https://docs.unity3d.com/353/Documentation/Components/SL-DepthTextures.html)

### Inferences
- [I] For a 2D shallow-water sim the water-column thickness is *already known per cell* (sim depth h). Per pixel you can therefore compute thickness two ways and cross-check: (a) scene depth minus surface depth along the view ray (what every reference does), (b) h / cos(theta_refracted) from the sim's own depth field. (a) is needed for arbitrary props standing in the water; (b) needs no depth texture at all and is the fallback on hardware without INTZ/RAWZ, or in a d3d9 proxy that cannot get at the game's depth buffer.
- [I] In a flooded corridor the numbers from the Unreal doc (0.001-0.003 m^-1) are useless; dirty water is in the 1-10 m^-1 range (turbid-tank data above). With 20-unit cells and a water depth of a few units, pick per-channel k such that 1/k is roughly the designed visibility (e.g. 2-6 m), and bias k_red > k_green > k_blue for brown/green ship water (absorption) rather than the sea's blue.
- [I] The Crysis "disable the offset when water depth > world depth" and Catlike's `saturate(depthDifference)` are the same fix; the GPU Gems 2 alpha-mask is the SM2/SM3-friendly equivalent that avoids a second depth read. With planar reflection + refraction passes already in place, the alpha-mask variant costs nothing extra.

The Hugh Malan Horizon Forbidden West 2022 talk explicitly excludes underwater/shallow effects. — [SIGGRAPH 2022 Advances, Malan](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2022/SIGGRAPH2022-Advances-Water-Malan.pdf)

## Key question 2: Subsurface-scattering approximations for water and foam, and SM3 cost

### Takeaway
Shipped games do not solve light transport in the water; they blend a "sub-surface" colour in with a cheap view/sun/thickness term (Sea of Thieves: view angle x sun direction x wave-peak mask; Frostbite-style "translucency" wrap: pow(saturate(dot(V, -(L + N*distortion))), power) x thickness). Both are a handful of ALU ops and fit ps_3_0 trivially.

### Cited Findings
- Sea of Thieves (SIGGRAPH 2018 talk, Rare): "The water colour is based on scattering approximations. We blend between a deep water colour and a sub-surface water colour based on a combination of view angle, sun direction and a wave peak mask. The wave peak mask is generated from the FFT choppiness vertex offsets. Where the choppiness offset is greater, this corresponds to wave peaks, which show more sub-surface due to shorter distance traveled by light through the water." [V] — [The Technical Art of Sea of Thieves (PDF)](https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2018-Talks-Ang_The-Technical-Art-of-Sea-of-Thieves.pdf)
- Sea of Thieves foam: generated at wave peaks per Tessendorf, plus "around objects that intersect the water surface within a camera centered window using depth buffer comparisons. We progressively blur the result of the foam buffer with feedback to simulate the foam dispersing and to give us a softer mask"; the mask is blended with artist textures; calm/normal/stormy states change foam amount. Large low-sun highlight uses Karis's closest-point-on-sphere area light; Snell's window when viewed from below. [V] — [Sea of Thieves PDF](https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2018-Talks-Ang_The-Technical-Art-of-Sea-of-Thieves.pdf)
- Sea of Thieves shallow water (deck splashes): GPU surface simulation based on Mei, Decaudin, Hu 2007 ("Fast hydraulic erosion simulation and visualization on GPU"); for waterfalls/streams they project the camera depth buffer into the texture space of the shallow-water sim so a character occludes the stream and foam forms at their feet. [V] — [Sea of Thieves PDF](https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2018-Talks-Ang_The-Technical-Art-of-Sea-of-Thieves.pdf)
- Frostbite 2 translucency (Barre-Brisebois & Bouchard, GDC 2011; GPU Pro 2 "Real-Time Approximation of Light Transport in Translucent Homogenous Media", pp. 173-184) is the usual source of the "wrap" term used for foam/crests. [V that the talk/chapter exist; the formula itself was not retrieved from the primary] — [Barre-Brisebois GDC 2011 post](https://colinbarrebrisebois.com/2011/03/07/gdc-2011-approximating-translucency-for-a-fast-cheap-and-convincing-subsurface-scattering-look/); [GPU Pro 2 entry](https://gameenginegems.com/gemsdb/article.php?id=1111)
- A 2023 paper reusing that model writes it as a clamped dot product of the view vector with the normal-distorted light vector raised to a power, divided by (local thickness + epsilon), with distortion 0.08 and power 16. [S] — [MARF, arXiv 2307.00037](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.00037)
- Practical gating noted by implementers: drop the scattering term where the surface is not sun-lit, otherwise it glows unnaturally; pass the sun vector into the material. [S] — [VaOcean readme](https://github.com/mozgiii/vaocean); [UnityPlayground](https://github.com/techizgit/UnityPlayground)
- Realistic ocean lighting (Bruneton, Neyret, Holzschuch) separates sun, environment and underwater scattering contributions with a per-pixel BRDF on a projected grid. [S] — [Realtime Realistic Ocean Lighting PDF](https://cgg.mff.cuni.cz/%7Ejaroslav/teaching/2012-npgr031/prezentace/Realtime%20Realistic%20Ocean%20Lighting.pdf)
- Bagar, Scherzer, Wimmer 2010 ("A Layered Particle-Based Fluid Model for Real-Time Rendering of Water"): water and foam volumes are approximated by storing their boundary surfaces in depth maps, which "allows us to calculate the attenuation of light rays that pass through these volumes very efficiently". [V abstract] — [Bagar 2010](https://www.cg.tuwien.ac.at/research/publications/2010/BAGAR-2010-LPB/)

### Inferences
- [I] For indoor water with no sun, replace "sun direction" with the dominant local light (the per-room key light or the brightest point light the water shader already receives); the Sea of Thieves blend becomes `lerp(deepColor, subsurfaceColor, saturate(a*viewTerm + b*lightTerm + c*peakMask))` where peakMask comes from the shallow-water sim (height above mean, or |velocity| for churn) instead of FFT choppiness.
- [I] Foam/scum is where the wrap term pays off: foam on dirty water is a thin bright layer that should be lit by wrap/translucency (brighter at grazing angles against a light), not by N.L. The Sea of Thieves "foam buffer with feedback blur" is a 2D sim-space render target (ping-pong), trivially SM3; the depth-buffer comparison part needs the depth texture from question 1.
- [I] SM3 cost: both terms are < 10 ALU instructions plus zero extra samples (thickness from the sim height field); the 512-slot minimum on ps_3_0 is not a concern.

- Could not fetch the Frostbite/EA page (redirected to ea.com) nor the GPU Pro 2 text, so the exact Frostbite shader constants are not verified; the MARF form is a reimplementation.
- No "Rendering of Thin Water Sheets" paper was found under that title; nearest matches are Baboud & Decoret 2006 and Bagar 2010 (see question 3).

## Key question 3: Floating particulate / detritus inside the volume; floor parallax; what shipped games do for murky indoor water

### Takeaway
No public technical write-up was found for how BioShock, Dead Space, Alien Isolation, Amnesia, Still Wakes the Deep or Hydrophobia render murky interior water; the published building blocks are (a) ray-marching the water slab between two height-fields in the pixel shader (Baboud & Decoret 2006), (b) stacking a few planes/layers inside the volume (Crysis god-rays, Sea of Thieves cloud noise-by-distance), and (c) depth-map attenuation of a foam/particle layer (Bagar 2010). The "interior layers at parallax offsets" approach for particulate is my synthesis.

### Cited Findings
- Baboud & Decoret, "Realistic Water Volumes in Real-Time" (Eurographics NPH 2006): the water is "the space between a ground heightfield and a water-surface heightfield"; a simplified ray-tracing approach in the shader "correctly handles reflections and refractions and allows complex effects such as light absorption, refracted shadows and refracted caustics". [S; the EG digital-library page returned 403] — [Realistic Water Volumes in Real-Time](https://diglib.eg.org/xmlui/handle/10.2312/NPH.NPH06.025-032)
- Bagar 2010: water and foam represented as layered volumes whose boundaries are depth maps; light attenuation through those thin layers is computed from the depth maps. [V abstract] — [Bagar 2010](https://www.cg.tuwien.ac.at/research/publications/2010/BAGAR-2010-LPB/)
- Crysis underwater god rays: reuse the procedural caustics shader (sun-direction based), "project the effect onto multiple planes in front of the camera", render into a target 4x smaller than the screen, add to the frame buffer. [V] — [Crysis Next Gen Effects slides](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/crysis-nextgen-effects-gdc-2008/25051981)
- Sea of Thieves clouds (not water, but the same "fake depth" trick): "blend between low-frequency and high-frequency noise to further give the impression of depth, based on the distance of the pixel from the camera", composited from a quarter-res buffer with depth in alpha. [V] — [Sea of Thieves PDF](https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2018-Talks-Ang_The-Technical-Art-of-Sea-of-Thieves.pdf)
- GPU Gems 1 ch. 2 gives the exact ray/plane intercept used to find where a refracted vertical ray hits the floor plane: `distance = (planeD - lineP.z) / lineN.z; return lineP + lineN * distance;` (used for caustics there, usable for floor parallax in reverse). [V] — [GPU Gems ch.2](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-2-rendering-water-caustics)
- Unreal's Single Layer Water explicitly models the volume as a homogeneous scattering medium (scattering coefficient + phase function) rather than textured particulate, and its "Color Scale Behind Water" is a flat brightness multiplier on the floor. [V] — [Unreal Single Layer Water docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/single-layer-water-shading-model-in-unreal-engine)
- HydroEngine (Hydrophobia) public descriptions only cover the simulation ("physically accurate real-time fluid dynamics", water that flows from one area to another, ripples visible because of how light falls on the reactive surface); none mention caustics, wetness or particulate rendering. [S] — [Wikipedia: Hydrophobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrophobia_(video_game)); [Gematsu interview, Rob Hewson](https://gematsu.com/2010/04/interview-hyrdrophobias-rob-hewson); [MCV: Blade sharpens HydroEngine](https://www.mcvuk.com/development/blade-sharpens-hydroengine)

### Inferences
- [I] Floor parallax from water depth, exactly: you know the surface point P, the per-pixel surface normal N (ripple texture), the view vector V and the floor height (from the sim's bed height field or a constant per room). Refract V through N with eta = 1/1.33, intersect the refracted ray with the floor plane (the GPU Gems ch.2 intercept above), and use that hit point to (i) look up the floor's *world-space* texture if the floor is a simple plane, or (ii) convert it back to screen space and sample the refraction target there. (ii) is the "physically correct" version of the screen-space normal.xy offset: the offset magnitude grows with depth automatically, and shallow water stops distorting, which is exactly the Catlike `saturate(depthDifference)` hack done properly. Cost: one refract (about 6 ALU), one intercept (3 ALU), one matrix multiply to screen. Entirely ps_3_0.
- [I] Particulate inside the volume: use the same refracted ray and sample 2-3 tiling "detritus" textures (or one texture with different scroll/scale) at the points where the refracted ray crosses planes at fixed sub-depths (e.g. 25%, 55%, 85% of the local depth h). Each layer's contribution is attenuated by exp(-k * pathLengthToThatLayer) and modulated by the layer's alpha; sum them then do the floor. Because layer positions follow the refracted ray and the depth, the specks parallax against the floor and against each other, which is the cue that reads as "stuff floating in the volume". This is the water-slab analogue of the Crysis "multiple planes in front of the camera" god-ray trick and the Sea of Thieves distance-blended noise. SM3 cost: N extra tex2D (3) + N exps; with ps_3_0's no-dependent-read limit the dependent UVs are fine.
- [I] Screen-space scattering (blur the refraction target by thickness) is a cheap addition: sample the refraction target with a small 4-tap kernel whose radius scales with thickness, so deep regions look soft and shallow regions crisp; this also hides the half-res refraction targets common on 2011 hardware (Crysis used half-res reflection).
- [I] Depth-sliced particle sprites (actual soft particles under the surface) are the "expensive but artist-friendly" route; they need the depth texture for soft intersection with the floor, and since the water surface does not write depth they must be drawn before the water surface and then fogged by the water shader.

### Gaps
- No primary or secondary source describes the murky-water rendering of BioShock, Dead Space, Alien Isolation, Amnesia, Still Wakes the Deep, or Hydrophobia; this remains inference.
- The exact cvar names supplied as hooks (hydro.wetbump*, hydro.godraysoverwater, caustic_omnilight.frg, reflectRefractPlanar.frg) returned nothing on the web; any meaning assigned to them must come from the game's own files.

## Key question 4: Wet surfaces and water lines (wet-map darkening, specular change; rising water leaving a wet line)

### Takeaway
Lagarde's Remember Me recipe is the SM3-era reference: darken diffuse by `lerp(1, 0.2, porosity)` scaled by wet level, push gloss toward 1 with half that factor, blend the normal map toward the vertex normal as water accumulates (puddles go flat), treat water F0 as 0.02 and skip a true dual-layer BRDF as too expensive for 360/PS3; a wet line is just this wetness mask driven by (current water height + max height reached) with a slow decay.

### Cited Findings
- Lagarde, Water drop 3b: "Most use the same eye-calibrated factors to attenuate diffuse and boost specular (old fashion) on every wet surfaces of the scene regardless of material properties"; his model uses open (effective) porosity only. [S/V] — [Water drop 3b](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2013/04/14/water-drop-3b-physically-based-wet-surfaces/)
- DoWetProcess (verbatim): `float Porosity = tex2D(GreyTextures, uv).g; float factor = lerp(1, 0.2, Porosity); Diffuse *= lerp(1.0, factor, WetLevel); Gloss = lerp(1.0, Gloss, lerp(1, factor, 0.5 * WetLevel));`; metal-aware variant `factor = lerp(1, 0.2, (1 - Metalness) * Porosity)` with `Metalness = saturate((dot(specular, 0.33) * 1000 - 500))`; porosity from gloss when no texture exists: `saturate(-2.5 * Gloss + 1.25)`. The 0.2 floor comes from measured wet/dry albedo ratios and the 0.3 in Nakamae et al. [V] — [Water drop 3b](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2013/04/14/water-drop-3b-physically-based-wet-surfaces/)
- Water F0 is about 0.02 (IOR 1.33) vs 0.04 for common dielectrics; Lagarde judged the difference too small to model; "a dual layer lighting model is expensive", so instead `AccumulatedWater` blends the normal map toward the vertex normal (thin film = slight smoothing + stronger specular; puddles fully flat). Drying: `rho_d(t) = alpha(t) rho_dry + (1 - alpha(t)) rho_wet` with a sigmoid alpha; in-game a noise mask + sigmoid or a plain lerp over about a minute; puddles dry slower. The approach is stated to be suitable for Xbox 360/PS3. [V] — [Water drop 3b](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2013/04/14/water-drop-3b-physically-based-wet-surfaces/)
- Water drop 2b (dynamic rain): gloss is pushed toward its maximum and specular toward the water reflectance (F0 0.02) as accumulated water increases. [S] — [Water drop 2b](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2013/01/03/water-drop-2b-dynamic-rain-and-its-effects/)
- Water drop 3a caution: optics papers' "albedo" includes some specular reflection and does not match the CG diffuse albedo. [S] — [Water drop 3a](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2013/03/19/water-drop-3a-physically-based-wet-surfaces/)
- Physics of darkening (forum/quiz sources, consistent with Lekner & Dorf 1988): a water film returns light reflected from the substrate back toward it at the water-air interface where it is absorbed again; on porous materials the white multiply-scattered component is what is lost. [S] — [physicsforums](https://www.physicsforums.com/threads/if-water-is-clear-why-does-it-make-materials-appear-dark.699454/post-4431838); [Tel Aviv quiz answer](https://www.tau.ac.il/~kantor/QUIZ/96/A11.96.html)
- Uncharted 4 (Brinck, SIGGRAPH 2016 "Technical Art of Uncharted 4", wetness shading section) as summarised by a third-party plugin README: a wet surface is a blend of regions — a flat-water core, a surface-tension "clinging" edge with a shrink-wrapped look, and a saturated region "showing a darkening of the albedo but not significantly affecting normals or specular response". [S; the 13 MB slide PDF could not be fetched] — [Technical Art of Uncharted 4 page](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/other/2016/naughty_dog/index.html); [plugin README summary](https://science.ecosyste.ms/projects/162085)
- Crysis caustics pass "darkens the result slightly to suggest a wet surface". [V] — [Crysis Next Gen Effects slides](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/crysis-nextgen-effects-gdc-2008/25051981)

### Inferences
- [I] Rising water line: keep a per-surface (or per-world-height) wetness value `wet = saturate((hMaxReached - y) / band)` where hMaxReached is the highest water level the sim has reached at that XY (track a max-height field alongside the sim's height field; one extra 2D target, cheap on SM3), and decay hMaxReached toward the current level at the drying rate. Feed `wet` as WetLevel into Lagarde's DoWetProcess. The water line is the band where wet goes 1 -> 0; add a thin darker streak and a slight gloss bump just above the current surface for the meniscus.
- [I] "Hydrophobia-style wet bump" is most plausibly the `AccumulatedWater` normal blend above (normal map flattened toward vertex normal where water sits, plus a ripple normal from the sim for surfaces being splashed). Not verified against the game.
- [I] On a forward SM3 renderer the wet process costs one extra texture (porosity in a grey channel) and about 8 ALU per pixel; in a d3d9 proxy without shader source you cannot modify the material shader and would have to fake it with a multiplicative decal / projected darkening pass plus a reflective overlay, which only achieves the albedo darkening, not the gloss change.

### Gaps
- Primary slides for Uncharted 4 wetness were not readable (file too large for the fetch tool); roughness treatment there is unverified.
- Rust (Facepunch) wetness implementation was not researched.

## Key question 5: Caustics on the floor (projected textures vs computed from surface normals), cost

### Takeaway
Two shipped approaches: (1) Crysis projects 3 procedural layers along the light direction in an extra pass over opaque and transparent receivers (cheap, no coupling to the surface); (2) GPU Gems 1 ch. 2 computes caustics from the surface gradient by refracting a vertical light ray at the surface, intersecting the floor and looking up a "sun" map, rendered into a texture-space light map that can be mip-mapped. For a height-field sim with known normals, (2) can be driven directly by the sim's normal field.

### Cited Findings
- GPU Gems 1 ch. 2 (Guardado & Sanchez-Crespo, 2004): assumes the light is directly overhead so vertical rays reach the floor; for each floor point project vertically to the wave surface, take the gradient (dz/dx, dz/dy), refract with Snell (n = 1.33), intersect the floor plane (`distance = (planeD - lineP.z) / lineN.z; return lineP + lineN * distance;`) and use the hit as a lookup into a "Sun" map whose centre texels are bright (caustics are strongest where the refracted ray stays near vertical; deeper floors shrink the effective light source and sharpen the caustic). Per-pixel Cg version: `dxdy = gradwave(x, y, Timer); intercept = line_plane_intercept(pos, float3(dxdy, saturate(pos.w)), float3(0,0,1), -0.8); colour = tex2D(LightMap, intercept.xy * 0.8) + tex2D(GroundMap, uv);` with `saturate(pos.w)` attenuating the effect to cut aliasing. Rendered into a fixed-res texture-space light map (constant cost, mip/aniso filterable) vs screen space (cost scales with visible pixels, cannot mip). Wave function uses 5 octaves. No hardware figures beyond "programmable vertex and pixel shaders". [V] — [GPU Gems ch.2](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-2-rendering-water-caustics)
- Crysis procedural caustics: extra pass, works for opaque and transparent surfaces, projected along the real sun direction, 3 procedural layers, chromatic-dispersion approximation, slight darkening to suggest wetness. The same shader is reused for underwater god rays on stacked planes at 1/4 res. [V] — [Crysis Next Gen Effects slides](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/crysis-nextgen-effects-gdc-2008/25051981)
- Iwasaki, Dobashi, Nishita 2003, "A Fast Rendering Method for Refractive and Reflective Caustics Due to Water Surfaces" (Eurographics) is the hardware-accelerated reference for caustics from an actual surface mesh. [S] — [EG paper](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/8784)
- Gruen's later caustics mapping (photons traced from a rasterised water mesh, reconstructed in screen space) is cited in Ray Tracing Gems II as an improvement over the GPU Gems chapter. [S] — [RTG2 ch.30 preprint](https://developer.download.nvidia.com/ray-tracing-gems/rtg2-chapter30-preprint.pdf)
- Unreal's water material does not compute caustics; "Color Scale Behind Water" is purely visual. [V] — [Unreal Single Layer Water docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/single-layer-water-shading-model-in-unreal-engine)

### Inferences
- [I] For an indoor scene with point/spot lights (no sun), the GPU Gems "vertical light" assumption fails but the structure survives: in the caustic light-map pass, for each sim texel refract the vector from the light to the surface point (not a vertical ray) through the sim normal and intersect the floor; splat/lookup as before. Doing this in the sim's own 2D texture space (one quad over the height-field texture, output to a caustic texture tiled in world XY) is what the "caustic_omnilight" name suggests, but that is only a guess from the name.
- [I] Cost: the light-map version is one pass over the sim texture (e.g. 256x256 or 512x512) per light, plus one extra tex2D in the floor shader (or a projected decal pass if the floor shader cannot be changed, as in a d3d9 proxy). The projected-texture route (Crysis) is the one that works from a proxy: it only needs a projective pass with the floor depth.
- [I] Attenuate caustic intensity by exp(-k*depth) and by the same dirt/turbidity used for the volume so caustics vanish in very murky water (physically, scattering kills focused light); dirty water with crisp caustics reads as wrong.

### Gaps
- No cost figures (ms) were found for either shipped approach.

## Key question 6: Layered material models (clear coat, thin film, parallax layered / Unreal clear-coat-with-bottom-normal) — are they the right model for water over a visible floor?

### Takeaway
Clear coat models two BRDF lobes at one surface (coat + base, optionally with separate normals); it does not transport light through a thick, absorbing, refracting slab or parallax the base. For water with a visible floor, the Single-Layer-Water style (surface BRDF + volume BSDF + refracted scene colour) is the right model; clear-coat ideas are only useful for the thin wet film on surrounding surfaces, and even there Lagarde judged a dual-layer BRDF too costly for 2011-class consoles.

### Cited Findings
- Unreal Clear Coat dual normals: the main material Normal input defines the clear coat surface; a second normal map (via the ClearCoatBottomNormal custom output, after enabling "Clear Coat Enable Second Normal" in project settings and restarting) describes the layer under the coat, "for complex Materials like carbon fiber" where "the underlying layer has different geometric surface properties than the clear coat layer"; inputs are Clear Coat (scalar, default 1.0 in the tutorial) and Clear Coat Roughness (0.1225 in the tutorial). Nothing in the doc models thickness, absorption, refraction offset or parallax between the layers. [V] — [Unreal: dual normals with clear coat](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/using-dual-normals-with-clear-coat-in-unreal-engine)
- Unreal 4.25 improved shading models (clear coat with second normal exposure). [S] — [Unreal tech blog](https://www.unrealengine.com/tech-blog/improved-shading-models-in-unreal-engine-4-25-and-beyond)
- Unreal's Single Layer Water by contrast has a BRDF (surface) and a BSDF (volume) with Opacity controlling the balance, refracts scene colour/depth, and composites reflections separately. [V] — [Unreal Single Layer Water docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/single-layer-water-shading-model-in-unreal-engine)
- Lagarde: "a dual layer lighting model is expensive" for the Remember Me target; the thin water film is approximated by blending normals toward the vertex normal and lerping gloss/diffuse. [V] — [Water drop 3b](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2013/04/14/water-drop-3b-physically-based-wet-surfaces/)
- Baboud & Decoret's water volume (two height-fields, ray-traced in the shader) is the published "parallax layered" formulation for water over a floor. [S] — [Realistic Water Volumes in Real-Time](https://diglib.eg.org/xmlui/handle/10.2312/NPH.NPH06.025-032)

### Inferences
- [I] Mapping onto the user's renderer: the water surface = {Fresnel-weighted planar reflection (coat specular lobe), refracted + depth-fogged scene (base), optional wrap/SSS tint and foam}. That is structurally a clear coat whose "base" is the refracted scene rather than a diffuse BRDF, and whose base is displaced by the refracted ray (question 3 inference). So the clear-coat mental model is fine for the *reflection* lobe (F0 0.02, roughness from ripple magnitude), but the base must be the volume + floor, not a second lobe.
- [I] Where clear-coat-with-bottom-normal genuinely applies: puddles and the wet band on walls/floor (Lagarde's AccumulatedWater: top normal = flat/ripple, bottom normal = material normal map). On SM3 this is affordable because both normals share one light loop; the extra cost is a second specular evaluation.
- [I] Thin-film interference (oil-slick rainbows on dirty ship water) is a separate effect: a view-angle-dependent colour ramp (1D texture indexed by NdotV and film thickness) added into the reflection term; no source for it was researched here.

### Gaps
- No source was found on thin-film / iridescence models for water surfaces (Unity HDRP iridescence, Belcour-Barla 2017) in this pass.
- No source compares clear coat vs volume water directly; the "right model" verdict is inference from the two Unreal docs.

## Key question 7: Which of these run on D3D9 ps_3_0 and which need SM4+ / compute

### Takeaway
Everything above that the 2005-2011 games shipped (Far Cry, Crysis, Remember Me) ran on SM3-class or weaker hardware; only true 3D volumetrics, texture arrays and GPU-side spatial reductions are SM4+/compute. ps_3_0 gives you 512+ instruction slots, 16 samplers, 32 temps, dynamic branching (depth 24), gradient instructions, vFace/vPos, and no dependent-read limit, which is more than enough for the refracted-ray + 3-layer particulate + fog + wet shader.

### Cited Findings
- ps_3_0 minimums (Microsoft): instruction count 512 up to MaxPixelShader30InstructionSlots (<= 32768), executed instructions >= 2^16 via loops; 16 texture samplers; 32 temporaries; dynamic flow control (if bool / break) nesting depth 0-24; call/loop/rep nesting depth 4; arbitrary swizzle; gradient instructions dsx/dsy/texldd; predication; "no dependent read limits"; "no limit on texture instructions"; new face (vFace) and position (vPos) registers; required caps include volume maps (D3DPTEXTURECAPS_VOLUMEMAP, MIPVOLUMEMAP), cube maps, 4K textures, 16x aniso, MRT independent write masks and post-pixel-shader blending if MRT is supported, 6 user clip planes with vs_3_0. [V] — [Microsoft ps_3_0 reference](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/direct3dhlsl/dx9-graphics-reference-asm-ps-3-0)
- texldd costs 3 slots; texldp cost depends on texture type. [S] — [Microsoft ps_3_0 instructions](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-ie/Windows/Win32/direct3dhlsl/dx9-graphics-reference-asm-ps-instructions-ps-3-0)
- Cg ps_3_0 profile: data-dependent loops limited to 256 iterations, four nesting levels. [S] — [Cg ps_3_0 profile](https://developer.nvidia.com/w/cg/ps_3_0.html)
- Depth textures on D3D9: INTZ (DX10-class GPUs only), RAWZ (GeForce 6/7), DF16/DF24 (AMD/Intel), with the sample-while-test caveats and the AMD RESZ workaround above. [V] — [D3D9 GPU Hacks](https://www.aras-p.info/texts/D3D9GPUHacks.html)
- GPU Gems 2 refraction: author states it "could be extended to pixel shader versions 1.1 to 1.4". [V] — [GPU Gems 2 ch.19](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems2/part-ii-shading-lighting-and-shadows/chapter-19-generic-refraction-simulation)
- Lagarde's wet process is explicitly "suitable for XBOX360/PS3 games" (SM3-class). [V] — [Water drop 3b](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2013/04/14/water-drop-3b-physically-based-wet-surfaces/)
- Crysis (DX9/DX10, 2007-08) shipped back-buffer refraction with depth-based masking, depth-difference shore blending, soft water particles, projected procedural caustics and quarter-res god rays. [V] — [Crysis Next Gen Effects slides](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/crysis-nextgen-effects-gdc-2008/25051981)
- Unreal's Single Layer Water falls back on mobile/low-end to a plain translucent material with no volume integration and no SSR, implying the full path assumes a modern deferred pipeline. [V] — [Unreal Single Layer Water docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/single-layer-water-shading-model-in-unreal-engine)

### Inferences (SM3 feasibility table)
- Runs on ps_3_0 (and mostly ps_2_0): refraction via normal-perturbed screen UV; alpha-channel refraction mask; depth-fog exp(-k*d) from INTZ/RAWZ/DF24 or an R32F depth copy (needs a depth-writing pre-pass or MRT if the engine has none); saturate(depthDifference) shoreline softening and foam; Sea-of-Thieves style deep/subsurface colour blend; Frostbite wrap term; refracted-ray floor parallax; 2-3 parallax particulate layers (3 extra samples); 4-tap thickness-scaled refraction blur; Lagarde wet process incl. normal flattening; GPU Gems texture-space caustic light map; Crysis projected caustics and plane-stacked god rays; the 2D shallow-water sim itself as ping-pong render targets (Mei et al. 2007 is a GPU-shader method; Sea of Thieves used it).
- [I] Texture arrays do not exist in D3D9; substitute a volume texture (a required cap on SM3) or an atlas for the particulate/detritus layer set.
- [I] Needs SM4+ or compute: true ray-marched volumetric scattering with many steps per pixel (a budget problem, not a capability one: 512 slots and loops of 256 would allow it, fill-rate on 2011 GPUs would not); GPU-side reductions (max water height, foam-buffer statistics) are doable with ping-pong downsample passes but awkward; MSAA depth reads need RESZ (AMD/Intel only on D3D9) or a non-MSAA depth copy; INTZ is absent on pre-DX10 GPUs, so a 2011 SM3-only (GeForce 6/7, X1xxx) path must use RAWZ/DF24 or an R32F depth copy.
- [I] In a d3d9 proxy (no access to the game's shader source): you can add passes (projected caustics, wet decals, refraction-mask bookkeeping, depth copy by substituting INTZ at CreateDepthStencilSurface time), but you cannot change the game's own water shader except by intercepting SetPixelShader and substituting your own; the per-cell sim data would then have to be re-uploaded as textures you own.

### Gaps
- None of the sources give measured per-pass millisecond costs on 2011 hardware; all cost statements here are structural (sample/ALU counts).

## Compact synthesis for the implementer [I unless cited]

1. Depth source: INTZ where available (GeForce 8+/HD4xxx+), else R32F depth copy; d = linearEye(sceneDepth) - surfaceEyeDepth; never let d < 0 (clamp, and kill the refraction offset when it is) — [Aras](https://www.aras-p.info/texts/D3D9GPUHacks.html), [Crysis](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/crysis-nextgen-effects-gdc-2008/25051981), [Catlike](https://catlikecoding.com/unity/tutorials/flow/looking-through-water/).
2. Refraction sample point: refract V through N (eta 1/1.33), hit the floor plane at bed height from the sim, project to screen; blend with the unperturbed sample using the alpha refraction mask — [GPU Gems 2](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems2/part-ii-shading-lighting-and-shadows/chapter-19-generic-refraction-simulation), [GPU Gems ch.2 intercept](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-2-rendering-water-caustics).
3. Volume colour: `T = exp(-k_rgb * pathLen)` with k of order 1-10 m^-1 for dirty water (tank data); colour = lerp(murkColour, refractedColour, T) (the `murkColour * (1 - T)` part is the in-scatter); split murk into a deep colour vs a shallow tint like 0 A.D. — [Unreal units](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/single-layer-water-shading-model-in-unreal-engine), [0 A.D.](https://trac.wildfiregames.com/ticket/1706).
4. Particulate: 2-3 detritus layers at fractional depths along the refracted ray, each attenuated by its own T; optional 4-tap blur with radius proportional to d.
5. Surface: Fresnel (bias 0.2, pow 5) x planar reflection + Sea-of-Thieves subsurface blend (view, light, sim peak/velocity mask) + foam from a feedback-blurred foam buffer lit with a wrap term — [GPU Gems 2](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems2/part-ii-shading-lighting-and-shadows/chapter-19-generic-refraction-simulation), [Sea of Thieves](https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2018-Talks-Ang_The-Technical-Art-of-Sea-of-Thieves.pdf).
6. Edges: foam/soft edge = smoothstep on d; refraction offset *= saturate(d) — [NVIDIA soft particles](https://developer.download.nvidia.com/whitepapers/2007/SDK10/SoftParticles_hi.pdf), [Catlike](https://catlikecoding.com/unity/tutorials/flow/looking-through-water/).
7. Floor caustics: texture-space light map from the sim normals (per light, refract light->surface ray, intersect floor) or Crysis-style projected procedural layers; attenuate by T — [GPU Gems ch.2](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-2-rendering-water-caustics), [Crysis](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/crysis-nextgen-effects-gdc-2008/25051981).
8. Wetness: max-height-reached field -> WetLevel -> Lagarde DoWetProcess (diffuse *= lerp(1, lerp(1,0.2,porosity), wet); gloss toward 1 at half rate; normal toward vertex normal where water sits) — [Lagarde 3b](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2013/04/14/water-drop-3b-physically-based-wet-surfaces/).
