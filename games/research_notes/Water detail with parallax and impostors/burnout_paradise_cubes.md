# Burnout Paradise "distorted cubes" and the box/cube-impostor family (interior mapping, box-projected / parallax-corrected cubemaps, layered parallax) — can it render ripples and detritus on shallow water in D3D9 / SM3?

Research date: 2026-10-09. Scope: (1) what Criterion actually published about Burnout Paradise rendering, (2) whether any documented "cube" technique matches the "distorted cubes" memory, (3) the lineage and math of box/cube tricks, (4) limits, (5) prior use for liquids/ice/puddles, (6) fit for D3D9/SM3.

Headline: no Criterion primary source describing Burnout Paradise's reflection, window or road-wetness rendering could be found or fetched. The "distorted cubes" memory cannot be matched to a documented Criterion technique. The technique family itself (interior mapping, box-projected cubemaps, inner-layer depth parallax) is thoroughly documented, is SM2/SM3-era by origin, and the water-cell idea maps almost one-to-one onto Chris Oat's 2006 ATI "multiple layers + depth parallax" method plus van Dongen's "furniture plane", which is the best-fitting published ancestor for "a floor plus a few layers of suspended detritus distorted by the surface normal".

## Key question 2: Is there a documented "cube" effect in Burnout Paradise (parallax-corrected cubemaps on cars/roads, interior-mapped windows, impostor boxes, crash-camera deformation)? Candidates with evidence.

### Takeaway
No. None of the candidate cube-like techniques is documented for Burnout Paradise by Criterion or by a reliable technical write-up. The "distorted cubes" memory cannot be matched to a documented technique; the candidates below are ranked by plausibility with the (thin) evidence each has.

### Cited Findings
- Interior mapping was published in 2008 (CGI 2008) by Joost van Dongen, the same year Paradise shipped; its paper cites GTA, Crackdown and Superman Returns as motivating city games and names no shipped user — [van Dongen, Interior Mapping (CGI 2008) PDF](https://www.proun-game.com/Oogst3D/CODING/InteriorMapping/InteriorMapping.pdf)
- The first shipped games van Dongen himself names as users are SimCity (2013) ("A SimCity (2013) programmer told him the game used interior mapping") and, as an unconfirmed inference, Marvel's Spider-Man (2018) — [Game Developer, "Interior Mapping: rendering real rooms without geometry"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/interior-mapping-rendering-real-rooms-without-geometry)
- Box-projected cubemaps were first posted by "behc" on 20 April 2010 (GameDev.net), two years after Paradise; parallax-corrected cubemaps with console numbers date to SIGGRAPH 2012 — [GameDev.net, "Box Projected Cubemap Environment Mapping"](https://www.gamedev.net/forums/topic/568829-box-projected-cubemap-environment-mapping/); [SIGGRAPH 2012 talk listing](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=140207)
- The older sphere-proxy "localized" cubemap (GPU Gems ch. 19, 2004) predates Paradise and is SM2-compatible Cg code — [GPU Gems ch.19, Image-Based Lighting (Bjorke)](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-iii-materials/chapter-19-image-based-lighting)
- Standard racing-game practice per a developer thread: "it's quite common to render a dynamic cubemap every frame from roughly the position of the player car, and use that for the reflections on that car", mixed "with other static or dynamic cubemaps placed throughout the track to handle reflections on other cars or surfaces" — [GameDev.net, cheap paint-job reflections thread](https://gamedev.net/forums/topic/708272-cheap-way-to-do-paint-job-reflections/5432258)
- Burnout 2 did exactly that (128x128 cube per frame on the car) — [xboxrecomp PR #3](https://github.com/phobos665/xboxrecomp/pull/3)
- Burnout 3 exposed "track reflections" as a separate drawing flag — [mattkc forum](https://forum.mattkc.com/viewtopic.php?p=1183)
- Paradise PC port features (soft shadows, motion blur, high res) say nothing about reflections — [bit-tech press release](https://bit-tech.net/press-releases/ea-s-burnout-paradise-revs-its-engines-on-the-pc/1/)

### Inferences (candidate ranking; all unverified for Paradise)
1. Per-frame low-resolution dynamic cube map on the player car (the Burnout 2 lineage, and the genre norm). A low-res cube refreshed every frame on highly reflective paint reads as a "warped/distorted box" sliding over the car, especially where the cube's six faces meet — the most likely origin of a "distorted cubes" memory. Evidence: Burnout 2 precedent + genre practice only.
2. Static environment cubemaps on building glass / wet road, without parallax correction. On large flat surfaces a plain cubemap looks wrong in exactly the way behc describes ("Pre-rendered cubemaps are good for complex objects but wrong for big flat elements, like a polished floor") — the artifact is a distorted box-shaped reflection. Evidence: technique timing only (box projection post-dates the game, so if Paradise used cubes on flat glass/roads they were almost certainly uncorrected).
3. Interior-mapped windows: technically possible in 2008 (the paper says it fits "within the 64 instructions allowed in pixel shader model 2.0" and is "efficient enough ... for the Xbox 360, Playstation 3"), but nothing links it to Paradise, and van Dongen's own first named adopter is SimCity 2013. Low plausibility.
4. Cube-map "impostor boxes" for distant buildings, Big Surf Island water, crash-camera deformation: no evidence found for any of them. The "deformation" of cars at crash time is mesh deformation, not a cube technique, and nothing links it to cubes.

### Gaps
- The memory may refer to something visual (e.g., low-res dynamic cube faces on paint, or the 360's tiled-render artifacts) that no text source describes. A RenderDoc/PIX capture of the PC version would settle which of candidates 1–2 are real; no such public capture was found.

## Key question 3: How do box-projected / parallax-corrected cubemaps and interior mapping work mathematically (ray vs AABB, divisions, SM3 cost), and could a shallow water cell be treated as a box with a floor plus suspended detritus layers, distorted by the surface normal?

### Takeaway
All three techniques are the same primitive: a per-pixel ray (view, reflected, or refracted) intersected with axis-aligned planes in a local space, needing one reciprocal per plane axis (three for a box, one per horizontal layer), then a texture lookup at the hit. Published implementations were written for SM2 (van Dongen: fits 64 ps_2_0 instructions; GPU Gems 2004 Cg; Oat 2006 HLSL on ATI X1xxx/SM3). A water cell as "floor + N detritus layers, offset by the refracted view vector" is literally Oat's 2006 "depth parallax" layered material plus van Dongen's "furniture plane", so it is squarely within D3D9/SM3 budget.

### Cited Findings
Interior mapping (van Dongen 2008):
- Core: "For each pixel, the ray from the camera to the point on the building that is being rendered is intersected with the walls of the interior. Because the interior walls are regularly spaced, the ray can be collided in constant time, regardless of the number of rooms" — [van Dongen 2008 PDF](https://www.proun-game.com/Oogst3D/CODING/InteriorMapping/InteriorMapping.pdf)
- Plane selection: ceilings at spacing d; "taking the ceiling-function of the y-position of the pixel, divided by the distance d between ceilings ... In Cg, this simply comes down to ceil(y/d) · d. When the camera is tilted downwards ... the height is at (ceil(y/d) − 1) · d"; then the ray is intersected with that plane and "the x- and z-coordinates of the intersection as the uv-coordinates for a texture read" — same source
- Walls: "the same calculations ... but now with XY- and YZ-planes ... Of the resulting three intersections, the one closest to the camera will be used" — same source
- Division hazard: "Note that an exactly horizontal ray will result in a division by zero. However, GPUs do not crash on this and the visual artefacts this results in are so rare in practice that this problem is negligible" — same source
- Furniture plane (the "layer of stuff inside" primitive): "an extra plane that is parallel to the surface of the building, but displaced a fixed distance to the inside ... It is intersected with the ray ... and if the intersection is closer than any of the intersections of the interior walls, then the furniture is shown"; "the alpha channel of the texture that is used for the furniture plane determines whether the colour of the furniture plane is discarded or not"; "By using an animated texture, the objects on the furniture plane can actually move"; "Through the use of render to texture, more complex animations could be shown" — same source
- Cost/SM: "the number of shader instructions is small enough to be able to calculate the effect within the 64 instructions allowed in pixel shader model 2.0, and also add a diffuse and reflection map in the same pass"; on a GeForce 6600GT at 1024x768: diffuse+reflection material 5,100 frames/5 s vs 999 with Interior Mapping; z-prepass raised 881 to 1265 frames (+44%); texture resolution 256→16 only gained 5%; room count 1,000 to 4,000,000 "does not influence the performance at all"; "efficient enough to be applied to games ... for the Xbox 360, Playstation 3" — same source
- Later optimisation: only three of six planes need testing ("we only need to consider 3 of those since we know in which direction we're looking"); in 2007 van Dongen packed the three plane tests into float4 ops — [Game Developer article](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/interior-mapping-rendering-real-rooms-without-geometry)
- UE4 ships it as the standard "InteriorCubeMap" material function — same source; the UE3/UDK-era gem documents a material version that "could be expanded to incorporate ... animating textures on the virtual walls and or floors" — [UDK Development Kit Gems: Interior Mapping](https://docs.unrealengine.com/udk/Three/DevelopmentKitGemsInteriorMapping.html) (page 403 on fetch; excerpt from search)

Box-projected / parallax-corrected cubemaps:
- behc (2010): "a simplistic ray-tracing approach with a cubemap texture lookup, which works well when the scene is modeled as empty axis-aligned boxes"; "4 cube-maps pre-rendered at somewhere near center of each room"; "edge artifacts can't be eliminated because the cube-map contains limited information about the scene, and using different AABB sizes produces some discontinuities"; a commenter: "for 100% reflective mirrors, the artefacts might be too much, but for subtle glossy surfaces this is absolutely great" — [GameDev.net thread](https://www.gamedev.net/forums/topic/568829-box-projected-cubemap-environment-mapping/)
- Lagarde (2012): "In the shader, we perform an intersection between the reflection vector and the geometry proxy"; per axis the code computes the ray's exit distance against the box slab planes, takes the min of the three, hit P, corrected lookup R' = P − C (C = cubemap capture position); an OBB variant transforms ray and position into unit-box space; a cheaper tuned-offset approximation is credited to older sources and "requires tuning" — [Lagarde, Image-based lighting approaches and parallax-corrected cubemap](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2012/09/29/image-based-lighting-approaches-and-parallax-corrected-cubemap/)
- Lagarde's console costs: "For 25% screen coverage each new cubemaps to mix is around 0.25ms and for 75% it is around 0.75ms on PS3"; with their mixing step an additional cubemap costs "only 0.08ms" — same source
- SIGGRAPH 2012 abstract: per-object cubemaps (Half-Life 2 style) "suffers from lighting seams and parallax issues"; the method "avoids these artifacts" at "extremely high performance" on PS3 and Xbox 360 — [SIGGRAPH 2012 listing](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=140207)
- Sphere-proxy ancestor (GPU Gems 19): reflection vector intersected "with a sphere of radius = 1.0, centered at the origin of light space"; quadratic `b = -2*dot(reflVect, LightingPos); c = dot(LightingPos,LightingPos) - 1; discrim = b*b - 4*c;` then `reflVect = nearT*reflVect - LightingPos; texCUBE(EnvMap, reflVect)`; the cube shape "is really a convenience for the texturing hardware"; works best near the proxy centre — [GPU Gems ch.19](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-iii-materials/chapter-19-image-based-lighting)
- Valve/Source implementation uses "a bounding box brush to bake their reflection based on a specified area around them, and a custom shader" — [Valve Developer Wiki, Parallax Corrected Cubemaps](https://developer.valvesoftware.com/wiki/Parallax_Corrected_Cubemaps) (403 on fetch; excerpt from search)

Layered depth parallax (the direct ancestor of the water-cell idea):
- Oat (ATI, SIGGRAPH 2006 course ch.4): goal is "to approximate the overall look of volumetric, multi-layered and semi-transparent materials using efficient real-time rendering techniques"; cues are layer compositing, "Depth parallax (parallax due to layer depth and thickness)" and "Light diffusion"; "Layer blending is achieved by performing a simple lerp ... based on the outer layer's opacity map"; "there's no limit to the number of material layers" — [Oat, Rendering Gooey Materials with Multiple Layers (PDF)](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2006/Chapter4-Rendering_gooey_materials_with_multiple_layers.pdf)
- The inner-layer offset "by making the simplifying assumption that the layers are parallel in tangent space ... allows us to avoid performing any kind of ray-tracing"; transmission vector = reflection vector with z negated; "The transmission distance s ... is then computed by dividing the layer thickness d by the z component of the unit-length transmission vector" — same source. Listing 1 (HLSL):
  ```hlsl
  float3 ParallaxOffsetAndDepth(float2 vTexCoord, float3 vViewTS, float3 vNormalTS, float fLayerThickness)
  {
    float3 vReflectionTS = reflect(-vViewTS, vNormalTS);
    float3 vTransTS = float3(vReflectionTS.xy, -vReflectionTS.z);
    float fTransDist = fLayerThickness / abs(vTransTS.z);
    float2 vTexelSize = float2(1.0/1024.0, 1.0/1024.0);
    float2 vOffset = vTexelSize * fTransDist * vTransTS.xy;
    return float3(vTexCoord + vOffset, fTransDist);
  }
  ```
  — same source
- Oat's GDC 2006 ATI session was billed as "thin volume rendering for realistic, creepy looking biological materials" — [Game Developer, ATI sponsored sessions](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/feature-sponsored-sessions-ati-gdctv-); parallax occlusion mapping was first published 2005 (Brawley & Tatarchuk, ShaderX3) and showcased in ATI's SM3.0 "ToyShop" demo on Radeon X1800 — [Wikipedia, Parallax occlusion mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallax_occlusion_mapping); [ToyShop, SIGGRAPH history](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=48401)
- A modern minimal "ice" variant (Godot, CC0) bends the view ray by the normal and scales by depth: `vec3 rd = normalize(p - ro - normal * refractive_index) * depth; vec2 offset = rd.xz; under = texture(under_texture, UV + offset + normal.xy)`; the author describes `depth` as "how far away the bottom texture appears to be"; a commenter: "only seems to work well on flat surfaces" — [godotshaders.com, Parallax Ice](https://godotshaders.com/shader/parallax-ice-for-fake-depth-to-plane-meshes/)

### Inferences (math and SM3 budget; my derivation unless cited)
- Unified ray–AABB step. In box space with half-extents h and box-space ray origin P, direction D: `t_i = (sign(D_i)*h_i − P_i) / D_i` for i∈{x,y,z}; `t = min(t_x,t_y,t_z)`; hit `Q = P + t·D`; lookup `texCUBE(cube, Q − C)` (Lagarde) or `tex2D(face_i, Q.jk)` (interior mapping, face chosen by which axis gave the min). The divisions are three reciprocals (a `rcp` per component on a float3, i.e. three scalar RCPs in ps_3_0; D3D9 HLSL `1/D` compiles to RCP+MUL). van Dongen's `ceil(y/d)` adds one more divide per axis unless d is pre-reciprocated (`ceil(y*invD)*d`), so his per-pixel core is ~3 rcp + a handful of mad/min/cmp plus 1–4 texture reads, which is why it fit ps_2_0's 64 slots.
- Water-cell geometry. If the water cell is viewed from above and the only interior surfaces are the bed and horizontal detritus layers, the walls are never needed: each layer at depth d_k below the surface is a plane parallel to the surface, so Oat's closed form applies per layer: `s_k = d_k / |T.z|`, `uv_k = uv + s_k * T.xy * texelScale`. That is one `rcp` (shared across layers if you compute `1/|T.z|` once) plus one mad and one texture read per layer. With a bed + 3 detritus layers that is 4 tex2D, ~1 rcp, ~10 ALU — negligible even on ps_2_0; ps_3_0 (D3D9 PC, 512+ instruction slots, dynamic branching) is not a constraint.
- Refraction instead of Oat's "negated reflection". Oat's transmission vector is a mirror of the reflection vector (a convenience, not Snell). For water the perceptually right choice is the refracted view vector: `T = refract(-V_ts, N_ts, 1/1.33)` (HLSL `refract` is available in ps_3_0; it expands to ~8–10 ALU). Because the per-pixel normal N comes from the animated ripple normal map, every layer's lookup is displaced by `d_k * T.xy/|T.z|`, so deeper layers swing more with the ripple than shallow ones — exactly the "depth and ripples" cue sought. The Godot shader is the cheap approximation of this (`V − N*k`, no Snell).
- Reading as a "box": the cube/box machinery (three slabs) only earns its cost if the cell has visible vertical sides (aquarium walls, pool edges, a rock pool with a rim). For open water with tiled cells, vertical faces would create visible cell seams (van Dongen's figure 8 seam problem), so prefer planes only. If side faces are wanted, use Lagarde's min-of-three-slabs with a single cube texture per cell type.
- Composition order per pixel (inference): sample layers back-to-front (bed, then detritus k = N..1), `lerp` by each layer's alpha (Oat's layer blending), optionally attenuate each by an exponential of `s_k` for water absorption (Oat's "transmission distance" output exists for exactly this), then apply Fresnel-weighted reflection on top (van Dongen: "By blending the interior's colour with a reflection map, both the interior and the reflections in the windows can be shown").

## Key question 4: Known limits — box edges and seams, parallax failing when the ray exits the box, tiling of interior cubes, animated contents (video / flipbook / flow-advected), blending with real refraction passes.

### Takeaway
The documented failure modes are: edge/seam discontinuities where the proxy box meets a differently-sized neighbour or where the cube faces meet; wrong results when the reflected/refracted camera or the hit falls outside the box (worse for glossy, worse when blending multiple probes); grazing-angle blow-up of the one division; and seams at tile corners. Animated interiors are explicitly endorsed by the original paper (animated textures, render-to-texture) but no source documents flow-map advection of interior textures; that is an inference.

### Cited Findings
- behc: "edge artifacts can't be eliminated because the cube-map contains limited information about the scene, and using different AABB sizes produces some discontinuities in the projection" — [GameDev.net 2010 thread](https://www.gamedev.net/forums/topic/568829-box-projected-cubemap-environment-mapping/)
- Lagarde: "the parallax-correction step describe above only work perfectly with mirror surface"; for glossy "artists must ensure that the reflected camera will always remain within the box volume"; normal perturbation adds distortion that "increases with the angle between the reflection plane normal and the perturbed normal"; concave geometry stays wrong; blending is "even worse when blending multiple cubemaps"; object-based cubemaps have "lighting seams between adjacent objects using different cubemaps" — [Lagarde 2012 blog](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2012/09/29/image-based-lighting-approaches-and-parallax-corrected-cubemap/)
- Prefiltering vs. parallax: "applying the pre-filtering before the parallax correction will result in errors, even if the proxy geo is a perfect fit"; "the basic problems of being limited to essentially projecting from the 2d walls of the cubemaps AABB will always be there"; mitigation: omit in-room objects from the cube and "composite them over the top using a different technique (like screen-space reflections, planar mirrors, etc)" — [GameDev.net, Localizing image-based reflections](https://www.gamedev.net/forums/topic/667710-localizing-image-based-reflections-issues-questions/)
- Engine practice: Bevy's default proxy boxes are "slightly larger than the room to avoid artifacts along the edges of the room that occur due to rounding error" — [bevy docs, ParallaxCorrection](https://doc.qu1x.dev/bevy_trackball/bevy_light/enum.ParallaxCorrection.html); in Qt Quick 3D, "with parallax correction turned on the size is also used to calculate the distance of the reflections in the cube map" — [Qt ReflectionProbe docs](https://doc.qt.io/qt-6/qml-qtquick3d-reflectionprobe.html)
- Cube-face seams are a filtering/hardware issue independent of parallax: standard cubemap filtering "performs filtering independently on each cube face" and "bilinear texel filtering cannot fetch across cube faces, producing a hard seam artifact"; fix is per-mip seam averaging/angular-extent filtering — [SIGGRAPH history listing (cubemap filtering)](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=99907)
- Interior-mapping seams: "there is a seam at the corner of the building ... this seam is hidden by a piece of exterior wall at the corner"; furniture planes "will show distortions and even seams" on curved or cornered geometry; "it is not advisable to add furniture planes to highly curved objects"; the horizontal-ray divide-by-zero is "negligible" in practice — [van Dongen 2008 PDF](https://www.proun-game.com/Oogst3D/CODING/InteriorMapping/InteriorMapping.pdf)
- Naive alternative fails: "if you just use a simple UV offset type parallax you end up with an infinite interior and nasty perspective distortion. Using a box fixes this"; a curved building was "broke[n] up into various material IDs so that we could alter the orientation of the mapping" — [Polycount thread (developer comment)](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2651410)
- Animated contents: van Dongen — "By using an animated texture, the objects on the furniture plane can actually move. Animated textures usually require a lot of storage, so only short animations can be done this way"; "Through the use of render to texture, more complex animations could be shown on the furniture plane" — [van Dongen 2008 PDF](https://www.proun-game.com/Oogst3D/CODING/InteriorMapping/InteriorMapping.pdf); UDK gem: material "could be expanded to ... use animating textures on the virtual walls and or floors" — [UDK gem](https://docs.unrealengine.com/udk/Three/DevelopmentKitGemsInteriorMapping.html)
- Variation to defeat tiling: per-room 3D noise for lit/unlit (`r < c`), texture atlas with random room pick, bounded random wall displacement ("only two walls need to be checked") — [van Dongen 2008 PDF](https://www.proun-game.com/Oogst3D/CODING/InteriorMapping/InteriorMapping.pdf); UE5 asset picks rooms "from a Texture2D Array using facade UVs, object position, and ISM/HISM per-instance randomness" — [UE forum, Roberto Filip interior cubemap system](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/roberto-filip-interior-cubemap-system-parallax-facade-shader/2779225)
- Curved/tiled surfaces: Houdini's Karma Room Map notes "traditional interior mapping normally works on a single flat plane" while its version "supports even tiled and curved surfaces" — [SideFX Karma Room Map docs](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/solaris/karma_room_map.html)
- Opaque, one-node implementations exist (UE5 "Fake Interior Pro": one Custom HLSL node on an opaque default-lit surface, so "avoids interior geometry and translucency sorting") — [Fab listing](https://www.fab.com/listings/a5062af1-de74-4b3b-97ff-67fd4dca53db)
- Spider-Man 2 shipped two window systems: "parallax occlusion interiors (not to be confused with the RT interiors) which use emissive cubemaps and required a custom baking pipeline" — [Jtuason ArtStation, Spider-Man 2 Window Shader and Pipeline](https://jtuason.artstation.com/projects/XgVxQL) (403 on fetch; from search excerpt)

### Inferences
- "Ray exits the box": for a top-viewed water cell the entering ray always hits the far side of the slab stack; the exit problem only appears (a) at grazing angles where `1/|T.z|` explodes — clamp `|T.z|` to a minimum (e.g. 0.1) or clamp `s_k`, which Oat does not do in Listing 1, and (b) if a cube with vertical faces is used and the refracted ray reaches a side face at the cell boundary, where it should instead continue into the neighbouring cell. Using repeating (wrap) 2D layer textures with world-space UVs removes (b) entirely because there are no side faces; the "box" degenerates into an infinite slab, which is what Oat and the Godot shader do.
- Flow-advected interiors: any per-layer `tex2D(layer, uv_k)` can be replaced by a two-phase flow-map lookup (`uv_k + flow*t0`, `uv_k + flow*t1`, lerp by phase) at +1 texture read and ~6 ALU per layer; nothing in the literature does this for interior mapping specifically (gap), but it is a straightforward composition because the layer lookup is just a 2D UV. Flipbook/video on a layer is exactly van Dongen's animated furniture plane.
- Mixing with a real refraction pass: because the fake layers are composed inside the water material (opaque or a single alpha pass), a conventional grab-pass refraction of real submerged geometry can be sampled at the same distorted screen UV and composited behind the fake layers using the scene-depth difference; the two compete only where real geometry sits between two fake layers. The GameDev.net advice (keep dynamic things out of the cube and composite them "over the top") is the same principle.
- SM3-specific: cube texture reads (`texCUBE`) and `refract`/`reflect` are all ps_2_0/ps_3_0 features; dynamic branching in ps_3_0 lets layers be skipped where alpha is 0 (Tiny Tina's Wonderlands used dynamic branching for its ice parallax on Xbox One, see Q5).

### Gaps
- No source quantifies the artifact-free angular range of box projection or interior mapping; the clamp values above are engineering judgement.
- No source on flow-map-advected interior textures.

## Key question 5: Later games or papers that used the "cube/slab with stuff inside" trick for water, ice, resin, aquarium volumes, snow globes or puddles.

### Takeaway
There is a clear lineage of layered-parallax "stuff inside" materials: Oat's 2006 ATI gooey/volumetric layers (SM3), DICE's Star Wars Battlefront (2015) frozen lake on Hoth (parallaxed distorted layers plus a negated cubemap for fake translucency), Dragon Age: Inquisition frozen lakes (per a Skyrim modder's comparison), Tiny Tina's Wonderlands ice (cheap parallax over the landscape material, dynamic branching for Xbox One), plus hobby/engine shaders (Godot "Parallax Ice", Leadwerks ice, Skyrim multilayer parallax). No published use of full box/cube interior mapping for water or aquariums was found; the liquid/ice uses are all slab (horizontal-layer) variants, which is the right primitive for the water-cell idea.

### Cited Findings
- Oat 2006: two-layer human heart demo (outer albedo+normal+opacity, inner albedo of "subsurface vein networks"), "no limit to the number of material layers", depth parallax + light scattering — [Oat, Rendering Gooey Materials (PDF)](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2006/Chapter4-Rendering_gooey_materials_with_multiple_layers.pdf)
- Star Wars: Battlefront (Hoth): the frozen lake and ice blocks "used parallaxing distorted texture layers, plus a negated cubemap for fake translucency" — [John Troive, ArtStation: Twilight on Hoth – Frozen ice lake](https://johntroive.artstation.com/projects/Vg5Von) (403 on fetch; from search excerpt)
- Tiny Tina's Wonderlands: the ice material "uses a cheap parallax effect over a landscape material beneath it, with dynamic branching to run on Xbox One" — [Sasha Mills, ArtStation](https://sashamills.artstation.com/projects/eJ5rYP) (from search excerpt)
- Skyrim "Multilayer Parallaxing" mod: adds a subsurface texture "that seems to sit inside the geometry and shifts as the view changes"; author states "Dragon Age: Inquisition's frozen lakes use a more advanced version of this effect" — [Nexus Mods 65531](https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrim/mods/65531) (403 on fetch; from search excerpt)
- Leadwerks ice: parallax occlusion on the diffuse only, "keeps the surface normals and the outer texture coordinates unshifted, so the surface stays put while the ice appears to sit beneath it" — [Leadwerks blog, Cool ice effect](https://www.leadwerks.com/community/blogs/entry/2815-cool-ice-effect/)
- Godot "Parallax Ice": depth-scaled, normal-bent view offset on a displacement "under" texture, blended over albedo; same setup "gives a gemstone look" — [godotshaders.com](https://godotshaders.com/shader/parallax-ice-for-fake-depth-to-plane-meshes/)
- Valve PaintBlob ice interior shader "simulates the appearance of ice as a translucent material", blending "a blurred environment map, the interior color and ambient lighting" (flagged as broken in SFM) — [Valve Developer Wiki, PaintBlob](https://developer.valvesoftware.com/wiki/PaintBlob)
- Chris Oat's GDC 2006 slide deck is cited by a parallax-layers guide for "computing the inner layer's texture coordinates from viewing angle and layer depth" — [Oregon State ShaderTricks.pdf](https://web.engr.oregonstate.edu/~mjb/cs557/Projects/Papers/ShaderTricks.pdf)
- Spider-Man (2018) windows: van Dongen: Spider-Man's windows "very likely" use interior mapping but "I haven't seen any official statement by Insomniac"; artifacts cited: a door where a window should be, different interiors from different corners — [Game Developer article](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/interior-mapping-rendering-real-rooms-without-geometry); Spider-Man 2 added people in windows — [Automaton West](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/20231201-23558/); Spider-Man 2 "parallax occlusion interiors ... use emissive cubemaps" — [Jtuason ArtStation](https://jtuason.artstation.com/projects/XgVxQL); Insomniac's SIGGRAPH Asia 2018 brief covers "IG-Impostors" for distant city, not windows — [SIGGRAPH Asia 2018, Spider-Man IG-impostors](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=73423); GDC 2019 "Marvel's Spider-Man: A Technical Postmortem" — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026496)
- Zucconi frames interior mapping as "a very advanced parallax effect" and notes alternatives using "reflection probes and cube maps" — [Alan Zucconi](https://www.alanzucconi.com/?p=10105)

### Inferences
- The shipped liquid/ice examples all drop the vertical faces and keep (a) a surface normal map that stays put, (b) one or more inner layers offset by view (sometimes bent by the normal), (c) a cubemap for reflection/translucency. That is the exact recipe for "ripples + suspended detritus on shallow water": surface normal animates, detritus layers at 2–3 depths get offset by the refracted view, bed at the bottom, Fresnel reflection on top.
- Nothing found uses a true cube (six-face) interior for water. The cube form matters only when vertical sides must be seen (puddle rims, aquarium glass); for those, the Battlefront "ice block" case is the closest shipped analogue, but its implementation details beyond the quoted sentence are unknown.

### Gaps
- Battlefront, Dragon Age: Inquisition and Wonderlands details come from artist portfolio excerpts, not engineering write-ups; depth counts, costs and shader code are not public.
- No paper or talk was found specifically on "parallax interior for puddles" or "snow globe / aquarium box impostor".

## Key question 6 (constraint): What fits Direct3D 9 / Shader Model 3?

### Takeaway
Everything in this family was born on SM2/SM3 hardware: interior mapping fits ps_2_0's 64 instructions (van Dongen), GPU Gems' localized cubemap is 2004 Cg, Oat's layered depth parallax is 2006 ATI SM3 HLSL, and Lagarde's parallax-corrected cubemaps ran on PS3/360 (SM3-class GPUs) at ~0.25 ms per 25% screen coverage. A water-cell shader with bed + 3 detritus slabs + refracted offsets + cube reflection is comfortably inside a single ps_3_0 pass.

### Cited Findings
- Interior mapping "within the 64 instructions allowed in pixel shader model 2.0 ... and also add a diffuse and reflection map in the same pass"; benchmarked on a GeForce 6600GT (SM3 card) — [van Dongen 2008 PDF](https://www.proun-game.com/Oogst3D/CODING/InteriorMapping/InteriorMapping.pdf)
- Lagarde's method: "extremely high performance" on PS3 and Xbox 360; 0.25 ms (25% coverage) / 0.75 ms (75%) per mixed cubemap on PS3 — [SIGGRAPH 2012 listing](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=140207); [Lagarde blog](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2012/09/29/image-based-lighting-approaches-and-parallax-corrected-cubemap/)
- Oat's HLSL (ATI Research, 2006 course) — [Oat PDF](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2006/Chapter4-Rendering_gooey_materials_with_multiple_layers.pdf); parallax occlusion mapping showcased on Radeon X1800 "Ultra-Threaded SM 3.0" (ToyShop) — [Wikipedia POM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallax_occlusion_mapping)
- GPU Gems ch.19 Cg code (2004) for sphere-proxy localized cubemaps — [GPU Gems ch.19](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-iii-materials/chapter-19-image-based-lighting)
- UDK (UE3, D3D9-capable) shipped an interior-mapping material gem — [UDK gem](https://docs.unrealengine.com/udk/Three/DevelopmentKitGemsInteriorMapping.html)
- Burnout Paradise PC (2009) itself ran on D3D9-era hardware at high res with soft shadows and motion blur — [bit-tech press release](https://bit-tech.net/press-releases/ea-s-burnout-paradise-revs-its-engines-on-the-pc/1/)

### Inferences
- Suggested SM3 water-cell budget (estimate): 1 normal-map read (ripples, optionally 2 reads for flow), `refract` (~10 ALU), `rcp(|T.z|)` once, per layer 1 mad + 1 tex2D + 1 lerp (+1 exp for absorption), 1 texCUBE reflection + Fresnel (~6 ALU), optional grab-pass refraction read. For bed + 3 layers: ~7 texture reads, ~40–60 ALU — well under ps_3_0 limits and comparable to van Dongen's SM2 budget plus a cube read. Use `clamp(abs(T.z), 0.1, 1)` to avoid the grazing blow-up.
- Avoid ps_2_0 if flow-advected layers and absorption are wanted (texture reads and dependent reads add up); ps_3_0 is the right target.

### Gaps
- No measured D3D9 timing exists for any of these shaders on specific SM3 GPUs beyond van Dongen's 6600GT frame counts and Lagarde's PS3 ms figures.
