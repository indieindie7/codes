# Real elevation data as game-terrain base + biome/climate layout layer

Scope: using public DEM patches (FUEL-style) as the base of 1-8 km game maps for a Python pipeline producing 128-256 cell heightmaps for an old engine (UE2); choosing patches by landform class; laying out biomes/materials by climate-like rules; building a reference set for scoring generated terrain. Research date 2026-10-06. Items marked **[inference]** are my reasoning, not sourced claims; items marked **[unverified memory]** are from training knowledge that I could not confirm with a fetched source in this pass.

## Key Question 1 — Data sources, access, licences; which give the best 10 m-class data for 2-5 km maps

### Takeaway
For a 2-5 km patch, the only free 10 m-class global-ish option is USGS 3DEP 1/3 arc-second (CONUS only; public domain; reachable via py3dep or OpenTopography's 3DEP raster API without academic status). Outside the US you are at 30 m (Copernicus GLO-30 is the best 30 m quality/licence combination; SRTM GL1 and ASTER are public domain but noisier; AW3D30 needs a JAXA credit). A 30 m tile gives only ~67-167 cells across a 2-5 km map, so a 128-256 cell map at that scale requires amplification (Q3). 1 m lidar exists for most of the US (AWS `usgs-lidar-public`, public domain) but is a point cloud/DSM-DTM product that needs more processing.

### Inferences
- **[inference]** For the user's hobby/non-commercial mod work, every dataset above is usable; only AW3D30 (credit line) and Copernicus (attribution) need a line in the mod readme; MERIT's NC clause is irrelevant for a non-commercial mod but would bite if the maps are ever sold.
- **[inference]** Cell-count arithmetic: a 2 km map at 30 m = 67 cells; 5 km at 30 m = 167 cells; 8 km at 30 m = 267 cells. So a 30 m DEM natively fits a 128-256 heightmap only for maps of roughly 4-8 km. 3DEP 10 m fits 1.3-2.6 km at 128-256 cells. For "best 10 m-class data for 2-5 km maps" the answer is 3DEP 1/3 arc-second (US), via py3dep or OpenTopography's 3DEP API (no academic status needed); outside the US, GLO-30 or SRTM GL1 plus amplification.
- **[inference]** For the pipeline: fetch once with a per-run cache (50 calls/day non-academic is tight for iterative experiments; the AWS Copernicus COGs have no call quota and can be windowed-read directly with rasterio's `/vsis3/` or HTTPS COG range requests, which is the better route for repeated patch extraction).
- **[inference]** DSM vs DTM matters for the 30 m optical products: AW3D30/ASTER/Copernicus include canopy and buildings, so forested valleys will look 20-30 m "filled in". SRTM partially penetrates canopy; 3DEP is bare earth. For a bare-ground game heightmap, prefer 3DEP (US) or SRTM/Copernicus with a vegetation mask correction (see Q3).

## Key Question 2 — Choosing regions: landform classification of DEMs; famous real places games copied

### Takeaway
Three off-the-shelf classification schemes exist and one (Geomorpho90m) is pre-computed globally at 90 m under CC BY 4.0, including a geomorphon layer with 10 landform classes and ruggedness/curvature layers; use it to search for "fluvial hills", "mesa/tableland", "alpine", "coastal" candidate windows, then fetch the fine DEM only for the winners. Hammond's classes (slope + relief + profile) are the natural vocabulary for the user's "landform class" tags.

### Cited Findings
- Geomorpho90m (Amatulli et al. 2020, Scientific Data): 26 standardized variables derived from MERIT DEM at 3 arc-sec (~90 m), also 100 m Equi7 and 7.5 arc-sec (~250 m). Variables: 11 first-order (slope, aspect, aspect-cosine/sine, eastness, northness, convergence, compound topographic index, stream power index, dx, dy); 5 second-order (profile curvature, tangential curvature, dxx, dyy, dxy); 9 ruggedness (elevation std-dev, TRI, roughness, VRM, TPI, dev-magnitude, dev-scale, rough-magnitude, rough-scale); 1 geomorphon layer. Downloads: 100 m Equi7 at Spatial-Ecology, 90 m WGS84 at OpenTopography, 250 m at PANGAEA. Licence CC BY 4.0 — [Amatulli et al. 2020 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7256046/); project page [glowabio.org Geomorpho90m](https://glowabio.org/project/geomorpho90m/).
- Geomorphons (Jasiewicz & Stepinski 2013): ternary line-of-sight patterns; 498 distinct geomorphons reduced to 10 common landform classes: flat, peak/summit, ridge, shoulder, spur, slope, hollow, footslope, valley, pit/depression — [Geomorphons paper (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169555X12005028); class list also in [Amatulli 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7256046/).
- Iwahashi & Pike (2007): unsupervised pixel-based classification of SRTM30 into 16 terrain types from three variables: slope, surface texture, local convexity — [Geomorphometric atlas of Romania (describes the method)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17445647.2024.2354712); review context [Geomorphometry and terrain analysis review (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012825222002756).
- Hammond landform classification: landform = slope + local relief + profile type, computed in a 9.65 km neighbourhood window; profile "separates uplands from lowlands... and identifies tablelands"; grouped classes: nearly flat plains, rolling/irregular plains, plains with widely spaced hills or mountains, partially dissected tablelands, hills, low mountains, high mountains. Karagulle et al. modelled global Hammond landforms from 250 m elevation — [Improved Hammond's Landform Classification (OSU)](https://ir.library.oregonstate.edu/downloads/4x51hk80p); [Karagulle et al. (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/315059734_Modeling_global_hammond_landform_regions_from_250-m_elevation_data_Karagulle); [ESRI ModelBuilder landform paper](https://proceedings.esri.com/library/userconf/proc05/papers/pap2206.pdf).
- Real places copied in games: MotorStorm (2006) is set in Monument Valley (Arizona/Utah border; sandstone buttes); Evolution Studios shipped helicopter flyover footage as an extra to show how accurately the landscape was represented — [MotorStorm wiki: Monument Valley](https://motorstorm.fandom.com/wiki/Monument_Valley); [Making of MotorStorm (Definition Magazine)](https://definitionmagazine.com/features/making-of-motorstorm/). FUEL (Asobo/Codemasters 2009): "5,000 square miles... blends satellite data and procedurally generated data", regions "from the Utah salt-plains, to the Grand Canyon and snow-capped Mount Rainier, all modelled from satellite data" — [VideoGamer FUEL interview](https://www.videogamer.com/previews/fuel-interview/); map ~14,000 km² (128 km x 128 km), deterministic procedural content, apparent ~1 m resolution — [The Escapist: The Future is Procedural](https://www.escapistmagazine.com/the-future-is-procedural/); Guinness record for largest console driving environment — [Wikipedia: Fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_(video_game)). Ghost Recon Wildlands used an on-site Bolivia reference trip and 11 ecosystems studied by "heat, drought, wetness, erosion, rocks, vegetation, colors" — [80.lv Wildlands landscape pipeline](https://80.lv/articles/landscape-and-material-pipeline-of-ghost-recon-wildlands). Asobo's later Flight Simulator terrain talk is the matured version of the FUEL approach — [Asobo: Designing the Terrain System of Flight Simulator (PDF)](https://www.asobostudio.com/files/inline-images/Designing_Terrain_System_Fuentes_Lionel.pdf).

### Inferences
- **[inference]** Practical "region picker" for the pipeline: download the Geomorpho90m geomorphon + slope + TRI + TPI layers for a few continents, slide a window the size of the target map (e.g. 4 km = ~44 cells at 90 m), compute the class histogram and relief stats per window, and tag windows by simple rules: *fluvial hills* = high valley+spur+slope share, moderate relief (100-400 m), low flat share; *mesa/badlands* = bimodal elevation, high flat share at two levels, shoulder+footslope ring, high TRI at the step; *alpine* = high peak/ridge share, relief > 800 m, high slope; *coastal* = window touches the ocean mask with a gradient from flat to hills. Keep the top-N windows per tag as the reference set.
- **[inference]** Because Geomorpho90m is 90 m, use it only to *find* regions; then fetch the finer DEM (3DEP 10 m / GLO-30) for the chosen window.
- **[inference]** The same per-window statistics (geomorphon histogram, slope distribution, hypsometric curve, TPI spectrum) form the scoring vector for generated terrain: compute them on the generated heightmap at matched cell size and compare with the reference class's distribution (e.g. Wasserstein distance on the slope histogram, chi-square on the geomorphon histogram).
- **[inference]** Famous real-place candidates matching the user's tags (my suggestions, not sourced): mesa/badlands -> Monument Valley, Badlands NP (SD), Canyonlands; fluvial hills -> Appalachian Plateau (KY/WV), Palouse (WA); alpine -> Sierra Nevada, Tetons; coastal -> Big Sur, Oregon coast. All are in 3DEP 10 m coverage (US), which is why a US-first reference set is convenient.

## Key Question 3 — Adapting a real patch to a game map (resampling, relief scaling, edge blending, DSM->DTM, seams, amplification, UE2 16-bit range)

### Takeaway
Workflow: reproject to a local metric CRS, window-read, resample with area averaging when shrinking and bicubic/Lanczos when enlarging, optionally amplify with Guérin-style dictionary synthesis (or a Perlin/erosion detail pass) when the source is 30 m and the target cell is 10-20 m, rescale relief to the design range, blend edges into generated terrain with a distance-based falloff, and quantize to UE2's 16-bit G16 heightmap. Hard sourced numbers for UE2's height-unit formula were not found; treat it as a calibration step.

### Cited Findings
- DSM vs DTM: DSM "includes all objects"; DTM is "the bare ground surface"; DTMs "are created from high resolution DSM datasets using complex algorithms to filter out buildings and other objects... 'bare-earth extraction'" — [Wikipedia: DEM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_elevation_model). 3DEP DEMs are bare-earth lidar-derived products; the lidar point clouds on AWS are classified LAZ/EPT — [AWS registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/usgs-lidar/).
- Terrain amplification: Guérin et al. 2016, "Sparse representation of terrains for procedural modeling" (Computer Graphics Forum / Eurographics 2016) represents elevation as a sparse linear combination of landform "atoms" from a dictionary built from exemplar DEMs; applications include "terrain amplification and synthesis from a coarse sketch"; a Matlab implementation is on GitHub — [GitHub eric-guerin/terrain-amplification](https://github.com/eric-guerin/terrain-amplification); [paper (Wiley)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/cgf.12821). Follow-up: "Multi-theme generative adversarial terrain amplification" (SIGGRAPH Asia 2019) — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3355089.3356553).
- UE2 heightmaps are 16-bit grayscale (TEXF_G16, Format value 10 in package data); UnrealEd 3 / BatchExportCommandlet export BMP; "almost no regular image manipulation tool supports 16 bit grayscale images" (G16ed is the usual editor) — [Unreal Wiki: UE2 Exporting heightmaps](https://unrealarchive.org/wikis/unreal-wiki/UE2:Exporting_heightmaps.html). TerrainInfo.TerrainScale X/Y set horizontal cell size and Z the height; 8-bit maps can be imported and converted to G16 with the "convert to G16" context menu; a 256x256 TerrainMap with TerrainScale 1 covers 256 grid units — [BeyondUnreal Wiki: Terrain](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/terrain.html); [Creating A Terrain](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/creating-a-terrain.html); [Legacy:Terrain](https://unrealarchive.org/wikis/unreal-wiki/Legacy:Terrain.html).
- Ghost Recon Wildlands used World Machine heightmaps processed through Houdini for tiling/LOD, later replaced with an in-engine sculpt editor; procedural material placement by "slope, height, flow map, density map, deposition map, curvature" — [80.lv Wildlands pipeline](https://80.lv/articles/landscape-and-material-pipeline-of-ghost-recon-wildlands); GDC 2017 talk by Guillaume Werlé and Benoit Martinez — [Vimeo: Terrain Technology and Tools](https://vimeo.com/207479253).

### Inferences (procedure for the Python pipeline)
- **[inference]** Reproject first: pick a UTM zone (pyproj) for the patch and `rasterio.warp.reproject` into a square metric grid at the target cell size; working in lat/lon squashes cells away from the equator.
- **[inference]** Resampling choice: downsampling (e.g. 10 m -> 20 m) use `Resampling.average` (area) to avoid aliasing ridges; upsampling (30 m -> 15 m) use `Resampling.cubic` or `lanczos`, then add detail, because bicubic alone yields a blurry "soap" surface at 2x+ magnification.
- **[inference]** Amplification ladder, cheapest first: (a) bicubic + band-limited fractal noise scaled by local slope (detail only on steep cells); (b) bicubic + a short thermal/hydraulic erosion pass that re-carves gullies consistent with the coarse flow field (the pipeline already has erosion); (c) Guérin dictionary synthesis using 10 m 3DEP patches of the same landform class as the dictionary — this is the method that literally fits the user's "reference set by landform class" idea, since the dictionary *is* the reference set.
- **[inference]** Relief scaling: game maps usually need exaggerated relief at small footprints; scale heights by a factor k but clamp slopes (recompute gradient after scaling, and if max slope exceeds the engine's walkable/visual limit, locally reduce k). Keep hypsometric shape (normalize to 0-1 by percentile 0.5-99.5 to kill lidar spikes).
- **[inference]** DSM -> DTM cheap fix for 30 m products: a morphological opening (minimum filter of radius ~2-3 cells followed by maximum) removes tree-canopy blobs and buildings on flat land; or subtract a canopy height estimate where a vegetation mask (e.g. from the geomorphon "flat" + a crude texture measure) says forest. For lidar, use the ground-classified points (class 2) to grid a DTM with PDAL from the EPT bucket; roads then appear as smooth surfaces and can be left or re-noised.
- **[inference]** Edge blending into generated terrain: compute a signed-distance falloff w(d) from the patch boundary (e.g. smoothstep over 10-15 % of the map width), match mean and slope at the seam by first fitting a low-order plane/bicubic surface to the generated terrain in the ring and offsetting the real patch, then blend `h = w*real + (1-w)*generated`; run a few erosion iterations across the seam to hide the frequency mismatch ("seam-hiding").
- **[inference]** UE2 16-bit range: quantize to 0-65535 after the final relief scale; set TerrainScale.Z so the full range spans the design height; UE2's exact UU-per-height-step formula is not sourced here (**[unverified memory]**: height units are divided by 256 before multiplication by TerrainScale.Z in UT2004's TerrainInfo, i.e. 65535 steps span 256*Z UU) — calibrate empirically once by importing a two-level test map and reading actor Z in the editor.
- **[inference]** Minimize quantization banding: use the whole 16-bit range (don't leave 90 % unused), and dither the quantization if any slope is < 1 step per cell.

## Key Question 4 — Biome/climate layout rules usable at 1-8 km (Whittaker, moisture, lapse rate, aspect, tree line; Red Blob; Minecraft/Terraria/Valheim; Far Cry 2, Wildlands, Horizon Zero Dawn)

### Takeaway
At 1-8 km the climate is essentially one point on the Whittaker diagram, so the "biome" layer becomes a *local* climate field: elevation lapse (temperature), flow accumulation / distance to water (moisture), and aspect (insolation), fed into a Whittaker-style lookup to get 3-6 local biome bands plus riparian/wetland classes. Shipped games (Far Cry 2, Wildlands, Horizon) implemented this as artist-authored rule layers (density maps by slope/height/flow/curvature per ecosystem), which is exactly the data model to adopt.

### Cited Findings
- Whittaker's scheme classifies biomes by mean annual precipitation (x) versus mean annual temperature (y); gradient analysis across "intertidal levels, climatic moisture, altitudinal temperature, and latitudinal temperature"; 26 biome-types including tropical/temperate rainforests, temperate deciduous/evergreen forest, taiga, thorn forest/scrub, woodland, shrublands, savanna, temperate grassland, alpine grassland, tundra, four desert types and six wetland types; a simplification of Holdridge's life zones (which uses "30 humidity provinces" in a triangular diagram) — [Wikipedia: Biome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biome); [Whittaker (ecologist)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Whittaker_(ecologist)).
- Lapse rate: Wikipedia's alpine climate page gives a normal lapse rate of 5.5 °C per 1000 m — [Wikipedia: Alpine climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_climate). (The 6.5 °C/km figure is the International Standard Atmosphere environmental lapse rate **[unverified memory]**; both are in common use.)
- Red Blob Games (Patel): elevation as temperature proxy ("higher elevations have lower temperatures"), moisture from a second independent noise map in the noise tutorial, and from distance to fresh water in the polygon map generator; biome by Whittaker-style lookup; thresholds published in code, e.g. `e<0.1 OCEAN; e<0.12 BEACH; e>0.8: m<0.1 SCORCHED, m<0.2 BARE, m<0.5 TUNDRA, else SNOW ...`, "these thresholds will need tuning to match your generator" — [Red Blob: Making maps with noise functions](https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/terrain-from-noise/); polygon map FAQ: "moisture is defined based on distance from fresh water" and used "for distributing vegetation" — [Polygon map generation FAQ](https://simblob.blogspot.com/2012/02/polygon-map-generation-faq.html); code [redblobgames/mapgen2](https://github.com/redblobgames/mapgen2).

### Inferences
- **[inference]** Local climate field at 1-8 km: T(x) = T0 - L*(h(x) - h0) with L in 5.5-6.5 °C/km; over 1 km of relief that is only ~6 °C, i.e. one or two Whittaker rows — enough for a forest -> subalpine -> alpine progression on an alpine patch, nothing on a hills patch. Moisture M(x) = a*log(flow accumulation) + b*(1 - dist_to_water/dmax) - c*aspect_insolation + d*noise; wetlands where flow accumulation is high *and* slope is flat; riparian strip within N cells of channels. Aspect: in the northern hemisphere, south-/southwest-facing slopes get more sun and are drier (lower M), which produces the classic forested north slope / grassy south slope asymmetry at exactly this scale **[inference from general physical geography; not individually sourced]**.
- **[inference]** Tree line: set a cutoff on T (e.g. the mean-summer-temperature threshold) expressed as an elevation band, lowered on exposed ridges (high TPI) and raised in sheltered hollows; use the geomorphon class as the exposure proxy.
- **[inference]** Practical layout: choose the patch's *macro* biome from its real-world Whittaker point (fetch isn't needed; pick by design), then run the local rules to split into 3-6 local biomes; this matches Wildlands' "ecosystem-then-rules" and HZD's rule-graph approach with far less machinery.
- **[unverified memory]** HZD density-map details I recall from the GDC talk but could not confirm in fetched sources: placement rules output per-asset *density maps* built from terrain inputs (slope, altitude, ecotope/biome mask, wetness) via node graphs; asset types have *priority/rank* so higher-ranked assets suppress lower-ranked ones where they overlap ("overlap"/footprint rules), and placement is done by a dithered/jittered grid sampled against density on the GPU in tiles around the player. Treat as plausible but re-check in the talk video before citing.
- **[unverified memory]** Minecraft (post-1.18) uses multi-noise biome selection over temperature, humidity, continentalness, erosion, weirdness and depth; Terraria lays biomes as horizontal bands with hard-coded placement rules; Valheim arranges biomes radially by distance from the world centre (Meadows -> Black Forest -> Swamp/Mountain -> Plains -> Mistlands -> Ashlands/Deep North). Not confirmed in this pass.

## Key Question 5 — Material/texture implications: mapping biome + slope + moisture to the 4-6 layers UE2 can show

### Takeaway
UE2 terrain draws one base texture plus alpha-blended layers; the Wildlands "Terrain Procedural Set" parameter list (slope, height, flow, density, deposition, curvature) is the right rule vocabulary and can be collapsed into 4-6 alpha maps: cliff (slope), deposit/sediment (low slope + deposition), wet/riparian (flow + low slope), dry/exposed (high insolation + convex), snow/alpine (T below threshold), with the biome lookup choosing *which* textures fill those slots.

### Cited Findings
- UE2 terrain: TerrainInfo.Layers[] each with a Texture and an AlphaMap; terrain is a polygon grid from a 16-bit TerrainMap centred on the TerrainInfo actor — [BeyondUnreal Wiki: Terrain](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/terrain.html); [Creating A Terrain](https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/creating-a-terrain.html); [Legacy:Terrain](https://unrealarchive.org/wikis/unreal-wiki/Legacy:Terrain.html).
- Wildlands material rules by "slope, height, flow map, density map, deposition map, curvature", with per-ecosystem rule sets and "cliff, road detections" — [80.lv GRW pipeline](https://80.lv/articles/landscape-and-material-pipeline-of-ghost-recon-wildlands).
- Red Blob: moisture data reused "for distributing vegetation" beyond biome lookup — [Polygon map FAQ](https://simblob.blogspot.com/2012/02/polygon-map-generation-faq.html).

### Inferences
- **[inference]** Suggested 5-layer stack for a UE2 map (each alpha = smoothstep of a rule; normalized so the stack sums to ~1 per cell): L0 base = biome ground (grass/sand/tundra from the Whittaker lookup); L1 cliff = slope > ~35-40° weighted by convex curvature (rock); L2 deposit = low slope and high deposition/flow-accumulation footslopes (gravel/scree/alluvium); L3 wet = high flow accumulation or within riparian distance and T above freezing (mud/dark soil/moss); L4 snow/high = T below threshold, reduced on steep aspects (snow) — or, in hot patches, "dry exposed" = high insolation convex cells (bleached rock/dust). A 6th optional layer: path/detail noise to break tiling.
- **[inference]** Keep alpha maps at the heightmap resolution (128-256) and author them as 8-bit; the hard "cliff" edge is what sells a mesa, so compute slope on the *final quantized* heightmap, not the pre-scaled one, so the rule matches what the engine renders.
- **[inference]** Because UE2 layers are expensive per draw, evaluate which 4 of the 5 rules matter for each biome class (e.g. alpine: base/cliff/scree/snow; badlands: base/cliff/deposit/dry; fluvial hills: base/cliff/wet/deposit) and drop the rest.

### Gaps
- Exact UE2 layer limits (hard maximum and practical per-pass count) and whether the layer textures and alpha maps have resolution constraints were not stated in the fetched wiki pages.
