# Example-based and machine-learned terrain generation from real DEMs (for a 128-1024 cell heightmap pipeline on one RTX 4070 12 GB)

Research date: 2026-10-06. Scope: methods that copy or learn from real elevation data (patch synthesis, GANs, diffusion, super-resolution/amplification), what code and weights are actually downloadable, dataset building from public DEMs, and an honest view of where ML fails versus patch methods for small grids. "Inference" labels are the researcher's own reasoning, not sourced claims.

## Key Question 1: Patch-based synthesis from DEMs (Zhou et al. 2007, Tasse et al. 2012, Guérin et al. 2016 sparse/amplification)

### Takeaway
Patch-based methods are the most "honest" reuse of real DEMs: they paste real relief, so drainage and ridge detail are real by construction, and they need only one or a few exemplar DEMs instead of a training set. Of the three classic papers, only Guérin 2016's amplification has official code (Matlab), but there is a current open-source C++ reimplementation (HighMap) that runs 512^2 in under a second; Zhou 2007 and Tasse 2012 have no public code.

### Cited Findings
- Zhou, Sun, Turk, Rehg 2007, "Terrain Synthesis from Digital Elevation Models", IEEE TVCG 13(4):834-848: patches from an example height field are placed under a user-sketched feature map (ridges/valleys), joined with graph cuts and Poisson editing, ordered by breadth-first traversal of a feature tree. — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Terrain-Synthesis-from-Digital-Elevation-Models-Zhou-Sun/7d78c2882f91262bde731ec646492583d48cb6eb); [dblp](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/tvcg/ZhouSTR07.html)
- Zhou 2007 project page: example DEMs were Mount Jackson, Mount Vernon, Grand Canyon, Flathead; Cape Girardeau listed as a failure case; outputs 2500x2500 to 4097x4097; full-res PDF (75 MB) and pre-print (5 MB) downloadable; code "Coming soon! I am working on porting my code as a World Machine Plug-in" (never released); Terragen terrain/world files for five examples are downloadable. — [howardzzh.com/research/terrain](http://www.howardzzh.com/research/terrain/)
- Tasse, Gain, Marais 2012, "Enhanced Texture-Based Terrain Synthesis on Graphics Hardware", CGF: curvilinear features extracted from a 2D sketched feature map constrain patch-based synthesis of real landscapes; a new patch-merging method reduces boundary artifacts from overlapping patches; GPU parallel implementation gives "a significant speedup depending on the size of the example terrain"; claimed experimentally more realistic than prior example-based methods. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-8659.2012.03076.x)
- Gain, Merry, Marais 2015, "Parallel, Realistic and Controllable Terrain Synthesis", CGF 34(2) — the follow-up by the same group (listed in search results; not fetched in detail). — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/cgf.12545)
- Guérin, Digne, Galin, Peytavie 2016, "Sparse representation of terrains for procedural modeling", CGF 35(2) (Eurographics 2016): terrain as a sparse combination of dictionary primitives ("Sparse Construction Tree"); dictionary learned automatically; demonstrated for inverse procedural modeling, terrain amplification, and synthesis from coarse sketches. — [Guérin blog](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/eguerin/new/blog/sparse-representation-of-terrains-for-procedural-modeling/); [EG diglib](https://diglib.eg.org/items/c92c2a43-3857-48d2-92d5-11761e2dea73)
- Official code: `eric-guerin/terrain-amplification` is a Matlab implementation needing `ompbox` and `ksvdbox`; main call `terrain_super_resolution(factor, exemplarhr, inputterrain, output, masksize, offset_analysis, offset_synthesis)` where `exemplarhr` is a high-res exemplar file and `inputterrain` is the coarse input; users must cite the paper. — [GitHub](https://github.com/eric-guerin/terrain-amplification); [README](https://github.com/eric-guerin/terrain-amplification/blob/master/README.md)
- Modern reimplementation: HighMap (C++ heightmap library) PR #439 "add exemplar-based terrain super-resolution" implements Guérin 2016: dictionary of block-mean-downsampled exemplar patches paired with high-res counterparts, orthogonal matching pursuit (OMP) sparse coding of input patches, coefficients applied to the paired high-res atoms, mask-weighted blending; API `hmap::terrain_super_resolution(array, exemplar, factor, patch_size, analysis_stride, synthesis_stride, sparsity)`; sparsity=1 is nearest-atom copy, >1 is full OMP with least-squares refinement; example "completes in well under a second" for 512^2 output with 225 atoms; regions the mask cannot reach fall back to bicubic with warnings. — [HighMap PR 439](https://github.com/ottolink-dev/HighMap/pull/439)
- Lochner 2023 (diffusion paper) summarises the field's view of patch/texture synthesis: GPU acceleration [Tasse 2012, Gain 2015] and patch blending exist, but "these techniques suffer from the same limitations as texture synthesis" and do not "always respect global properties, such as realistic drainage patterns." — [Lochner et al. 2023 PDF](https://people.cs.uct.ac.za/~jgain/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/lochner2023.pdf)

### Inferences
- Data needs are tiny compared with ML: one exemplar DEM tile (e.g., one 1x1 degree 3DEP/SRTM tile) is enough for Zhou/Tasse-style synthesis or Guérin amplification; this fits the hobby pipeline's public-domain-DEM allowance with no training step. (Inference from the papers' single-exemplar formulations.)
- At 256^2 the Guérin amplification is effectively instantaneous (HighMap reports sub-second 512^2); a 64^2 sketch -> 256^2 via factor 4 is exactly its intended use ("inputterrain" coarse, "exemplarhr" fine). Quality at factor 4+ in one step is unverified here; the 2016 paper's figures should be checked for the factors they demonstrate (gap).
- Zhou 2007 would need a from-scratch reimplementation (graph cuts + Poisson blend + feature-tree ordering); Python with `pymaxflow`/`scipy` is feasible but is a multi-day project. (Inference.)
- Because patches are real DEM data, the erosion/scoring stages already in the pipeline get realistic drainage locally; the known failure is at patch seams and global flow consistency, which the pipeline's erosion pass may partially repair. (Inference, consistent with Lochner's critique.)

### Gaps
- No run-time figures for Zhou 2007 or Tasse 2012 at 256^2 were found (project page gives none; Wiley abstract gives none).
- The Guérin 2016 paper's demonstrated amplification factors, patch sizes, and timings were not retrievable (blog page lacks them; PDF not fetched).
- No public code for Zhou 2007 or Tasse 2012 found; the 2015 Gain et al. follow-up's code status unknown.

## Key Question 2: GAN approaches (Guérin et al. 2017 cGAN, Spick & Walker 2019, Beckham & Pal 2017, dandrino, Panagiotou & Charou 2020) and public weights

### Takeaway
The 2017 Guérin cGAN is the one GAN with official code, dataset AND a pre-trained model downloadable (pix2pix-tensorflow fork with 16-bit PNG); dandrino's progressive GAN also ships weights (Google Drive) but is unconditional. The others (Spick & Walker, Beckham & Pal, Panagiotou) have papers and in two cases code, but no confirmed pretrained weights. The 2023 Lochner user study found cGAN outputs have small grid artifacts and no notion of style, and users could tell them from real terrain in several categories.

### Cited Findings
- Guérin, Digne, Galin, Peytavie, Wolf, Benes, Martinez 2017, "Interactive Example-Based Terrain Authoring with Conditional Generative Adversarial Networks", ACM TOG 36(6) art. 228 (SIGGRAPH Asia 2017): each synthesizer is a cGAN trained on real terrains paired with sketched counterparts; artist sketches rivers, valleys, ridges (plus elevation cues per the video description), and an "erosion synthesizer can also generate terrain evolution by erosion at a very low computational cost." — [Guérin project page](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/eguerin/new/blog/interactive-example-based-terrain-authoring-with-conditional-generative-adversarial-networks/); [HAL record](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01583706/); [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5w685udM838)
- Official "Deep Terrains" code & data page: TensorFlow pix2pix fork with `--png16bits` (branch `png16bits-support` of `eric-guerin/pix2pix-tensorflow`); downloads `deepterrains-dataset.tgz` (training set for sketch->terrain) and `multi_train.tgz` (pre-trained model, offered "for those who don't have a Titan X"); training command `python pix2pix.py --png16bits --mode train --output_dir multi_train --max_epochs 500 --input_dir multi/train --which_direction AtoB`; "provided without any warranty", no formal licence, credit required. — [Deep Terrains code and data](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/eguerin/new/blog/deep-terrains-code-and-data/); [fork](https://github.com/eric-guerin/pix2pix-tensorflow/tree/png16bits-support)
- Independent reimplementation `nanoxas/sketch-to-terrain` (Keras/TF, pix2pix U-Net with noise injected in the bottleneck; data from NASA Visible Earth topography processed with PySheds/GeoRasters; sketches of ridges, rivers, peaks, basins; author says it replicates the paper's results; no pretrained weights; 10 commits). — [GitHub](https://github.com/nanoxas/sketch-to-terrain)
- Other pix2pix terrain repos: `jayin92/pix2pix-terrain-generator` (Google API altitude + satellite data; adds ridges/wall texture/rivers to coarse maps; separate datasets repo; no resolution/weights info in README) and `jayin92/TerrainGAN` (VAE+GAN). — [pix2pix-terrain-generator](https://github.com/jayin92/pix2pix-terrain-generator); [TerrainGAN](https://github.com/jayin92/vae-pix2pix-terrain-generator)
- Lochner et al. 2023 trained the Guérin cGAN ("using the code from" the authors) on the same dataset as their diffusion model and ran a 2AFC user study (n = 41, 256x256 terrains): users reliably distinguished real from generated for hills but not plains, cliffs, mountains; diffusion beat cGAN for plains and hills; "The cGAN approach lacks any discernible notion of style ... It also has a tendency to produce small grid artifacts", which "can pass for plausible detail at medium viewing distances". — [Lochner 2023 PDF](https://people.cs.uct.ac.za/~jgain/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/lochner2023.pdf)
- Beckham & Pal 2017, "A step towards procedural terrain generation with GANs" (arXiv 1707.03383): two-stage GAN pipeline using openly available NASA satellite imagery; synthesises 512x512 heightmaps plus matching textures (geometry and appearance decoupled). Code: `christopher-beckham/gan-heightmaps` (contains `pix2pix.py`). Pretrained weights not confirmed. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.03383); [GitHub](https://github.com/christopher-beckham/gan-heightmaps); [MIT Tech Review coverage](https://www.technologyreview.com/2017/07/20/150400/deep-learning-creates-earth-like-terrain-by-studying-nasa-satellite-images/)
- Spick & Walker 2019, "Realistic and Textured Terrain Generation using GANs", CVMP 2019: Spatial GAN trained on a satellite dataset of heightmaps + textures as 4-channel images; produces many variants of a target region. Open-access PDF; no code link found. — [White Rose PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/153088/1/Real_world_Textured_terrain_generation_using_GANs_1_.pdf); [York record](https://pure.york.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/realistic-and-textured-terrain-generation-using-gans/)
- A 2019 IEEE CoG paper on Spatial GANs for terrain reports PSGAN heightmaps "exhibit noticeable noise in elevation values and show repeated small-scale undulations" and that GANs "struggle to generate globally coherent terrain structures, such as continuous ridgelines." — [IEEE CoG 2019 paper 57](https://ieee-cog.org/2019/papers/paper_57.pdf)
- dandrino `terrain-erosion-3-ways` (GAN section): Progressive Growing GAN (Karras et al.) trained on ~1000 USGS NED 1x1 degree tiles (3600x3600, 1 arc-second), 512x512 crops, filtered for flatness/corruption/low Shannon entropy, augmented by rotation/flip to ~180,000 samples; "Expect around a week even with a beefy Nvidia Tesla GPU"; results "basically indistinguishable from real-world elevation data"; drawbacks: costly training, "Limited creative control", hard to scale resolution; trained weights are provided via Google Drive link; `download_ned_zips.py` and `generate_training_images.py` are in the repo. — [GitHub](https://github.com/dandrino/terrain-erosion-3-ways); [download_ned_zips.py](https://github.com/dandrino/terrain-erosion-3-ways/blob/master/download_ned_zips.py); [generate_training_images.py](https://github.com/dandrino/terrain-erosion-3-ways/blob/master/generate_training_images.py)
- Panagiotou & Charou 2020, "Procedural 3D Terrain Generation using GANs" (SETN 2020, arXiv 2010.06411): ProGAN generates satellite RGB, a cGAN ("ImageToDEM") predicts the DEM from it; repo `Panagiotou/Procedural3DTerrain` has a download script for paired RGB+DEM but no stated resolution, hardware, or pretrained weights. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.06411); [GitHub](https://github.com/Panagiotou/Procedural3DTerrain)
- Newer cGAN variants exist (e.g., Wu 2026 "Feature-Driven DEM Generation With Enhanced Detail Preservation and Noise Mitigation Using Conditional GANs", Transactions in GIS; a 2026 "Terrain-Factor-Constrained GAN" for DEM downscaling using relief and gradient as physics loss terms) — code status not checked. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/tgis.70232); [MDPI IJGI](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijgi15080353)

### Inferences
- Trainability on a 4070 in hours: pix2pix at 256^2 is a small U-Net; the Guérin recipe is 500 epochs on a Titan X (12 GB, 2016-era) — a 4070 is substantially faster per FLOP and has the same 12 GB, so retraining on their released dataset should take hours to one day, not weeks. The exact epoch count needed for usable results is not stated; the page offers the pre-trained `multi_train.tgz` precisely to avoid training. (Inference; numbers from the Deep Terrains page.)
- dandrino's ProGAN (512^2, ~1 week on a Tesla) is not a hobby-retrain target, but its released weights plus its NED download/preprocessing scripts are directly reusable as a dataset-building template. (Inference.)
- For the pipeline's "designer control" goal, the Guérin cGAN is the only GAN with sketch conditioning and ready weights; Spick/Beckham/dandrino are unconditional (random samples), so they only provide "realistic noise", not control. (Inference.)

### Gaps
- Guérin 2017 resolution, dataset size, GPU training time, and inference time are not given on the pages fetched (HAL PDF blocked by an anti-bot page; ResearchGate 403). The user study and limitations section of the 2017 paper were not read.
- Sizes of `deepterrains-dataset.tgz` and `multi_train.tgz` are not stated; links were not test-downloaded, so whether they still resolve is unverified.
- Spick & Walker and Panagiotou: no confirmation of code or weights availability beyond what is noted.

## Key Question 3: Diffusion models for terrain/DEMs 2022-2026 (released code/weights, VRAM)

### Takeaway
Diffusion has clearly displaced GANs for this task since 2023. Three systems have downloadable code and weights that are relevant: xandergos "Terrain Diffusion"/InfiniteDiffusion (MIT, HF weights, ~2.2 GB peak VRAM at inference, coarse-map/sketch conditioned, 30 m and 90 m per pixel), TerraFusion (SD-based LDM + ControlNet sketches of valleys/ridges/cliffs, 512^2, weights available but PolyForm Noncommercial), and MESA (text-conditioned, Copernicus-trained, MIT, 8 GB VRAM minimum). Lochner et al. 2023 (sketch + style, 256^2, trained on USGS 2.39 m data) say code/add-on/weights are available, but no repository URL was found.

### Cited Findings
- Lochner, Gain, et al. 2023, "Interactive Authoring of Terrain using Diffusion Models", CGF (Pacific Graphics 2023): time-conditioned U-Net with cross-attention, conditioned on a feature sketch (drainage green, ridges red, cliffs blue, flat areas outlined white) and a style vector; training data from USGS elevation + co-registered satellite data over North America at ~2.39 m/px, ~6 million 256x256 float tiles filtered to 3.84 million usable tiles via artifact classifier; tiles concatenated 8x8 and downsampled to 256x256 (~4.9x4.9 km, ~19.1 m/px), ~200,000 tiles, augmented 8x to 1,600,000; "GPU memory limitations impose a cap on image size — 256 x 256 pixels"; batch size 8 "as the largest value that does not result" in OOM; each model trained 72 hours on a partitioned Nvidia A100 (20 GB); cascaded super-resolution models upsample the result; Blender add-on via Blender's Python API; "The full source code, blender add-on, and pre-trained models are available." Limitations: elevation constraints at peaks/junctions "impossible to achieve alignment"; elevation-to-satellite texture synthesis "particularly noisy"; text conditioning "deeply disappointing"; performance "approaches but does not cross the threshold of real-time response (20Hz)"; "flickering and inconsistent outputs after minor adjustments to the canvas." — [Lochner 2023 PDF](https://people.cs.uct.ac.za/~jgain/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/lochner2023.pdf); [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/cgf.14941); [EG diglib](https://diglib.eg.org/items/23e7aa82-e1cb-437d-b1b7-ae99e1933054)
- xandergos "Terrain Diffusion" / "InfiniteDiffusion" (arXiv 2512.08309; repo labelled SIGGRAPH 2026): "A practical, learned successor to Perlin noise for infinite, deterministic, and randomly-accessible real-time terrain generation"; users give coarse maps (procedural or hand-drawn) that are refined to 30 m/px heightmaps, coarse maps upscaled 256x per axis; optional GeoTIFF conditioning on elevation, temperature, precipitation, variability; two HF models: `terrain-diffusion-30m` (30 m/px, 7.7 km coarse, "best for playable worlds") and `terrain-diffusion-90m` (90 m/px, 23 km coarse, more coherent); MIT licence; CUDA GPU recommended, CPU possible but slow. — [GitHub](https://github.com/xandergos/terrain-diffusion); [HF 30m](https://huggingface.co/xandergos/terrain-diffusion-30m); [HF 90m](https://huggingface.co/xandergos/terrain-diffusion-90m)
- InfiniteDiffusion paper numbers: EDM2 backbone with sCM (consistency) modifications; coarse model at 23 km/px; core latent model on 512x512 patches at 90 m (46 km tiles) predicting a 64x64 low-frequency channel + latent; consistency-model decoder; training data MERIT DEM (90 m) + ETOPO1 bathymetry + WorldClim, 2048x2048 equal-area tiles, 80/20 split, "signed-sqrt space"; "Training the entire pipeline end-to-end takes approximately two weeks on an RTX 3090 Ti" (or one week on an RTX 5090); inference on 3090 Ti: time-to-first-tile 1.72 +/- 0.19 s, subsequent tiles 0.66 s, peak VRAM ~2214 MB (1846 MB in half precision); Laplacian low/residual encoding improved FID 21.51 -> 8.11; FID vs baselines: Perlin blending 186.70, naive tiling 74.44, InfiniteDiffusion T=2 14.78; limitations: artifacts at T=2 on ambiguous prompts (T=5 typically needed), inefficient for small uncached regions, no scattered point queries, "Coarse-layer training data acquisition remains challenging." — [arXiv HTML 2512.08309](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08309); [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08309)
- HF card for the 30m model says "trained on the 30m Copernicus GLO-30 dataset" (the 90m card says MERIT DEM). — [HF 30m](https://huggingface.co/xandergos/terrain-diffusion-30m); [HF 90m](https://huggingface.co/xandergos/terrain-diffusion-90m)
- TerraFusion (Sato et al., arXiv 2505.04050, 2025): joint heightmap+texture latent diffusion based on Stable Diffusion VAE/U-Net with extended channels and a dedicated heightmap VAE; 512x512 outputs; data: NASADEM 30 m, 4,119 paired samples from 276 regions, elevations >2000 m and human-modified/cloudy regions excluded; unconditional model ~2 days on RTX A6000 (200 epochs), ControlNet sketch model ~7 days on RTX 6000 Ada (500 epochs); inference ~75 s per sample at 1000 steps unconditional, ~2 s at 20 steps conditional; sketches: valleys red, ridgelines green, cliffs blue (extracted from flow accumulation + Canny); limitations: texture artifacts, "Some heightmaps exhibit unnatural structures", 512^2 cap, no rivers/lakes/tree lines/snow. Code + pretrained weights: `millennium-nova/terra-fusion`, PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.04050v2); [GitHub](https://github.com/millennium-nova/terra-fusion)
- MESA (Borne-Pons et al., CVPR 2025 workshop MORSE, arXiv 2504.07210): text -> paired optical image + elevation using Stable Diffusion 2.1, trained on the Major TOM Core-DEM extension (Copernicus data); inference code `PaulBorneP/MESA`, weights `NewtNewt/MESA`, "Minimum 8GB VRAM recommended", NVIDIA only, Python 3.11, MIT. — [GitHub](https://github.com/PaulBorneP/MESA); [HF](https://huggingface.co/NewtNewt/MESA); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07210v2)
- Terrain Diffusion Network (Chen et al. 2023, arXiv 2308.16725): latent diffusion with pre-trained terrain autoencoders, three synthesizers (structure/intermediate/fine), geological sketch guidance (rivers, ridges, basins, peaks), dataset from NASA topology images; "code and dataset will be publicly available" — no repo found. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.16725)
- Earthbender (2025, ACM): interactive pipeline translating a high-level semantic sketch into a heightmap with a guided diffusion model, trained with HF Accelerate. Code status not checked. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3769047.3769053)
- Other 2023-2026 CGF diffusion/authoring papers (no code checked): "Adaptive & Multi-Resolution Procedural Infinite Terrain Generation with Diffusion Models and Perlin Noise" (2022), Perche et al. 2023 "Authoring Terrains with Spatialised Style", Guérin et al. 2022 "Gradient Terrain Authoring", Grenier 2024 "Real-time Terrain Enhancement with Controlled Procedural Patterns", Huftier "Terrain Synthesis and Authoring based on Iso-Contours". — [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3571600.3571657); [Perche](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cgf.14936); [Gradient](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cgf.14460); [Grenier](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/cgf.14992); [Huftier](https://dx.doi.org/10.1111/cgf.70389)
- Smaller GitHub projects: `cssu/terrain-diffusion`, `ruribev/terrain-difussion`, `VVittgenstein/LEM` (Landlab/Fastscape landscape-evolution data + cascade diffusion, early stage), `finnnosam/Planetary-Terrain-Diffusion`; a search-result description mentions a hybrid DiT model producing 256x256 heightmaps at 30 m/px with a DC-AE decoder, 20,000 updates (repo not identified). — [cssu](https://github.com/cssu/terrain-diffusion); [ruribev](https://github.com/ruribev/terrain-difussion); [LEM](https://github.com/VVittgenstein/LEM); [finnnosam](https://github.com/finnnosam/Planetary-Terrain-Diffusion)
- No Hugging Face model named "sd-heightmap" or a general "terrain-diffusion" SD fine-tune was found; the only HF heightmap-ish hits were xandergos's two models, NewtNewt/MESA, and `wdcqc/starcraft-terrain-64x64` (game tilemaps, not DEMs). — [HF search context](https://huggingface.co/papers?q=heightmap+predictor); [wdcqc](https://huggingface.co/wdcqc/starcraft-terrain-64x64)
- ControlLoRA (~7M params) is a lightweight alternative to full ControlNet for adding spatial conditioning to SD — relevant if fine-tuning SD on heightmaps on 12 GB. — [HF](https://huggingface.co/HighCWu/ControlLoRA); [GitHub](https://github.com/HighCWu/ControlLoRA)

### Inferences
- VRAM fit on a 4070 12 GB: xandergos inference (~2.2 GB peak) fits trivially; MESA (8 GB min) fits; TerraFusion inference at 512^2 is an SD-1.x-class LDM + ControlNet and should fit in 12 GB with fp16 (its training used 48 GB cards). Lochner's 256^2 pixel-space U-Net needed batch 8 on a 20 GB A100 slice, so inference will fit but retraining on a 4070 would mean smaller batches and a longer run than 72 h. (Inference from the cited numbers.)
- Control match to the pipeline: TerraFusion and Lochner both take exactly the kind of ridge/valley/cliff line sketch the pipeline wants; xandergos takes a coarse elevation map (ideal for "64^2 designer sketch -> 512^2 detail") but is tuned to planetary 30-90 m/px scales, so a 256-cell game map at 30 m/px represents ~7.7 km — plausible for a level, but the style cannot be re-trained cheaply (two weeks on a 3090 Ti). (Inference.)
- Licence: TerraFusion's PolyForm Noncommercial is fine for a hobby mod (the user's memory notes mods are non-commercial), but Lochner/xandergos/MESA (MIT or unspecified) are cleaner. (Inference.)
- Lochner's negative results (elevation constraints impossible to align; text prompts useless; flicker on small edits) are a strong hint that "altitude cues" sketch channels are the weakest part of sketch-conditioned diffusion; the pipeline should keep altitude control in its own procedural/erosion stage and use ML only for relief style. (Inference.)

### Gaps
- Lochner 2023 repository URL / Blender add-on download not found despite the paper's availability claim; the EG diglib and Wiley pages were blocked (403).
- xandergos: parameter counts, 4070-specific speeds, and exact sketch format are not in the fetched pages; discrepancy between the HF 30m card (Copernicus GLO-30) and the paper (MERIT 90 m) is unresolved — likely the two models are trained on different DEMs, but this is not confirmed.
- Terrain Diffusion Network and Earthbender: no code found.

## Key Question 4: Super-resolution / amplification (64^2 sketch -> 512^2; Guérin 2016, Paris 2019, DEM SR CNN/GAN/diffusion)

### Takeaway
Two families: dictionary/patch amplification (Guérin 2016, HighMap port) that is sub-second and needs one exemplar, and learned DEM SR (cascaded diffusion in Lochner 2023, image-guided diffusion for DSMs, task-specific CNN/GANs). Paris et al. 2019 is not a heightmap method at all — it adds implicit 3D overhang/arch features — so it is tangential to a 2D heightmap engine.

### Cited Findings
- Guérin 2016 amplification: `terrain_super_resolution(factor, exemplarhr, inputterrain, ...)` upsamples a coarse terrain using a high-res exemplar dictionary (Matlab; see KQ1). — [GitHub](https://github.com/eric-guerin/terrain-amplification)
- HighMap C++ port: factor, patch_size, strides, sparsity parameters; 512^2 in "well under a second" with 225 atoms; bicubic fallback where the mask cannot reach. — [HighMap PR 439](https://github.com/ottolink-dev/HighMap/pull/439)
- Paris, Galin, Peytavie, Guérin, Gain 2019, "Terrain Amplification with Implicit 3D Features", ACM TOG (SIGGRAPH Asia 2019): source code at `aparis69/Implicit-Volumetric-Terrains`; the method amplifies terrain with implicit volumetric 3D features (overhangs, arches), i.e., not a heightmap output. — [GitHub](https://github.com/aparis69/Implicit-Volumetric-Terrains); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/335488340_Terrain_Amplification_with_Implicit_3D_Features)
- Lochner 2023 uses cascaded super-resolution diffusion models (output of one as input to the next) conditioned on the low-resolution terrain instead of a feature sketch to reach high resolution while preserving global structure; the 256^2 cap applies per stage. — [Lochner 2023 PDF](https://people.cs.uct.ac.za/~jgain/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/lochner2023.pdf)
- xandergos: coarse maps upscaled 256x on both axes via the refinement cascade (23 km/px -> 90 m/px). — [GitHub](https://github.com/xandergos/terrain-diffusion)
- "Guided Super-Resolution of Digital Elevation Models with Diffusion-Based Image Generators" (arXiv 2609.11886, 2026): 5 m satellite DSM -> 0.5 m (10x) using high-res optical imagery as guidance; cities in Central Europe; code availability not stated. Urban DSM focus, not natural relief. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11886)
- Other DEM SR work found (not fetched in detail): GSRMTL multi-task HRSI-guided DEM SR with the GDEMSR benchmark dataset "to be publicly released"; Real-GDSR 2024 (edge-enhancing residual network for guided DSM SR); a 2025 bathymetry-constrained DDPM for seafloor DEM SR; "Efficient Terrain Stochastic Differential Equations for Multipurpose DEM Restoration" (arXiv 2407.01908); DEM void filling with feature-guided diffusion. — [ScienceDirect GSRMTL](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1569843226000154); [arXiv 2407.01908](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01908); [RG void filling](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/386302249_Void_filling_of_digital_elevation_models_based_on_terrain_feature-guided_diffusion_model)
- General single-image diffusion SR with released code that could be fine-tuned on DEM tiles: SinSR (CVPR 2024, single step), OSEDiff (NeurIPS 2024, one step), InvSR (CVPR 2025). — [SinSR](https://github.com/wyf0912/SinSR); [OSEDiff](https://github.com/cswry/OSEDiff); [InvSR](https://github.com/zsyOAOA/InvSR)

### Inferences
- For "64^2 sketch -> 512^2" the realistic path is two-stage: (a) something that gives large forms consistent with the sketch (patch synthesis, the Guérin cGAN, or diffusion), then (b) exemplar amplification (Guérin 2016/HighMap) for detail, followed by the pipeline's own erosion. Pure learned SR from a 64^2 sketch is 8x and would hallucinate; Lochner cascades 256-cap stages for that reason. (Inference.)
- Most academic DEM SR papers target 2-4x, geodetic accuracy, and urban DSMs; they optimise RMSE, which rewards blur — the opposite of what a game heightmap wants. Exemplar amplification trades accuracy for plausible high-frequency relief, which suits the pipeline better. (Inference, consistent with the RMSE-driven framing of the DSM SR papers.)

### Gaps
- No DEM-specific SR network with released weights trained on natural terrain (not urban DSM) was identified in this pass; GSRMTL/GDEMSR release status unverified.
- Guérin 2016 amplification quality at factor 4-8 vs. 2 not documented in fetched sources.

## Key Question 5: Practical dataset building from 3DEP / SRTM / Copernicus (tools, tile sizes, normalisation, licences, training time on a 4070)

### Takeaway
Tooling is mature: py3dep (HyRiver) pulls 3DEP at any resolution for a bbox/polygon; OpenTopography serves SRTM/Copernicus GLO-30/90 via API key; dandrino's and Lochner's recipes give concrete tile/filter/augment numbers (512^2 crops or 8x8-concatenated 256^2 tiles at ~19 m/px, entropy/flatness/artifact filtering, 8x flip/rotate augmentation). 3DEP is public domain; Copernicus DEM is free under the Copernicus licence, not public domain. Reported training costs range from "hours-to-a-day" (pix2pix 256^2, inferred) to 72 h on an A100 slice (Lochner), a week on a Tesla (dandrino ProGAN), and two weeks on a 3090 Ti (xandergos).

### Cited Findings
- py3dep (HyRiver): "access topography data at any resolution via the 3DEP web service of USGS"; `get_dem` for a Shapely polygon or bbox; layers include DEM, slope, aspect, hillshade; requires libgdal; optional requests-cache. — [HyRiver docs](https://docs.hyriver.io/readme/py3dep.html); [GitHub](https://github.com/hyriver/py3dep)
- OpenTopography: DEM download as GeoTIFF requires an API key from the MyOpenTopo dashboard (as used by pytopotoolbox `load_opentopography`); it hosts Copernicus GLO-30 (OTSDEM.032021.4326.3) and the Copernicus collection; also point-cloud tile-index tutorials. — [pytopotoolbox](https://topotoolbox.github.io/pytopotoolbox/_autosummary/topotoolbox.load_opentopography.html); [OT GLO-30](https://portal.opentopography.org/raster?opentopoID=OTSDEM.032021.4326.3); [OT Copernicus collection](https://portal.opentopography.org/datasetMetadata?otCollectionID=OT.032021.4326.1); [OT blog](https://opentopography.org/blog/programmatic-access-opentopographys-point-cloud-data-tile-indexes)
- `opendem` (PyPI) collects elevation from AWS or Thredds, merges/reprojects/resamples, no API key. — [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/opendem/)
- Copernicus DEM is on the AWS Open Data registry and in Google Earth Engine (GLO-30 2024_1); DE Africa docs describe COP-DEM specs. — [AWS registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/copernicus-dem/); [GEE](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/COPERNICUS_DEM_GLO30_2024_1); [DE Africa](https://docs.digitalearthafrica.org/en/latest/data_specs/COP_DEM_specs.html)
- Licences: GLO-30/GLO-90 are "available worldwide with a free license" but "not public domain: it is free for the general public under the Copernicus licence"; USGS 3DEP "is public domain: the metadata sidecar records access constraints as 'None' and asks only for acknowledgement"; SRTM uses EGM96 vertical datum vs EGM2008 for Copernicus. — [geodocs free DEM sources](https://geodocs.io/en/blog/free-dem-elevation-data-sources); [Sentinel Online](https://sentinels.copernicus.eu/-/copernicus-dem-30-metre-dataset-now-freely-available)
- dandrino recipe: ~1000 NED 1-degree tiles (3600x3600, 1 arc-s) -> 512x512 samples with aspect-ratio compensation -> filter flat/corrupt/low-entropy -> rotate/flip -> ~180,000 samples; scripts `download_ned_zips.py`, `generate_training_images.py`. — [GitHub](https://github.com/dandrino/terrain-erosion-3-ways)
- Lochner recipe: USGS tiles at 2.39 m/px as 256x256 float arrays, ~6 M downloaded, artifact classifier -> 3.84 M usable, 8x8 concatenation + downsample to 256^2 (~19.1 m/px, 4.9 km extent) -> ~200,000 tiles -> 8x augmentation -> 1.6 M; a light Gaussian blur is applied before feature extraction (following Guérin 2017); drainage networks detected by simulating water flow; 90% train split. — [Lochner 2023 PDF](https://people.cs.uct.ac.za/~jgain/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/lochner2023.pdf)
- TerraFusion recipe: NASADEM 30 m, 4,119 pairs from 276 regions, drop >2000 m elevation and human-modified/cloudy regions. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.04050v2)
- xandergos normalisation: elevation in "signed-sqrt space"; Laplacian split into blurred low-frequency (downsampled 8x8, sigma 5) and residual, residuals ~30x smaller in magnitude; 2048^2 equal-area tiles with longitude stretched by latitude. — [arXiv HTML 2512.08309](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08309)
- Training costs reported: Guérin 2017 model offered pre-trained "for those who don't have a Titan X", 500 epochs default; Lochner 72 h per model on a 20 GB A100 partition; dandrino ProGAN ~1 week on a Tesla; TerraFusion 2 days (A6000) + 7 days (6000 Ada); xandergos ~2 weeks on a 3090 Ti. — [Deep Terrains](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/eguerin/new/blog/deep-terrains-code-and-data/); [Lochner PDF](https://people.cs.uct.ac.za/~jgain/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/lochner2023.pdf); [dandrino](https://github.com/dandrino/terrain-erosion-3-ways); [TerraFusion](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.04050v2); [InfiniteDiffusion](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08309)
- Guérin 2017 data format: 16-bit PNG pairs (sketch A -> terrain B) in pix2pix's side-by-side layout. — [Deep Terrains](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/eguerin/new/blog/deep-terrains-code-and-data/)

### Inferences
- A hobby dataset: pick 20-50 mountainous 1-degree 3DEP tiles (public domain, 1 arc-s ~30 m or 1/3 arc-s ~10 m via py3dep), cut 256^2 crops at a fixed m/px that matches the game's cell size, drop low-variance/low-entropy crops as dandrino does, store float32 metres (or 16-bit PNG normalised per tile as Guérin does), augment 8x. Tens of thousands of crops are enough for pix2pix-scale models; millions are only needed for diffusion from scratch. (Inference from the recipes above.)
- "Is a 2017 cGAN at 256^2 trainable in hours on a 4070?" Yes with high probability: the reference is 500 epochs on a 12 GB Titan X; the 4070 has equal VRAM and roughly several times the throughput, so a few hours to a day for the Guérin dataset; but one can skip training entirely using `multi_train.tgz`. (Inference; no direct 4070 benchmark found.)
- Diffusion from scratch at 256^2 pixel-space (Lochner-style) on a 4070 is a multi-day to multi-week job and VRAM-limited to small batches; fine-tuning an existing LDM (TerraFusion/SD + ControlLoRA) or just using xandergos weights is the realistic route. (Inference.)
- Copernicus licence allows free use with attribution for a mod; for zero friction use 3DEP (public domain) when the region is in the US, which is also where the best-quality 1 m/3 m data is. (Inference from licence statements.)

### Gaps
- No source gives measured 4070 training throughput for any of these models.
- `elevation` (Python package) and `rasterio` specifics were not fetched; both are standard but no citation collected here.
- Exact Copernicus licence text (attribution wording) not fetched.

## Key Question 6: Honest assessment — where ML terrain fails, and do patch methods beat it for small grids?

### Takeaway
Documented ML failure modes: small grid/checkerboard artifacts and no style control (cGAN), noisy elevations and repeated small undulations and broken ridgelines (spatial GANs), need for post-hoc Gaussian blur (Beckham & Pal), unnatural structures and a hard 256-512 resolution cap (TerraFusion, Lochner), flicker between edits and failed altitude constraints (Lochner), and blurry/RMSE-driven output in DEM SR. Drainage consistency is only approached by diffusion (xandergos claims hydrologically consistent features; Lochner adds breaching post-processing), and no source shows ML beating real patches on drainage realism at 256^2. For a 128-256 grid fed into an erosion pass, patch synthesis/amplification from one real tile is cheaper, licence-clean, and loses only global coherence at seams — which is also the thing the pipeline's erosion and scoring already target.

### Cited Findings
- Lochner 2023 on cGANs: "tendency to produce small grid artifacts", "lacks any discernible notion of style"; on their own diffusion: not real-time (below 20 Hz), "flickering and inconsistent outputs after minor adjustments", elevation constraints "impossible to achieve alignment", text conditioning "deeply disappointing"; GPU memory caps images at 256^2. — [Lochner 2023 PDF](https://people.cs.uct.ac.za/~jgain/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/lochner2023.pdf)
- Lochner 2023 on texture synthesis/patch methods: they do not "always respect global properties, such as realistic drainage patterns"; the paper evaluates with drainage-area comparisons and uses minimal breaching "to ensure free drainage" as post-processing. — [Lochner 2023 PDF](https://people.cs.uct.ac.za/~jgain/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/lochner2023.pdf)
- Spatial GAN terrain (IEEE CoG 2019): "noticeable noise in elevation values", "repeated small-scale undulations", GANs "struggle to generate globally coherent terrain structures, such as continuous ridgelines". — [IEEE CoG 2019](https://ieee-cog.org/2019/papers/paper_57.pdf)
- Beckham & Pal 2017 applied a Gaussian blur to the final heightmaps to smooth small-scale artifacts. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1707.03383)
- dandrino: GAN gives "Limited creative control—terrain quality depends entirely on training samples", hard to scale resolution. — [GitHub](https://github.com/dandrino/terrain-erosion-3-ways)
- TerraFusion: "Some heightmaps exhibit unnatural structures", 512^2 cap, lacks rivers/lakes. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.04050v2)
- Terrain Diffusion Network: prior GANs "often struggle to fulfill the requirements of flexible user control and maintain generative diversity." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.16725)
- xandergos/InfiniteDiffusion claims "sharp, varied, and hydrologically consistent features without observable windowing or tiling artifacts" and FID 14.78 vs naive tiling 74.44 / Perlin blending 186.70; limitations are artifacts at low step counts and data acquisition for the coarse layer. — [emergentmind summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2512.08309); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08309)
- DEM generalisation/downscaling literature notes "geographic alienation" — ridge breakage and gradient distortion — when methods lack global awareness of macro terrain structure; physics-constrained GAN losses (relief, gradient) are proposed as a fix. — [MDPI IJGI 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijgi15080353)
- Zhou 2007 explicitly lists a failure case (Cape Girardeau) on its project page, i.e., patch synthesis also fails on some terrain types. — [howardzzh.com](http://www.howardzzh.com/research/terrain/)
- Galin et al. 2019, "A Review of Digital Terrain Modeling", CGF 38(2) is the standard survey covering example-based, procedural, and simulation methods. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cgf.13657)

### Inferences
- At 128-256 cells, a single real 3DEP tile contains thousands of candidate 32-64 px patches, so patch synthesis has plenty of variety without a training set; the ML advantage (global coherence, style interpolation) matters most above 512^2 and for infinite worlds, which this pipeline does not need. (Inference.)
- Flat valley floors and blur: pixel-space GAN/diffusion models trained with L1/L2 terms regress toward the mean in low-relief areas; patch copies do not, but may leave seam steps. Since the pipeline already runs erosion, seams and minor drainage breaks are repairable, whereas blurred ML relief is hard to re-sharpen without inventing detail. (Inference; direct "flat valley floor" evidence was not found in sources, so this is reasoning from the artifact reports above.)
- Recommended ordering for the pipeline: (1) exemplar-based synthesis/amplification (HighMap port or a Python OMP reimplementation, one 3DEP tile) as the baseline; (2) Guérin 2017 pre-trained cGAN as a zero-training sketch->256^2 option to A/B against it; (3) xandergos weights as a "coarse 64^2 map -> detail" option if the 30 m/px scale suits; (4) only then consider fine-tuning (ControlLoRA on TerraFusion/SD) if style control is still lacking. (Inference.)

### Gaps
- No paper found that directly benchmarks patch synthesis against GAN/diffusion at 256^2 on drainage metrics; Lochner compares diffusion vs cGAN vs real only.
- No source explicitly documents "flat valley floor" failures; this remains an inference.
- Guérin 2017's own limitations section was not read (PDF access blocked).
