# Academic methods: real-time shallow-water (height-field) fluids with complex static boundaries and two-way coupled moving bodies

Scope note: notes gathered 2026-10-06 from primary PDFs (Chentanez & Müller 2010, Thürey et al. 2007, Yuksel et al. 2007, Kurganov & Petrova 2007, Mei et al. 2007, Cords & Staadt 2009, Müller GDC 2008 slides, Özgen et al. 2017 porosity SWE, Ryoo & Mandli 2021 barrier/cut-cell SWE, Jeschke et al. 2020 wave cages, Bridson 2005 SWE lecture slides) plus abstracts/snippets for the rest. Formulas are transcribed from the papers; "Inference" sections are my own reading for the game context (Hydrophobia-style explicit HLL SWE on 20-unit cells, CFL 0.5, per-cell wall flags, radius-stamped object velocities, Havok buoyancy; plus a 2D blood-pool sim).

Notation used throughout: h = water depth, H or B = bed height, η (eta) = h + B = free surface, (u,w) or (u,v) = depth-averaged horizontal velocity, g = gravity, Δx = cell size, Δt = time step.

## Key question 1 — Static boundaries not aligned to the grid: cut-cell / porosity SWE, obstacle masks, SDF/wall normals, thin walls (face vs cell flags), stairs/slopes as bed height field

### Takeaway
The flood-modelling literature offers three graded answers to sub-grid geometry: (a) *porosity* SWE (Guinot & Soares-Frazão 2006 single porosity; Sanders et al. 2008 integral/anisotropic porosity with a *storage* porosity φ per cell and a *conveyance* porosity ψ per cell edge), which is exactly a "wetted fraction per cell + per face" extension of a finite-volume HLL solver and costs almost nothing extra; (b) *cut-cell / embedded-boundary* methods (Causon et al. 2000/2001; Ryoo & Mandli 2021 zero-width barriers as flux interfaces) that are geometrically exact but need a small-cell fix (cell merging or state redistribution) to keep the CFL step; (c) the graphics shortcut of marking *faces* (not cells) as reflective and treating any steep bed jump as a wall or a waterfall (Chentanez & Müller 2010). Stairs and slopes are handled by every SWE paper simply by putting the scene geometry into the bed height field B and using a well-balanced (hydrostatic-reconstruction / lake-at-rest) discretization so that stationary water on a staircase does not spontaneously flow.

### Cited Findings

**Porosity (wetted-fraction) shallow water — flood modelling**
- Single-porosity model (Guinot & Soares-Frazão 2006): ∂(φq)/∂t + ∂(φf)/∂x + ∂(φg)/∂y = φ s_U + s_φ, where φ is "the storage porosity expressing the fraction of the control volume that is not occupied by structures", q=(h, uh, vh), f,g the usual SWE fluxes, s_U = bed-slope + friction/drag source, and s_φ = (0, g h²/2 ∂φ/∂x, g h²/2 ∂φ/∂y) is "the porosity source term that describes the momentum variation due to the variation in porosity". — [Özgen et al. 2017, Water Sci. Eng. (open access), eqs. 1–4](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/1482522)
- Wave speeds of the single-porosity model are the same as classical SWE: eigenvalues λ = u−c, u, u+c with c = √(gh). — [Özgen et al. 2017, eqs. 10–11](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/1482522)
- Building drag is added as an extra quadratic drag coefficient alongside bed friction: s_fx = −(c_D^f + c_D^b)‖v‖u, s_fy = −(c_D^f + c_D^b)‖v‖v, "where c_D^f is the drag coefficient due to bottom friction, c_D^b is the drag coefficient due to buildings". — [Özgen et al. 2017, eq. 6](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/1482522)
- Integral (anisotropic) porosity model of Sanders et al. 2008: derived "by applying Reynolds transport theorem to a finite control volume encompassing buildings"; it uses "storage porosity computed from the computational cell and conveyance porosity defined as the fraction of a cell boundary contributing to mass and momentum exchange". — [Sanders, Schubert, Gallegos 2008, J. Hydrology 362:19–38, DOI 10.1016/j.jhydrol.2008.08.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhydrol.2008.08.009) (abstract via [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022169408004216)); summarised in [Özgen et al. 2017 §2.2](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/1482522)
- Porosities are computed from a binary phase function ϕ(x,y) (1 = void/water-able, 0 = obstacle): storage porosity φ = (1/|Ω|)∫_Ω ϕ dΩ over the cell, conveyance porosity ψ = (1/|Γ|)∫_Γ ϕ dΓ over the cell boundary (edge). — [Özgen et al. 2017, eqs. 14–15](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/1482522)
- Discrete integral-porosity update is a finite-volume sum over edges with the edge porosity multiplying the flux: ∂(φq)/∂t = −Σ_j ψ_j (F·n)_j l_j + s, "where l_j is the length of the j-th boundary edge"; the paper stresses "it can only be solved by means of a finite volume method". — [Özgen et al. 2017, eq. 18](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/1482522)
- Differential form of the integral-porosity model: ∂(φq)/∂t + ∂(ψf)/∂x + ∂(ψg)/∂y = φ s_U + ∂(ψ s_Γx)/∂x + ∂(ψ s_Γy)/∂y, where s_Γ is a boundary source "which arises due to unresolved solid-fluid interface pressures" of the form g h_η0²/2 (depth evaluated at the cell's constant average water level). — [Özgen et al. 2017, eqs. 16–19](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/1482522)
- The reason to prefer integral over single porosity: "The limitation of the SP model is its inability to represent directionality and blocking effects"; conveyance porosity on edges gives anisotropy (a wall blocks flow across it even though the cell is mostly wet). — [Özgen et al. 2017 §2.2](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/1482522)
- Known issue: the two models "converge if the conveyance porosity in the AP model is close to the storage porosity. If the porosity values differ significantly, the AP model yields different wave propagation speeds and numerical fluxes"; the ratio of conveyance to storage porosity is "the most significant parameter". — [Özgen et al. 2017 abstract](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/1482522)
- Review of porosity models for urban flooding (SP, IP, dual-IP, depth-dependent porosity) exists: [Porosity Models for Large-Scale Urban Flood Modelling: A Review, Water 13(7):960, 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/w13070960) (MDPI page returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch; content not read directly).
- A 2026 arXiv paper generalises this to an "unstructured second-order subgrid method for the shallow water equations" with subgrid wet area/volume fractions; it notes porosity models "consider the wet [fraction]" and that the CFL condition "is primarily determined by the coarse mesh, meaning that the number of time steps is" reduced relative to resolving the obstacles. — [arXiv 2601.01895](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.01895)

**Cut-cell / embedded thin walls**
- Causon, Ingram, Mingham: "Cartesian cut cells provide a fully boundary-fitted gridding capability on a regular background Cartesian grid, where solid objects are cut out of the background mesh leaving a set of irregularly shaped cells fitted to the boundary"; handles "shallow water problems having arbitrarily complex geometries and moving boundary elements" with "no mesh generation ... in the conventional sense". Papers: Adv. Water Resources 23:545–562 (2000) and 24:899–911 (2001, moving boundaries). — [ScienceDirect abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0309170801000100); [Developments in Cartesian cut cell methods (Ingram, Causon, Mingham)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Developments-in-Cartesian-cut-cell-methods-Ingram-Causon/e5af9b3d7c3f4682185fc2878e773dc428049cce)
- Ryoo & Mandli 2021 model a sea wall "as a zero-width embedded boundary element that acts as a flux interface" that "either completely blocks off incoming waves or admits some waves over the barrier while reflecting others depending on its height"; results "are virtually the same as resolving the barrier". — [arXiv 2112.09696](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.09696)
- The price of cutting cells with an arbitrary line is the small-cell problem: cut cells "can get arbitrarily small. This causes strict time step restriction via CFL condition"; cut-cell methods "apply special treatment to cut cells and their neighboring cells to lift the CFL restriction". They use the State Redistribution (SRD) method (merge/average small-cell states with a regular neighbour after each step) and report "reducing time steps fivefold when compared to simulation with same resolution using adaptive refinement at the barrier", while "remaining conservative". — [arXiv 2112.09696](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.09696)
- Immersed-boundary SWE exists for unstructured FV: Lagrangian markers transmit information to the Eulerian mesh via moving least squares. — [An immersed boundary method for unstructured meshes in depth averaged shallow water models (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/284123850_An_immersed_boundary_method_for_unstructured_meshes_in_depth_averaged_shallow_water_models)

**Graphics practice: face flags, steep-bed-as-wall, waterfalls**
- Chentanez & Müller 2010 store h and B at cell centres and velocities on faces (staggered/MAC grid "following [Bri05]"). "For a face that is marked by the user as reflective such as walls or permanent static obstacles, the velocity value is always set to 0 at the end of every time step and is not updated in the advection step." — [hfFluid.pdf §2.1.4](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)
- Automatic wall/dry-front rule on *faces*: face (i+½,j) is treated as reflective if "h_{i,j} ≤ ε and H_{i,j} > η_{i+1,j}" or "h_{i+1,j} ≤ ε and H_{i+1,j} > η_{i,j}", with ε = 10⁻⁴Δx: "the water level in a wet cell needs to be higher than the terrain level in the neighboring dry cell before the flow starts. Otherwise, the face behaves as a wall." — [hfFluid.pdf §2.1.4](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)
- Steep bed drops become *waterfall faces*: face (i+½,j) is a waterfall if (H_{i,j} − H_{i+1,j})/Δx > ∇H_cap and H_{i,j} > η_{i+1,j} (or the mirror case), with ∇H_cap = 3 "in all examples". The lower cell treats the face as reflecting; the face velocity is updated as u −= gΔt(−h_{i,j} − ∇H_cap)/Δx (case 1) and the volume that would cross the face is carried by splash particles seeded in the box between the top cell's bed and the bottom cell's surface; this "also makes the simulation more stable because steep terrain slopes cause numerical instabilities". — [hfFluid.pdf §2.4.3](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)
- Müller GDC 2008 (wave-equation height field): irregular domains via "Sparse tiling", "General terrain ... z-buffer resolves this" (water columns below terrain are simply hidden), clamped boundary "u[-1,j] = u[0,j]" gives reflection, and an open-boundary ghost column g with update g_new = (cΔt·u + g·h)/(h + cΔt) lets waves of speed c leave. — [GDC 2008 slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc08/slides/S6509i1.pdf)
- Cords & Staadt 2009 (wave equation on GPU): boundary conditions for collision objects are set "by rendering directly into the simulation grid" — "Adapting f(x,t_i) to constant values at the boundary of collision objects within the liquid models objects and, hence, the corresponding reflection of waves." Depth-dependent wave speed and damping are supplied as a "velocity map" and "damping map". — [open_water.pdf §4](http://hilkocords.de/publications/open_water.pdf)
- Bridson's lecture notes (the reference discretization graphics papers cite): heights at cell centres, u on x-faces, w on z-faces; for semi-Lagrangian advection at solid walls "velocity should be velocity of wall (e.g. zero)"; "Technically only normal component of velocity needs to be taken from wall, in absence of viscosity the tangential component may be better extrapolated from the fluid". — [cs533d SWE slides](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~rbridson/courses/533d-fall-2005/nov22-cs533d-slides.pdf)
- Wave Particles reflect off boundaries analytically: "Wave particles bounce back from the boundaries ... When the boundary is curved, this reflection changes the dispersion angle of the wave particle according to the curvature of the boundary"; reflection times are precomputed into an event timetable so iteration cost is "related to the number of events, but not directly related to the total number of wave particles". — [Yuksel et al. 2007 §3.3, §5](https://www.cemyuksel.com/research/waveparticles/)
- For *procedural* (FFT/Gerstner) waves, Jeschke et al. 2020 bound each surface point's trajectory inside an ellipsoidal "wave cage" fitted to not intersect local terrain, with a soft clip n(δ)=tanh(β|δ|)·δ/|δ|, β=0.5, then mapped into the cage axes; this "guarantees that the water surface never penetrates the sea floor" at "interactive frame rates on a commodity PC". — [JeschkeWaveCages.pdf](https://pub.ista.ac.at/~chafner/JeschkeWaveCages.pdf)

### Inferences
- A per-cell wall flag (Hydrophobia style) is the φ∈{0,1} special case of single porosity; moving to Sanders-style integral porosity means storing one wetted fraction per cell (φ) and one per face (ψ_x, ψ_y), multiplying each HLL face flux by ψ and dividing the cell update by φ, and adding the g h²/2 ∂φ term (or the s_Γ interface-pressure term). Everything else in an explicit HLL solver is unchanged, so this is a cheap upgrade that gives sub-cell curved walls, furniture legs and door jambs without refining the grid. Face porosities are what make a thin wall between two mostly-wet cells block flow (ψ=0 on that face) — the per-cell flag cannot express that.
- Thin walls aligned with faces are free in a face-flag/face-porosity scheme; thin walls at arbitrary angles are the cut-cell case. For a game, rasterising the wall into face flags (Bresenham over the two face sets) is the pragmatic middle; the SRD paper's "zero-width flux interface" is the rigorous version and its small-cell fix only matters if you actually cut cell areas.
- Stairs: a staircase is a bed field B with steps of height ≤ cell size — fine for any well-balanced scheme; large drops (ledges, tub rims, balconies) trip the Chentanez waterfall rule (bed slope > 3) and are better handled by emitting particles than by letting the SWE drive a near-vertical column. Set the ∇H_cap threshold relative to your 20-unit cells.
- Wall normals for reflection are implicit in face flags (reflection = zero normal flux). An SDF of the room is still useful for (a) computing the φ/ψ fractions by sampling, (b) clamping particle/foam advection, and (c) the Yuksel-style analytic reflection if you add a wave-particle layer.
- Low-φ cells carry the same stiffness as cut cells: a cell with φ→0 receives finite flux into tiny volume and its depth can spike. Flood codes mitigate by clamping φ ≥ φ_min (inference from the small-cell discussion in Ryoo & Mandli and the arXiv subgrid paper; no explicit φ_min value was found).

- Sanders et al. 2008 full text was not retrievable (paywalled); the storage/conveyance formulation above comes from Özgen et al. 2017's restatement.
- MDPI porosity review (2021) and Kellomäki 2014 (blocker-based coupling) returned HTTP 403; not read directly.

- No graphics paper found that uses porosity SWE explicitly; it appears to be a flood-modelling idea that has not crossed into games (as far as this search went).

## Key question 2 — Moving bodies, one-way (body → water): stamping velocity/displacement, Archimedes/submerged-volume approaches, wave particles, wavelets

### Takeaway
Every real-time height-field paper couples body → water by injecting *displaced volume* (Archimedes), not by imposing velocity alone: Müller GDC 2008 and Thürey 2007 store a per-column "replaced by solid" value b(x) and distribute its per-step change Δb to the neighbouring columns (so submerged bodies do not leave a hole); Chentanez & Müller 2010 sweep each sub-triangle, add V_disp = n·v_rel A Δt to the cell depth (decayed by e^{−depth}), and *blend* the face velocity toward the body velocity with a coefficient capped at 1; Yuksel 2007 turns the same per-face displaced volume into outward-travelling wave particles; Cords & Staadt 2009 rasterise the hull/surface intersection each frame and set bow/stern waves from the set difference S_t \ S_{t−1}. Fast bodies need substepping along the path (Chentanez) to avoid tunnelling through cells.

### Cited Findings
- Müller GDC 2008 (wave equation): "Object pushes columns beneath it down. Add the removed water in the vicinity!" For fully immersed bodies a naive push leaves "a hole ... above the body. Non-physical". Fix: "New state variable r[i,j]: Each column stores the part r[i,j] of u[i,j] currently replaced by solids ... Δr[i,j] = r_t[i,j] − r_{t−1}[i,j] is distributed as water Δu to the neighboring columns. In case of a negative difference water is removed". — [GDC 2008 slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc08/slides/S6509i1.pdf)
- Thürey et al. 2007 (SWE, staggered grid, semi-Lagrangian): the naive push-down "cannot handle the situation when a body is pulled beneath the surface". They store b(x), "the water volume that is displaced by one or more rigid bodies", computed each step by projecting each body onto the plane and measuring "the length along the column at x that is covered by rigid bodies". Δb = b(t) − b(t−1) is distributed to the four neighbours: h(x', t+1) = h(x', t) + α·Δb(x)/4. "The scheme conserves volume even for 0 < α < 1. By changing α the amplitudes of waves generated by bodies can be adjusted." Positive/negative Δb "give rise to both, the bow wave in front and the wake at the back of a body that is dragged through the water." — [breakingWaves.pdf §7, eq. 10](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/breakingWaves.pdf)
- Chentanez & Müller 2010 Algorithm 2 (Solids → Fluid), per sub-triangle (subdivided until area < Δx²): num_substeps = max(1, ⌊|v − v_y ŷ|Δt/Δx + 0.5⌋); V_disp = n·v_rel A Δt; for each substep at point p_s along the path: depth = η_{i,j} − p_y; if depth > 0: decay = e^{−β·depth}; h_{i,j} += decay·V_disp·C_dis/(num_substeps·Δx²); coeff = min(1, decay·C_adapt·sign·(depth·Δt/η_{i,j})·A/Δx²); u_{i+½,j} += coeff(v_x − u_{i+½,j}); w += coeff(v_z − w). Constants: β = 1, C_dis = 1.0, C_adapt = 0.2 "in all examples". — [hfFluid.pdf §2.3.2](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)
- Chentanez & Müller note the coupling step does not conserve volume ("the coupling and the overshooting reduction do not"), and suggest a global mass adjustment as in Hess 2007 if needed. — [hfFluid.pdf §3](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)
- Wave Particles 2007: displaced volume per object face V = A_face (U·N) Δt ("positive if the fluid is pushed by the face, and negative if the fluid is pulled"), "exponentially scale[d] down ... as the depth into the fluid increases". Faces with direct access to the surface spawn a ripple (wave particle with 2π dispersion); otherwise the volume is distributed "to the nearest points that have a direct connection to the fluid surface" (boundary pixels of a top-view silhouette), and waves move outward "unless the object is fully submerged". "Instead of actually modifying a wave particle interacting with an object, a new particle is generated that accounts for the modification ... a negative wave created on top of an existing wave would effectively delete it." — [Yuksel et al. 2007 §4.1, eq. 12](https://www.cemyuksel.com/research/waveparticles/)
- Wave particle mechanics: deviation D_i = a_i W_i(|x−x_i|) with W(u) = ½(cos(πu/r_i)+1)·Π(u/2r_i); constant speed; particles carry a dispersion angle and are subdivided into 3 children (amplitude and angle /3, same radius) when neighbour spacing exceeds half the radius; "the maximum deviation of the waveform shape from ideal is less than 3% of the peak amplitude" at that spacing. A longitudinal component L_i(u) = −sin(πu/r_i)·Π(u/2r_i) û_i gives Gerstner-like circular motion; the height field is rendered by splatting points and filtering with the kernel (separable approximation). "Unconditionally stable". — [Yuksel et al. 2007 §3](https://www.cemyuksel.com/research/waveparticles/)
- Cords & Staadt 2009: wave creation only on surface crossing (state flag above/under water) or motion; for boats "we generate a map containing the intersection of the boat's hull and the water surface" (surface approximated as a plane), combined with the previous frame's: "Bow wave: S_t \ S_{t−1}, Body: B_t = S_t ∩ S_{t−1}, Stern wave: S_{t−1} \ S_t"; intensities are added to (interfere with) or replace grid values; engine wakes are extra circular wave generators at e_y = −w/2 + i·w/m + w/2m with size ∝ boat speed, plus a diffusing foam map f ← d·f + (1−d)/4·Σ neighbours. — [open_water.pdf §4.2](http://hilkocords.de/publications/open_water.pdf)
- Cords & Staadt limitation: "the wave reflection cannot be used while wave creation is enabled, since both methods replace the grid values." — [open_water.pdf §4.2.2](http://hilkocords.de/publications/open_water.pdf)
- Water Surface Wavelets (Jeschke, Skřivan, Müller-Fischer, Chentanez, Macklin, Wojtan 2018) discretise waves as amplitude functions over space × frequency × direction so that FFT-class cost gains "wave interactions with moving obstacles in real time"; extensions include "pre-computed wave paths and two-way solid fluid coupling"; a CPU reference implementation is on GitHub (lecopivo/WaterSurfaceWavelets). — [IST Austria project page](https://visualcomputing.ist.ac.at/publications/2018/WSW/)
- Kellomäki 2014 (IJCGT) takes the opposite approach: "bodies block water rather than having water flow through them", improving "the effect of water on objects", "more general blocker geometry", and "replacing ad hoc solutions with a more physically-based approach". — [Wiley abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2014/580154) (full text 403)
- Hydrophobia-adjacent patent: "Two-way rigid body coupling in shallow water simulations" (US 8,041,550) exists from the AGEIA/NVIDIA group. — [USPTO PDF](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8041550) (not read)

### Inferences
- Hydrophobia's "stamp object velocity within a radius" is the velocity half of Chentanez's Algorithm 2 but without the displaced-volume half and without the depth decay / coeff cap. Adding V_disp = n·v_rel A Δt per rasterised body sample (or the cheaper Thürey Δb column-length method) is what produces bow waves and wakes; velocity stamping alone mostly produces drag-along, not waves.
- The Thürey Δb scheme is the cheapest correct option for a game: one extra float per cell, a top-down rasterisation of each body's column coverage, and a 4-neighbour distribution. It handles submersion (no hole) and gives an artist knob α for wave amplitude while conserving volume.
- Chentanez's coeff cap (min(1, ...)) is a stability device: it blends face velocity toward the body velocity instead of overwriting, so a fast body cannot inject more momentum than one step of fluid can absorb. Hydrophobia's hard stamping is the coeff=1 case.
- Fast bodies (thrown objects, characters sprinting) need the num_substeps path sweep; otherwise a body that moves more than one cell per step skips cells and the wake breaks into dots.
- For a 2D blood pool (thin layer, gravity-driven spreading), a character's foot is a body with a large V_disp relative to depth; the depth-decay term is irrelevant (depth ~ 0) and the main risk is h going negative, so clamp h ≥ 0 and conserve mass by pushing the deficit to neighbours (Thürey).

### Gaps
- Kellomäki 2014's blocker-cell method (how body geometry is rasterised, how water flows around blockers) not readable (403).
- Water Surface Wavelets two-way coupling details (how body motion becomes amplitude sources) not extracted — only the abstract was accessible.
- No paper found that quantifies the error of "velocity stamping only" vs displacement injection.

## Key question 3 — Two-way coupling (water → body): buoyancy, drag, lift from the height field; stability / feedback oscillation; game approximations

### Takeaway
All real-time papers compute buoyancy from the height field with Archimedes (F = −ρ g V_displaced, V from signed prisms between surface and body triangles, or per-column replaced height, or GPU top-view render of depth), plus quadratic drag and lift per face using relative velocity against the fluid velocity sampled from the grid, with user drag/lift coefficients and an "effective area" blend α between projected and total area. Stability is handled not by coupling theory but by smoothing the discontinuity at the free surface (Cords: buoyancy ramps linearly to zero over a distance d_t), capping the effective area (Yuksel/Chentanez α<1 "to dampen object motion"), and in games by adding explicit damping proportional to submerged fraction (linear or quadratic) and "critical damping" so the body never overshoots.

### Cited Findings
- Müller GDC 2008: "Archimedes' principle. Each column below the object applies force f = −ρ Δu h² g to body at its location; Δu is the height replaced by the body, ρ water density, g gravity" (h = column width). — [GDC 2008 slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc08/slides/S6509i1.pdf)
- Thürey et al. 2007: force per covered grid node F = ρ h b u(x) + ρ g Δx Δy b(x) (first term: fluid momentum flux, second: buoyancy from displaced column b), "where ρ is a constant to set the density of the fluid. Integrating these forces over the region of the rigid body and over time, will cause the rigid body to float or sink depending on its mass, and swim along with the fluid velocities." Boxes "become submerged, are dragged along with the fluid, or float on the surface ... The simulation remains stable even during quick movements." — [breakingWaves.pdf §7, eq. 11](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/breakingWaves.pdf)
- Chentanez & Müller 2010 (Fluid → Solids): triangles subdivided until area < εΔx² (ε=1). Buoyancy per sub-triangle: f = 0 if η(p) < p_y, else ρ g A (η(p) − p_y) n_y ŷ, "Volume is added for the downward facing triangles and subtracted for the upward facing triangles" (signed prisms to the surface), η(p) by bilinear interpolation. Drag f_drag = −½ ρ C_D A_eff |v_rel| v_rel; lift f_lift = −½ ρ C_L A_eff |v_rel| (v_rel × n × v_rel)/|n × v_rel|; v_rel = v − v_fluid with v_fluid = [u, u ∂η/∂x + w ∂η/∂z, w]ᵀ (vertical fluid velocity reconstructed from surface slope). A_eff = 0 if η(p) < p_y or n·v_rel < 0, else (α n·v_rel/|v_rel| + (1−α)) A, "0 ≤ α ≤ 1 is a user defined parameter for adjusting the effective area". Forces applied at p; for soft bodies distributed barycentrically. — [hfFluid.pdf §2.3.1, eqs. 14–17](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)
- Wave Particles 2007: F_buoyancy = −g ρ V_displaced applied "to the centroid of the part of the object inside the fluid"; V_displaced computed on GPU by rendering the object top-down with additive blending (back faces +depth, front faces −depth, fragments above water discarded) and multiplying by pixel area. Drag F = −½ ρ C_D |A| |U| U, lift F = −½ ρ C_L A |U| U × (N×U)/|N×U|, with A = (α N·U/|U| + (1−α)) A_face; "In our experiments we set alpha to one. Smaller values can be used to dampen object motion." Fluid velocity inside the volume is the surface velocity "exponentially scale[d] down ... with depth". — [Yuksel et al. 2007 §4.2, eqs. 13–16](https://www.cemyuksel.com/research/waveparticles/)
- Wave Particles timing: boat in tank (512×128 field, ≤100k particles) total sim 5.87 ms = 170 fps; boat & 125 boxes (6176 faces, 600k particles) 24.49 ms = 40.8 fps; 1681 boats 4.84 fps; 9261 boxes ~1 fps "the rigid body simulation becomes the bottle-neck". GeForce 7900, 2.13 GHz Core2. — [Yuksel et al. 2007 Table 1](https://www.cemyuksel.com/research/waveparticles/)
- Cords & Staadt 2009: bodies discretised as particle sets; buoyancy only for particles below the surface. Stability: "the discontinuity of buoyancy at the free surface can lead to instantaneous force changes if a particle is passing the free surface. To avoid such side effects, the buoyancy force is scaled linearly according to a threshold distance d_t under water, such that the buoyancy force reaches zero at the surface. Consequently, rigid-body movement is smooth — even for sparsely-sampled objects." — [open_water.pdf §4.1.1](http://hilkocords.de/publications/open_water.pdf)
- Cords & Staadt cost split (projected grid 1×1, GTX 280, two 2048² + 1024² wave grids): rigid bodies 1.3–12.3 %, liquid+foam 23–36 %, surface extraction/rendering 63–74 % of frame; "Another bottleneck of our method is the memory read back from GPU. Since rigid-body simulation is executed on the CPU, the local area surrounding each object has to be read back". — [open_water.pdf Tables 1–2](http://hilkocords.de/publications/open_water.pdf)
- Game practice (boat model article, Gamasutra/Game Developer): hull triangles cut by the water surface ("Each triangle of the original mesh can produce 0, 1, or 2 fully submerged triangles"), hydrostatic force per submerged triangle with pressure from depth at the centroid along the triangle normal; viscous resistance from the ITTC 1957 line; pressure/suction drag normal to the surface for planing; a slamming force with a blending function; and damping "proportional to submerged area ratio" using "either linear or quadratic formulations: F_damp ∝ v × (submerged_area/total_area)". — [Water interaction model for boats in video games: Part 2](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/water-interaction-model-for-boats-in-video-games-part-2)
- Search-snippet evidence on oscillation (source body not readable): "Applying buoyancy force to a heavy object keeps the body alive, which causes small vibrations in most rigid body solvers and makes the problem visible. With critical damping, the forces are strong enough to prevent oscillations altogether"; unstable buoyancy "can result in visually disturbing flickering". — attributed by the search engine to [Kellomäki 2014](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2014/580154) (full text not verified).
- Guendelman, Selle, Losasso, Fedkiw 2005 "Coupling water and smoke to thin deformable and rigid shells" is the offline reference for two-way thin-shell coupling cited by Yuksel; not read here. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1360612.1360645) (listing)

### Inferences
- The classic feedback oscillation (body pushes water down → local η drops → buoyancy drops → body sinks further → water rebounds → body pops) is tamed in the papers by three independent levers: (1) sample η with bilinear interpolation over a footprint, not a point; (2) ramp buoyancy to zero over a band at the surface (Cords d_t) so the force is C⁰ in height; (3) quadratic drag with a relative velocity that includes the fluid's own velocity, which is a true dissipative term. Hydrophobia's Havok-side buoyancy likely already has (1); adding (2) and a submerged-fraction-scaled damping (game article) is the cheapest stability win.
- A per-column force (Müller/Thürey) is O(cells under body) and matches the Δb column representation; the per-triangle prism method (Chentanez/Yuksel) is more accurate for hulls and gives lift, but costs triangle subdivision; for furniture/debris in a flooded room the column method is sufficient (inference).
- Volume non-conservation in coupling (Chentanez admits it) is the hidden cause of slow water-level drift in long sessions; the GDC 2008 "distribute V − V_new evenly within connected regions" trick is the standard fix and is O(N) per step.
- Two-way coupling is sequential in all these systems (water step → body step → couple), i.e. explicit/weak coupling; none of them iterate to convergence. This is why they all need a stability lever rather than a monolithic solve.

### Gaps
- No paper found that analyses the explicit water–body feedback loop stability formally (e.g. a limit on body density or Δt vs added-mass); the oscillation remedies above are empirical.
- Kellomäki 2014's critical-damping formula not verifiable (403).
- Hydrophobia's actual Havok buoyancy formula is from the game code (outside this research), so the comparison is qualitative.

## Key question 4 — Effects that sell it: wakes, splash particle generation criteria from the SWE, foam, breaking waves, wave particles for large bodies, wavelets

### Takeaway
The SWE itself only gives wakes and bow waves; everything else is particles spawned by thresholds on the height field. Chentanez & Müller 2010 give the most reusable criteria: a cell spawns splash if |∇η| > α_minSplash·gΔt/Δx AND (h − h_prev)/Δt > v_minSplash AND ∇²η < l_minSplash (steep, rising fast, near a crest), with values 0.45, 4, −4; body-generated splashes come from triangles sweeping the surface faster than v_thres with particle velocity built from normal/tangential reflection coefficients; waterfalls spawn particles in a box between the two cells' surfaces. Thürey 2007 detects breaking fronts where |∇H| > p_H gΔt/Δx (p_H = ¼) and ∇H·u < 0, tracks them as lines and extrudes connected-particle sheets. Foam is a decaying scalar advected with the flow (Chentanez) or a diffusing map seeded at engines (Cords). Wave particles and wavelets replace the grid for large open water where only object-made waves matter.

### Cited Findings
- Chentanez & Müller 2010 breaking-wave criterion, cell (i,j) breaks if all hold: (1) |∇η_{i,j}| > α_minSplash·gΔt/Δx ("Steep enough to break"); (2) (h_{i,j} − h^prev_{i,j})/Δt > v_minSplash ("Raising fast and is front of a wave"); (3) ∇²η_{i,j} < l_minSplash ("Is top of a wave"). Values: v_minSplash = 4, α_minSplash = 0.45, l_minSplash = −4. Gradient uses the max-magnitude one-sided difference; Laplacian is the 5-point stencil. Condition 2 "we found ... to be more robust than the condition ∇η·u < 0 of [TMFSG07]". — [hfFluid.pdf §2.4.2, eqs. 19–20](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)
- Particles are seeded in a rectangle perpendicular to the gradient through the cell centre, total volume ∝ |∇η|, positions jittered ±Δx/2; horizontal velocity = wave velocity clamped to −(∇η/|∇η|)√(g·min(h, h_avgmax)); vertical = α_y (h − h_prev)/Δt with α_y = 0.1; a fraction labelled spray. — [hfFluid.pdf §2.4.2](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)
- Mass/momentum exchange: a particle of radius r adds/removes height V_eff/Δx² where V_eff = C_deposit·(4/3)πr³, C_deposit ∈ [1,10] "can be used to control the number of particles active"; on return the face velocity is mass-averaged: u ← (u h Δx² + v_par^x V_eff)/(h Δx² + V_eff). Spray/splash are "simple non-interacting point masses". Foam is spawned probabilistically on splash impact, "advected by the velocity field of the fluid simulation and projected onto the fluid surface", lifetime user-defined with noise. — [hfFluid.pdf §2.4.1, eq. 18](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)
- Body splashes: for each solid triangle that sweeps through the height field with speed above v_thres, subdivide to splash-particle size; v_rel = v_s − v_fluid; v_rn = (v_rel·n_s)n_s; v_rp = v_rel − v_rn; v_par = v_fluid + α_rn v_rn + α_p v_rp + α_n n_s|v_rel·ŷ| + α_r(v_rel − 2(ŷ·v_rel)ŷ), with α_rn = 0.86, α_p ∈ [0.10, 0.23], α_n ∈ [0, 0.01], α_r ∈ [0.17, 0.61]; particles at p_s + 2 r q n_s, count ∝ |v_s| − v_thres. — [hfFluid.pdf §2.4.4](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)
- Chentanez & Müller timings (GTX 480, Core i7 2.67 GHz, Δt = 16.66 ms, 20 ms for boat): GPU boat scene 900×135 grid, 250k particles: total 18.05 ms (HF 2.19, particle gen 1.36, particles 1.15, coupling on CPU 2.32); CPU 4-thread version 69.75 ms; 128² scenes 3.8–9.9 ms total; 256² ocean 13.13 ms. "Porting the solid coupling part to the GPU is part of future work." — [hfFluid.pdf Tables 1–2](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)
- Sub-grid ripples: an FFT wave texture F(s,t) with periodic BC is looked up with three sets of semi-Lagrangian-advected texture coordinates reset every τ ≈ 3 s with phase shift τ/3; weight ω = e^{−μ γ} where γ is the max |eigenvalue| of the Green strain of the advected coordinates (μ ∈ [0.5, 2]) so stretched ripples fade — "constant weight case contains unnaturally distorted small waves". — [hfFluid.pdf §2.5.2](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)
- Thürey et al. 2007 breaking detection: points where |∇H(x)| > t_H and ∇H(x)·u(x) < 0, with t_H = p_H gΔt/Δx and p_H = ¼; set broadened by p_d = 2Δx, flood-filled into regions, a line L built along the height-field tangent with step Δx, re-centred on the steepest-gradient point; points removed if displacement exceeds 2cΔt (c = √(gH)) or |∇H| < t_H/2; resampled to keep spacing in [Δx/2, 2Δx]. — [breakingWaves.pdf §4, eqs. 4–8](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/breakingWaves.pdf)
- Thürey wave patch: particles spawned every t_g along the line, connected as quads; sheet velocity u_s = (1 + p_v g (H(x) − H_i)) u_l; Euler integration under gravity; on hitting the surface the height field is distorted H(x) −= p_m with the eight neighbours displaced by p_m/8, and standard particles spawn for drops/foam. "we do not explicitly transport fluid with the wave patches, as a modification of the height field along the wave front would distort its motion." — [breakingWaves.pdf §5, eq. 9](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/breakingWaves.pdf)
- Thürey performance (single core, Core 2 Duo 2.13 GHz, GeForce 7950): 160k–200k grid points at 40–75 fps including rendering; SWE 39.6 %, breaking waves + particles 21.7 %, mesh generation 18.9 %, rendering 19.8 %. Limitation: "doesn't properly handle cases with chaotic waves ... not suitable for handling situations that would require many small splashes or drops". — [breakingWaves.pdf Tables 1–2, §8](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/breakingWaves.pdf)
- Wave Particles: "when the amplitude of a wave is greater than half of its length, the final wave shape intersects with itself ... waves become unstable and break when the amplitude is greater than one-seventh of the wave length. Therefore, when a wave particle with high amplitude is introduced, it could be used as an indication for secondary effects like splash and foam." — [Yuksel et al. 2007 §7](https://www.cemyuksel.com/research/waveparticles/)
- Cords & Staadt compare to Wave Particles: FDM wave equation "allows us to simulate Huygen's principle"; with wave particles "an object hitting the water surface would cause a radially propagating wave front with a flat surface inside. Similarly, diffraction cannot be represented using particles"; but "Wave particles are very fast, but computational cost increases with the number of waves". — [open_water.pdf §6](http://hilkocords.de/publications/open_water.pdf)
- Chentanez, Müller, Kim 2015 couple a 3D grid/particle region to an outer SWE height field "such that waves travel naturally across the border" (whale breach etc. at interactive rates). — [TVCG 2015 preprint](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hybridsim_preprinted.pdf); [PubMed listing](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26340036)
- Thürey, Sadlo, Schirm, Müller-Fischer, Gross 2007 add bubbles and foam to a shallow-water framework (SCA 2007). — [Thue07a PDF](https://cgl.ethz.ch/Downloads/Publications/Papers/2007/Thue07a/Thue07a.pdf) (not read)
- Survey: Darles, Crespin, Ghazanfarpour, Gonzato, "A Survey of Ocean Simulation and Rendering Techniques in Computer Graphics", CGF 30(1):43–60, 2011; arXiv 1109.6494. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1109.6494)

### Inferences
- For a flooded-room game the Chentanez criteria are directly portable to an HLL solver: the gradient and Laplacian stencils exist already; the only new state is h_prev. The threshold α_minSplash·gΔt/Δx is resolution-aware, so it survives changing from 20-unit cells to finer ones.
- Body splashes need the triangle sweep test (triangle crossed η this step) — expensive per triangle but trivially parallel; a cheaper proxy for debris is "cell depth increased by more than v_thres·Δt under a body footprint this step".
- For a blood pool (very shallow, viscous, dark), breaking/splash is irrelevant; the selling effects are the Thürey Δb bow/stern wave from a foot, a foam-like "surface film" scalar advected with the velocity field (streaks), and well-balanced wet/dry fronts so the edge creeps rather than jitters.
- Wave particles are the right tool only where there is no global flow (Yuksel's own restriction) — e.g. a still pool in a corridor; a draining room with a current is SWE territory.

### Gaps
- Thürey/Sadlo bubbles & foam paper and Chentanez 2011 "Real-time Eulerian water simulation using a restricted tall cell grid" not read.
- No quantitative study found on how many particles per second a given splash criterion emits for given wave statistics (all papers tune C_deposit/C by eye).

## Key question 5 — Stability/accuracy over first-order HLL: MUSCL + limiters, Kurganov–Petrova central-upwind, well-balanced wet/dry, implicit/semi-Lagrangian height fields, pipe model vs SWE

### Takeaway
Two families coexist. The flood-modelling family keeps conservation and shocks: Kurganov & Petrova 2007 is the canonical second-order scheme that is simultaneously well-balanced (lake at rest on arbitrary bed) and positivity-preserving (h ≥ 0) by reconstructing w = h + B with a generalised minmod limiter (θ ∈ [1,2]), correcting the reconstruction where it would dip below the piecewise-linear bed, desingularising u = √2 h (hu)/√(h⁴ + max(h⁴, ε)) with ε = Δx⁴, and using one-sided speeds a± with CFL ≤ ½ and SSP-RK3. The graphics family gives up conservation of momentum for unconditional-ish stability: semi-Lagrangian/MacCormack advection on a staggered grid plus explicit height/velocity integration with ad-hoc clamps (Chentanez: |u| ≤ αΔx/Δt with α = 0.5; a depth cap h_avgmax = βΔx/(gΔt) with β = 2; edge-overshoot damping), or the Kass–Miller ADI implicit wave equation (one iteration per frame), or the virtual pipe model (O'Brien & Hodgins 1995; Mei 2007) whose flux-scaling K guarantees h ≥ 0 but introduces its own flow-rate error.

### Cited Findings

**Kurganov–Petrova 2007 (second-order, well-balanced, positivity-preserving central-upwind)**
- Variables w := h + B and hu; 1-D system w_t + (hu)_x = 0, (hu)_t + ((hu)²/(w−B) + g(w−B)²/2)_x = −g(w−B)B_x. Semi-discrete FV: dŪ_j/dt = −(H_{j+½} − H_{j−½})/Δx + S̄_j, with central-upwind flux H_{j+½} = [a⁺F(U⁻_{j+½}) − a⁻F(U⁺_{j+½})]/(a⁺ − a⁻) + [a⁺a⁻/(a⁺ − a⁻)](U⁺_{j+½} − U⁻_{j+½}). — [KPSV.pdf §2, eqs. 2.1–2.3](https://people.tamu.edu/~gpetrova//KPSV.pdf)
- Earlier versions "could be made either well-balanced or positivity preserving, but fail to satisfy both properties simultaneously"; the fix "heavily relies on the fact that we replace the original bottom function B with its continuous piecewise linear (bilinear in 2-D) approximation", which "is also beneficial for the handling of sharp bottom" (discontinuous topography). — [KPSV.pdf abstract, §2](https://people.tamu.edu/~gpetrova//KPSV.pdf)
- Well-balanced source quadrature: S̄_j^(2) = −g [(B(x_{j+½}) − B(x_{j−½}))/Δx]·[(w⁻_{j+½} − B(x_{j+½})) + (w⁺_{j−½} − B(x_{j−½}))]/2. — [KPSV.pdf eq. 2.6](https://people.tamu.edu/~gpetrova//KPSV.pdf)
- Generalised minmod limiter: (U_x)_j = minmod(θ(Ū_j − Ū_{j−1})/Δx, (Ū_{j+1} − Ū_{j−1})/(2Δx), θ(Ū_{j+1} − Ū_j)/Δx), θ ∈ [1,2]; "larger values of θ correspond to less dissipative but, in general, more oscillatory reconstructions." — [KPSV.pdf eqs. 2.12–2.13](https://people.tamu.edu/~gpetrova//KPSV.pdf)
- Positivity correction of the w reconstruction (only w, only two cases): if w⁻_{j+½} < B_{j+½} then set w⁻_{j+½} = B_{j+½}, w⁺_{j−½} = 2w̄_j − B_{j+½}; if w⁺_{j−½} < B_{j−½} then w⁻_{j+½} = 2w̄_j − B_{j−½}, w⁺_{j−½} = B_{j−½}. This "will remain conservative and will stay above the piecewise linear approximant of the bottom". — [KPSV.pdf eqs. 2.15–2.16](https://people.tamu.edu/~gpetrova//KPSV.pdf)
- Velocity desingularisation: u = √2 h (hu)/√(h⁴ + max(h⁴, ε)), ε = (Δx)⁴; then "the entire algorithm remains consistent only if we recompute the discharge (hu) := h·u ... if (hu) is not replaced by h·u ... the scheme may produce negative values of h". The simpler u = h(hu)/(h² + ε) "may lead to huge values of u and thus to dramatic slowdown". — [KPSV.pdf eqs. 2.17–2.21](https://people.tamu.edu/~gpetrova//KPSV.pdf)
- One-sided speeds a⁺_{j+½} = max(u⁺ + √(gh⁺), u⁻ + √(gh⁻), 0), a⁻_{j+½} = min(u⁺ − √(gh⁺), u⁻ − √(gh⁻), 0); positivity proven under "the CFL restriction λa ≤ 1/2" with λ = Δt/Δx and a = max(a⁺, −a⁻); time stepping by third-order SSP Runge–Kutta (convex combination of forward-Euler steps keeps positivity). — [KPSV.pdf eqs. 2.22–2.23, §2.3](https://people.tamu.edu/~gpetrova//KPSV.pdf)
- A triangular-grid version exists (Bryson, Epshteyn, Kurganov, Petrova 2011, M2AN 45:423) and adaptive variants. — [Bryson et al. PDF](http://www.math.utah.edu/~epshteyn/Bryson-Epshteyn-Kurganov-Petrova.pdf); [arXiv 2011.06143](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.06143)

**Graphics explicit SWE with stability clamps (Chentanez & Müller 2010)**
- Staggered grid; velocity advection "using an unconditionally stable modified MacCormack method as in [SFK08] and fall back to the semi-Lagrangian method if the resulting value is not within the bounds" of the interpolated values. Height: ∂h/∂t = −((h̄u)_{i+½} − (h̄u)_{i−½} + (h̄w)_{j+½} − (h̄w)_{j−½})/Δx with h̄ the *upwind* depth (h_{i+1} if u ≤ 0 else h_i); "This guarantees mass conservation ... more stable ... than the choice of h̄ = (h_i + h_{i+1})/2 of [Hes07]". Velocity: u_{i+½} += (−g(η_{i+1} − η_i)/Δx + a_ext)Δt. — [hfFluid.pdf §2.1.1–2.1.3, eqs. 3–9](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)
- Why explicit: implicit SWE (Layton & van de Panne 2002, Wang et al. 2007, Angst et al. 2008) "guarantees unconditional stability. However, they either require an iterative method which incurs a significant run time cost or need to pre-factorize the matrix which means boundary conditions cannot be changed." — [hfFluid.pdf §2.1](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)
- Stability enhancements: clamp h ≥ 0; clamp |u|,|w| < αΔx/Δt with α = 0.5 ("places a limit on the wave speed ... but greatly improves the stability for scenes with violent water"); limit depth used in height integration: h̄ − h_adj with h_adj = max(0, (Σ four neighbours h̄)/4 − h_avgmax), h_avgmax = βΔx/(gΔt), β = 2 ("a limit on the wave lengths and amplitudes in deep water"). — [hfFluid.pdf §2.1.5](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)
- Overshooting reduction at wave fronts entering shallows: if η_i − η_{i−1} > λ_edge and η_i > η_{i+1} then h_i += α_edge(max(0, ½(h_i + h_{i+1})) − h_i) (mirror case likewise), λ_edge = 2Δx, 0.1 < α_edge < 0.5; "explicit integration ... tends to amplify the edge of the first wave front". — [hfFluid.pdf §2.2](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)
- Open boundaries via a Perfectly-Matched-Layer-like damping region (10 cells) with fields σ, ψ: h += (−σ(h − h_rest) + ψ)Δt; u += −½(σ_{i+1} + σ_i)uΔt; ψ += −α_update σ (w_{j+½} − w_{j−½})/Δx Δt; ψ *= α_decay; α_decay = 0.9, α_update = 0.1; σ increases quadratically/cubically toward the border. — [hfFluid.pdf §2.1.4, eqs. 10–13](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)
- Honest limitation: "our stability enhancements cannot guarantee unconditional stability. However, they allow us to explicitly integrate with a reasonable time step in all of our examples." — [hfFluid.pdf §3](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)

**Thürey 2007 / Layton & van de Panne discretisation**
- SWE as H_t = −u·∇H − H(u_x + v_y), u_t = −u·∇u − g H_x, v_t = −u·∇v − g H_y, solved on "a staggered grid together with a semi-Lagrangian advection step [Stam 99] ... as described by Layton et al."; "solving the SW equations does not require a pressure correction step". Wave speed c = √(gH). — [breakingWaves.pdf §3, eqs. 1–3, 8](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/breakingWaves.pdf)

**Wave equation (Müller GDC 2008; Cords & Staadt 2009; Kass & Miller 1990)**
- GDC 2008 minimal solver: v += c²(u_{i+1}+u_{i−1}+u_{j+1}+u_{j−1} − 4u)/h² Δt; u += vΔt; CFL "c < h/Δt". Stabilisers: velocity scaling v *= s (s<1), or slope clamping: offset = (avg of 4 neighbours) − u; maxOffset = maxSlope·h; if |offset| > maxOffset, pull u toward the limit ("unphysical, direct control"). Volume loss fix: "V = Σ u, V_new = Σ u_new; Distribute V − V_new evenly within connected regions". — [GDC 2008 slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc08/slides/S6509i1.pdf)
- Cords & Staadt explicit FDM: z^{t+1}_{ij} = a Σ_{4 nbrs} z^t + (2 − 4a) z^t_{ij} − z^{t−1}_{ij}, a = c²Δt²/h²; "The simulation becomes unstable, if condition (c²Δt²)/(h²) ≤ 0.5 is not adhered"; grid translation split into integer part (shift indices) and fractional part (sampling offset) "Hence, numerical dissipation is suppressed". Two grids of 2048² and 1024² solved on GPU at 23–70 fps including rendering (GTX 280). — [open_water.pdf §4, eqs. 2–3, Table 1](http://hilkocords.de/publications/open_water.pdf)
- Kass & Miller 1990: "a version of the wave equation on a height-field where the wave velocity is proportional to the square root of the depth of the water ... solved with an alternating-direction implicit method on a uniform finite-difference grid. A single iteration per frame suffices in most cases". — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/97880.97884); [PDF](https://ohiostate.pressbooks.pub/app/uploads/sites/45/2017/09/kass-miller.pdf)
- Chentanez on wave equation vs SWE: "it only works with a vertical velocity field. Therefore, effects that are based on horizontal motion such as whirlpools or the flow of a river cannot be treated correctly ... The presence of a horizontal velocity field allows us to correctly advect floating objects and foam particles"; also "The main drawback of methods based on the wave equation or the pipe model is that vortices ... are not present in the model." — [hfFluid.pdf §1, §1.1](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)

**Pipe model (O'Brien & Hodgins 1995; Mei, Decaudin, Hu 2007)**
- Per-cell outflow flux to each of 4 neighbours: f^L_{t+Δt} = max(0, f^L_t + Δt·A·g·Δh^L/l), Δh^L = b + d − (b_L + d_L) (terrain + water difference), A pipe cross-section, l pipe length. Non-negativity by scaling: K = min(1, d·l_x l_y / ((f^L + f^R + f^T + f^B)Δt)), f ← K f. Water update ΔV = Δt(Σ inflow − Σ outflow), d_2 = d_1 + ΔV/(l_x l_y). Velocity from fluxes: u = [(f^R(x−1) − f^L(x) + f^R(x) − f^L(x+1))/2]/(l_y d̄), d̄ = (d_1 + d_2)/2. Boundary: set outflow flux through boundary faces to zero (no-slip). — [FastErosion_PG07.pdf §3.2, eqs. 2–9](http://www-evasion.inrialpes.fr/Publications/2007/MDH07/FastErosion_PG07.pdf)
- Pipe model stability: "an explicit method for shallow-water equations ... the scaling step might also introduce instability ... if the outflow flux of one cell is scaled by the factor K, the outflow flux of its neighbors should be adjusted correspondingly to avoid a numerical error in flow rate, which is not considered in the method"; CFL Δt·u ≤ l_x, Δt·v ≤ l_y. Kass & Miller's implicit method "requires many iterations over the 2D grid at each time step" (Benes: 5 fps at 300² for erosion). Sediment advected semi-Lagrangian (Stam). — [FastErosion_PG07.pdf §3.2](http://www-evasion.inrialpes.fr/Publications/2007/MDH07/FastErosion_PG07.pdf)
- Kellomäki 2017 "Fast Water Simulation Methods for Games" (Computers in Entertainment) "compares an implementation of the virtual pipe method with more sophisticated solvers and compares two different implementations of physics coupling". — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/2700533) (abstract only)
- Kallin (SIGRAD 2008, "Real Time Large Scale Fluids for Games", quadtree SWE) reports "central differences resulted in high frequency noise and undampened oscillations", spontaneous oscillations when cell size is small, and uses velocity diffusion "to dampen high frequency oscillations". — [ecp083410.pdf](https://ep.liu.se/ecp/034/010/ecp083410.pdf)

### Inferences
- Relative to Hydrophobia's first-order HLL + hydrostatic reconstruction + midpoint, the single most valuable upgrade is Kurganov–Petrova's package: MUSCL on w (not h) with minmod θ≈1.3, the two-case bed-clamp of the reconstruction, the √2 h(hu)/√(h⁴+max(h⁴,ε)) velocity, and recomputing hu := h·u. It removes the wet/dry front noise and the "thin film racing" that first-order schemes show on slopes/stairs, at roughly 2× the flux cost (two reconstructed states per face) and no new state. The HLL flux can stay; central-upwind and HLL differ only in the a± weighting.
- Keep CFL ≤ 0.5 (KP's proof needs λa ≤ ½; Hydrophobia already uses 0.5). Midpoint (RK2) is not SSP in the KP sense; Heun/SSP-RK2 is the drop-in replacement and keeps positivity as a convex combination of Euler steps.
- Chentanez's clamps (|u| < 0.5Δx/Δt, depth cap βΔx/(gΔt)) are what let a game solver survive kinematic characters and 20-unit cells; they are non-physical but resolution-scaled and cheap. Use them as a safety net *after* adopting KP, not instead of it.
- For a 2D blood pool, the pipe model is tempting (trivially positive via K, no Riemann solver), but its lack of momentum advection means no streaking or vortices; a KP-SWE with a strong Manning-type friction (c_D‖v‖v) and h-dependent viscosity gives thick-fluid behaviour while keeping the same solver as the water.
- Volume drift: KP-SWE height update is exactly conservative; only the coupling injects/removes volume, so the GDC 2008 "connected-region redistribution" needs to run only on the coupling residual.

### Gaps
- No direct benchmark found comparing HLL-MUSCL vs Kurganov–Petrova vs semi-Lagrangian SWE in frame-time for game grids; the costs above are per-paper and on different hardware.
- Kass & Miller full text not retrieved (download was a stub); ADI details from abstract only.
- O'Brien & Hodgins 1995 original pipe-model paper not read (Mei 2007 restates it).

## Key question 6 — Surveys and reference texts

### Takeaway
There is no single survey dedicated to height-field + rigid-body coupling for games; the closest are Darles et al. 2011 (ocean simulation/rendering taxonomy: spatial-domain height fields, spectral, hybrid), the related-work sections of Chentanez & Müller 2010 and Jeschke et al. 2020 (which enumerate the 2.5D family: wave equation, pipe model, SWE Eulerian/SPH, wave particles, wave packets, wavelets), Bridson's course notes (staggered SWE discretization graphics papers cite), and Kellomäki's 2017 "Fast Water Simulation Methods for Games".

### Cited Findings
- Darles, Crespin, Ghazanfarpour, Gonzato 2011: methods "mainly rely on two main approaches: those which approximate ocean dynamics with parametric, spectral or hybrid models and use empirical laws from oceanographic research", covering spatial-domain height-field methods, spectral and hybrid approaches. — [arXiv 1109.6494](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1109.6494)
- Jeschke et al. 2020 taxonomy of 2.5D methods: wave equation on a 2D grid [KM90, Tes99]; pipe models [OH95, vBBK08]; Lagrangian wave particles [YHK07] and wave packets [JW17]; Eulerian SWE [CL95, LvdP02, HHL05, WMT07, ATBG08] and 2D SPH SWE [LH10, SBC11]; wavelets [JSMF18]; fundamental solutions for wave–obstacle interaction [SHW19]. — [JeschkeWaveCages.pdf §2](https://pub.ista.ac.at/~chafner/JeschkeWaveCages.pdf)
- Chentanez & Müller 2010 related work: SWE introduced to graphics by Layton & van de Panne 2002; Hagen et al. 2005 GPU finite-volume SWE on terrain; Wang, Miller, Turk 2007 surface tension on SWE; Angst et al. 2008 implicit Newmark to reduce advection dissipation; Lee & O'Sullivan 2007 collocated grid ignoring divergence; Hess 2007 (ETH MSc) explicit SWE with two-way rigid-body coupling and wet-dry tracking; Yu et al. 2009 stream-function rivers. — [hfFluid.pdf §1.1](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/hfFluid.pdf)
- Bridson's 2005 SWE lecture: η = h + H; Dη/Dt = −η∇·u, Du/Dt = −g∇h; semi-Lagrangian advection ("completely stable ... take Δt as big as you want"), MAC grid with heights at centres and velocities on faces. — [cs533d SWE slides](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~rbridson/courses/533d-fall-2005/nov22-cs533d-slides.pdf); book: Bridson, *Fluid Simulation for Computer Graphics*, A K Peters 2008 (cited as [Bri08] in hfFluid.pdf).
- NVIDIA GTC/SIGGRAPH Asia 2011 talk "Realtime Water Simulation on GPU" (Chentanez) summarises the height-field + particle pipeline. — [WaterSim_Chentanez.pdf](https://developer.download.nvidia.com/GTC/SIGGRAPH_Asia_2011/PDF/WaterSim_Chentanez.pdf) (slides, not read in depth)
- Kellomäki 2017, Computers in Entertainment: "how water simulation could add emergent interaction elements in 3D games, analyzes barriers for games with large playing areas, and examines how to couple water and physics simulations". — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/2700533)

### Inferences
- For the write-up, the cleanest framing is the Jeschke 2020 taxonomy (wave equation → pipe → SWE → Lagrangian wave methods → wavelets), with Hydrophobia sitting in "Eulerian SWE, explicit FV", and the proposals being: flood-modelling upgrades to the FV core (KP + porosity) and graphics upgrades to coupling/effects (Thürey Δb, Chentanez forces/particles).
