# Game flesh, wounds and skinning techniques (2000-2026) — what shipped, how, and what a UE2 / D3D9 wrapper mod could borrow

Scope note: ~25 tool calls of research. Several key studio pages (Game Informer/TrueAchievements/TweakTown FLESH interviews, Epic UDK HitMask doc, Valve wiki jigglebone page) returned HTTP 403 to the fetcher, so some findings rest on search-result snippets of those pages rather than full reads. Those are flagged "(snippet)". Hardware labels used below: **VS** = vertex shader, **RTT** = render-to-texture, **CS** = compute shader, **OFF** = offline tool/precompute, **CPU** = can be done on CPU per frame.

## Skinning quality: LBS artefacts and the fixes (DQS, optimized CoR, Delta Mush/DDM, PSD/correctives, helper bones) and their vertex-shader cost

### Takeaway
Dual-quaternion skinning (Kavan 2007/2008) is the cheapest drop-in fix for LBS candy-wrapper/volume loss (about +7 VS instructions, 8 floats per bone) but bulges at bends; Le & Hodgins 2016 optimized centres of rotation fixes both families with an offline per-vertex precompute and runtime cost close to LBS; correctives/PSD remain the artist-controlled production standard; Direct Delta Mush (EA SEED 2019) makes Delta Mush real-time-capable but needs heavier per-vertex data. All of these are SM2/SM3-feasible in principle — the hard question for UE2 is whether the engine skins on CPU (see Inferences).

### Cited Findings
- LBS and DQS "are the most widely used techniques for real-time applications" but have well-known artefacts: collapsing elbows, candy-wrapper twists, and bulging around joints — [Le & Hodgins 2016, Disney Research](https://studios.disneyresearch.com/?p=39548); [paper PDF](https://la.disneyresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/Real-time-Skeletal-Skinning-with-Optimized-Centers-of-Rotation-Paper.pdf)
- Optimized CoR: pre-computes an optimized centre of rotation per vertex from the rest pose and skinning weights; at runtime these centres are used to interpolate the rigid transform per vertex; claims significant artefact reduction vs LBS and DQS "while maintaining real-time performance and backwards compatibility with the animation pipeline" (same weights/skeleton) — [Disney Research](https://studios.disneyresearch.com/?p=39548); [SIGGRAPH 2016 archive](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=102760). Open-source implementations exist — [GitHub pmbittner/OptimisedCentresOfRotationSkinning](https://github.com/pmbittner/OptimisedCentresOfRotationSkinning)
- DQS: blending dual quaternions in a VS has complexity comparable to linear blending; the reported implementation needs 7 more VS instructions than LBS; scale/shear support via two-phase skinning costs about 29 extra VS instructions; DQs take 8 floats per bone vs 12 for a 3x4 matrix (so more bones fit in SM2/SM3 constant registers) — [Kavan et al., Skinning with Dual Quaternions project page](https://users.cs.utah.edu/~ladislav/dq/); [Kavan et al. TOG 2008 "Geometric Skinning with Approximate Dual Quaternion Blending" PDF](https://users.cs.utah.edu/~ladislav/kavan08geometric/kavan08geometric.pdf); [I3D 2007 paper](https://www.cs.utah.edu/~ladislav/kavan07skinning/kavan07skinning.pdf)
- Direct Delta Mush (Le & Lewis, SIGGRAPH 2019): Delta Mush "largely solved the rig authoring problem" but its iterative smoothing "prevented direct adoption in real-time engines"; DDM derives a direct (non-iterative) form, with explicit rotation/translation decomposition giving independent control of bending vs twisting and a skin-sliding effect — [EA SEED news](https://www.ea.com/seed/news/siggraph2019-direct-delta-mush); [slides PDF](https://media.contentapi.ea.com/content/dam/ea/seed/presentations/direct-delta-mush-and-variants.pdf). A 2021 follow-up targets DDM data compression ("with continuous examples"), indicating per-vertex storage is the practical cost — [EA SEED DDM compression](https://www.ea.com/seed/amp/news/ddm-compression-with-continuous-examples); [arXiv 2101.02798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.02798v1)
- Pose Space Deformation (Lewis, Cordner, Fong, SIGGRAPH 2000): artist poses the skeleton, sculpts the desired shape, PSD interpolates these example displacements as a function of pose (in local bone frames) — used for muscle bulge, joint protrusion, armpit/elbow volume. RBF solve: factor a p×p matrix for p poses at init — [PSD paper PDF (scribblethink)](https://www.scribblethink.org/Work/PSD/PSD.pdf); [Wikipedia: Pose space deformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pose_space_deformation); [skinning.org course notes, example-based](https://skinning.org/example-based.pdf)
- ML deformers: Ziva Real-Time (Ziva RT) trains a model on representative high-quality mesh shapes/poses to learn deformation and runs "even on consumer hardware (GPU or CPU)"; cited as used in Ninja Theory's Senua's Saga: Hellblade II — [CG Channel, Ziva RT 2.0 (2023)](https://www.cgchannel.com/2023/07/unity-releases-ziva-real-time-2-0/); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/unity-released-ziva-real-time-2-0/)
- Unity stopped selling/supporting Ziva VFX, Ziva Real-Time and Face Trainer effective 2 April 2024; DNEG acquired an exclusive perpetual licence of the Ziva IP — [Unity blog "An update about Ziva"](https://unity.com/blog/news/update-about-ziva); [CG Channel 2024](https://www.cgchannel.com/2024/04/unity-discontinues-the-ziva-products/); [3DVF](https://3dvf.com/en/unity-is-no-longer-selling-or-supporting-ziva-dneg-acquires-exclusive-license/)

### Inferences
- Cost ranking per vertex in a VS (my synthesis from the above): LBS < DQS (+~7 instr) ≈ optimized CoR (DQS/quaternion blend + one precomputed float3 per vertex, needs an extra vertex stream and an OFF precompute) < correctives/PSD (one extra delta stream per active shape; SM3 vertex texture fetch or CPU blend) < DDM (needs per-vertex precomputed matrices — much more vertex data; realistic only as CS or CPU in older pipelines) < ML deformers (CS/CPU, OFF training). Ziva-style ML is effectively out of reach for a 2005 engine and is now a discontinued product anyway.
- For a mod: DQS and CoR need only *bone transforms + weights* at draw time. If Unreal Engine 2 skins skeletal meshes on the CPU (my understanding for UE2-era builds, unverified here), a D3D9 wrapper only sees already-skinned vertices and cannot swap the skinning method in a shader; the change would have to be in the engine's CPU skinning code (e.g. via the existing Ghidra/decompile work) or by intercepting vertex buffers. Correctives/helper bones, by contrast, can be done at the content level (extra bones driven by script, or extra morph targets) without touching the renderer.
- Helper/twist bones are the cheapest artist-side fix for candy-wrapper (split forearm twist across 2-3 bones, CPU drive) — this is standard rigging practice but I did not fetch a primary game-studio source for it.

### Gaps
- No primary source fetched for helper/twist-bone or RBF-corrective usage in a named shipped game (e.g. God of War, Uncharted); widely known but uncited here.
- Classic (iterative) Delta Mush origin (Mancewicz et al., Rhythm & Hues 2014) not fetched.
- No measured ms/vertex numbers for CoR or DDM on GPU were retrieved (only qualitative "real-time" claims).

## Muscle, fat and flesh damage systems in shipped games

### Takeaway
Shipped gore systems fall into three families: (1) zone/part swap on skinned meshes (GHOUL 2000, most dismemberment through the 2010s); (2) layered anatomy that is peeled/revealed by a damage state per region (Dead Space remake "peeling", Dead Island 2 FLESH, RE Engine remakes); (3) active-ragdoll body reaction (Euphoria) that sells mass and pain rather than tissue. Primary technical write-ups are scarce: most detail comes from developer interviews, and the deepest primary item (Naughty Dog GDC 2021) is behind GDC Vault.

### Cited Findings
- **Soldier of Fortune (Raven, 27 March 2000)** was the first game on Raven's GHOUL damage-model engine; character models are built from body parts that each sustain damage independently — 26 "gore zones" per model; head shots can remove the head leaving a neck stump, shotgun to stomach shows viscera; SoF II: Double Helix used GHOUL 2.0 with 36 zones; GHOUL 2 also powered Jedi Knight II: Jedi Outcast — [Wikipedia: Soldier of Fortune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soldier_of_Fortune_(video_game)); [GOG retrospective](https://gog.com/news/a_look_back_at_soldier_of_fortune_an_actionpacked_shooter_celebrating_its_20th_anniversary) (secondary sources; no Raven technical paper found)
- **Dead Island 2 (Dambuster, 2023) FLESH** = "Fully Locational Evisceration System for Humanoids", a procedural gore system; developers modelled skin, muscle, bone and organs; bladed weapons tear through layers (repeated slicing cuts through clothing and skin, reaches muscle, then bone), blunt weapons crush bones; damage looks differ by weapon type and by zombie type (e.g. Crushers more visceral). Named developers: Aaron Ridge (Senior Render Programmer), Dan Evans-Lawes (Technical Art Director); original source a Game Informer interview, footage from a Nov 2022 WIP build — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/dead-island-2-flesh-system-and-procedural-gore-showcased-in-new-gameplay); [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/dead-island-2-shows-off-its-impressive-procedural-dismemberment-in-new-gameplay-footage/amp/); [TweakTown (snippet)](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/88189/dead-island-2-devs-made-their-own-gore-tech-system-called-flesh/)
- **Dead Space remake (EA Motive, 2023) "peeling" system**: each Necromorph modelled from the skeleton outward — internal organs, flesh, tendons, subcutaneous fat, skin — all of which can be peeled off in chunks; purpose is health feedback without a health bar (player reads weakened areas) and to give non-cutting weapons visible impact; team studied crime-scene and accident photos/video. Quotes from Gameplay Tech Lead Pierre-Vincent Belisle and Technical Animation Director Nate Burnett (Oct 2022). Note: the EA/Epic article URL slug says "dead-space-2" but the content describes the 2023 remake — [Epic Games Store news / EA "Inside Dead Space: the new Necromorph nightmare"](https://store.epicgames.com/news/inside-dead-space-2-the-new-necromorph-nightmare); [EA forums copy](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/dead-space-franchise-discussion-en/inside-dead-space%E2%84%A2-2-the-new-necromorph-nightmare/7156055)
- **Resident Evil 2 remake (Capcom, RE Engine, 2019)**: director Kazunori Kadoi said they focused on display and impact of each shot to make wounds "realistic and visceral" ("wet gore"); zombies show instant visible per-hit damage — [SYFY Wire interview with Kadoi](https://www.syfy.com/syfy-wire/resident-evil-2-remake-horror-gore-zombie-evolution); [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/entertainment/video-games/resident-evil-2-wet-gore-remake-director-kazunori-kadoi-zombies-shotgun-night-1305780); [GameSpot E3 2018](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/e3-2018-resident-evil-2-remake-takes-gore-to-new-l/1100-6459797/). No CEDEC/primary technical talk on the RE Engine damage system was found.
- **The Last of Us Part II (Naughty Dog, 2020)**: GDC 2021 talk "Character Technology of The Last of Us Part II" by Wasim Khan covers character authoring, shaders, **gore tools** and optimization, with a "non-destructive, artist-driven workflow" — [GDC Vault abstract](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027376/Character-Technology-of-The-Last). Druckmann on intent: "Realism" — [Engadget 2017](https://www.engadget.com/2017/10/30/the-last-of-us-part-ii-doesnt-shy-away-from-violence-and-gor/). Talk content itself not accessible without Vault.
- **Ziva RT** shipped in Hellblade II (see previous section) — the one well-attested shipped use of ML soft-tissue deformation — [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2023/07/unity-releases-ziva-real-time-2-0/)
- **Euphoria (NaturalMotion)** — "Dynamic Motion Synthesis", from Oxford University research, simulating a nervous system to synthesize animation in real time; unlike limp ragdolls, it combines physics, AI and authored animation so characters react to force magnitude, direction and the body part hit; used in GTA IV (Rockstar announced 2007) — [Oxford University Innovation](https://innovation.ox.ac.uk/?p=8954); [Giant Bomb (secondary)](https://giantbomb.com/euphoria-engine/3015-704/); [GTA4.net 2007](https://gta4.net/news/3828/euphoria-engines-possibilities-for-gta-iv)

### Inferences
- Common shipped architecture for layered gore (FLESH, peeling, RE): the outer skin mesh carries a per-region damage mask; when a region's damage passes thresholds, the shader reveals an underlying "muscle/fat" material and/or hides skin triangles, and pre-authored inner geometry (muscle, bone, organ meshes, skinned to the same skeleton) becomes visible. This is consistent with all three descriptions but none of the fetched sources spells out the exact rendering method — treat as a likely pattern, not a citation.
- For a UE2 mod, the GHOUL approach (26-36 named zones, per-zone mesh/texture swaps and attached gib/stump meshes) is the most directly portable: it is CPU-side bookkeeping plus content (hidden sections, attached static meshes, texture swaps), needs no shaders, and matches a 2000-2003 engine generation. The memory notes say AdventMod/U2Gore already do decals and gibs — GHOUL-style zone stumps would be the next step.
- Euphoria-like "pain reaction" is reachable in UE2 only via the Karma ragdoll plus blended powered joints — this aligns with the existing "Advent physics plan" (powered ragdolls) rather than a renderer change.

### Gaps
- No primary technical source found for: Mortal Kombat X/11 X-ray, Fight Night Champion flesh/swelling, Metal Gear Solid V body damage, original Dead Space (2008) dismemberment implementation, RE4 remake gore, TLOU2 wound shader specifics. - Dead Island 2 FLESH: the primary Game Informer interview text was not retrievable (403s); exact rendering method (mask in UV space vs volumetric/SDF cut vs mesh slicing) is unconfirmed.
- No primary NaturalMotion technical paper on Euphoria's controllers was fetched.

## Impact and wound deformation techniques (masks, render targets, decals, holes, mesh swaps)

### Takeaway
Two production-proven ways to make wounds stick to an animated character: (a) paint hits into a per-character UV-space render target (Epic's UE3 HitMask; Ryan Bruck's GDC demos) — robust but needs unique UVs and costs an RTT draw per hit; (b) store a handful of hit points in the reference (bind) pose and evaluate sphere masks in the pixel shader — no RTT, constant cost, limited to ~3-5 hits. Both are feasible on D3D9; (b) is the better fit for a wrapper.

### Cited Findings
- Render-target wound masks: Ryan Bruck's GDC demos splat spheres onto a character into a render target that the character material uses as a wound mask, solving decals sliding on animated characters; drawbacks: two render targets per character, unique UVs required, and an RTT draw on every hit — [Tom Looman, "Rendering Wounds on Characters"](https://tomlooman.com/unreal-engine-render-character-wounds/); [80.lv summary](https://80.lv/articles/tutorial-wounds-on-characters-in-unreal)
- Looman measured "1.6-4.5ms" spikes from the DrawMaterialToRenderTarget approach and replaced it with hit positions stored as material parameters in **pre-skinned (reference-pose) local space** (world hit transformed back via the hit bone), evaluated as SphereMasks in the shader; masks stay locked to the skin through animation; practical limit "3-5 hits" since each adds constant shader cost; can grow/fade masks over time; HeightLerp for better falloff — [Tom Looman](https://tomlooman.com/unreal-engine-render-character-wounds/)
- Unreal Engine 3 shipped a "HitMask" feature (UV-space hit mask render target for skeletal meshes) documented in UDK — [UDK HitMask doc](https://docs.unrealengine.com/udk/Three/HitMask.html) (page returned 403; existence from search listing only)
- Dead Space remake/Dead Island 2 show the "layer reveal" end of this: progressive exposure of fat/muscle/bone per hit region — [Epic/EA](https://store.epicgames.com/news/inside-dead-space-2-the-new-necromorph-nightmare); [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/dead-island-2-flesh-system-and-procedural-gore-showcased-in-new-gameplay)

### Inferences
- D3D9/SM2-3 feasibility:
  - Reference-pose sphere masks: needs the bind-pose position per vertex passed to the pixel shader (an extra texcoord stream). With CPU skinning in UE2, the wrapper sees skinned positions only; a workaround is to put bind-pose position into a spare UV channel at import time (e.g. via the U2Golem/importer pipeline) so the wrapper's replacement pixel shader can read it. SM2 has 8 interpolators; 3-5 float4 hit constants are trivial.
  - UV-space render target: D3D9 supports RTT; the wrapper would need to render a splat into a per-character texture (requires knowing which draw call is which character and having non-overlapping UVs — many 2005 models mirror UVs, which breaks this).
  - Vertex dents/bulges at the hit point: in a VS, displace along normal by a falloff around the hit point (same sphere-distance math as the mask). With CPU skinning, do it on CPU or in the wrapper's VS on the already-skinned positions with world-space hit points updated per frame (needs the hit bone's current transform — not available to a wrapper without engine cooperation).
  - Parallax/relief wound decals: pixel-shader-only (SM3 for steep parallax loops; SM2 for single-step offset parallax) — feasible in the wrapper.
  - Stencil-cut holes with inner meat geometry: draw inner "wound cup" mesh, use stencil or alpha-clip on the skin around it; needs extra geometry attached to the bone (engine/script side) plus the clip mask in the skin shader.
  - "Wound mesh" swaps (GHOUL style): pure content + script; no shader work.

### Gaps
- No primary GDC slide deck from Ryan Bruck was fetched (only Looman's secondary account).
- No shipped-game write-up confirming which titles used stencil-cut holes vs mesh swaps (Fallout/Sniper Elite X-ray etc. not covered).

## Jiggle / secondary motion (fighting games, Source Filmmaker community "soft body" look)

### Takeaway
The SFM/Source "soft" look is mostly flex (morph) shapes for faces/correctives plus spring-damper "jiggle bones" simulated per bone on the CPU — cheap, engine-agnostic, and directly portable to UE2 as script-driven bones; true volumetric soft bodies (Blender cloth/softbody bakes) are offline.

### Cited Findings
- `$jigglebone` is a QC command in all Source games since Source 2007; it marks skeleton bones to be dynamically simulated at runtime for secondary motion "such as wiggling antennas, bouncy flesh, floppy ears"; property groups `is_rigid`, `is_flexible`, `has_base_spring`, and `is_boing` (Source 2013 only); `is_rigid`/`is_flexible` mutually exclusive, as are `has_base_spring`/`is_boing`; properties include stiffness, damping, mass and constraints — [Valve Developer Community: $jigglebone (snippet)](https://developer.valvesoftware.com/wiki/$jigglebone); [VDC: Jiggle Bone](https://developer.valvesoftware.com/wiki/Jiggle_Bone)

### Inferences
- A jiggle bone is a damped spring on a bone tip: per frame, integrate tip position toward the animated target with stiffness k and damping c, clamp to angle/length limits, re-aim the bone. This is a few dozen floating-point ops per bone on CPU — trivially affordable in UE2 UnrealScript if script can set bone rotations. Memory notes say script bone posing in UE2/U2 is blocked; if so, jiggle would have to be done in native/engine code (patched CPU skinning) or by baking jiggle as extra animation channels.
- The "soft body" look of SFM/Blender renders the user associates with is largely: (1) jiggle bones on belly/chest/cheeks/ears, (2) corrective flex shapes at joints, (3) offline cloth/softbody sims in Blender — only (1) and (2) are real-time.

### Gaps
- No primary sources fetched for fighting-game jiggle (Dead or Alive "soft engine", Tekken, Street Fighter) — a known topic but uncited here.
- Valve page body itself could not be fetched (403); exact parameter list and simulation integration method are from the snippet only.
